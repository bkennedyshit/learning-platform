# 🛤️ Latin — Learning Path

> This document is **hand-curated** and should remain stable.

---

## 📋 Prerequisites

Before starting this track, you should have:

1. **English grammar fluency** — Latin grammar explanations use terms like nominative, accusative, genitive, dative, ablative, subjunctive, gerund, gerundive, indirect statement, ablative absolute. These are not intuitive without an English grammar foundation. See [[38 - English/Subject_Plan|Track 19 English]] if needed.
2. **Spanish, French, or Italian (strongly recommended, not required)** — Romance language background makes Latin vocabulary acquisition ~3× faster. Latin paradigms will feel like an underlying pattern you're finally seeing in full.
3. **A spaced-repetition tool** — Anki. Latin vocabulary must be learned with its principal parts (4 forms per verb, full paradigm per noun declension).
4. **Wheelock's Latin (7th ed.) or free equivalent** — Tex's French Grammar is not applicable here; for Latin, Wheelock's or the free LLPSI (Lingua Latina per se Illustrata) are the primary textbooks.

## 📖 Recommended Chapter Order

### Phase 1: The Architecture — Alphabet Through Pronouns (Weeks 1–6)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale | Est. Time |
|-------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | [[43.1 - The Latin Alphabet, Pronunciation & Quantities]] | Latin pronunciation is fully reconstructable; read aloud from day one | 0.5 week |
| 2 | [[43.2 - The Case System — Nouns & The 5 Declensions]] | The entire language rests on cases — do not rush this; drill all 5 paradigms until automatic | 3 weeks |
| 3 | [[43.3 - Adjectives, Pronouns & Agreement]] | Agreement logic + demonstratives; requires solid noun paradigms as foundation | 2.5 weeks |

**Phase 1 deliverable:** Parse any 1st or 2nd declension noun form (case + number) without hesitation. Decline *puella*, *servus*, *bellum* completely from memory. Understand a simple Latin sentence: *"Puella aquam portat."*

> ⚠️ **The most common beginner mistake:** Skipping paradigm memorization. Latin cannot be "intuited" the way spoken Romance languages partially can. The declension and conjugation tables **must** be committed to memory. There is no shortcut. Flashcards + daily recitation for the first 6 weeks.

### Phase 2: The Verb System — Active & Passive (Weeks 7–14)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 4 | [[43.4 - The Verb System — Present System Active & Passive]] | Present, imperfect, future — active and passive; introduces esse/sum |
| 5 | [[43.5 - The Perfect System & Infinitives]] | Complete the indicative + introduces indirect statement (the key Latin construction) |

**Phase 2 deliverable:** Conjugate any regular Latin verb in all 6 tenses (indicative active + passive) from memory. Parse *"Gallia est omnis divisa in partes tres"* (Caesar I.1) completely. Construct an indirect statement sentence.

### Phase 3: Advanced Grammar (Weeks 15–22)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 6 | [[43.6 - Participles, Gerunds & The Subjunctive]] | Participles and ablative absolute are everywhere in Caesar; subjunctive unlocks all subordinate clauses |
| 7 | [[43.7 - Advanced Syntax — Subordinate Clauses & Rhetorical Style]] | Sequence of tenses + conditions + periodic sentence — required for Cicero |

**Phase 3 deliverable:** Parse and translate an ablative absolute. Correctly identify a purpose clause vs. a result clause. Translate a 5-sentence passage of adapted Caesar with a dictionary.

### Phase 4: Reading Classical Latin (Weeks 23–36+)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 8 | [[43.8 - Reading Latin — Caesar, Cicero, Virgil & Ovid]] | The payoff — reading authentic texts with Perseus + commentary support |

**Phase 4 deliverable:** Read Caesar *BG* I.1 cold (no prior preparation). Work through Cicero *In Catilinam* I.1 with commentary. Scan and translate 4 lines of Virgil's *Aeneid*.

## 🔀 Non-Negotiable Daily Habits (Every Phase)

| Daily habit | Time | Why |
|-------------|------|-----|
| Paradigm recitation (declension tables) | 10 min (Phase 1–2) | The only way to internalize case endings is repetition — not reading about them |
| Verb conjugation drills | 10 min (Phase 2–3) | All 4 conjugations, all 6 tenses, active + passive |
| Anki — Latin vocabulary (principal parts) | 15–20 min | Each verb needs 4 forms memorized; top 500 Latin words cover 70% of any text |
| Reading session with parsing | 20–30 min (Phase 2+) | Use Perseus or Whitaker's Words; parse every ambiguous form |

> **The Latin plateau:** Most learners plateau after completing grammar study because they don't have enough vocabulary to read fluently. The fix is: read short passages (even 5 sentences) daily from chapter 43.5 onward, even while still learning grammar. Vocabulary builds from exposure, not from lists alone.

## ➡️ What Comes Next

After completing this Latin track:

1. **Italian** — [[42 - Italian/Subject_Plan|Track 19 Italian]] if not already done; Latin → Italian is the most natural bridge
2. **Greek** — Ancient Greek uses a similar case system (5 cases) plus the dual number; Latin is the perfect preparation
3. **Medieval Latin** — Church Latin (Augustine, Thomas Aquinas, Dante's *De Vulgari Eloquentia*) uses the same grammar with a simplified vocabulary
4. **Latin prose composition** — Bradley's Arnold or North & Hillard workbooks; formal Latin writing for advanced mastery
5. **Deeper classical reading** — Livy, Tacitus, Sallust (historians); Plautus, Terence (comedy); Pliny (natural history and letters); Lucretius *De Rerum Natura* (Epicurean philosophy in poetry)

## 🚫 What This Track Does NOT Cover

- **Ancient Greek** — related but a separate language and script; Latin is excellent preparation
- **Medieval / Neo-Latin** — uses classical Latin grammar; covered by extending this curriculum with appropriate texts
- **Ecclesiastical Latin pronunciation** — this track teaches reconstructed classical pronunciation; chapter 43.1 notes the differences
- **Latin composition at advanced level** — Bradley's Arnold is the standard resource; beyond this track's scope
