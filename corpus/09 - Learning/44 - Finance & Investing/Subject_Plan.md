## 📝 3. Chapter Outline

| # | Chapter | Core skill |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 57.1 | **Money Fundamentals & Personal Finance** | The time value of money (TVM) — why a dollar today is worth more than a dollar tomorrow; compound interest (the most important mathematical concept in finance: A = P(1+r/n)^nt); inflation and purchasing power erosion; budgeting frameworks (50/30/20, zero-based); emergency fund (3-6 months expenses, liquid); debt hierarchy (interest rates — pay highest first); credit scores (what they are, what affects them, why they matter for funding); net worth calculation (assets minus liabilities) as the actual financial metric. |
| 57.2 | **Investing Fundamentals — How Markets Work** | Asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate, commodities, cash); risk/return trade-off (higher expected return = higher risk — this is not negotiable, not a trick to beat); diversification (the only free lunch in investing — reducing unsystematic risk without reducing expected return); market efficiency (most active managers underperform the index over 15+ year periods — the data is clear); index funds vs. active funds (Bogle's argument, the fee drag math); dollar-cost averaging (investing fixed amounts regularly regardless of price — removes the timing problem); compound growth over time (the math of long-term investing). |
| 57.3 | **Stocks — Understanding Equity** | What a share actually is (fractional ownership of a business); market cap vs. enterprise value; price/earnings ratio (P/E) and what it measures; price/book, EV/EBITDA, and other valuation multiples; dividend investing vs. growth investing; stock analysis: top-down (economy → sector → company) vs. bottom-up (company fundamentals first); why Warren Buffett's results are nearly impossible to replicate (survivorship bias, scale advantages, insurance float); the case for not trying to pick stocks. |
| 57.4 | **Reading Financial Statements** | The three financial statements and how they connect: Income Statement (revenue → gross profit → operating income → net income), Balance Sheet (assets = liabilities + equity — a snapshot in time), Cash Flow Statement (operating + investing + financing cash flows — the most honest of the three); key metrics from each (gross margin, operating margin, net margin, current ratio, debt-to-equity, free cash flow); why cash flow ≠ profit (accrual accounting); red flags in financial statements (revenue recognition tricks, goodwill impairment, off-balance-sheet liabilities). |
| 57.5 | **Startup Finance & Equity** | How startup funding works: pre-seed → seed → Series A/B/C → IPO; equity and dilution (if you raise $500K for 20% of your company, your 80% is worth more in absolute terms but you own less percentage — understanding when dilution is good); cap tables (who owns what, how it changes with each round); SAFEs and convertible notes (alternative to priced rounds in early stages); term sheets (key terms: valuation, liquidation preference, anti-dilution, pro-rata rights, board seats); founder vesting (4-year vesting, 1-year cliff — why this protects everyone); option pools and employee equity (RSUs vs. options, exercise prices, 83(b) elections). |
| 57.6 | **Tax Fundamentals for Entrepreneurs** | Income tax structure (marginal vs. effective tax rate — most people confuse these); self-employment tax (15.3% on top of income tax — often the surprise for first-time entrepreneurs); business structures (sole proprietor vs. LLC vs. S-corp vs. C-corp — and the tax implications of each); business deductions (home office, equipment, software, health insurance, retirement contributions); retirement accounts for self-employed (Solo 401k, SEP-IRA — dramatically reduce taxable income); capital gains tax (short-term vs. long-term — the 12-month holding threshold matters enormously); estimated quarterly taxes (pay as you go — not at year end). |
| 57.7 | **Personal Wealth Building — The Long Game** | Asset allocation strategy (age-based rules of thumb vs. risk-tolerance-based allocation); tax-advantaged accounts (401k/IRA contribution limits, Roth vs. Traditional, the lifetime value of tax-free growth); index fund selection (total market vs. S&P 500 vs. international — the three-fund portfolio); rebalancing (why and when — not too often); real estate as an asset class (leverage, cash flow, appreciation, tax advantages — and why it's not for everyone); the FIRE movement (Financial Independence, Retire Early) math: 25× annual expenses = your number (the 4% safe withdrawal rate); net worth milestones and their meaning. |
| 57.8 | **Behavioral Finance — Why Smart People Make Dumb Money Decisions** | Loss aversion (Kahneman & Tversky: losses feel ~2× worse than equivalent gains feel good — this distorts every financial decision); overconfidence bias (90% of investors think they're above average); anchoring (the first number you hear becomes a reference point regardless of its relevance); recency bias (the last 2 years feel like the permanent state of the market); the disposition effect (selling winners too early, holding losers too long); mental accounting (treating money differently based on where it came from); the psychology of the investor who consistently outperforms: index-fund discipline, ignoring the noise, not checking the account every day. Morgan Housel's framework throughout. |
