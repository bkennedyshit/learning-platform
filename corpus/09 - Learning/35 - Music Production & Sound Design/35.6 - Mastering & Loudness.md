# 35.6 — Mastering & Loudness

> *"Mastering is not about making things loud. It's about making things ready."*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

1. Explain the difference between **LUFS** (integrated loudness), **dBFS** (peak), and **dBTP** (true peak).
2. State the correct LUFS and true peak targets for Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube, and CD.
3. Set up a mastering chain: EQ → M/S processing → bus comp → saturation → stereo imager → limiter.

## 📚 1. Loudness Measurements Explained

### 1.1 dBFS — Digital Full Scale

dBFS measures the **instantaneous peak level** relative to the maximum digital level (0 dBFS). Simple but doesn't reflect perceived loudness.

- **0 dBFS = digital clip** (distortion)
- A kick drum hit might peak at -2 dBFS while the track feels quiet overall

### 1.2 LUFS — Loudness Units Full Scale

LUFS (Loudness Units relative to Full Scale) measures **perceived loudness** over time, using a frequency-weighting curve that models human hearing (K-weighting).

| Measurement | Time window | Use |
|-------------|-----------|-----|
| **Integrated LUFS** | Entire program | Platform targets — this is what you're matching |
| **Short-term LUFS** | 3 seconds | Monitoring loudness in sections |
| **Momentary LUFS** | 400ms | Monitoring instantaneous loudness |
| **LRA** (Loudness Range) | Full program | Dynamic range measurement — high LRA = varied dynamics |

### 1.3 dBTP — True Peak

True peak measures inter-sample peaks — brief overshoots that occur between samples due to reconstruction filtering. A file can have peaks at -0.5 dBFS but have true peaks at +0.5 dBFS.

**Streaming platforms and codecs (AAC, MP3)** can create additional inter-sample peaks during encoding. This is why platform guidelines specify -1 dBTP (not 0 dBFS) as the ceiling.

## 📚 2. Platform Targets (2026)

| Platform | Integrated LUFS | True Peak max | Notes |
|----------|---------------|--------------|-------|
| Spotify | -14 LUFS | -1 dBTP | Normalises down but not up |
| Apple Music | -16 LUFS | -1 dBTP | Atmos option |
| YouTube | -14 LUFS | -1 dBTP | Content loudness normalisation |
| Amazon Music | -14 LUFS | -2 dBTP | |
| Tidal | -14 LUFS | -1 dBTP | |
| SoundCloud | -14 LUFS | -1 dBTP | |
| CD (Redbook) | -9 to -12 LUFS | 0 dBTP | No normalisation — compete for loudness |
| Podcast (Apple) | -16 LUFS | -1 dBTP | |
| Broadcast TV (ATSC A/85) | -24 LUFS | -2 dBTP | US broadcast standard |

**Platform normalisation:** Streaming services normalise your track to their target. If your track is louder than -14 LUFS, it gets turned DOWN. There is no benefit to being louder than the target on streaming. Master to the target.

**Practical implication:** The "loudness war" is irrelevant for streaming. Master dynamically (-14 LUFS) with preserved transients and dynamics rather than brick-wall limiting.

## 📚 3. The Mastering Chain

### 3.1 Recommended Chain Order

```
1. Reference comparison (monitor mix against commercial reference)
2. Linear-phase EQ (correct spectral balance)
3. M/S processing (side EQ, width adjustment)
4. Bus compression (glue)
5. Saturation / harmonic exciter (warmth, loudness without limiting)
6. Stereo imager (fine-tune width; LP bass to mono)
7. Limiter (set ceiling -1 dBTP; bring integrated LUFS to target)
8. Final loudness meter check (Youlean)
```

### 3.2 Step-by-Step

**Step 1 — Reference:**
Import a commercial reference at the same LUFS as your mix. A/B between them. Identify the biggest spectral differences.

**Step 2 — Linear-Phase EQ:**
Make broad, gentle adjustments: low shelf, high shelf, one or two bells. Avoid surgical cuts at this stage (mastering is not mixing). Typical adjustments: +1.5 dB at 80 Hz shelf (add weight), +2 dB at 12 kHz shelf (add air).

**Step 3 — M/S Processing:**
- Mid channel: EQ the centre image (vocals, kick, bass)
- Side channel: EQ the stereo content; cut lows below 150–200 Hz (clean up side bass)
- May add subtle saturation to Mid only (add warmth without affecting stereo width)

**Step 4 — Bus Compression:**
- Ratio: 1.5:1 to 2:1
- Attack: 30–100ms (very slow — let transients through)
- Release: 300ms
- GR: 1–3 dB max
- Adds "glue" — the sense that the whole mix breathes together

**Step 5 — Saturation/Exciter:**
Gentle saturation (tube or tape) adds harmonics → increases perceived loudness without changing peak level → allows more headroom before limiting. Target +0.5–1 LUFS from saturation before limiter.

**Step 6 — Stereo Imager:**
Optional. Widen the sides slightly if the mix needs it. Never go too wide — check mono. Use Mid/Side EQ to keep bass in mono.

**Step 7 — Limiter:**
Set **ceiling to -1.0 dBTP**. Increase input gain until integrated LUFS reaches target (-14 LUFS for most streaming). Use true peak mode (oversampling limiter).

Free limiter: GVST GMax. Decent free option: Loudmax. Professional free: Limitless by Tokyo Dawn.

## 📚 4. Codec Impact

When you upload a 24-bit WAV to Spotify, it gets transcoded to OGG Vorbis (or AAC). The codec:
- Adds inter-sample peaks (reason for -1 dBTP ceiling)
- May affect transient clarity slightly
- Can cause high-frequency attenuation at lower bitrates

**Test before release:** Transcode your master to 256kbps AAC → listen on cheap earbuds → if it sounds bad, the issue is in the master, not the codec.

## 📚 5. Delivery Format Checklist

**Standard streaming master:**
- Format: WAV
- Bit depth: 24-bit
- Sample rate: 44.1 kHz
- Peak: -1 dBFS peak, -1 dBTP true peak
- Integrated LUFS: -14 LUFS ±0.5
- Metadata: Artist, Title, ISRC code (from your distribution service)

**Video/sync master:**
- Sample rate: 48 kHz (video standard)
- Otherwise same as above

**CD master:**
- Sample rate: 44.1 kHz
- Bit depth: 16-bit (dither from 24-bit)
- DDP image file for CD replication

**Streaming distribution services:** DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, Amuse — all accept 24-bit WAV.

## ⚠️ 6. Common Misconceptions

1. **"Louder masters sound better."** With streaming normalisation, they don't — they sound the same and have worse dynamics. A well-mastered track at -14 LUFS with 8 LRA sounds better than a brick-walled track at -6 LUFS.

2. **"I need expensive plugins to master."** SPAN (free) + Youlean (free) for metering. A decent free limiter + EQ. Reference tracks. Your ears. These are sufficient for self-mastered releases that meet platform specs.

3. **"Mastering fixes bad mixes."** Mastering can adjust the overall spectral balance. It cannot fix a kick that's too quiet, a vocal buried in the mix, or resonant frequencies. Fix the mix before mastering.

4. **"My master sounds great in my studio — it'll sound great everywhere."** Your monitors/room colour your perception. That's why reference tracks, loudness metering, and listening on different playback systems (phone, car, earbuds) are essential before release.

5. **"I need to hire a mastering engineer."** For learning and low-budget releases, self-mastering is viable. For important commercial releases, a professional mastering engineer adds significant value — they have calibrated rooms, reference library, and fresh ears.

6. **"Integrated LUFS is measured from any 3-second window."** Integrated LUFS is measured over the ENTIRE program (gated — silence excluded). It's not the same as short-term or momentary LUFS. Always check integrated LUFS, not short-term, for streaming compliance.
