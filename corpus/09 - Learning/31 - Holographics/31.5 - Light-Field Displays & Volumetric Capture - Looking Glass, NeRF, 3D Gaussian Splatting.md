## 📚 1. The Plenoptic Function

For each point in space + time + wavelength, the light field encodes radiance for every direction:
$$
L(x, y, z, \theta, \phi, \lambda, t)
$$

Most displays simplify to the **4D light field** $L(x, y, \theta, \phi)$ — radiance crossing a 2D surface in each 2D direction.

A "true hologram" is the *coherent* version of this — encoding phase as well. A "light-field display" is the *incoherent* version — many pixels × many directions.

## 🪟 2. Display Form Factors

### Looking Glass (Hololuminescent / HLD)
- **Up to 100 discrete views** within a ~58° cone.
- Lenticular / parallax-barrier optics over a high-PPI panel.
- 4K Looking Glass 2025 — $499–$2,750 depending on size.
- "**No headset, no enclosure, no moving parts.**" — paraphrased from [Looking Glass HLD overview](https://lookingglassfactory.com/hld-overview) (rephrased for compliance).

### Sony Spatial Reality Display (ELF-SR1 / SR2)
- Single-viewer, head-tracked, high-quality.
- For close-range professional 3D content review.

### Leia Inc. (mobile)
- Light-field LCD layer atop a phone screen — naked-eye 3D for handheld.

### Voxon Photonics (swept-volume)
- High-speed projector on a vertically-oscillating screen → physical voxel volume.
- Genuinely volumetric (parallax in all directions).

### Femtosecond aerial volumetric (Ochiai et al., 2015 onwards)
- Focused fs-laser ionizes air → emits light at 3D points.
- Demonstrated; not commercial.
- See [Fairy Lights in Femtoseconds (arXiv 1506.06668)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06668).

### Holographic-light-field 3D printing (Nature 2026)
- "**Sub-second volumetric 3D printing by synthesis of holographic light fields.**" — paraphrased from [Nature 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10114-5) (rephrased for compliance).
- Print an entire 3D object in the time light traverses a vat.

## 📦 3. Looking Glass Authoring Workflow

Looking Glass content is a **quilt** — a 2D atlas of N×M sub-views.

```
For each sub-view (i, j):
    place camera at virtual pose offset (i, j) in the light-field cone
    render
    paste into quilt cell (i, j)
```

Workflow options:
- **Unity / Unreal** plugins ship a "Looking Glass Camera" that produces the quilt.
- **Blender** add-on produces quilts.
- **Three.js / WebGL** via Looking Glass Bridge.
- **Python + glDraw** for offline quilt generation.

Display via **Looking Glass Bridge** (a small server on the host that sends the quilt to the device).

## 🌐 4. Volumetric Capture (NeRF + 3D Gaussian Splatting)

### NeRF (Mildenhall et al. 2020)
A neural network mapping $(x, y, z, \theta, \phi) \to (RGB, \sigma)$. Trained from posed 2D photos.
- Workflow: COLMAP (camera-pose recovery) → NeRF training → render novel views.
- Modern: **Nerfstudio** is the de-facto framework.
- Real-time variants: Instant-NGP, MipNeRF, K-Planes, ZipNeRF.

### 3D Gaussian Splatting (Kerbl et al. 2023)
Represent the scene as millions of anisotropic 3D Gaussians with color + opacity. Render by splatting.
- **Real-time** rendering (60+ FPS on consumer GPU).
- Editable, exportable, increasingly the dominant volumetric format in 2025–2026.
- Frameworks: **gsplat**, **3DGS** original, **gaussian-splatting-cuda**, integration into **Three.js**, **Unity**, **Unreal**.

### Pipeline for a 2026 arch-viz volumetric capture
1. Capture 100–500 photos with phone or DSLR.
2. COLMAP for camera poses.
3. Train 3DGS or NeRF (Nerfstudio).
4. Export PLY / SPZ.
5. Import into Looking Glass / Vision Pro / Unity.

---

## 🛠️ 5. Worked Example (skeleton) — Quilt + 3DGS Combo

1. Capture a small interior space with 200 phone photos.
2. Run **gsplat** to produce a 3DGS model.
3. Inside a 3DGS-aware viewer (Unity, Three.js, or a custom WebGL canvas), set up 45 cameras at the Looking Glass cone.
4. Render to a 9×5 quilt.
5. Push to Looking Glass via Bridge.

You now have a **walkable volumetric capture on a hologram-display device** — a deliverable few competitors can ship in 2026.

## ⚠️ 7. Common Misconceptions

- **"Light-field display = hologram."** Different physics — light fields are incoherent multi-view; holograms are coherent diffraction.
- **"NeRF is dead now that 3DGS exists."** They have different sweet spots (NeRF for distant scenes / extrapolation; 3DGS for fast real-time + editable).
- **"More views in a Looking Glass = better."** Past a point you trade brightness for parallax discreteness without visible gain.
- **"Volumetric capture is one-click."** Quality requires careful capture (overlap, exposure, focus), pose recovery, and post-processing.
