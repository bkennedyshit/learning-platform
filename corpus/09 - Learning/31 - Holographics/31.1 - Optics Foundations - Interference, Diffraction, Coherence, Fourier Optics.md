# 31.1 — Optics Foundations: Interference, Diffraction, Coherence, Fourier Optics

> *"In ray optics, light is a particle. In wave optics, light is a Fourier transform. Hold that second view and holography becomes inevitable."*

## 📚 1. The Wave Equation & Helmholtz

In a homogeneous medium with speed $c$, the scalar wave equation:
$$
\nabla^2 U - \frac{1}{c^2}\frac{\partial^2 U}{\partial t^2} = 0
$$

For monochromatic light $U(r, t) = u(r)\,e^{-i\omega t}$, the spatial part obeys the **Helmholtz equation**:
$$
\nabla^2 u + k^2 u = 0,\quad k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}
$$

---

## 🌊 2. Two-Beam Interference

Two coherent plane waves of amplitudes $A_1, A_2$, wave-vectors $\mathbf{k}_1, \mathbf{k}_2$:
$$
I(x) = |A_1 + A_2|^2 = |A_1|^2 + |A_2|^2 + 2|A_1||A_2|\cos[(\mathbf{k}_1 - \mathbf{k}_2)\cdot \mathbf{r} + \Delta\phi]
$$

The **fringe spacing** is $\Lambda = \lambda / (2\sin(\theta/2))$ for two beams crossing at angle $\theta$.

**This is the hologram-recording equation.** Replace one beam with a structured "object wave" and the recorded interference pattern *is* the hologram.

---

## ⚡ 3. Fraunhofer & Fresnel Diffraction

The Rayleigh-Sommerfeld integral propagates a field $u_0(x_0, y_0)$ at $z=0$ to $u(x, y, z)$:
$$
u(x, y, z) = \frac{1}{i\lambda} \iint u_0(x_0, y_0)\,\frac{e^{ikr}}{r}\,dx_0\,dy_0
$$

Two limiting regimes:

- **Fraunhofer (far-field):**
$$
u(x, y, z) \propto \mathcal{F}\{u_0\}\big|_{f_x = x/(\lambda z),\ f_y = y/(\lambda z)}
$$
  — diffraction = Fourier transform of the aperture.

- **Fresnel (near-field):** quadratic phase factor before / after the FT — close-range propagation.

### Angular Spectrum
A bandwidth-friendly numerical propagator:
1. $\tilde u_0(f_x, f_y) = \mathcal{F}\{u_0\}$
2. $\tilde u(f_x, f_y) = \tilde u_0 \cdot e^{ikz\sqrt{1 - (\lambda f_x)^2 - (\lambda f_y)^2}}$
3. $u(x, y) = \mathcal{F}^{-1}\{\tilde u\}$

This is **two FFTs + a multiplication** — the workhorse of [[31.3 - Computer-Generated Holography - Algorithms, FFT, Neural CGH|CGH]].

## 🔆 4. Coherence

| Type | Characterization | Why holography needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Temporal | Spectral bandwidth $\Delta\nu$ → coherence time $\tau_c = 1/\Delta\nu$ | Need fringes stable over recording time |
| Spatial | Source size & geometry | Need plane-wave-like reference |
| Combined | Mutual coherence $\Gamma_{12}(\tau)$ | Visibility = $|\gamma_{12}|$ |

A laser gives you both. A lightbulb gives you neither.

---

## 🔍 5. Fourier Optics in One Sentence

A thin converging lens of focal length $f$ placed between two planes one focal length apart performs a **2D Fourier transform** of the front-focal-plane field onto the back focal plane.

That is the foundation of **4-f systems**, **spatial filtering**, and **most modern computational displays**.

---

## 🛠️ 6. Worked Example (skeleton) — Numerical Diffraction in NumPy

```python
import numpy as np

def angular_spectrum(u0, dx, wavelength, z):
    Ny, Nx = u0.shape
    fx = np.fft.fftfreq(Nx, dx)
    fy = np.fft.fftfreq(Ny, dx)
    FX, FY = np.meshgrid(fx, fy, indexing='xy')
    k = 2*np.pi/wavelength
    kz_sq = 1 - (wavelength*FX)**2 - (wavelength*FY)**2
    H = np.exp(1j*k*z*np.sqrt(np.maximum(kz_sq, 0)))
    return np.fft.ifft2(np.fft.fft2(u0) * H)
```

Use it to propagate a circular aperture — you should see Airy rings appear (Fraunhofer regime) at large $z$.

## ⚠️ 8. Common Misconceptions

- **"Wave optics is just optics with extra steps."** Wave + ray descriptions agree at large apertures, but every interesting holographic effect lives in the regime where they disagree.
- **"FFT diffraction is exact."** Angular spectrum + Fresnel are valid in different regimes; outside their validity the numerical answer is wrong.
- **"Coherence is just lasers vs not."** Partial coherence is a continuum; many displays exploit *partial spatial coherence*.
- **"Fraunhofer = far field at infinity."** Fraunhofer regime is reached when the Fresnel number $F = a^2/(\lambda z) \ll 1$ — practically a few wavelengths of aperture suffice for visible light at meter distances.
