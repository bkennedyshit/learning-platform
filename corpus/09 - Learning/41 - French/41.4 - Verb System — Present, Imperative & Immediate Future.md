# 41.4 — Verb System: Present, Imperative & Immediate Future

*Back to [[Bill's Vault/05-Knowledge_Foundation/09 - Learning/41 - French/Subject_Plan]] | [[41.3 - Pronouns — Subject, Object & Reflexive|← 41.3]] | [[41.5 - Past Tenses — Passé Composé, Imparfait & Plus-que-parfait|41.5 →]]*

> **Chapter goal:** Command the full French present tense across all three verb groups and the most common irregular verbs; master the imperative; use *aller + infinitif* for the immediate future; and handle negation, modal constructions, and reflexive verbs with confidence.

---

## 1. The Three Verb Groups

French classifies verbs by their infinitive ending:

| Group | Infinitive ending | Example | Size |
|-------|-----------------|---------|------|
| **1st group** | **-ER** | *parler, aimer, manger, acheter* | ~90% of all verbs; almost all new verbs coined |
| **2nd group** | **-IR** (with -iss- infix) | *finir, choisir, réfléchir, obéir* | ~300 verbs; very regular |
| **3rd group** | Everything else: -RE, -IR (no -iss-), -OIR | *vendre, partir, voir, vouloir* | Irregular; must be memorised individually |

> **Practical insight:** If you encounter a new verb ending in -ER, it conjugates exactly like *parler*. If it ends in -IR and the *nous*-form has -issons, it conjugates like *finir*. Everything else belongs to the irregular third group.

## 2. Present Tense — Regular Conjugations

### 2.1 Group 1: -ER verbs (*parler* — to speak)

Remove -ER → stem **parl-** + endings:

| Person | Ending | Form | Pronunciation |
|--------|--------|------|--------------|
| je | **-e** | *parle* | /paʁl/ — final -e silent |
| tu | **-es** | *parles* | /paʁl/ — final -es silent |
| il/elle/on | **-e** | *parle* | /paʁl/ — same as je |
| nous | **-ons** | *parlons* | /paʁlɔ̃/ |
| vous | **-ez** | *parlez* | /paʁle/ |
| ils/elles | **-ent** | *parlent* | /paʁl/ — final -ent **completely silent** |

> ⚠️ **The -ent trap:** *ils parlent* sounds **identical** to *il parle*. French grammar is carried in writing, not in spoken verb endings for group 1. Context and subject pronouns carry all the person/number information in speech.

**Spelling-change -ER verbs (stem changes, not irregular):**

| Type | Rule | Example |
|------|------|---------|
| *-ger* verbs | Add *-e* before *-ons*: | *manger → nous mangeons* |
| *-cer* verbs | Change *c → ç* before *-ons*: | *commencer → nous commençons* |
| *e + consonant + -er* | *e → è* in stressed stems: | *acheter → j'achète, nous achetons* |
| *é + consonant + -er* | *é → è* in stressed stems: | *préférer → je préfère, nous préférons* |
| *-eler / -eter* verbs | Double consonant OR add grave: | *appeler → j'appelle; jeter → je jette* |
| *-yer* verbs | *y → i* before silent -e: | *payer → je paie, nous payons* |

### 2.2 Group 2: -IR verbs with -iss- (*finir* — to finish)

Remove -IR → stem **fin-** + endings (note -iss- in plural forms):

| Person | Ending | Form |
|--------|--------|------|
| je | **-is** | *finis* |
| tu | **-is** | *finis* |
| il/elle/on | **-it** | *finit* |
| nous | **-issons** | *finissons* |
| vous | **-issez** | *finissez* |
| ils/elles | **-issent** | *finissent* |

Other group 2 verbs: *choisir* (to choose), *réfléchir* (to think/reflect), *obéir* (to obey), *grandir* (to grow), *rougir* (to blush), *réussir* (to succeed), *remplir* (to fill), *punir* (to punish).

### 2.3 Group 3: -RE verbs (*vendre* — to sell)

Remove -RE → stem **vend-** + endings:

| Person | Ending | Form |
|--------|--------|------|
| je | **-s** | *vends* |
| tu | **-s** | *vends* |
| il/elle/on | **∅** (nothing) | *vend* |
| nous | **-ons** | *vendons* |
| vous | **-ez** | *vendez* |
| ils/elles | **-ent** | *vendent* |

Other -RE verbs: *répondre* (to answer), *attendre* (to wait), *entendre* (to hear), *rendre* (to return/give back), *perdre* (to lose), *descendre* (to go down).

## 4. Modal Verbs + Infinitive

*Vouloir, pouvoir, devoir, savoir* function as modals — they take a following infinitive directly (no preposition):

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| *vouloir + inf* | to want to | *Je veux partir.* — I want to leave. |
| *pouvoir + inf* | to be able to / can | *Tu peux venir.* — You can come. |
| *devoir + inf* | to have to / must | *Il doit travailler.* — He must work. |
| *savoir + inf* | to know how to | *Elle sait nager.* — She knows how to swim. |

> *Savoir* vs. *connaître*: **savoir** = know a fact or how to do something; **connaître** = know a person, place, or work (*Je connais Paris. / Je sais parler français.*).

## 5. Negation in the Present Tense

Standard negation wraps the verb in **ne ... pas**:

```
Subject + ne (n') + verb + pas + rest of sentence
Je ne parle pas anglais.       I don't speak English.
Il ne sait pas conduire.       He doesn't know how to drive.
```

**Other negation patterns:**

| Pattern | Meaning | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| *ne ... plus* | no longer / not anymore | *Je ne fume plus.* |
| *ne ... jamais* | never | *Il ne ment jamais.* |
| *ne ... rien* | nothing | *Je ne vois rien.* |
| *ne ... personne* | nobody | *Elle ne connaît personne.* |
| *ne ... que* | only | *Je n'ai que dix euros.* |
| *ne ... ni ... ni* | neither ... nor | *Il ne boit ni café ni thé.* |

> **Spoken French:** In casual speech, the *ne* is almost always dropped: *"Je sais pas."* / *"C'est pas vrai."* — you will hear this constantly. Never drop *pas* though.

---

## 6. The Immediate Future: *aller* + infinitive

The most common way to express the near future in spoken French:

```
Subject + conjugated form of aller + infinitive
Je vais manger.          I'm going to eat. / I'm about to eat.
Nous allons partir.      We're going to leave.
Il va pleuvoir.          It's going to rain.
```

**Negation:** wrap *aller* in ne...pas, leaving the infinitive outside:
```
Je ne vais pas sortir ce soir.     I'm not going to go out tonight.
```

**With object pronouns:** pronoun goes before the **infinitive**:
```
Je vais le faire.        I'm going to do it.
Tu vas lui parler.       You're going to talk to him/her.
```

> The immediate future is used far more than the simple future (*je ferai*) in everyday spoken French — especially for anything happening within the current day or in the near term.

## 7. The Imperative

Used to give commands, instructions, invitations, suggestions.

**Formation:** take the present-tense form for *tu*, *nous*, *vous* — **drop the subject pronoun** — and for -ER verbs, **drop the -s** in the *tu* form:

| Infinitive | tu (imperative) | nous | vous |
|------------|----------------|------|------|
| *parler* | **Parle !** (not *parles*) | **Parlons !** | **Parlez !** |
| *finir* | **Finis !** | **Finissons !** | **Finissez !** |
| *vendre* | **Vends !** | **Vendons !** | **Vendez !** |
| *être* | **Sois !** | **Soyons !** | **Soyez !** |
| *avoir* | **Aie !** | **Ayons !** | **Ayez !** |
| *savoir* | **Sache !** | **Sachons !** | **Sachez !** |
| *aller* | **Va !** | **Allons !** | **Allez !** |

> ⚠️ The *tu* -ER form drops its -s **except** when followed by *y* or *en* (to preserve liaison): *Vas-y !* (Go ahead! / Go there!), *Manges-en !* (Eat some of it!).

**Object pronouns with the imperative:**

- *Positive imperative:* pronoun comes **after** the verb, joined by a hyphen. *me/te* become **moi/toi**:
  - *Parle-moi !* — Talk to me!
  - *Donne-le-lui !* — Give it to him/her!
  - *Mangez-en !* — Eat some!

- *Negative imperative:* pronoun stays **before** the verb (standard position):
  - *Ne me parle pas !* — Don't talk to me!
  - *Ne le fais pas !* — Don't do it!

## 8. Reflexive Verbs in the Present Tense

Reflexive verbs (*se lever, se coucher, s'appeler*, etc.) conjugate normally — the reflexive pronoun changes with each person and precedes the verb:

| *se lever* (to get up) | |
|------------------------|--|
| je **me** lève | nous **nous** levons |
| tu **te** lèves | vous **vous** levez |
| il/elle **se** lève | ils/elles **se** lèvent |

> Note the stem change: *lever* has *e → è* in stressed syllables (same pattern as *acheter*).

**Common reflexive verbs:**
*se lever, se coucher, se réveiller, se laver, se brosser (les dents), s'habiller, se dépêcher, se souvenir de, s'appeler, s'ennuyer, se sentir, se reposer, se promener, se retrouver, se disputer*

## 9. Uses of the Present Tense

French present covers more ground than English:

| Use | French | English |
|-----|--------|---------|
| Current action | *Il mange.* | He is eating. / He eats. |
| Habitual action | *Je prends le métro.* | I take the metro. |
| General truth | *L'eau bout à 100°C.* | Water boils at 100°C. |
| Near future (with time marker) | *Je pars demain.* | I'm leaving tomorrow. |
| Historical present (narrative) | *En 1789, la Révolution éclate.* | In 1789, the Revolution breaks out. |
| Since (*depuis*) + present | *J'habite ici depuis 5 ans.* | I've been living here for 5 years. (NOT past tense in French) |

> **The *depuis* rule is critical:** French uses the **present tense** where English uses the present perfect to express an action that started in the past and is still ongoing: *"Je travaille ici depuis deux ans."* = "I have been working here for two years."

---

## 10. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **Using *savoir* for knowing people:** *Je sais Marie* is wrong. *Je connais Marie.* — Know a person → *connaître*.
2. **Pronouncing -ent on 3rd plural:** *ils parlent* = /paʁl/. The -ent is always silent on verbs.
3. **Not dropping -s on *tu* imperative for -ER verbs:** *"Parles français!"* → wrong. *"Parle français!"*
4. **Putting pronouns after the verb in negative imperative:** *"Ne fais-le pas"* → wrong. *"Ne le fais pas."*
5. **Using *depuis* + past tense:** *J'ai habité ici depuis 5 ans* → wrong for an ongoing situation. *J'habite ici depuis 5 ans.*

## 9. Uses of the Present Tense

| Use | French example | English equivalent |
|-----|---------------|-------------------|
| Current action | *Il mange.* | He is eating. / He eats. |
| Habitual action | *Je prends le métro.* | I take the metro. |
| General truth | *L'eau bout à 100°C.* | Water boils at 100°C. |
| Near future | *Je pars demain.* | I'm leaving tomorrow. |
| *depuis* + ongoing action | *J'habite ici depuis 5 ans.* | I've been living here for 5 years. |

> **The *depuis* rule:** French uses the **present tense** where English uses the present perfect for ongoing actions. *J'étudie le français depuis trois ans.* = "I have been studying French for three years."

---

## 10. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **Using *savoir* for knowing people:** *Je sais Marie* → wrong. *Je connais Marie.*
2. **Pronouncing -ent on 3rd plural:** *ils parlent* = /paʁl/. The -ent is always silent.
3. **Not dropping -s on *tu* imperative for -ER verbs:** *"Parles français!"* → wrong. *"Parle français!"*
4. **Putting pronouns after verb in negative imperative:** *"Ne fais-le pas"* → wrong. *"Ne le fais pas."*
5. **Using *depuis* + past tense for ongoing situation:** *J'ai habité ici depuis 5 ans* → wrong. *J'habite ici depuis 5 ans.*
