# 41.5 — Past Tenses: Passé Composé, Imparfait & Plus-que-parfait

*Back to [[Bill's Vault/05-Knowledge_Foundation/09 - Learning/41 - French/Subject_Plan]] | [[41.4 - Verb System — Present, Imperative & Immediate Future|← 41.4]] | [[41.6 - Future, Conditional & The Subjunctive|41.6 →]]*

> **Chapter goal:** Master the three French past tenses — their formation, their meaning, and most critically the decision tree for choosing between passé composé and imparfait in any given sentence.

---

## 1. The Two Main Past Tenses: A Conceptual Map

French has two primary past tenses that **divide up the English simple past** between them:

| | Passé composé | Imparfait |
|--|--------------|-----------|
| **English equivalent** | Simple past or present perfect: "I ate / I have eaten" | "I was eating / I used to eat / I would eat (habitually)" |
| **View of the action** | **Bounded** — a completed event with a clear start or end | **Unbounded** — ongoing, habitual, background, or interrupted |
| **Answers the question** | "What happened?" | "What was the situation? What was going on?" |
| **Metaphor** | Foreground of a film — the plot events | Background of a film — the scenery and atmosphere |

> **The core principle:** The passé composé advances the narrative (things happen, one after another). The imparfait paints the background (what was already in motion, what the scene looked like).

---

## 2. Passé Composé — Formation

**Structure:** conjugated form of *avoir* or *être* (present tense) + **past participle**

### 2.1 Past Participle Formation

| Verb group | Rule | Examples |
|------------|------|---------|
| -ER verbs | Remove -ER, add **-é** | *parler → parlé, manger → mangé, aller → allé* |
| -IR verbs (group 2) | Remove -IR, add **-i** | *finir → fini, choisir → choisi* |
| -RE verbs | Remove -RE, add **-u** | *vendre → vendu, attendre → attendu* |

**Irregular past participles — must be memorised:**

| Infinitive | Past participle | Infinitive | Past participle |
|-----------|----------------|-----------|----------------|
| *être* | **été** | *avoir* | **eu** |
| *faire* | **fait** | *dire* | **dit** |
| *prendre* | **pris** | *mettre* | **mis** |
| *voir* | **vu** | *lire* | **lu** |
| *écrire* | **écrit** | *ouvrir* | **ouvert** |
| *venir* | **venu** | *tenir* | **tenu** |
| *pouvoir* | **pu** | *vouloir* | **voulu** |
| *savoir* | **su** | *devoir* | **dû** |
| *boire* | **bu** | *croire* | **cru** |
| *recevoir* | **reçu** | *naître* | **né** |
| *mourir* | **mort** | *courir* | **couru** |

### 2.2 Which Auxiliary? *avoir* vs. *être*

**Default: use *avoir*** (with the vast majority of verbs)

**Use *être* with:**

1. **DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP verbs** (all intransitive motion/state-change verbs):

| Verb | Past participle |
|------|----------------|
| **D**evenir | devenu |
| **R**evenir | revenu |
| **M**onter | monté |
| **R**ester | resté |
| **S**ortir | sorti |
| **V**enir | venu |
| **A**ller | allé |
| **N**aître | né |
| **D**escendre | descendu |
| **E**ntrer | entré |
| **R**entrer | rentré |
| **T**omber | tombé |
| **R**etourner | retourné |
| **A**rriver | arrivé |
| **M**ourir | mort |
| **P**artir | parti |

2. **All reflexive/pronominal verbs**: *se lever → je me suis levé(e)*

### 2.3 Past Participle Agreement with *être*

When *être* is the auxiliary, the past participle **agrees with the subject** in gender and number (like an adjective):

| Subject | Agreement | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| Masculine singular | no change | *Il est parti.* |
| Feminine singular | add -e | *Elle est partie.* |
| Masculine plural | add -s | *Ils sont partis.* |
| Feminine plural | add -es | *Elles sont parties.* |

> **With *avoir*:** the past participle does **not** agree with the subject. However, it agrees with a preceding direct object: *La lettre que j'ai écrite.* (The letter that I wrote — *lettre* is fem., precedes the verb → *écrite*.)

### 2.4 Passé Composé Examples

```
J'ai mangé une pomme.         I ate / have eaten an apple.
Elle a fini ses devoirs.       She finished her homework.
Nous avons vu ce film.         We saw / have seen that film.
Il est allé au marché.         He went to the market.
Elles sont arrivées en retard. They (f.) arrived late.
Je me suis réveillé(e) tôt.   I woke up early.
```

**Negation:** ne...pas wraps the **auxiliary**:
```
Je n'ai pas mangé.             I didn't eat.
Elle n'est pas partie.         She didn't leave.
```

## 3. Imparfait — Formation

**Structure:** Take the **nous-form of the present tense**, remove -ons → add imparfait endings.

| Person | Ending | *parler* (parl-) | *finir* (finiss-) | *vendre* (vend-) |
|--------|--------|-----------------|------------------|----------------|
| je | **-ais** | *parlais* | *finissais* | *vendais* |
| tu | **-ais** | *parlais* | *finissais* | *vendais* |
| il/elle | **-ait** | *parlait* | *finissait* | *vendait* |
| nous | **-ions** | *parlions* | *finissions* | *vendions* |
| vous | **-iez** | *parliez* | *finissiez* | *vendiez* |
| ils/elles | **-aient** | *parlaient* | *finissaient* | *vendaient* |

> **Only one irregular imparfait:** *être* → stem **ét-**: *j'étais, tu étais, il était, nous étions, vous étiez, ils étaient*. Every other verb uses the nous-present stem.

**Spelling-change preservation:**
- *manger*: nous mangeons → stem *mange-* → *je mangeais, il mangeait* (keeps -e- throughout)
- *commencer*: nous commençons → stem *commenc-* → *je commençais*

## 4. The PC vs. Imparfait Decision Tree

This is **the most important concept in French past-tense grammar.** Apply this tree to every verb in a past-tense narrative:

```
Is the action COMPLETED with a defined beginning or end?
    → YES: Passé composé
    → NO: Imparfait

Is the action HABITUAL or REPEATED in the past?
    → YES (habitual): Imparfait   (Il jouait au foot tous les samedis.)
    → YES (listed/counted events): Passé composé  (Il a joué trois fois.)

Is the action BACKGROUND STATE / DESCRIPTION / ONGOING CONDITION?
    → YES: Imparfait
    → NO: Passé composé

Is the action INTERRUPTED by another?
    → Interrupted action: Imparfait
    → Interrupting action: Passé composé
```

**Classic pattern — interrupted action:**
```
Je dormais (imparfait — ongoing) quand le téléphone a sonné (PC — event).
I was sleeping when the phone rang.
```

**Classic pattern — narrative sequence (all PC):**
```
Je me suis levé, j'ai pris une douche, j'ai mangé et je suis parti.
I got up, took a shower, ate, and left.
```

**Classic pattern — description (all imparfait):**
```
Il faisait beau. Les enfants jouaient dans le parc. Tout était calme.
The weather was nice. The children were playing in the park. Everything was calm.
```

### Verbs That Change Meaning: PC vs. Imparfait

| Verb | Imparfait | Passé composé |
|------|-----------|--------------|
| *savoir* | knew (ongoing state) | found out / learned (at a moment) |
| *connaître* | knew / was acquainted with | met for the first time |
| *pouvoir* | was able to / could (general) | managed to / succeeded in |
| *vouloir* | wanted (ongoing) | decided to / tried to |
| *devoir* | was supposed to | had to (and did) |

*Je savais la réponse.* (I knew the answer — general knowledge)
*J'ai su la réponse.* (I found out the answer — at a specific moment)

## 5. Plus-que-parfait (Past Perfect)

**Meaning:** "had done" — an action completed **before** another past action.
**Formation:** *avoir/être* in **imparfait** + past participle (same auxiliaries and agreement rules as passé composé)

| | *avoir* | *être* |
|--|--------|--------|
| je | *avais* + pp | *étais* + pp |
| tu | *avais* + pp | *étais* + pp |
| il/elle | *avait* + pp | *était* + pp |
| nous | *avions* + pp | *étions* + pp |
| vous | *aviez* + pp | *étiez* + pp |
| ils/elles | *avaient* + pp | *étaient* + pp |

**Examples:**
```
Quand je suis arrivé, elle était déjà partie.
When I arrived, she had already left.

Il avait mangé avant de venir.
He had eaten before coming.

Nous avions fini le travail quand le patron est arrivé.
We had finished the work when the boss arrived.
```

**Primary uses:**
1. **Sequence of past events:** the earlier action → plus-que-parfait; the later action → passé composé
2. **Reported speech / indirect speech** shifting a passé composé back one tense: *Il m'a dit qu'il avait vu ce film.* (He told me that he had seen that film.)
3. **Hypothetical past with si:** *Si j'avais su, j'aurais agi différemment.* (If I had known, I would have acted differently.) — see chapter 41.6 for si-clauses.

## 6. The Passé Simple (Literary Past)

The **passé simple** is a written literary past tense — you will encounter it constantly in French literature, journalism, and formal writing, but you should be able to **recognise** it rather than produce it in everyday French.

**Formation:** infinitive stem + passé simple endings:

| | -ER verbs | -IR/-RE verbs |
|--|----------|--------------|
| je | -ai | -is |
| tu | -as | -is |
| il/elle | -a | -it |
| nous | -âmes | -îmes |
| vous | -âtes | -îtes |
| ils/elles | -èrent | -irent |

*parler → il parla / ils parlèrent*
*finir → il finit / ils finirent*
*vendre → il vendit / ils vendirent*

**Irregular passé simple stems (most common):**

| Infinitive | Passé simple stem | Example |
|-----------|------------------|---------|
| *être* | fu- | *il fut* |
| *avoir* | eu- | *il eut* |
| *faire* | fi- | *il fit* |
| *venir* | vin- | *il vint* |
| *voir* | vi- | *il vit* |
| *prendre* | pri- | *il prit* |
| *dire* | di- | *il dit* |
| *écrire* | écrivi- | *il écrivit* |

> **Rule of thumb:** In literary texts, wherever you would use the passé composé in spoken/informal French, a French author uses the passé simple. *"Il entra dans la pièce et s'assit."* = *"Il est entré dans la pièce et s'est assis."* — same meaning, different register.

## 7. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **Defaulting to passé composé for all past actions:** *"Hier, il faisait beau"* (imparfait — description of conditions) vs. *"Hier, il a plu"* (PC — specific event that happened).
2. **Forgetting past participle agreement with *être* verbs:** *"Elle est parti"* → wrong. *"Elle est partie."*
3. **Past participle agreement with preceding direct object (avoir verbs):** *"Les fleurs que j'ai achetées"* — *fleurs* is feminine plural and precedes the verb → *achetées*.
4. **Using PC for habitual past:** *"Quand j'étais enfant, j'ai joué au foot tous les jours"* → wrong. *"... je jouais au foot tous les jours."*
5. **Confusing plus-que-parfait auxiliary:** Uses the same *avoir/être* rules as passé composé — reflexive verbs still use *être*: *Je m'étais levé tôt.*
