# 41.2 — Nouns, Articles, Gender & Adjective Agreement

*Back to [[Bill's Vault/05-Knowledge_Foundation/09 - Learning/41 - French/Subject_Plan]] | [[41.1 - Phonetics, Liaison & The French Sound System|← 41.1]] | [[41.3 - Pronouns — Subject, Object & Reflexive|41.3 →]]*

> **Chapter goal:** Internalize French gender as a type system, master all article forms, and understand adjective agreement and placement — the grammatical scaffolding of every French sentence.

---

## 1. Gender — A Type System, Not a Mystery

French nouns are either **masculine** or **feminine** — there is no neuter. Unlike English, where "the chair" and "the table" are both neutral, French assigns gender to all nouns: *le bureau* (masculine), *la chaise* (feminine).

**Key insight:** Gender is not arbitrary for ~80% of nouns — **noun endings are highly predictive:**

### Masculine endings (strong predictors)
| Ending | Examples | Exceptions to watch |
|--------|---------|---------------------|
| -age | *le fromage, le voyage, le mariage* | *la plage, la cage, la page* |
| -ment | *le gouvernement, le département* | (very reliable) |
| -isme | *le capitalisme, le tourisme* | (reliable) |
| -eau | *le bureau, le gâteau, le chapeau* | *la peau, l'eau* |
| -eur (agent) | *le directeur, le vendeur* | *la chaleur, la douleur, la fleur, la peur* |
| -et, -at, -ot | *le projet, le chat, le mot* | varies |
| -ail, -eil | *le travail, le soleil* | (reliable) |
| -ing | *le parking, le camping* | (loanwords: reliable) |

### Feminine endings (strong predictors)
| Ending | Examples | Exceptions to watch |
|--------|---------|---------------------|
| -tion, -sion | *la nation, la passion, la décision* | (very reliable) |
| -té, -tié | *la liberté, la beauté, la moitié* | *le côté, le comité* |
| -ure | *la nature, la culture, la voiture* | *le murmure* |
| -ance, -ence | *la chance, la science, la violence* | *le silence* |
| -ette | *la baguette, la serviette, la cassette* | (reliable) |
| -esse | *la jeunesse, la richesse* | (reliable) |
| -ière | *la boulangère, la rivière* | (reliable) |
| -ie | *la vie, la pharmacie, la biologie* | *le génie, le parapluie* |

> **The 20% that aren't predictable must be learned with the article.** Always enter new nouns into Anki as *le + word* or *la + word* — never the word alone.

## 2. Articles — All Forms

### 2.1 Definite Articles (the)

| | Masculine | Feminine | Before vowel/mute-h | Plural |
|--|-----------|---------|---------------------|--------|
| **Form** | *le* | *la* | *l'* | *les* |
| **Example** | *le livre* | *la femme* | *l'ami, l'heure* | *les livres, les femmes* |

**Uses of the definite article (different from English):**
- With abstract nouns: *la liberté est importante* (Liberty is important) — English uses no article
- With languages: *j'apprends le français* (I'm learning French)
- With countries: *la France, le Japon, les États-Unis* (feminine countries use *en*, masculine use *au/aux*)
- With days of the week (habitual): *le lundi* = "on Mondays" (every Monday)

### 2.2 Indefinite Articles (a/an, some)

| | Masculine | Feminine | Plural |
|--|-----------|---------|--------|
| **Form** | *un* | *une* | *des* |
| **Example** | *un livre* | *une femme* | *des livres* |

> **After negation:** *un/une/des* → *de* (d' before vowel): *Je n'ai pas de voiture.* (I don't have a car.) *Il n'y a pas d'argent.* (There's no money.)

### 2.3 Partitive Articles (some — uncountable nouns)

Used with uncountable nouns (substances, abstractions) where English often uses "some" or no article:

| | Masculine | Feminine | Before vowel/mute-h |
|--|-----------|---------|---------------------|
| **Form** | *du* (= de + le) | *de la* | *de l'* |
| **Example** | *du pain* (some bread) | *de la eau* → *de l'eau* | *de l'argent* (some money) |

> **After negation:** partitive → *de*: *Je ne mange pas de viande.* (I don't eat meat.)

### 2.4 Contracted Articles (prepositions de + le, à + le)

| Preposition + article | Contracted form |
|-----------------------|----------------|
| *de + le* | *du* |
| *de + les* | *des* |
| *à + le* | *au* |
| *à + les* | *aux* |
| *de la, de l'* | (no contraction) |
| *à la, à l'* | (no contraction) |

*Je parle du professeur.* / *Je vais au cinéma.* / *Il parle aux étudiants.*

## 3. Adjective Agreement

French adjectives agree with the noun they modify in **gender and number** (4 forms for most adjectives):

| | Masc. singular | Fem. singular | Masc. plural | Fem. plural |
|--|---------------|--------------|-------------|------------|
| **petit** | *petit* | *petite* | *petits* | *petites* |
| **grand** | *grand* | *grande* | *grands* | *grandes* |
| **heureux** | *heureux* | *heureuse* | *heureux* | *heureuses* |
| **beau** | *beau* (bel)* | *belle* | *beaux* | *belles* |

*\*bel* before masculine noun beginning with vowel/mute-h: *un bel homme*

### Irregular patterns
| Masculine → Feminine rule | Examples |
|--------------------------|---------|
| Add -e | *petit → petite, grand → grande* |
| -eux → -euse | *heureux → heureuse, sérieux → sérieuse* |
| -f → -ve | *actif → active, neuf → neuve* |
| -er → -ère | *premier → première, étranger → étrangère* |
| -el/-eil/-ien/-on/-et → double + e | *bel → belle, pareil → pareille, ancien → ancienne, bon → bonne, muet → muette* |
| Irregular | *blanc → blanche, doux → douce, faux → fausse, long → longue* |

---

## 4. Adjective Placement — The BANGS Rule

**Default:** French adjectives follow the noun they modify (opposite of English):
*une voiture rouge* (a red car), *un homme intelligent* (an intelligent man)

**Exceptions — BANGS adjectives go BEFORE the noun:**
- **B**eauty: *beau/belle, joli(e)*
- **A**ge: *jeune, vieux/vieille, nouveau/nouvelle, ancien(ne)*
- **N**umber/Order: *premier(ière), dernier(ière), deuxième, plusieurs, quelques*
- **G**oodness/Value: *bon(ne), mauvais(e), meilleur(e), gentil(le)*
- **S**ize: *grand(e), petit(e), gros(se), long(ue), court(e), haut(e)*

> **Meaning changes with placement:** Some adjectives have different meanings before vs. after the noun:
> - *un grand homme* (a great man) vs. *un homme grand* (a tall man)
> - *un ancien professeur* (a former teacher) vs. *un professeur ancien* (an old/ancient teacher)
> - *ma propre chambre* (my own room) vs. *une chambre propre* (a clean room)
> - *un certain problème* (a certain problem) vs. *un problème certain* (a definite/sure problem)

---

## 5. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **Treating gender as arbitrary:** The ending-based rules cover ~80% of nouns. Learn to recognize the patterns.
2. **Forgetting partitive articles:** English says "I want water"; French requires *Je veux de l'eau* — a partitive article is obligatory.
3. **Not contracting de + le → du:** Saying *"je parle de le professeur"* is a basic error. The contraction is mandatory.
4. **Not changing adjective forms:** English speakers forget that *petit(e), petits, petites* are four distinct forms in writing (though only two distinct pronunciations: /pəti/ vs. /pətit/).
