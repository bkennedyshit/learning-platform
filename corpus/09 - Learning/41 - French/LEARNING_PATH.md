> This document is **hand-curated** and should remain stable.

## 📋 Prerequisites

Before starting this track, you should have:

1. **English grammar foundation** — knowing what subject, object, verb, adjective, adverb, and clause mean speeds up every grammar chapter. See [[38 - English/Subject_Plan|Track 19 English]] if needed.
2. **Spanish (optional but transformative)** — if you've completed [[36 - Spanish/Subject_Plan|Track 17 Spanish]] to A2 or above, your French timeline is cut by 50%. See the transfer notes below.
3. **A spaced-repetition tool** — Anki (free). French vocabulary includes gendered nouns — every new word must be learned with its gender from day one.
4. **Headphones** — French phonetics (nasal vowels, liaison, elision) cannot be learned from text alone.

### Spanish → French Transfer Accelerators
If you have Spanish background, these chapters will be much faster:
- **41.2** (gender + articles) — same logic as Spanish; French has fewer forms
- **41.5** (past tenses) — passé composé = passado compuesto; imparfait = imperfecto; decision tree is identical
- **41.6** (subjunctive) — same trigger categories; French has fewer irregular subjunctive forms than Spanish
- **41.7** (idioms) — ~60% of idiom structures are calques from shared Latin

## 📖 Recommended Chapter Order

### Phase 1: Sound System & Foundation — A2 (Weeks 1–7)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale | Est. Time |
|-------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | [[41.1 - Phonetics, Liaison & The French Sound System]] | **Non-negotiable first.** French phonetics is the hardest part for English speakers — front-load it or you'll develop permanent errors | 1.5 weeks |
| 2 | [[41.2 - Nouns, Articles, Gender & Adjective Agreement]] | The type system — gender + article logic underlies every sentence | 1.5 weeks |
| 3 | [[41.3 - Pronouns — Subject, Object & Reflexive]] | Clitic pronouns and their ordering are unlike English; master early | 1.5 weeks |
| 4 | [[41.4 - Verb System — Present, Imperative & Immediate Future]] | The engine — with this chapter you can build any present-tense conversation | 2.5 weeks |

**Phase 1 deliverable:** Hold a 5-minute present-tense conversation in French. Introduce yourself, describe your daily routine, ask basic questions. Pronounce all nasal vowels correctly; correctly apply liaison in obligatory contexts.

### Phase 2: Past & Future — B1 (Weeks 8–18)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 5 | [[41.5 - Past Tenses — Passé Composé, Imparfait & Plus-que-parfait]] | The most important grammar chapter — passé composé vs. imparfait is the core French past tense decision |
| 6 | [[41.6 - Future, Conditional & The Subjunctive]] | Conditional and subjunctive open up hypotheticals, polite speech, and complex clauses |

**Phase 2 deliverable:** Tell a story set in the past. Use the conditional for polite requests. Use the subjunctive correctly in 5 different trigger constructions. Navigate a restaurant, hotel, or travel situation entirely in French.

### Phase 3: Fluency — B2/C1 (Weeks 19–28)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 7 | [[41.7 - Idioms, Register & Regional Variation]] | The difference between correct French and natural French |
| 8 | [[41.8 - Reading, Listening & Cultural Immersion]] | Transition from structured study to native media immersion |

**Phase 3 deliverable:** Watch an InnerFrench episode at "Advanced" pace and understand ≥80%. Read a *Le Monde* article with ≤10% dictionary lookups. Understand Verlan in casual speech context.

## 🔀 Non-Negotiable Daily Habits (Every Phase)

| Daily habit | Time | Why |
|-------------|------|-----|
| Anki vocabulary (with gender) | 15–20 min | Learn *le/la + word* as one unit — never the word alone |
| French audio (Français Authentique / InnerFrench) | 20–30 min | Phonetic training; comprehensible input |
| Language Transfer episode | 20 min (Phase 1–2) | Active grammar derivation |
| Pronunciation shadowing | 10 min | Pick 30 seconds of native audio; shadow it |

> **The être-vs-avoir trap:** 90% of learners default to *avoir* and get past participle agreement wrong for years. The solution is daily flashcard drilling of DR & MRS VANDERTRAMP / house verbs from week one of chapter 41.5. It must become reflex, not a rule you recall.

## 🚫 What This Track Does NOT Cover

- **French composition** (formal letters, academic writing) — this track targets spoken fluency and reading comprehension
- **Old French / Middle French** — historical French linguistics; separate domain
- **Creole languages** (Haitian Creole, Louisiana Creole) — built on French but structurally distinct
