# 6.7 — Boundary Layer Theory: Blasius Solution

> *"The most important advance in fluid mechanics in the twentieth century was Prandtl's boundary layer concept — it reconciled the mathematical elegance of potential flow with the physical reality of viscous drag."* — paraphrased from H. Schlichting, *Boundary-Layer Theory* (1955).

Prandtl's 1904 insight resolved d'Alembert's paradox: viscous effects are confined to a thin layer near solid surfaces (the boundary layer), while the outer flow remains essentially inviscid. Within this thin layer, the no-slip condition creates enormous velocity gradients that produce drag. This chapter derives the boundary layer equations, solves the Blasius equation for flow over a flat plate, and develops the integral methods (von Kármán) that enable engineering calculations of drag and separation.

## 📚 1. Definitions

### Definition 6.7.1 — Boundary Layer

The **boundary layer** is the thin region adjacent to a solid surface where viscous effects are significant — where the velocity transitions from zero (at the wall, no-slip) to the free-stream value $U_e$.

The boundary layer thickness $\delta$ is conventionally defined as the distance from the wall where $u = 0.99\, U_e$.

### Definition 6.7.2 — Displacement Thickness

The **displacement thickness** $\delta^*$ measures the distance by which streamlines are displaced outward due to the velocity deficit in the boundary layer:

$$
\delta^* = \int_0^\infty \left(1 - \frac{u}{U_e}\right) dy.
$$

### Definition 6.7.3 — Momentum Thickness

The **momentum thickness** $\theta$ measures the loss of momentum flux due to the boundary layer:

$$
\theta = \int_0^\infty \frac{u}{U_e}\left(1 - \frac{u}{U_e}\right) dy.
$$

### Definition 6.7.4 — Shape Factor

The **shape factor** $H = \delta^*/\theta$ characterizes the boundary layer profile shape. For Blasius (flat plate): $H \approx 2.59$. Larger $H$ indicates a profile closer to separation.

### Definition 6.7.5 — Skin Friction Coefficient

The **local skin friction coefficient** is:

$$
c_f = \frac{\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U_e^2}, \qquad \tau_w = \mu\left.\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right|_{y=0}.
$$

---

## 📐 2. Axioms / Postulates

### Axiom 6.7.A — Prandtl's Boundary Layer Hypothesis

At high Reynolds number ($\text{Re} \gg 1$), the flow field divides into two regions:
1. An **outer region** where viscous effects are negligible (governed by Euler's equation).
2. A thin **inner region** (boundary layer) of thickness $\delta \ll L$ where viscous and inertial forces are of the same order.

### Axiom 6.7.B — Matching Condition

The boundary layer solution must match the outer (inviscid) solution at the edge of the boundary layer:

$$
u(x, y \to \infty) = U_e(x),
$$

where $U_e(x)$ is the inviscid velocity at the wall (from potential flow theory).

---

## 🛡️ 3. Lemmas

### Lemma 6.7.1 — Order-of-Magnitude Estimates in the Boundary Layer

Within the boundary layer: $x \sim L$, $y \sim \delta$, $u \sim U$, and from continuity $v \sim U\delta/L$. The boundary layer thickness scales as:

$$
\frac{\delta}{L} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_L}}, \qquad \delta \sim \sqrt{\frac{\nu L}{U}}.
$$

**Proof.** Balance inertial and viscous terms in the $x$-momentum equation:

$$
\underbrace{u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}}_{\sim U^2/L} \sim \underbrace{\nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}}_{\sim \nu U/\delta^2}.
$$

$$
\frac{U^2}{L} \sim \frac{\nu U}{\delta^2} \implies \delta^2 \sim \frac{\nu L}{U} \implies \frac{\delta}{L} \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{UL/\nu}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_L}}. \quad \blacksquare
$$

## 👑 4. Theorems

### Theorem 6.7.1 — Prandtl's Boundary Layer Equations (2D, Steady, Incompressible)

$$
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = U_e \frac{dU_e}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2},
$$

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0.
$$

The pressure is imposed by the outer flow: $-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} = U_e \frac{dU_e}{dx}$ (from Bernoulli in the outer region).

### Theorem 6.7.2 — Blasius Equation (Flat Plate, Zero Pressure Gradient)

For a flat plate ($U_e = U = \text{const}$, $dU_e/dx = 0$), the boundary layer equations admit a similarity solution. Defining:

$$
\eta = y\sqrt{\frac{U}{2\nu x}}, \qquad \psi = \sqrt{2\nu U x}\, f(\eta),
$$

the velocity is $u = Uf'(\eta)$ and $f(\eta)$ satisfies the **Blasius equation**:

$$
f''' + f\, f'' = 0,
$$

with boundary conditions: $f(0) = 0$, $f'(0) = 0$, $f'(\infty) = 1$.

### Theorem 6.7.3 — Blasius Results

From the numerical solution of the Blasius equation:
- $f''(0) = 0.4696$ (determines wall shear stress)
- Boundary layer thickness: $\delta_{99} \approx 5.0\sqrt{\nu x / U}$
- Displacement thickness: $\delta^* = 1.7208\sqrt{\nu x / U}$
- Momentum thickness: $\theta = 0.6641\sqrt{\nu x / U}$
- Local skin friction: $c_f = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}$
- Total drag coefficient (plate of length $L$): $C_D = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_L}}$

### Theorem 6.7.4 — Von Kármán Integral Momentum Equation

For a 2D boundary layer with outer velocity $U_e(x)$:

$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx} + (2 + H)\frac{\theta}{U_e}\frac{dU_e}{dx} = \frac{c_f}{2},
$$

where $H = \delta^*/\theta$ is the shape factor.

## ✍️ 5. Proofs / Derivations

### Proof 5.1 — Derivation of the Boundary Layer Equations (Theorem 6.7.1)

**Goal:** Systematically simplify the N-S equations using $\delta/L \ll 1$.

**Step 1: Non-dimensionalize.** Let $x^* = x/L$, $y^* = y/\delta$, $u^* = u/U$, $v^* = v/(U\delta/L)$, $p^* = p/(\rho U^2)$.

**Step 2: The $x$-momentum equation becomes (dropping stars):**

$$
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = -\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + \frac{1}{\text{Re}_L}\frac{L^2}{\delta^2}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} + \frac{1}{\text{Re}_L}\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}.
$$

Since $\delta/L \sim 1/\sqrt{\text{Re}}$, we have $\text{Re} \cdot (\delta/L)^2 \sim 1$. The $\partial^2 u/\partial x^2$ term is $O(1/\text{Re})$ and negligible.

**Step 3: The $y$-momentum equation gives $\partial p/\partial y = O(\delta/L) \approx 0$.**

Therefore $p = p(x)$ only — pressure is constant across the boundary layer, imposed by the outer flow.

**Step 4: In the outer flow (inviscid), Bernoulli gives:**

$$
p + \frac{1}{2}\rho U_e^2 = \text{const} \implies -\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{dp}{dx} = U_e\frac{dU_e}{dx}.
$$

**Step 5: Final boundary layer equations (dimensional):**

$$
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = U_e\frac{dU_e}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}, \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0. \quad \blacksquare
$$

### Proof 5.2 — Derivation of the Blasius Equation (Theorem 6.7.2)

**Goal:** Reduce the boundary layer PDE to an ODE via similarity transformation.

**Step 1: For a flat plate, $U_e = U = \text{const}$, $dU_e/dx = 0$. The equations are:**

$$
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}, \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0.
$$

**Step 2: Introduce the streamfunction $\psi$ satisfying continuity automatically:** $u = \partial\psi/\partial y$, $v = -\partial\psi/\partial x$.

**Step 3: Seek a similarity solution.** The only length scale is $\sqrt{\nu x/U}$. Define:

$$
\eta = y\sqrt{\frac{U}{2\nu x}}, \qquad \psi = \sqrt{2\nu U x}\, f(\eta).
$$

**Step 4: Compute velocity components.**

$$
u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = \sqrt{2\nu U x} \cdot f'(\eta) \cdot \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial y} = \sqrt{2\nu U x} \cdot f' \cdot \sqrt{\frac{U}{2\nu x}} = U f'(\eta).
$$

$$
v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\sqrt{2\nu U x}\, f(\eta)\right].
$$

Computing $v$ (using $\eta = y\sqrt{U/(2\nu x)}$, so $\partial\eta/\partial x = -\eta/(2x)$):

$$
v = -\left[\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\nu U}{x}}\, f + \sqrt{2\nu U x}\, f' \cdot \left(-\frac{\eta}{2x}\right)\right] = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\nu U}{x}}(\eta f' - f).
$$

**Step 5: Compute the required derivatives.**

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = Uf'' \cdot \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x} = -\frac{U\eta f''}{2x}.
$$

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = Uf'' \cdot \sqrt{\frac{U}{2\nu x}}, \qquad \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = Uf''' \cdot \frac{U}{2\nu x}.
$$

**Step 6: Substitute into the momentum equation.**

$$
Uf' \cdot \left(-\frac{U\eta f''}{2x}\right) + \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\nu U}{x}}(\eta f' - f) \cdot Uf''\sqrt{\frac{U}{2\nu x}} = \nu \cdot Uf''' \cdot \frac{U}{2\nu x}.
$$

Simplify the second term: $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\nu U}{x}} \cdot Uf'' \cdot \sqrt{\frac{U}{2\nu x}} = \frac{U^2 f''}{2x}(\eta f' - f) \cdot \frac{1}{1}$... Let us be more careful:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2\nu U}{x}} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{U}{2\nu x}} = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{U}{x} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{2\nu U} \cdot 1}{\sqrt{x} \cdot \sqrt{2\nu x}} = \frac{U}{2x}.
$$

So the left side becomes:

$$
-\frac{U^2 \eta f' f''}{2x} + \frac{U^2 f''(\eta f' - f)}{2x} = \frac{U^2}{2x}\left[-\eta f'f'' + \eta f'f'' - ff''\right] = -\frac{U^2 ff''}{2x}.
$$

The right side: $\nu \cdot U f''' \cdot \frac{U}{2\nu x} = \frac{U^2 f'''}{2x}$.

**Step 7: Equate and simplify.**

$$
-\frac{U^2 ff''}{2x} = \frac{U^2 f'''}{2x}.
$$

$$
f''' + ff'' = 0. \quad \blacksquare
$$

## 🧮 6. Worked Examples

### Example 6.7.1 — Boundary Layer Thickness on a Flat Plate

**Problem.** Air ($\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s) flows at $U = 30$ m/s over a flat plate. Find the boundary layer thickness at $x = 1$ m.

**Solution.**

**Step 1:** $\text{Re}_x = Ux/\nu = 30 \times 1 / (1.5 \times 10^{-5}) = 2 \times 10^6$.

**Step 2:** Blasius gives $\delta_{99} = 5.0\sqrt{\nu x/U} = 5.0\sqrt{(1.5 \times 10^{-5})(1)/30}$.

$$
\delta = 5.0\sqrt{5 \times 10^{-7}} = 5.0 \times 7.07 \times 10^{-4} = 3.54 \times 10^{-3}\;\text{m} = 3.54\;\text{mm}.
$$

**Step 3:** Alternatively: $\delta/x = 5.0/\sqrt{\text{Re}_x} = 5.0/\sqrt{2 \times 10^6} = 5.0/1414 = 0.00354$. So $\delta = 0.00354 \times 1 = 3.54$ mm. ✓

---

### Example 6.7.2 — Drag on a Flat Plate

**Problem.** Find the total drag force on one side of a flat plate of length $L = 2$ m and width $b = 1$ m in the same airflow ($\rho = 1.225$ kg/m³).

**Solution.**

**Step 1:** $\text{Re}_L = UL/\nu = 30 \times 2 / (1.5 \times 10^{-5}) = 4 \times 10^6$.

**Step 2:** Blasius drag coefficient: $C_D = 1.328/\sqrt{\text{Re}_L} = 1.328/\sqrt{4 \times 10^6} = 1.328/2000 = 6.64 \times 10^{-4}$.

**Step 3:** Drag force:

$$
F_D = C_D \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 \cdot bL = 6.64 \times 10^{-4} \times \frac{1}{2}(1.225)(30)^2 \times 2 = 6.64 \times 10^{-4} \times 1102.5 = 0.732\;\text{N}.
$$

---

### Example 6.7.3 — Von Kármán Integral Method with Assumed Profile

**Problem.** Using the assumed velocity profile $u/U = 2(y/\delta) - (y/\delta)^2$ in the von Kármán equation, find $\delta(x)$ for a flat plate.

**Solution.**

**Step 1:** Let $\eta = y/\delta$. Then $u/U = 2\eta - \eta^2$.

**Step 2:** Compute $\delta^*$:

$$
\delta^* = \delta\int_0^1 (1 - 2\eta + \eta^2)\, d\eta = \delta\left[1 - 1 + \frac{1}{3}\right] = \frac{\delta}{3}.
$$

**Step 3:** Compute $\theta$:

$$
\theta = \delta\int_0^1 (2\eta - \eta^2)(1 - 2\eta + \eta^2)\, d\eta = \delta\int_0^1 (2\eta - 5\eta^2 + 4\eta^3 - \eta^4)\, d\eta.
$$

$$
= \delta\left[1 - \frac{5}{3} + 1 - \frac{1}{5}\right] = \delta\left[\frac{15 - 25 + 15 - 3}{15}\right] = \frac{2\delta}{15}.
$$

**Step 4:** Wall shear: $\tau_w = \mu(du/dy)|_0 = \mu U \cdot 2/\delta$.

So $c_f/2 = \tau_w/(\rho U^2) = \mu \cdot 2/(\rho U \delta) = 2\nu/(U\delta)$.

**Step 5:** Von Kármán equation for flat plate ($dU_e/dx = 0$): $d\theta/dx = c_f/2$.

$$
\frac{2}{15}\frac{d\delta}{dx} = \frac{2\nu}{U\delta}.
$$

**Step 6:** Separate and integrate:

$$
\delta\, d\delta = \frac{15\nu}{U}\, dx \implies \frac{\delta^2}{2} = \frac{15\nu x}{U} \implies \delta = \sqrt{\frac{30\nu x}{U}} = \frac{5.48}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}\, x.
$$

Compare with Blasius exact: $\delta = 5.0x/\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}$. The approximate method gives 5.48 vs 5.0 — about 10% error, which is acceptable for an integral method.

## 🧠 8. Extended Worked Examples & Deep Dives

### Example 8.1 — Complete Blasius Similarity Transformation

**Problem.** Starting from the 2D steady incompressible boundary layer equations for flow over a flat plate, derive the Blasius ordinary differential equation $f''' + \frac{1}{2}f f'' = 0$ with all intermediate steps shown.

<details>
<summary>🔍 Full step-by-step solution</summary>

#### Step 1: The Boundary Layer Equations

For steady, 2D, incompressible flow over a flat plate (zero pressure gradient, $dP/dx = 0$):

**Continuity:**

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} = 0.
$$

**$x$-momentum:**

$$
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}.
$$

**Boundary conditions:**
- At $y = 0$: $u = 0$, $v = 0$ (no-slip, no penetration).
- As $y \to \infty$: $u \to U$ (freestream velocity).

#### Step 2: Introduce the Stream Function

Define $\psi(x, y)$ such that $u = \partial\psi/\partial y$ and $v = -\partial\psi/\partial x$. This automatically satisfies continuity.

#### Step 3: The Similarity Hypothesis

The key insight (Blasius, 1908): the velocity profile $u/U$ should depend on $y$ only through the combination $\eta = y/\delta(x)$, where $\delta(x)$ is the boundary layer thickness. From dimensional analysis, $\delta \sim \sqrt{\nu x/U}$.

Define the similarity variable:

$$
\eta = y\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}}.
$$

And assume the stream function has the form:

$$
\psi(x, y) = \sqrt{\nu x U}\, f(\eta),
$$

where $f(\eta)$ is a dimensionless function to be determined.

#### Step 4: Compute $u$ from $\psi$

$$
u = \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial y} = \sqrt{\nu x U}\, f'(\eta) \cdot \frac{\partial \eta}{\partial y} = \sqrt{\nu x U}\, f'(\eta) \cdot \sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}} = U f'(\eta).
$$

This confirms that $u/U = f'(\eta)$ — the velocity profile is self-similar.

#### Step 5: Compute $v$ from $\psi$

$$
v = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x}.
$$

We need $\partial\psi/\partial x$ at constant $y$:

$$
\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left[\sqrt{\nu x U}\, f(\eta)\right] = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\nu U}{x}}\, f(\eta) + \sqrt{\nu x U}\, f'(\eta)\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x}.
$$

Now $\eta = y\sqrt{U/(\nu x)}$, so:

$$
\frac{\partial \eta}{\partial x} = y \cdot \frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\frac{U}{\nu x}\right)^{1/2} = y \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\frac{U}{\nu}\right)^{1/2} x^{-3/2} = -\frac{\eta}{2x}.
$$

Substituting:

$$
\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial x} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\nu U}{x}}\, f + \sqrt{\nu x U}\, f' \cdot \left(-\frac{\eta}{2x}\right) = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\nu U}{x}}\left(f - \eta f'\right).
$$

Therefore:

$$
v = -\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\nu U}{x}}\left(f - \eta f'\right).
$$

#### Step 6: Compute the Derivatives for the Momentum Equation

We need $\partial u/\partial x$, $\partial u/\partial y$, and $\partial^2 u/\partial y^2$.

$$
u = Uf'(\eta), \qquad \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = Uf''(\eta)\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial y} = Uf''\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}}.
$$

$$
\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = Uf'''(\eta)\left(\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial y}\right)^2 = Uf''' \cdot \frac{U}{\nu x} = \frac{U^2}{\nu x}f'''.
$$

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = Uf''(\eta)\frac{\partial\eta}{\partial x} = Uf''\left(-\frac{\eta}{2x}\right) = -\frac{U\eta f''}{2x}.
$$

#### Step 7: Substitute into the Momentum Equation

$$
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}.
$$

Left side, first term:

$$
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = Uf' \cdot \left(-\frac{U\eta f''}{2x}\right) = -\frac{U^2 \eta f' f''}{2x}.
$$

Left side, second term:

$$
v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = \left[-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\nu U}{x}}(f - \eta f')\right] \cdot \left[Uf''\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}}\right].
$$

$$
= -\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\nu U}{x}} \cdot U f'' \cdot \sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}} \cdot (f - \eta f') = -\frac{U^2 f''(f - \eta f')}{2x}.
$$

Sum of left side:

$$
-\frac{U^2 \eta f' f''}{2x} - \frac{U^2 f''(f - \eta f')}{2x} = -\frac{U^2 f''}{2x}\left[\eta f' + f - \eta f'\right] = -\frac{U^2 f f''}{2x}.
$$

Right side:

$$
\nu \cdot \frac{U^2}{\nu x}f''' = \frac{U^2}{x}f'''.
$$

#### Step 8: Equate and Simplify

$$
-\frac{U^2 f f''}{2x} = \frac{U^2}{x}f'''.
$$

Divide by $U^2/x$:

$$
-\frac{f f''}{2} = f''' \implies f''' + \frac{1}{2}f f'' = 0.
$$

This is the **Blasius equation** — a third-order nonlinear ODE.

#### Step 9: Boundary Conditions in Terms of $f$

- At $y = 0$ ($\eta = 0$): $u = 0 \implies f'(0) = 0$; $v = 0 \implies f(0) = 0$.
- As $y \to \infty$ ($\eta \to \infty$): $u \to U \implies f'(\infty) = 1$.

#### Step 10: Solution Properties

The Blasius equation must be solved numerically (it has no closed-form solution). The key result is:

$$
f''(0) = 0.33206,
$$

which determines the wall shear stress:

$$
\tau_w = \mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\bigg|_0 = \mu U f''(0)\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}} = 0.332\,\mu U\sqrt{\frac{U}{\nu x}}.
$$

The skin friction coefficient:

$$
c_f = \frac{\tau_w}{\frac{1}{2}\rho U^2} = \frac{0.664}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}.
$$

The boundary layer thickness (defined as $\eta_{99}$ where $f' = 0.99$): $\eta_{99} \approx 5.0$, giving:

$$
\delta_{99} = \frac{5.0 x}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}.
$$

**Final Answer:**

$$
f''' + \frac{1}{2}ff'' = 0, \quad f(0) = f'(0) = 0, \quad f'(\infty) = 1.
$$

</details>

### Example 8.2 — Momentum-Integral Method (Von Kármán)

**Problem.** Using the momentum-integral equation with an assumed cubic velocity profile $u/U = \frac{3}{2}(\eta) - \frac{1}{2}(\eta)^3$ where $\eta = y/\delta(x)$, find the boundary layer thickness $\delta(x)$ and skin friction coefficient $c_f(x)$ for a flat plate.

<details>
<summary>🔍 Full step-by-step solution</summary>

#### Step 1: The Von Kármán Momentum-Integral Equation

For a flat plate (zero pressure gradient):

$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx} = \frac{c_f}{2} = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho U^2},
$$

where $\theta$ is the momentum thickness:

$$
\theta = \int_0^\delta \frac{u}{U}\left(1 - \frac{u}{U}\right) dy.
$$

#### Step 2: Compute $\theta$ for the Cubic Profile

Let $g(\eta) = u/U = \frac{3}{2}\eta - \frac{1}{2}\eta^3$, $\eta = y/\delta$, $dy = \delta\, d\eta$:

$$
\theta = \delta \int_0^1 g(1-g)\, d\eta = \delta \int_0^1 \left(\frac{3}{2}\eta - \frac{1}{2}\eta^3\right)\left(1 - \frac{3}{2}\eta + \frac{1}{2}\eta^3\right) d\eta.
$$

Expand the integrand:

$$
g(1-g) = \frac{3}{2}\eta - \frac{9}{4}\eta^2 + \frac{3}{4}\eta^4 - \frac{1}{2}\eta^3 + \frac{3}{4}\eta^4 - \frac{1}{4}\eta^6.
$$

Wait — let me be more careful:

$$
g(1-g) = g - g^2 = \left(\frac{3}{2}\eta - \frac{1}{2}\eta^3\right) - \left(\frac{3}{2}\eta - \frac{1}{2}\eta^3\right)^2.
$$

$$
g^2 = \frac{9}{4}\eta^2 - \frac{3}{2}\eta^4 + \frac{1}{4}\eta^6.
$$

$$
g - g^2 = \frac{3}{2}\eta - \frac{1}{2}\eta^3 - \frac{9}{4}\eta^2 + \frac{3}{2}\eta^4 - \frac{1}{4}\eta^6.
$$

Integrate term by term from 0 to 1:

$$
\int_0^1 g(1-g)\, d\eta = \frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{8} - \frac{3}{4} + \frac{3}{10} - \frac{1}{28}.
$$

$$
= \frac{3}{4} - \frac{3}{4} - \frac{1}{8} + \frac{3}{10} - \frac{1}{28} = -\frac{1}{8} + \frac{3}{10} - \frac{1}{28}.
$$

Common denominator 280:

$$
= -\frac{35}{280} + \frac{84}{280} - \frac{10}{280} = \frac{39}{280}.
$$

Therefore: $\theta = \frac{39}{280}\delta$.

#### Step 3: Compute Wall Shear Stress

$$
\tau_w = \mu\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\bigg|_0 = \mu U \frac{dg}{d\eta}\bigg|_0 \cdot \frac{1}{\delta}.
$$

$$
g'(\eta) = \frac{3}{2} - \frac{3}{2}\eta^2 \implies g'(0) = \frac{3}{2}.
$$

$$
\tau_w = \frac{3\mu U}{2\delta}.
$$

#### Step 4: Substitute into the Momentum-Integral Equation

$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx} = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho U^2} \implies \frac{39}{280}\frac{d\delta}{dx} = \frac{3\mu}{2\rho U \delta} = \frac{3\nu}{2U\delta}.
$$

#### Step 5: Solve the ODE

$$
\delta\, d\delta = \frac{280}{39} \cdot \frac{3\nu}{2U}\, dx = \frac{420\nu}{39 U}\, dx = \frac{140\nu}{13 U}\, dx.
$$

Integrate with $\delta(0) = 0$:

$$
\frac{\delta^2}{2} = \frac{140\nu x}{13 U} \implies \delta = \sqrt{\frac{280\nu x}{13 U}} = \sqrt{\frac{280}{13}} \cdot \frac{x}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}.
$$

$$
\sqrt{280/13} = \sqrt{21.54} = 4.64.
$$

$$
\delta = \frac{4.64\, x}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}.
$$

Compare with Blasius exact: $\delta = 5.0\, x/\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}$. The cubic profile gives 4.64 — about 7% error.

#### Step 6: Skin Friction Coefficient

$$
c_f = \frac{2\tau_w}{\rho U^2} = \frac{2 \times 3\mu U/(2\delta)}{\rho U^2} = \frac{3\nu}{U\delta} = \frac{3}{4.64\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}{x} \cdot \frac{x}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}.
$$

More directly:

$$
c_f = \frac{3\nu}{U\delta} \cdot \frac{2}{\rho U^2 / (\rho U^2)} = \frac{3}{4.64} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}} \cdot 2 = ... 
$$

Let me use the clean formula: $c_f/2 = \tau_w/(\rho U^2) = 3\nu/(2U\delta)$:

$$
\frac{c_f}{2} = \frac{3\nu}{2U} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}{4.64\, x} = \frac{3}{2 \times 4.64} \cdot \frac{\nu}{Ux} \cdot \sqrt{\text{Re}_x} = \frac{3}{9.28} \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}.
$$

$$
c_f = \frac{6}{9.28\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}} = \frac{0.646}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}.
$$

Compare with Blasius exact: $c_f = 0.664/\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}$. Error: about 3%.

**Final Answer:**

$$
\delta = \frac{4.64\, x}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}, \qquad c_f = \frac{0.646}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_x}}.
$$

</details>

### Example 8.3 — Transition to Turbulence: Critical Reynolds Number

**Problem.** A flat plate of length $L = 2$ m is placed in an airflow at $U = 30$ m/s. Air properties: $\nu = 1.5 \times 10^{-5}$ m²/s. (a) Find the location of transition from laminar to turbulent boundary layer (using $\text{Re}_{x,\text{cr}} = 5 \times 10^5$). (b) Compute the laminar boundary layer thickness at the transition point. (c) Estimate the total drag coefficient using the mixed laminar-turbulent formula.

<details>
<summary>🔍 Full step-by-step solution</summary>

#### Step 1: Transition Location

$$
\text{Re}_{x,\text{cr}} = \frac{U x_{\text{cr}}}{\nu} = 5 \times 10^5.
$$

$$
x_{\text{cr}} = \frac{5 \times 10^5 \times \nu}{U} = \frac{5 \times 10^5 \times 1.5 \times 10^{-5}}{30} = \frac{7.5}{30} = 0.25\;\text{m}.
$$

Transition occurs at 25 cm from the leading edge — only 12.5% of the plate length.

#### Step 2: Boundary Layer Thickness at Transition

Using the Blasius result:

$$
\delta_{\text{cr}} = \frac{5.0\, x_{\text{cr}}}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_{x,\text{cr}}}} = \frac{5.0 \times 0.25}{\sqrt{5 \times 10^5}} = \frac{1.25}{707.1} = 1.77 \times 10^{-3}\;\text{m} = 1.77\;\text{mm}.
$$

#### Step 3: Reynolds Number at Plate End

$$
\text{Re}_L = \frac{UL}{\nu} = \frac{30 \times 2}{1.5 \times 10^{-5}} = 4 \times 10^6.
$$

#### Step 4: Mixed Laminar-Turbulent Drag Coefficient

The standard engineering formula for a flat plate with transition at $\text{Re}_{x,\text{cr}} = 5 \times 10^5$:

$$
C_D = \frac{0.074}{\text{Re}_L^{1/5}} - \frac{1742}{\text{Re}_L}.
$$

The first term is the fully turbulent drag (Prandtl's 1/7th power law); the second is a correction for the laminar portion.

$$
C_D = \frac{0.074}{(4 \times 10^6)^{0.2}} - \frac{1742}{4 \times 10^6}.
$$

$(4 \times 10^6)^{0.2}$: $\log_{10}(4 \times 10^6) = 6.602$, so $(4\times10^6)^{0.2} = 10^{1.320} = 20.93$.

$$
C_D = \frac{0.074}{20.93} - \frac{1742}{4 \times 10^6} = 3.536 \times 10^{-3} - 4.355 \times 10^{-4} = 3.10 \times 10^{-3}.
$$

#### Step 5: Total Drag Force (per unit span)

$$
D = C_D \cdot \frac{1}{2}\rho U^2 \cdot L = 3.10 \times 10^{-3} \times \frac{1}{2}(1.225)(30)^2 \times 2 = 3.10 \times 10^{-3} \times 1102.5 = 3.42\;\text{N/m}.
$$

#### Step 6: Comparison — Fully Laminar vs. Mixed

If the entire plate were laminar (hypothetical):

$$
C_{D,\text{lam}} = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{\text{Re}_L}} = \frac{1.328}{\sqrt{4 \times 10^6}} = \frac{1.328}{2000} = 6.64 \times 10^{-4}.
$$

The turbulent drag is $3.10 \times 10^{-3} / 6.64 \times 10^{-4} = 4.7$ times larger. Turbulence dramatically increases skin friction — this is why laminar flow control is a major research area in aerospace engineering.

**Final Answer:**

$$
x_{\text{cr}} = 0.25\;\text{m}, \quad \delta_{\text{cr}} = 1.77\;\text{mm}, \quad C_D = 3.10 \times 10^{-3}.
$$

</details>

## 📘 9. Appendix: Extended Derivations & Special Cases

### 9.1 The Falkner-Skan Family of Similarity Solutions

The Blasius equation is a special case ($\beta = 0$) of the more general **Falkner-Skan equation**, which describes boundary layers in flows with a power-law freestream velocity $U(x) = Cx^m$.

**Setup.** Consider a 2D boundary layer with outer velocity:

$$
U(x) = C x^m,
$$

where $m$ is a constant. This corresponds to flow past a wedge of half-angle $\beta\pi/2$, where $\beta = 2m/(m+1)$.

**Similarity variable and stream function:**

$$
\eta = y\sqrt{\frac{(m+1)U}{2\nu x}}, \qquad \psi = \sqrt{\frac{2\nu x U}{m+1}}\, f(\eta).
$$

**The Falkner-Skan equation:**

Substituting into the boundary layer equations (with pressure gradient $U\, dU/dx = mC^2 x^{2m-1}$) yields:

$$
f''' + ff'' + \beta(1 - f'^2) = 0,
$$

with boundary conditions $f(0) = f'(0) = 0$, $f'(\infty) = 1$.

**Special cases:**

| $m$ | $\beta$ | Physical flow |
|-----|---------|--------------|
| 0 | 0 | Flat plate (Blasius) |
| 1 | 1 | Stagnation-point flow (Hiemenz) |
| 1/3 | 1/2 | 90° wedge |
| $-0.0904$ | $-0.1988$ | Incipient separation ($f''(0) = 0$) |

**Key results:**

1. For $\beta > 0$ (favorable pressure gradient, accelerating flow): the boundary layer is thinner and more stable. The wall shear $f''(0)$ increases with $\beta$.

2. For $\beta < 0$ (adverse pressure gradient, decelerating flow): the boundary layer thickens and eventually separates. Separation occurs when $\tau_w = 0$, i.e., $f''(0) = 0$, at $\beta = -0.1988$ (Hartree's value).

3. The stagnation-point solution ($\beta = 1$, $m = 1$) gives $f''(0) = 1.2326$ and a boundary layer of constant thickness — the thickening due to viscous diffusion is exactly balanced by the thinning due to flow acceleration.

**Physical significance:** The Falkner-Skan solutions demonstrate how pressure gradients control boundary layer behavior:
- Favorable gradients stabilize and thin the layer.
- Adverse gradients destabilize and thicken the layer, eventually causing separation.
- This is why airfoils are designed with gradual pressure recovery (to delay separation) and why diffusers have limited expansion angles.

**Numerical solution:** The Falkner-Skan equation is solved as a boundary value problem using shooting methods. Start with a guess for $f''(0)$, integrate forward, and iterate until $f'(\infty) = 1$ is satisfied.

**References:** Falkner & Skan (1931), *Phil. Mag.*; Schlichting, *Boundary-Layer Theory*, Ch. 9; White, *Viscous Fluid Flow*, Ch. 4.

### 9.2 Derivation of the Von Kármán Momentum-Integral Equation

The momentum-integral equation is derived by integrating the boundary layer equations across the layer thickness. It trades detailed knowledge of the velocity profile for a simpler ODE relating integral quantities.

**Step 1: Start with the boundary layer $x$-momentum equation:**

$$
u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y} = U\frac{dU}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}.
$$

(We used Euler's equation in the freestream: $-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{dp}{dx} = U\frac{dU}{dx}$.)

**Step 2: Rewrite using continuity.** From $\partial u/\partial x + \partial v/\partial y = 0$:

$$
\frac{\partial(u^2)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial(uv)}{\partial y} = U\frac{dU}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}.
$$

Actually, a cleaner approach: write the momentum equation as:

$$
\frac{\partial(u^2)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial(uv)}{\partial y} - u\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right) = U\frac{dU}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}.
$$

The third term vanishes by continuity, giving:

$$
\frac{\partial u^2}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial(uv)}{\partial y} = U\frac{dU}{dx} + \nu\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2}.
$$

**Step 3: Integrate from $y = 0$ to $y = \delta$.**

$$
\int_0^\delta \frac{\partial u^2}{\partial x}\, dy + [uv]_0^\delta = U\frac{dU}{dx}\delta + \nu\left[\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\right]_0^\delta.
$$

At $y = \delta$: $u = U$, $\partial u/\partial y = 0$. At $y = 0$: $u = v = 0$, $\nu\partial u/\partial y|_0 = \tau_w/\rho$.

Using Leibniz rule and the continuity equation to express $v(\delta)$, after careful manipulation (subtracting $U$ times the integrated continuity equation), one arrives at:

$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx} + (2\theta + \delta^*)\frac{1}{U}\frac{dU}{dx} = \frac{c_f}{2},
$$

where:
- $\theta = \int_0^\infty \frac{u}{U}(1 - \frac{u}{U})\, dy$ is the **momentum thickness**.
- $\delta^* = \int_0^\infty (1 - \frac{u}{U})\, dy$ is the **displacement thickness**.
- $c_f = 2\tau_w/(\rho U^2)$ is the local skin friction coefficient.

For a flat plate ($dU/dx = 0$):

$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx} = \frac{c_f}{2} = \frac{\tau_w}{\rho U^2}.
$$

This is the **Von Kármán momentum-integral equation** — an exact consequence of the boundary layer equations, regardless of the assumed profile shape.

**References:** Von Kármán (1921); Schlichting, Ch. 8; Kundu & Cohen, §10.6.
