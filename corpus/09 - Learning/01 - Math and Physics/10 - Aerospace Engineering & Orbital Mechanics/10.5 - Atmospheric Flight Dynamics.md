# 10.5 — Atmospheric Flight Dynamics

> *"The airplane stays up because it doesn't have the time to fall."*
> — **Orville Wright** (attributed)

Atmospheric flight dynamics bridges aerodynamics and rigid-body mechanics. Unlike orbital mechanics where gravity is the sole force, atmospheric vehicles contend with lift, drag, thrust, and weight simultaneously. This chapter develops the equations of motion for aircraft, derives fundamental performance relationships, and analyzes static and dynamic stability.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Derive the **equations of motion** for an aircraft in the body-axis and wind-axis frames.
2. Apply the **lift equation** $L = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_L$ and **drag polar** $C_D = C_{D_0} + KC_L^2$.
3. Compute **maximum range**, **maximum endurance**, and **best glide ratio**.
4. Analyze **static longitudinal stability** via the static margin.
5. Derive the **phugoid** and **short-period** modes of longitudinal dynamics.
6. Calculate **rate of climb**, **ceiling**, and **specific excess power**.

## 📚 1. Definitions

### Definition 10.5.1 — Lift Force

**Lift** $L$ is the aerodynamic force component perpendicular to the freestream velocity:

$$
L = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_L = q_\infty S C_L
$$

where:
- $\rho$ = air density (kg/m³)
- $v$ = airspeed (m/s)
- $S$ = wing reference area (m²)
- $C_L$ = lift coefficient (dimensionless)
- $q_\infty = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2$ = dynamic pressure (Pa)

### Definition 10.5.2 — Drag Force and Drag Polar

**Drag** $D$ is the aerodynamic force component parallel to (opposing) the freestream velocity:

$$
D = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_D = q_\infty S C_D
$$

The **drag polar** relates drag coefficient to lift coefficient:

$$
C_D = C_{D_0} + \frac{C_L^2}{\pi e AR} = C_{D_0} + KC_L^2
$$

where:
- $C_{D_0}$ = zero-lift (parasite) drag coefficient
- $e$ = Oswald efficiency factor ($0.7$–$0.9$)
- $AR = b^2/S$ = aspect ratio (wingspan² / area)
- $K = 1/(\pi e\,AR)$ = induced drag factor

### Definition 10.5.3 — Lift-to-Drag Ratio (Aerodynamic Efficiency)

$$
\frac{L}{D} = \frac{C_L}{C_D} = \frac{C_L}{C_{D_0} + KC_L^2}
$$

Maximum $L/D$ occurs at:

$$
C_{L,\text{opt}} = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D_0}}{K}}, \quad \left(\frac{L}{D}\right)_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{KC_{D_0}}}
$$

### Definition 10.5.4 — Angle of Attack

The **angle of attack** $\alpha$ is the angle between the chord line of the wing and the relative wind (freestream velocity vector). For a linear lift curve:

$$
C_L = C_{L_\alpha}(\alpha - \alpha_0)
$$

where $C_{L_\alpha} \approx 2\pi$ rad⁻¹ (thin airfoil theory) and $\alpha_0$ is the zero-lift angle.

### Definition 10.5.5 — Flight Path Angle

The **flight path angle** $\gamma$ is the angle between the velocity vector and the horizontal plane. Positive $\gamma$ = climbing.

### Definition 10.5.6 — Load Factor

The **load factor** $n$ is the ratio of lift to weight:

$$
n = \frac{L}{W}
$$

In level flight: $n = 1$. In a banked turn at bank angle $\phi$: $n = 1/\cos\phi$.

### Definition 10.5.7 — Specific Excess Power

**Specific excess power** $P_s$ measures the rate of energy gain per unit weight:

$$
P_s = \frac{(T-D)v}{W} = \frac{dh}{dt} + \frac{v}{g}\frac{dv}{dt}
$$

This represents the aircraft's ability to climb and/or accelerate simultaneously.

### Definition 10.5.8 — Stall Speed

The **stall speed** is the minimum speed for level flight at maximum lift coefficient:

$$
v_{\text{stall}} = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S C_{L,\max}}}
$$

### Definition 10.5.9 — Standard Atmosphere Model

The **International Standard Atmosphere (ISA)** defines:
- Sea level: $T_0 = 288.15$ K, $p_0 = 101{,}325$ Pa, $\rho_0 = 1.225$ kg/m³
- Troposphere (0–11 km): $T = T_0 - 6.5h$ (K), lapse rate 6.5 K/km
- Density ratio: $\sigma = \rho/\rho_0$

## 📐 2. Axioms / Postulates

### Axiom 10.5.A1 — Rigid Body Assumption

The aircraft is treated as a rigid body with six degrees of freedom (3 translational + 3 rotational). Structural flexibility is neglected.

### Axiom 10.5.A2 — Flat Earth Approximation

For most atmospheric flight problems, the Earth is treated as flat and non-rotating. Curvature effects become significant only for hypersonic/orbital vehicles.

### Axiom 10.5.A3 — Quasi-Steady Aerodynamics

Aerodynamic forces and moments depend only on the instantaneous flight condition (angle of attack, Mach number, dynamic pressure). Unsteady effects (flutter, dynamic stall) are neglected in basic performance analysis.

### Axiom 10.5.A4 — Small Perturbation Assumption (Stability)

For stability analysis, the aircraft is perturbed slightly from a trimmed equilibrium condition. The resulting equations of motion are linearized about the trim state.

## 🛡️ 3. Lemmas

### Lemma 10.5.1 — Equations of Motion in the Wind Axis

**Statement:** For an aircraft in symmetric flight (no sideslip), the equations of motion in the vertical plane are:

$$
m\dot{v} = T\cos\alpha - D - W\sin\gamma
$$

$$
mv\dot{\gamma} = T\sin\alpha + L - W\cos\gamma
$$

$$
\dot{h} = v\sin\gamma
$$

<details>
<summary>🔍 Derivation</summary>

Resolve forces along and perpendicular to the flight path:

Along flight path (tangential): Net force = $T\cos\alpha - D - W\sin\gamma = m\dot{v}$

Perpendicular to flight path (normal): Net force = $T\sin\alpha + L - W\cos\gamma = mv\dot\gamma$ (centripetal)

Vertical velocity: $\dot{h} = v\sin\gamma$

For small $\alpha$: $T\cos\alpha \approx T$ and $T\sin\alpha \approx 0$, simplifying to:

$$
m\dot{v} = T - D - W\sin\gamma
$$

$$
mv\dot\gamma = L - W\cos\gamma
$$

$\blacksquare$

</details>

### Lemma 10.5.2 — Maximum L/D Condition

**Statement:** The maximum lift-to-drag ratio occurs when induced drag equals parasite drag:

$$
KC_L^2 = C_{D_0} \implies C_L^* = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D_0}}{K}}
$$

<details>
<summary>🔍 Proof</summary>

$$
\frac{L}{D} = \frac{C_L}{C_{D_0} + KC_L^2}
$$

Differentiate with respect to $C_L$ and set to zero:

$$
\frac{d}{dC_L}\left(\frac{C_L}{C_{D_0}+KC_L^2}\right) = \frac{(C_{D_0}+KC_L^2) - C_L(2KC_L)}{(C_{D_0}+KC_L^2)^2} = 0
$$

Numerator = 0:

$$
C_{D_0} + KC_L^2 - 2KC_L^2 = 0 \implies C_{D_0} = KC_L^2
$$

$$
C_L^* = \sqrt{C_{D_0}/K}
$$

Maximum $L/D$:

$$
\left(\frac{L}{D}\right)_{\max} = \frac{\sqrt{C_{D_0}/K}}{C_{D_0} + C_{D_0}} = \frac{\sqrt{C_{D_0}/K}}{2C_{D_0}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{KC_{D_0}}}
$$

$\blacksquare$

</details>

### Lemma 10.5.3 — Thrust Required for Level Flight

**Statement:** In steady level flight ($\gamma = 0$, $\dot{v} = 0$): $T = D$ and $L = W$. The thrust required as a function of speed is:

$$
T_R = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_{D_0} + \frac{2KW^2}{\rho v^2 S}
$$

This is a "U-shaped" curve with minimum at the speed for maximum $L/D$.

## 👑 4. Theorems

### Theorem 10.5.1 — Breguet Range Equation (Jet Aircraft)

For a jet aircraft in steady cruise at constant altitude and $L/D$:

$$
R = \frac{v}{c_T}\frac{L}{D}\ln\frac{W_i}{W_f}
$$

where $c_T$ = thrust-specific fuel consumption (N/N/s = 1/s) and $W_i/W_f$ = initial/final weight ratio.

### Theorem 10.5.2 — Breguet Endurance Equation (Jet Aircraft)

$$
E = \frac{1}{c_T}\frac{L}{D}\ln\frac{W_i}{W_f}
$$

Maximum endurance requires flight at maximum $L/D$ (minimum drag speed).

### Theorem 10.5.3 — Best Glide Range

For an unpowered glide ($T = 0$), the glide angle is:

$$
\tan\gamma_{\text{glide}} = \frac{D}{L} = \frac{1}{L/D}
$$

Maximum glide range from altitude $h$:

$$
R_{\text{glide}} = h \cdot \frac{L}{D}\bigg|_{\max} = \frac{h}{2\sqrt{KC_{D_0}}}
$$

### Theorem 10.5.4 — Rate of Climb

$$
RC = \frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{(T-D)v}{W} = P_s \quad \text{(at constant speed)}
$$

Maximum rate of climb occurs at the speed where excess power $(T-D)v$ is maximum.

### Theorem 10.5.5 — Static Longitudinal Stability Criterion

An aircraft is statically stable in pitch if the pitching moment coefficient decreases with increasing angle of attack:

$$
C_{m_\alpha} = \frac{\partial C_m}{\partial \alpha} < 0
$$

This requires the center of gravity to be forward of the neutral point (aerodynamic center of the complete aircraft):

$$
\text{Static Margin} = \frac{x_{np} - x_{cg}}{\bar{c}} > 0
$$

### Theorem 10.5.6 — Phugoid and Short-Period Modes

The linearized longitudinal dynamics decouple into two modes:

**Short-period mode** (fast, well-damped): Oscillation in $\alpha$ and pitch rate $q$ at nearly constant speed.

$$
\omega_{sp} \approx \sqrt{\frac{q_\infty S C_{L_\alpha}}{m} \cdot \frac{C_{m_\alpha}}{C_{m_q}}} \quad \text{(approximate)}
$$

**Phugoid mode** (slow, lightly damped): Oscillation in speed and altitude at nearly constant $\alpha$.

$$
\omega_p \approx \frac{g\sqrt{2}}{v_0}, \quad \zeta_p \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{1}{L/D}
$$

## ✍️ 5. Proofs / Derivations

### 5.1 Derivation of the Breguet Range Equation

**Goal:** Derive $R = \frac{v}{c_T}\frac{L}{D}\ln(W_i/W_f)$.

**Step 1:** In steady cruise: $T = D$ and $L = W$. Fuel consumption:

$$
-\frac{dW}{dt} = c_T \cdot T = c_T \cdot D = c_T \cdot \frac{W}{L/D}
$$

**Step 2:** Range increment: $dR = v\,dt$. From Step 1: $dt = -\frac{L/D}{c_T W}dW$.

$$
dR = v\,dt = -\frac{v}{c_T}\frac{L}{D}\frac{dW}{W}
$$

**Step 3:** Integrate from initial weight $W_i$ to final weight $W_f$:

$$
R = -\frac{v}{c_T}\frac{L}{D}\int_{W_i}^{W_f}\frac{dW}{W} = \frac{v}{c_T}\frac{L}{D}\ln\frac{W_i}{W_f}
$$

$\blacksquare$

### 5.2 Derivation of the Phugoid Frequency

**Goal:** Derive $\omega_p = g\sqrt{2}/v_0$.

**Step 1:** The phugoid is an exchange between kinetic and potential energy at constant angle of attack. Perturb from trim: $v = v_0 + \Delta v$, $h = h_0 + \Delta h$.

**Step 2:** Energy conservation (approximately):

$$
\frac{1}{2}m(v_0 + \Delta v)^2 + mg(h_0 + \Delta h) \approx \text{const}
$$

Linearize: $mv_0\Delta v + mg\Delta h \approx 0$, so $\Delta v = -g\Delta h/v_0$.

**Step 3:** Vertical force balance perturbation. Lift perturbation due to speed change:

$$
\Delta L = \rho v_0 S C_L \Delta v = \frac{2W}{v_0}\Delta v
$$

(using $L_0 = W = \frac{1}{2}\rho v_0^2 S C_L$, so $\rho v_0 S C_L = 2W/v_0$).

**Step 4:** Equation of motion for vertical displacement:

$$
m\ddot{h} = \Delta L - 0 = \frac{2W}{v_0}\Delta v = \frac{2W}{v_0}\left(-\frac{g\Delta h}{v_0}\right) = -\frac{2mg^2}{v_0^2}\Delta h
$$

Wait — more carefully. The net vertical force perturbation is $\Delta L = \frac{2W}{v_0}\Delta v$. But we need to account for the flight path angle change.

Using the simplified approach: $m\ddot{(\Delta h)} \approx -\frac{2g^2 m}{v_0^2}\Delta h$

$$
\ddot{(\Delta h)} + \frac{2g^2}{v_0^2}\Delta h = 0
$$

**Step 5:** This is SHM with frequency:

$$
\omega_p = \frac{g\sqrt{2}}{v_0}
$$

Period: $T_p = \frac{2\pi}{\omega_p} = \frac{\pi v_0\sqrt{2}}{g}$. For a typical cruise at 250 m/s: $T_p \approx 113$ s. $\blacksquare$

### 5.3 Derivation of Minimum Drag Speed

**Goal:** Find the speed for minimum total drag in level flight.

**Step 1:** Total drag in level flight ($L = W$, so $C_L = 2W/(\rho v^2 S)$):

$$
D = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_{D_0} + \frac{2KW^2}{\rho v^2 S}
$$

**Step 2:** Differentiate with respect to $v$ and set to zero:

$$
\frac{dD}{dv} = \rho v S C_{D_0} - \frac{4KW^2}{\rho v^3 S} = 0
$$

**Step 3:** Solve:

$$
\rho v^4 S C_{D_0} = \frac{4KW^2}{\rho S}
$$

$$
v_{\min D}^4 = \frac{4KW^2}{\rho^2 S^2 C_{D_0}}
$$

$$
v_{\min D} = \left(\frac{4KW^2}{\rho^2 S^2 C_{D_0}}\right)^{1/4} = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S}}\left(\frac{K}{C_{D_0}}\right)^{1/4}
$$

At this speed, $C_L = \sqrt{C_{D_0}/K}$ (confirming Lemma 10.5.2). $\blacksquare$

## 🧮 6. Worked Examples

### Example 10.5.1 — Maximum Range of a Jet Transport

**Given:** Boeing 737-class aircraft: $W_i = 65{,}000$ kg × 9.81 = 637,650 N, fuel fraction 25% ($W_f = 0.75W_i$), cruise speed $v = 230$ m/s, $L/D = 17$, $c_T = 1.8 \times 10^{-5}$ s⁻¹.

**Find:** Maximum range.

**Solution:**

$$
R = \frac{v}{c_T}\frac{L}{D}\ln\frac{W_i}{W_f} = \frac{230}{1.8\times10^{-5}} \times 17 \times \ln\frac{1}{0.75}
$$

$$
= 1.278\times10^7 \times 17 \times 0.2877 = 6.25\times10^7 \text{ m} = 6250 \text{ km}
$$

---

### Example 10.5.2 — Glide Performance

**Given:** Aircraft with $C_{D_0} = 0.020$, $K = 0.045$, $W = 50{,}000$ N, $S = 30$ m², altitude $h = 3000$ m.

**Find:** Best glide ratio, glide range, and speed for best glide.

**Solution:**

Step 1: Maximum $L/D$:

$$
(L/D)_{\max} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{KC_{D_0}}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{0.045 \times 0.020}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{9\times10^{-4}}} = \frac{1}{2\times0.03} = 16.7
$$

Step 2: Glide range:

$$
R = h \times (L/D)_{\max} = 3000 \times 16.7 = 50{,}000 \text{ m} = 50 \text{ km}
$$

Step 3: Speed for best glide ($\rho$ at 3000 m ≈ 0.909 kg/m³):

$$
v_{\text{BG}} = \sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S}}\left(\frac{K}{C_{D_0}}\right)^{1/4} = \sqrt{\frac{2\times50000}{0.909\times30}}\left(\frac{0.045}{0.020}\right)^{1/4}
$$

$$
= \sqrt{\frac{100000}{27.27}} \times (2.25)^{0.25} = \sqrt{3667} \times 1.225 = 60.6 \times 1.225 = 74.2 \text{ m/s}
$$

---

### Example 10.5.3 — Rate of Climb

**Given:** Aircraft: $W = 80{,}000$ N, $S = 40$ m², $C_{D_0} = 0.025$, $K = 0.04$, thrust available $T = 25{,}000$ N, $\rho = 1.225$ kg/m³.

**Find:** Maximum rate of climb and the speed at which it occurs.

**Solution:**

Excess power: $P_{\text{ex}} = (T-D)v$. For maximum RC, we need to maximize $(T-D)v$.

At the speed for maximum excess power (approximately $v \approx 1.3 v_{\min D}$):

$$
v_{\min D} = \left(\frac{4 \times 0.04 \times 80000^2}{1.225^2 \times 40^2 \times 0.025}\right)^{1/4} = \left(\frac{1.024\times10^{10}}{60.0}\right)^{1/4} = (1.707\times10^8)^{0.25} = 114.3 \text{ m/s}
$$

At $v = 100$ m/s: $C_L = 2W/(\rho v^2 S) = 160000/(1.225\times10000\times40) = 0.327$

$C_D = 0.025 + 0.04(0.327)^2 = 0.025 + 0.00428 = 0.0293$

$D = 0.5\times1.225\times10000\times40\times0.0293 = 7180$ N

$RC = (T-D)v/W = (25000-7180)\times100/80000 = 22.3$ m/s = 4380 ft/min

## 📎 Appendix — Additional Derivations

### A.1 Derivation of Thrust Required Curve

**Goal:** Derive $T_R(v) = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_{D_0} + \frac{2KW^2}{\rho v^2 S}$ and find the minimum.

**Step 1:** In steady level flight: $L = W$ and $T = D$.

$$
C_L = \frac{2W}{\rho v^2 S}
$$

**Step 2:** Drag:

$$
D = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_D = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S\left(C_{D_0} + KC_L^2\right)
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_{D_0} + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S K\left(\frac{2W}{\rho v^2 S}\right)^2
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_{D_0} + \frac{2KW^2}{\rho v^2 S}
$$

**Step 3:** The first term (parasite drag) increases with $v^2$; the second (induced drag) decreases with $v^2$. Minimum occurs where they are equal:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_{D_0} = \frac{2KW^2}{\rho v^2 S}
$$

$$
v_{\min D}^4 = \frac{4KW^2}{\rho^2 S^2 C_{D_0}}
$$

At this speed: $D_{\min} = 2\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_{D_0} \cdot \frac{2KW^2}{\rho v^2 S}} = 2W\sqrt{KC_{D_0}}$

And $(L/D)_{\max} = W/D_{\min} = 1/(2\sqrt{KC_{D_0}})$. $\blacksquare$

### A.2 Derivation of Maximum Range Speed (Jet)

**Goal:** For maximum range, a jet aircraft should fly at maximum $v \cdot L/D$ (since range $\propto v \cdot L/D$).

**Step 1:** Maximize $v \cdot L/D = v \cdot C_L/C_D$. In level flight $C_L = 2W/(\rho v^2 S)$, so:

$$
v\frac{C_L}{C_D} = v\frac{2W/(\rho v^2 S)}{C_{D_0} + K[2W/(\rho v^2 S)]^2} = \frac{2W/(\rho v S)}{C_{D_0} + 4KW^2/(\rho^2 v^4 S^2)}
$$

**Step 2:** Alternatively, maximize $C_L^{1/2}/C_D$ (since $v \propto C_L^{-1/2}$ for fixed $W$):

$$
\frac{d}{dC_L}\frac{C_L^{1/2}}{C_{D_0}+KC_L^2} = 0
$$

$$
\frac{\frac{1}{2}C_L^{-1/2}(C_{D_0}+KC_L^2) - C_L^{1/2}(2KC_L)}{(C_{D_0}+KC_L^2)^2} = 0
$$

Numerator = 0:

$$
\frac{1}{2}(C_{D_0}+KC_L^2) = 2KC_L^2
$$

$$
C_{D_0} = 3KC_L^2 \implies C_L = \sqrt{\frac{C_{D_0}}{3K}}
$$

The maximum-range speed is $\sqrt{3}$ times the minimum-drag speed (faster). $\blacksquare$

### A.3 Derivation of Load Factor in a Banked Turn

**Goal:** Derive $n = 1/\cos\phi$ and turn radius $R = v^2/(g\sqrt{n^2-1})$.

**Step 1:** In a coordinated banked turn at bank angle $\phi$:
- Vertical equilibrium: $L\cos\phi = W$
- Centripetal force: $L\sin\phi = mv^2/R$

**Step 2:** Load factor:

$$
n = \frac{L}{W} = \frac{1}{\cos\phi}
$$

**Step 3:** Turn radius from centripetal equation:

$$
\frac{mv^2}{R} = L\sin\phi = W\frac{\sin\phi}{\cos\phi}\cdot\frac{1}{1} = nW\sin\phi
$$

Wait: $L\sin\phi = nW\sin\phi$... Let me redo.

$L\sin\phi = mv^2/R$ and $L = nW = nmg$:

$$
nmg\sin\phi = \frac{mv^2}{R}
$$

$$
R = \frac{v^2}{ng\sin\phi} = \frac{v^2}{g\tan\phi} = \frac{v^2}{g\sqrt{n^2-1}}
$$

(using $\tan\phi = \sqrt{n^2-1}$ from $n = 1/\cos\phi$).

**Step 4:** Turn rate:

$$
\dot\psi = \frac{v}{R} = \frac{g\sqrt{n^2-1}}{v}
$$

$\blacksquare$

### A.4 Power Required and Power Available

**Power required** for level flight:

$$
P_R = Dv = T_R v = \frac{1}{2}\rho v^3 S C_{D_0} + \frac{2KW^2}{\rho v S}
$$

**Minimum power** occurs at a lower speed than minimum drag (at $C_L = \sqrt{3C_{D_0}/K}$):

$$
v_{\min P} = \left(\frac{4KW^2}{3\rho^2 S^2 C_{D_0}}\right)^{1/4} = \frac{v_{\min D}}{3^{1/4}}
$$

This is the speed for **maximum endurance** (minimum fuel flow rate for jets).

---

### Example 10.5.4 — Banked Turn Performance

**Given:** Aircraft at $v = 150$ m/s, bank angle $\phi = 45°$.

**Find:** Load factor, turn radius, and turn rate.

**Solution:**

$$
n = 1/\cos 45° = 1/0.707 = 1.414
$$

$$
R = \frac{v^2}{g\sqrt{n^2-1}} = \frac{150^2}{9.81\sqrt{1.414^2-1}} = \frac{22500}{9.81\times1.0} = 2294 \text{ m}
$$

$$
\dot\psi = v/R = 150/2294 = 0.0654 \text{ rad/s} = 3.74°/\text{s}
$$

Time for 360° turn: $360/3.74 = 96$ s.

### Example 10.5.5 — Service Ceiling

**Given:** Aircraft with $T_{\text{max}} = 40{,}000$ N (constant with altitude for simplicity), $W = 100{,}000$ N, $S = 50$ m², $C_{D_0} = 0.020$, $K = 0.04$.

**Find:** Absolute ceiling (where $RC = 0$).

**Solution:**

At ceiling, $T = D_{\min}$:

$$
D_{\min} = 2W\sqrt{KC_{D_0}} = 2\times100000\sqrt{0.04\times0.020} = 200000\times0.0283 = 5657 \text{ N}
$$

Since $T_{\text{max}} = 40{,}000$ N $> D_{\min} = 5657$ N at sea level, the aircraft can climb.

For a more realistic model where thrust decreases with altitude ($T \propto \rho$):

$$
T(\rho) = T_0 \frac{\rho}{\rho_0} = 40000\sigma
$$

At ceiling: $T = D_{\min}$ at the ceiling density. But $D_{\min}$ is independent of density (it equals $2W\sqrt{KC_{D_0}}$):

$$
40000\sigma = 5657 \implies \sigma = 0.1414
$$

From standard atmosphere: $\sigma = 0.1414$ corresponds to approximately $h \approx 16{,}000$ m (stratosphere).




### A.5 Derivation of Induced Drag from Lifting-Line Theory

**Goal:** Show that $C_{D_i} = C_L^2/(\pi e AR)$ for a finite wing.

**Step 1:** A finite wing generates trailing vortices that induce a downwash velocity $w$ at the wing. This tilts the local relative wind by the **induced angle of attack**:

$$
\alpha_i = \frac{w}{v_\infty}
$$

**Step 2:** For an elliptical lift distribution (optimal), the downwash is uniform:

$$
w = \frac{C_L v_\infty}{\pi AR}
$$

$$
\alpha_i = \frac{C_L}{\pi AR}
$$

**Step 3:** The induced drag is the component of lift tilted backward by $\alpha_i$:

$$
C_{D_i} = C_L\alpha_i = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi AR}
$$

**Step 4:** For non-elliptical distributions, introduce the Oswald efficiency factor $e \leq 1$:

$$
C_{D_i} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi e\,AR} = KC_L^2
$$

$\blacksquare$

### A.6 Mach Number Effects

At high subsonic speeds, compressibility affects the aerodynamic coefficients:

**Prandtl-Glauert correction** (subsonic):

$$
C_{L_\alpha,\text{comp}} = \frac{C_{L_\alpha,\text{incomp}}}{\sqrt{1-M^2}}
$$

**Critical Mach number** $M_{cr}$: the freestream Mach at which local flow first reaches $M = 1$ somewhere on the airfoil. Above $M_{cr}$, wave drag appears.

**Drag divergence Mach** $M_{dd}$: where $dC_D/dM = 0.1$ (rapid drag rise). Typically $M_{dd} \approx M_{cr} + 0.05$ to $0.10$.

For supersonic flight ($M > 1$), the wave drag coefficient for a thin airfoil:

$$
C_{D_w} = \frac{4\alpha^2}{\sqrt{M^2-1}}
$$

### Example 10.5.6 — Takeoff Distance Estimation

**Given:** Aircraft: $W = 70{,}000$ N, $S = 35$ m², $C_{L,\text{TO}} = 1.2$ (with flaps), $C_{D,\text{TO}} = 0.06$, $T = 30{,}000$ N, $\mu_r = 0.02$ (rolling friction), $\rho = 1.225$ kg/m³.

**Find:** Approximate ground roll distance.

**Solution:**

Liftoff speed: $v_{\text{LO}} = 1.1 v_{\text{stall}} = 1.1\sqrt{\frac{2W}{\rho S C_{L,\max}}} = 1.1\sqrt{\frac{2\times70000}{1.225\times35\times1.8}} = 1.1\times47.5 = 52.3$ m/s

(Using $C_{L,\max} = 1.8$ with full flaps for stall, and $C_{L,\text{TO}} = 1.2$ at liftoff angle.)

Average acceleration during ground roll (using average speed $\bar{v} = 0.7 v_{\text{LO}}$):

$$
\bar{a} = \frac{T - D - \mu_r(W-L)}{m} = \frac{T - \frac{1}{2}\rho\bar{v}^2 S C_{D,\text{TO}} - \mu_r(W - \frac{1}{2}\rho\bar{v}^2 S C_{L,\text{TO}})}{W/g}
$$

At $\bar{v} = 36.6$ m/s: $q = 0.5\times1.225\times36.6^2 = 820$ Pa

$D = 820\times35\times0.06 = 1722$ N

$L = 820\times35\times1.2 = 34440$ N

$\bar{a} = \frac{30000 - 1722 - 0.02(70000-34440)}{70000/9.81} = \frac{30000-1722-711}{7136} = \frac{27567}{7136} = 3.86$ m/s²

Ground roll: $s = v_{\text{LO}}^2/(2\bar{a}) = 52.3^2/(2\times3.86) = 2735/(7.72) = 354$ m

### Example 10.5.7 — Specific Excess Power Diagram

**Given:** Fighter aircraft at $M = 0.9$, altitude 5000 m: $T = 60{,}000$ N, $D = 45{,}000$ N, $v = 295$ m/s, $W = 90{,}000$ N.

**Find:** $P_s$ and what the aircraft can do.

**Solution:**

$$
P_s = \frac{(T-D)v}{W} = \frac{(60000-45000)\times295}{90000} = \frac{4{,}425{,}000}{90000} = 49.2 \text{ m/s}
$$

This means the aircraft can:
- Climb at 49.2 m/s (9690 ft/min) at constant speed, OR
- Accelerate at $a = P_s \cdot g/v = 49.2\times9.81/295 = 1.64$ m/s² at constant altitude, OR
- Some combination of both

$P_s > 0$ means the aircraft has excess energy — it can gain altitude or speed. $P_s = 0$ defines the performance envelope boundary.




### A.7 V-n Diagram (Flight Envelope)

The **V-n diagram** defines the structural flight envelope in terms of airspeed $v$ and load factor $n$:

**Positive stall boundary** (maximum $n$ at given $v$):

$$
n_{\max}(v) = \frac{\frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 S C_{L,\max}}{W} = \frac{v^2}{v_{\text{stall}}^2}
$$

**Design limits:**
- Positive limit load: $n_{\max} = +3.8$ (transport), $+6$ to $+9$ (fighter)
- Negative limit load: $n_{\min} = -1.5$ (transport), $-3$ (fighter)
- Never-exceed speed $V_{NE}$: structural divergence limit
- Corner speed $V^*$: where stall boundary meets limit load factor

$$
V^* = v_{\text{stall}}\sqrt{n_{\max}} = \sqrt{\frac{2n_{\max}W}{\rho S C_{L,\max}}}
$$

At $V^*$, the aircraft can pull maximum g at maximum $C_L$ — the tightest possible turn.

### A.8 Longitudinal Static Stability — Detailed

The pitching moment coefficient about the CG:

$$
C_m = C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha}\alpha + C_{m_{\delta_e}}\delta_e + C_{m_q}\frac{q\bar{c}}{2v}
$$

where:
- $C_{m_0}$ = zero-lift pitching moment (depends on airfoil camber and tail setting)
- $C_{m_\alpha} = C_{L_\alpha}(x_{cg} - x_{ac})/\bar{c}$ = pitch stiffness (must be negative for stability)
- $C_{m_{\delta_e}}$ = elevator control power
- $C_{m_q}$ = pitch damping derivative (always negative — stabilizing)

**Trim condition:** $C_m = 0$ determines the trim angle of attack:

$$
\alpha_{\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_0} + C_{m_{\delta_e}}\delta_e}{C_{m_\alpha}}
$$

**Neutral point:** The CG location where $C_{m_\alpha} = 0$:

$$
x_{np} = x_{ac,\text{wing}} + \eta\frac{S_t}{S}\frac{l_t}{\bar{c}}a_t(1-\frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha})
$$

where $\eta$ = tail efficiency, $S_t/S$ = tail volume ratio, $l_t$ = tail arm, $a_t$ = tail lift curve slope, $d\epsilon/d\alpha$ = downwash gradient.

**Static margin** = $(x_{np} - x_{cg})/\bar{c}$. Typical values: 5–15% for conventional aircraft, 0–5% for fighters (relaxed stability with fly-by-wire).

### Example 10.5.8 — Trim Calculation

**Given:** $C_{m_0} = 0.05$, $C_{m_\alpha} = -0.8$ /rad, $C_{m_{\delta_e}} = -1.2$ /rad, elevator deflection $\delta_e = -3° = -0.0524$ rad.

**Find:** Trim angle of attack and trim $C_L$.

**Solution:**

$$
\alpha_{\text{trim}} = -\frac{C_{m_0} + C_{m_{\delta_e}}\delta_e}{C_{m_\alpha}} = -\frac{0.05 + (-1.2)(-0.0524)}{-0.8}
$$

$$
= -\frac{0.05 + 0.0629}{-0.8} = -\frac{0.1129}{-0.8} = 0.1411 \text{ rad} = 8.09°
$$

With $C_{L_\alpha} = 5.0$ /rad and $\alpha_0 = -2°$:

$$
C_L = C_{L_\alpha}(\alpha - \alpha_0) = 5.0(0.1411 + 0.0349) = 5.0\times0.176 = 0.880
$$
