# 9.3 — The 1D Infinite Square Well & Harmonic Oscillator

> *"The career of a young theoretical physicist consists of treating the harmonic oscillator in ever-increasing levels of abstraction."* — Sidney Coleman

These two potentials — the infinite square well and the quantum harmonic oscillator — are the hydrogen atoms of quantum mechanics pedagogy. The well teaches boundary conditions and quantization from geometry; the oscillator teaches algebraic methods (ladder operators) that generalize to quantum field theory. Every quantum field is, at its core, an infinite collection of harmonic oscillators. Master the oscillator, and you hold the key to all of QFT.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Solve the TISE for the infinite square well and derive the quantized energy spectrum $E_n \propto n^2$.
2. Compute expectation values and uncertainties for well eigenstates.
3. Solve the harmonic oscillator algebraically using ladder operators $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{a}^\dagger$.
4. Derive the energy spectrum $E_n = (n + \frac{1}{2})\hbar\omega$ from the commutation relation $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1$.
5. Construct position-space wave functions for the oscillator using Hermite polynomials.
6. Compute matrix elements $\langle m\vert\hat{x}\vert n\rangle$ and transition amplitudes.
7. Verify the uncertainty principle for oscillator eigenstates.

## 📚 1. Definitions

### Definition 9.3.1 — Infinite Square Well Potential

The **infinite square well** (particle in a box) is defined by:

$$
V(x) = \begin{cases} 0 & 0 < x < L \\ \infty & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
$$

The boundary conditions require $\psi(0) = \psi(L) = 0$.

### Definition 9.3.2 — Harmonic Oscillator Potential

The **quantum harmonic oscillator** has potential:

$$
V(x) = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2,
$$

where $\omega$ is the classical angular frequency. The Hamiltonian is:

$$
\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\hat{x}^2.
$$

### Definition 9.3.3 — Ladder Operators (Creation and Annihilation)

The **lowering (annihilation) operator** and **raising (creation) operator** are:

$$
\hat{a} = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{x} + \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right), \qquad \hat{a}^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{x} - \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right).
$$

Inverting these relations:

$$
\hat{x} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger), \qquad \hat{p} = i\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}}(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a}).
$$

### Definition 9.3.4 — Number Operator

The **number operator** is:

$$
\hat{N} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}.
$$

Its eigenvalues are the non-negative integers: $\hat{N}\vert n\rangle = n\vert n\rangle$, $n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$

### Definition 9.3.5 — Hermite Polynomials

The **Hermite polynomials** $H_n(\xi)$ are defined by the Rodrigues formula:

$$
H_n(\xi) = (-1)^n e^{\xi^2}\frac{d^n}{d\xi^n}e^{-\xi^2}.
$$

First few: $H_0 = 1$, $H_1 = 2\xi$, $H_2 = 4\xi^2 - 2$, $H_3 = 8\xi^3 - 12\xi$.

### Definition 9.3.6 — Characteristic Length Scale

The **oscillator length** (natural length scale) is:

$$
x_0 = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}}.
$$

The dimensionless coordinate is $\xi = x/x_0 = x\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}$.

## 📐 2. Axioms / Postulates

### Postulate 9.3.P1 — Boundary Conditions for the Infinite Well

The wave function must vanish at the impenetrable walls:

$$
\psi(0) = 0, \qquad \psi(L) = 0.
$$

This is because the probability of finding the particle inside an infinite potential barrier is zero.

### Postulate 9.3.P2 — Regularity of the Harmonic Oscillator

The wave function must be normalizable: $\psi(x) \to 0$ as $|x| \to \infty$. This eliminates the exponentially growing solution and quantizes the energy spectrum.

### Postulate 9.3.P3 — Canonical Commutation Relation (Algebraic Foundation)

The entire harmonic oscillator algebra follows from:

$$
[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1.
$$

This single relation, combined with the requirement that the Hilbert space has a lowest-energy state, determines the complete spectrum.

## 🛡️ 3. Lemmas

### Lemma 9.3.1 — The Commutation Relation $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1$

**Proof.** Compute directly from the definitions:

$$
[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}.
$$

$$
\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x} + \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right)\left(\hat{x} - \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right).
$$

Expand:

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 - \frac{i\hat{x}\hat{p}}{m\omega} + \frac{i\hat{p}\hat{x}}{m\omega} + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2}\right).
$$

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2} + \frac{i}{m\omega}(\hat{p}\hat{x} - \hat{x}\hat{p})\right).
$$

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2} - \frac{i}{m\omega}[\hat{x},\hat{p}]\right).
$$

Using $[\hat{x},\hat{p}] = i\hbar$:

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2} + \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\right).
$$

Similarly:

$$
\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2} - \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\right).
$$

Subtracting:

$$
[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\cdot\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega} = 1. \quad \blacksquare
$$

### Lemma 9.3.2 — Hamiltonian in Terms of Ladder Operators

**Statement:**

$$
\hat{H} = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{N} + \frac{1}{2}\right).
$$

**Proof.** From the computation above:

$$
\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2}\right) - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{\hbar\omega}\left(\frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\hat{x}^2\right) - \frac{1}{2} = \frac{\hat{H}}{\hbar\omega} - \frac{1}{2}.
$$

Solving for $\hat{H}$:

$$
\hat{H} = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2}\right). \quad \blacksquare
$$

### Lemma 9.3.3 — Commutators of $\hat{H}$ with Ladder Operators

**Statement:**

$$
[\hat{H}, \hat{a}] = -\hbar\omega\,\hat{a}, \qquad [\hat{H}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = +\hbar\omega\,\hat{a}^\dagger.
$$

**Proof.** Using $\hat{H} = \hbar\omega(\hat{N} + \frac{1}{2})$ and $[\hat{N}, \hat{a}] = [\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}]$:

$$
[\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}] = \hat{a}^\dagger[\hat{a}, \hat{a}] + [\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}]\hat{a} = 0 + (-1)\hat{a} = -\hat{a}.
$$

(Using $[\hat{A}\hat{B}, \hat{C}] = \hat{A}[\hat{B},\hat{C}] + [\hat{A},\hat{C}]\hat{B}$ and $[\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}] = -[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = -1$.)

Therefore: $[\hat{H}, \hat{a}] = \hbar\omega[\hat{N}, \hat{a}] = -\hbar\omega\,\hat{a}$.

Similarly: $[\hat{N}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = \hat{a}^\dagger[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] + [\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}^\dagger]\hat{a} = \hat{a}^\dagger\cdot 1 + 0 = \hat{a}^\dagger$.

So $[\hat{H}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = +\hbar\omega\,\hat{a}^\dagger$. $\blacksquare$

### Lemma 9.3.4 — Action of Ladder Operators on Energy Eigenstates

**Statement:** If $\hat{H}\vert n\rangle = E_n\vert n\rangle$, then:

$$
\hat{H}(\hat{a}\vert n\rangle) = (E_n - \hbar\omega)(\hat{a}\vert n\rangle), \qquad \hat{H}(\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle) = (E_n + \hbar\omega)(\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle).
$$

**Proof.** Using $[\hat{H}, \hat{a}] = -\hbar\omega\hat{a}$, i.e., $\hat{H}\hat{a} = \hat{a}\hat{H} - \hbar\omega\hat{a}$:

$$
\hat{H}(\hat{a}\vert n\rangle) = (\hat{a}\hat{H} - \hbar\omega\hat{a})\vert n\rangle = \hat{a}(E_n\vert n\rangle) - \hbar\omega(\hat{a}\vert n\rangle) = (E_n - \hbar\omega)(\hat{a}\vert n\rangle). \quad \blacksquare
$$

### Lemma 9.3.5 — Normalization of Ladder Operator States

**Statement:**

$$
\hat{a}\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{n}\,\vert n-1\rangle, \qquad \hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{n+1}\,\vert n+1\rangle.
$$

**Proof.** We know $\hat{a}\vert n\rangle$ is proportional to $\vert n-1\rangle$. Write $\hat{a}\vert n\rangle = c_n\vert n-1\rangle$. Compute $|c_n|^2$:

$$
|c_n|^2 = \langle n\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\vert n\rangle = \langle n\vert\hat{N}\vert n\rangle = n.
$$

Choosing $c_n$ real and positive: $c_n = \sqrt{n}$.

Similarly, $\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle = d_n\vert n+1\rangle$ with:

$$
|d_n|^2 = \langle n\vert\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle = \langle n\vert(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + 1)\vert n\rangle = n + 1.
$$

So $d_n = \sqrt{n+1}$. $\blacksquare$

## 👑 4. Theorems

### Theorem 9.3.1 — Energy Spectrum of the Infinite Square Well

The allowed energies are:

$$
E_n = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}, \quad n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots
$$

with normalized eigenfunctions:

$$
\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right).
$$

### Theorem 9.3.2 — Energy Spectrum of the Harmonic Oscillator

The allowed energies are:

$$
E_n = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega, \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots
$$

The spectrum is **equally spaced** with gap $\Delta E = \hbar\omega$. The ground state energy $E_0 = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$ is the **zero-point energy** — a purely quantum effect with no classical analogue.

### Theorem 9.3.3 — Harmonic Oscillator Wave Functions

The normalized position-space wave functions are:

$$
\psi_n(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^n n!}}\,H_n(\xi)\,e^{-\xi^2/2}, \quad \xi = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}}\,x.
$$

### Theorem 9.3.4 — Orthonormality and Completeness of Well Eigenstates

$$
\int_0^L \psi_m^*(x)\psi_n(x)\,dx = \delta_{mn}, \qquad \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\psi_n(x)\psi_n(x') = \delta(x - x').
$$

### Theorem 9.3.5 — Selection Rule for the Harmonic Oscillator

The position matrix element is:

$$
\langle m\vert\hat{x}\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\sqrt{n}\,\delta_{m,n-1} + \sqrt{n+1}\,\delta_{m,n+1}\right).
$$

Only transitions with $\Delta n = \pm 1$ are allowed (dipole selection rule).

## ✍️ 5. Proofs / Derivations

### 5.1 Complete Solution of the Infinite Square Well

**Step 1:** Inside the well ($0 < x < L$), $V = 0$. The TISE is:

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = E\psi \implies \frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} = -k^2\psi, \quad k = \frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}.
$$

**Step 2:** General solution: $\psi(x) = A\cos(kx) + B\sin(kx)$.

**Step 3:** Apply $\psi(0) = 0$: $A\cos(0) + B\sin(0) = A = 0$. So $\psi(x) = B\sin(kx)$.

**Step 4:** Apply $\psi(L) = 0$: $B\sin(kL) = 0$. For $B \neq 0$: $kL = n\pi$, $n = 1, 2, 3, \ldots$

Therefore $k_n = \frac{n\pi}{L}$ and:

$$
E_n = \frac{\hbar^2 k_n^2}{2m} = \frac{n^2\pi^2\hbar^2}{2mL^2}.
$$

**Step 5:** Normalize. $\int_0^L B^2\sin^2(n\pi x/L)\,dx = B^2 \cdot L/2 = 1$, giving $B = \sqrt{2/L}$. $\blacksquare$

### 5.2 Algebraic Derivation of the Harmonic Oscillator Spectrum

**Step 1:** The Hamiltonian is $\hat{H} = \hbar\omega(\hat{N} + \frac{1}{2})$ (Lemma 9.3.2). If $\hat{N}\vert n\rangle = n\vert n\rangle$, then $E_n = \hbar\omega(n + \frac{1}{2})$.

**Step 2: Existence of a ground state.** The operator $\hat{N} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$ is positive semi-definite:

$$
\langle\psi\vert\hat{N}\vert\psi\rangle = \langle\psi\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\vert\psi\rangle = \|\hat{a}\vert\psi\rangle\|^2 \geq 0.
$$

Therefore all eigenvalues $n \geq 0$.

**Step 3: The ladder terminates.** Applying $\hat{a}$ repeatedly lowers $n$ by 1 each time. Since $n \geq 0$, there must exist a state $\vert 0\rangle$ such that:

$$
\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle = 0.
$$

This is the **ground state**. Its energy is $E_0 = \hbar\omega(0 + \frac{1}{2}) = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$.

**Step 4: Building the spectrum.** Starting from $\vert 0\rangle$, apply $\hat{a}^\dagger$ repeatedly:

$$
\vert n\rangle = \frac{(\hat{a}^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\vert 0\rangle.
$$

Each application raises the energy by $\hbar\omega$, giving $E_n = (n + \frac{1}{2})\hbar\omega$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots$ $\blacksquare$

### 5.3 Ground State Wave Function from $\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle = 0$

**Step 1:** In position representation, $\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle = 0$ becomes:

$$
\sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(x + \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\psi_0(x) = 0.
$$

**Step 2:** Simplify:

$$
x\psi_0 + \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d\psi_0}{dx} = 0 \implies \frac{d\psi_0}{dx} = -\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}x\,\psi_0.
$$

**Step 3:** This is a separable first-order ODE:

$$
\frac{d\psi_0}{\psi_0} = -\frac{m\omega}{\hbar}x\,dx.
$$

Integrate:

$$
\ln\psi_0 = -\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}x^2 + C \implies \psi_0(x) = A\,e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)}.
$$

**Step 4:** Normalize using $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\alpha x^2}dx = \sqrt{\pi/\alpha}$ with $\alpha = m\omega/\hbar$:

$$
|A|^2\sqrt{\frac{\pi\hbar}{m\omega}} = 1 \implies A = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}.
$$

$$
\psi_0(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)}. \quad \blacksquare
$$

### 5.4 Derivation of the Selection Rule $\langle m\vert\hat{x}\vert n\rangle$

**Step 1:** Express $\hat{x}$ in terms of ladder operators:

$$
\hat{x} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger).
$$

**Step 2:** Compute the matrix element:

$$
\langle m\vert\hat{x}\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\langle m\vert(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)\vert n\rangle.
$$

$$
= \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\langle m\vert\hat{a}\vert n\rangle + \langle m\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle\right).
$$

**Step 3:** Using Lemma 9.3.5:

$$
\hat{a}\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{n}\vert n-1\rangle, \quad \hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{n+1}\vert n+1\rangle.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\langle m\vert\hat{x}\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\sqrt{n}\,\delta_{m,n-1} + \sqrt{n+1}\,\delta_{m,n+1}\right).
$$

This is non-zero only when $m = n \pm 1$. $\blacksquare$

### 5.5 Expectation Values for the Oscillator Ground State

**$\langle x \rangle$ and $\langle p \rangle$:**

$$
\langle 0\vert\hat{x}\vert 0\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\langle 0\vert(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)\vert 0\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\langle 0\vert\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle + \langle 0\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\vert 0\rangle).
$$

$\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle = 0$ and $\hat{a}^\dagger\vert 0\rangle = \vert 1\rangle$, so $\langle 0\vert\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle = 0$ and $\langle 0\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\vert 0\rangle = \langle 0\vert 1\rangle = 0$.

Therefore $\langle x\rangle = 0$. Similarly $\langle p\rangle = 0$.

**$\langle x^2 \rangle$:**

$$
\hat{x}^2 = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)^2 = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}(\hat{a}^2 + \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger + \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2).
$$

$$
\langle 0\vert\hat{x}^2\vert 0\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\langle 0\vert(\hat{a}^2 + \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger + \hat{N} + (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2)\vert 0\rangle.
$$

Only $\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger$ survives: $\langle 0\vert\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger\vert 0\rangle = \langle 0\vert(\hat{N} + 1)\vert 0\rangle = 1$.

$$
\langle x^2\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}.
$$

**$\langle p^2 \rangle$:**

$$
\hat{p}^2 = -\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})^2 = -\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}((\hat{a}^\dagger)^2 - \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} - \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger + \hat{a}^2).
$$

$$
\langle 0\vert\hat{p}^2\vert 0\rangle = -\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}\langle 0\vert(-\hat{N} - (\hat{N}+1))\vert 0\rangle... 
$$

More carefully: $(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})^2 = (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2 - \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} - \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger + \hat{a}^2$.

$\langle 0\vert(\hat{a}^\dagger)^2\vert 0\rangle = \langle 0\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\vert 1\rangle = \sqrt{2}\langle 0\vert 2\rangle = 0$.

$\langle 0\vert\hat{a}^2\vert 0\rangle = 0$.

$\langle 0\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle = 0$.

$\langle 0\vert\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger\vert 0\rangle = 1$.

So $\langle 0\vert(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})^2\vert 0\rangle = 0 - 0 - 1 + 0 = -1$.

Therefore: $\langle p^2\rangle = -\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}\cdot(-1) = \frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}$.

**Uncertainty product:**

$$
\Delta x \cdot \Delta p = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar^2}{4}} = \frac{\hbar}{2}.
$$

The ground state of the harmonic oscillator is a **minimum-uncertainty state** (Gaussian). $\blacksquare$

## 🧮 6. Worked Examples

### Example 9.3.1 — Energy Spectrum via Ladder Operators (Step by Step)

**Problem:** Starting from $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1$ and $\hat{H} = \hbar\omega(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2})$, derive the complete energy spectrum.

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Let $\vert E\rangle$ be an energy eigenstate: $\hat{H}\vert E\rangle = E\vert E\rangle$.

**Step 2:** Show $E \geq \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$. Since $\hat{N} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$:

$$
E = \langle E\vert\hat{H}\vert E\rangle = \hbar\omega\left(\langle E\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\vert E\rangle + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \hbar\omega\left(\|\hat{a}\vert E\rangle\|^2 + \frac{1}{2}\right) \geq \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}.
$$

**Step 3:** The state $\hat{a}\vert E\rangle$ has energy $E - \hbar\omega$ (Lemma 9.3.4). Repeated application gives states with energies $E - \hbar\omega, E - 2\hbar\omega, \ldots$ But energy is bounded below, so the sequence must terminate.

**Step 4:** Termination occurs when $\hat{a}\vert E_0\rangle = 0$ (the zero vector). Then:

$$
\hat{H}\vert E_0\rangle = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2}\right)\vert E_0\rangle = \hbar\omega\left(0 + \frac{1}{2}\right)\vert E_0\rangle = \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\vert E_0\rangle.
$$

So $E_0 = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega$.

**Step 5:** The excited states are $\vert n\rangle = \frac{(\hat{a}^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\vert 0\rangle$ with:

$$
E_n = E_0 + n\hbar\omega = \left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right)\hbar\omega, \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots \quad \blacksquare
$$

### Example 9.3.2 — Transition Matrix Element $\langle 2\vert\hat{x}^2\vert 0\rangle$

**Problem:** Compute $\langle 2\vert\hat{x}^2\vert 0\rangle$ for the harmonic oscillator.

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Express $\hat{x}^2$:

$$
\hat{x}^2 = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)^2 = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\left(\hat{a}^2 + \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger + \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2\right).
$$

**Step 2:** Act on $\vert 0\rangle$:

$$
\hat{a}^2\vert 0\rangle = 0, \quad \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger\vert 0\rangle = (\hat{N}+1)\vert 0\rangle = \vert 0\rangle, \quad \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle = 0, \quad (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2\vert 0\rangle = \hat{a}^\dagger\vert 1\rangle = \sqrt{2}\vert 2\rangle.
$$

**Step 3:** Therefore:

$$
\hat{x}^2\vert 0\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\left(0 + \vert 0\rangle + 0 + \sqrt{2}\vert 2\rangle\right) = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\left(\vert 0\rangle + \sqrt{2}\vert 2\rangle\right).
$$

**Step 4:** Take the inner product with $\langle 2\vert$:

$$
\langle 2\vert\hat{x}^2\vert 0\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\left(\langle 2\vert 0\rangle + \sqrt{2}\langle 2\vert 2\rangle\right) = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\left(0 + \sqrt{2}\right) = \frac{\hbar\sqrt{2}}{2m\omega}.
$$

### Example 9.3.3 — Expectation Values $\langle x \rangle$ and $\langle x^2 \rangle$ for the $n$-th Well Eigenstate

**Problem:** Compute $\langle x\rangle$ and $\langle x^2\rangle$ for the state $\psi_n(x) = \sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$ in the infinite well.

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** $\langle x\rangle$:

$$
\langle x\rangle = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L x\sin^2\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx.
$$

Using $\sin^2\theta = \frac{1-\cos 2\theta}{2}$:

$$
= \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L x\,dx - \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L x\cos\left(\frac{2n\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{L}{2} - \frac{1}{L}\cdot 0 = \frac{L}{2}.
$$

(The cosine integral vanishes: $\int_0^L x\cos(2n\pi x/L)dx = \frac{L}{2n\pi}[x\sin(2n\pi x/L)]_0^L + \frac{L^2}{4n^2\pi^2}[\cos(2n\pi x/L)]_0^L = 0$.)

The particle is equally likely to be found on either side of the center, for all $n$. ✓

**Step 2:** $\langle x^2\rangle$:

$$
\langle x^2\rangle = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L x^2\sin^2\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L x^2\,dx - \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L x^2\cos\left(\frac{2n\pi x}{L}\right)dx.
$$

First integral: $\frac{1}{L}\cdot\frac{L^3}{3} = \frac{L^2}{3}$.

Second integral (integration by parts twice):

$$
\int_0^L x^2\cos\left(\frac{2n\pi x}{L}\right)dx = \frac{L^3}{2n^2\pi^2}.
$$

(Detailed: let $k = 2n\pi/L$. $\int_0^L x^2\cos(kx)dx = [x^2\frac{\sin kx}{k}]_0^L - \frac{2}{k}\int_0^L x\sin(kx)dx = 0 - \frac{2}{k}[-\frac{x\cos kx}{k}]_0^L + \frac{2}{k}\cdot\frac{1}{k}\int_0^L\cos(kx)dx = \frac{2L}{k^2}\cos(kL) + 0 = \frac{2L}{k^2} = \frac{L^3}{2n^2\pi^2}$.)

Therefore:

$$
\langle x^2\rangle = \frac{L^2}{3} - \frac{L^2}{2n^2\pi^2} = L^2\left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{2n^2\pi^2}\right).
$$

**Variance:**

$$
(\Delta x)^2 = \langle x^2\rangle - \langle x\rangle^2 = L^2\left(\frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{2n^2\pi^2} - \frac{1}{4}\right) = L^2\left(\frac{1}{12} - \frac{1}{2n^2\pi^2}\right).
$$

As $n \to \infty$: $(\Delta x)^2 \to \frac{L^2}{12}$, which is the classical result for a uniform distribution on $[0, L]$.

---

### Example 9.3.4 — Constructing $\psi_2(x)$ from the Ground State

**Problem:** Use $\hat{a}^\dagger$ twice on $\psi_0(x)$ to construct $\psi_2(x)$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** $\vert 2\rangle = \frac{(\hat{a}^\dagger)^2}{\sqrt{2!}}\vert 0\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\hat{a}^\dagger)^2\vert 0\rangle$.

In position space, $\hat{a}^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(x - \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d}{dx}\right)$.

**Step 2:** First application — compute $\psi_1$:

$$
\psi_1(x) = \hat{a}^\dagger\psi_0 = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(x - \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d}{dx}\right)\left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)}.
$$

The derivative: $\frac{d}{dx}e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)} = -\frac{m\omega x}{\hbar}e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)}$.

$$
\psi_1 = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\left(x + x\right)e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)} = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\cdot 2x\,e^{-m\omega x^2/(2\hbar)}.
$$

Using $\xi = x\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}$:

$$
\psi_1(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\sqrt{2}\,\xi\,e^{-\xi^2/2} = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^1 \cdot 1!}}H_1(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2}.
$$

(Since $H_1(\xi) = 2\xi$.) ✓

**Step 3:** Second application — compute $\psi_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}^\dagger\psi_1$:

$$
\hat{a}^\dagger\psi_1 \propto \left(x - \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d}{dx}\right)(2\xi\,e^{-\xi^2/2}).
$$

Converting to $\xi$: $\frac{d}{dx} = \sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}\frac{d}{d\xi}$, so $\frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\frac{d}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{m\omega/\hbar}}\frac{d}{d\xi} = x_0\frac{d}{d\xi}$ and $x = x_0\xi$.

$$
\hat{a}^\dagger \sim \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}x_0}\left(x_0\xi - x_0\frac{d}{d\xi}\right) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\xi - \frac{d}{d\xi}\right).
$$

Apply to $H_1(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2} = 2\xi e^{-\xi^2/2}$:

$$
\left(\xi - \frac{d}{d\xi}\right)(2\xi e^{-\xi^2/2}) = 2\xi^2 e^{-\xi^2/2} - 2\frac{d}{d\xi}(\xi e^{-\xi^2/2}).
$$

$$
= 2\xi^2 e^{-\xi^2/2} - 2(e^{-\xi^2/2} - \xi^2 e^{-\xi^2/2}) = 2\xi^2 e^{-\xi^2/2} - 2e^{-\xi^2/2} + 2\xi^2 e^{-\xi^2/2}.
$$

$$
= (4\xi^2 - 2)e^{-\xi^2/2} = H_2(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2}.
$$

After proper normalization:

$$
\psi_2(x) = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^2 \cdot 2!}}H_2(\xi)e^{-\xi^2/2} = \left(\frac{m\omega}{\pi\hbar}\right)^{1/4}\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(4\xi^2 - 2)e^{-\xi^2/2}. \quad \blacksquare
$$

### Example 9.3.5 — Time Evolution of a Coherent Superposition in the Well

**Problem:** A particle starts in $\Psi(x,0) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin^2(\pi x/L)$. Express this as a superposition of energy eigenstates and find $\Psi(x,t)$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Use the identity $\sin^2\theta = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos 2\theta)$:

$$
\Psi(x,0) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\left(1 - \cos\frac{2\pi x}{L}\right).
$$

**Step 2:** Express in terms of the well eigenstates $\psi_n = \sqrt{2/L}\sin(n\pi x/L)$. Compute the expansion coefficients:

$$
c_n = \int_0^L \psi_n^*(x)\Psi(x,0)\,dx = \frac{2}{L}\int_0^L \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx.
$$

Using $\sin^2(\pi x/L) = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos(2\pi x/L))$:

$$
c_n = \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx - \frac{1}{L}\int_0^L \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\cos\left(\frac{2\pi x}{L}\right)dx.
$$

First integral: $\frac{1}{L}\cdot\frac{L}{n\pi}[-\cos(n\pi x/L)]_0^L = \frac{1}{n\pi}(1 - \cos n\pi) = \frac{1}{n\pi}(1 - (-1)^n)$.

This is $\frac{2}{n\pi}$ for odd $n$ and $0$ for even $n$.

Second integral uses $\sin A\cos B = \frac{1}{2}[\sin(A+B) + \sin(A-B)]$:

$$
= \frac{1}{2L}\int_0^L\left[\sin\frac{(n+2)\pi x}{L} + \sin\frac{(n-2)\pi x}{L}\right]dx.
$$

For $n \neq 2$: each term gives $\frac{1}{(n\pm2)\pi}(1 - (-1)^{n\pm2})$. For $n = 2$: the second term is $\sin(0) = 0$.

The dominant terms are $c_1$ and $c_3$. After evaluation:

$$
c_1 = \frac{8}{3\pi}, \quad c_2 = 0, \quad c_3 = \frac{8}{15\pi}, \quad \ldots
$$

**Step 3:** Time evolution:

$$
\Psi(x,t) = \sum_n c_n\psi_n(x)e^{-iE_n t/\hbar}.
$$

The probability density oscillates with frequencies $\omega_{mn} = (E_m - E_n)/\hbar$.

### Example 8.1 — Full Derivation of Harmonic Oscillator Energies via Ladder Operators (Every Commutator Step)

**Problem:** Starting from $\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\hat{x}^2$, define ladder operators and derive the complete energy spectrum $E_n = \hbar\omega(n + 1/2)$ using only algebraic methods.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

#### Step 1: Define the Ladder Operators

$$
\hat{a} = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{x} + \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right), \qquad \hat{a}^\dagger = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}}\left(\hat{x} - \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right).
$$

#### Step 2: Compute $[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger]$ Explicitly

$$
[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left[\hat{x} + \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega},\; \hat{x} - \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right].
$$

Expand using bilinearity of the commutator:

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left([\hat{x}, \hat{x}] - \frac{i}{m\omega}[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] + \frac{i}{m\omega}[\hat{p}, \hat{x}] - \frac{1}{m^2\omega^2}[\hat{p}, \hat{p}]\right).
$$

Now: $[\hat{x}, \hat{x}] = 0$, $[\hat{p}, \hat{p}] = 0$, $[\hat{x}, \hat{p}] = i\hbar$, $[\hat{p}, \hat{x}] = -i\hbar$.

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(0 - \frac{i}{m\omega}(i\hbar) + \frac{i}{m\omega}(-i\hbar) - 0\right).
$$

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\frac{\hbar}{m\omega} + \frac{\hbar}{m\omega}\right) = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\cdot\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega} = 1.
$$

$$
\boxed{[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = 1.}
$$

#### Step 3: Express $\hat{H}$ in Terms of $\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger$

Compute $\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$:

$$
\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x} - \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right)\left(\hat{x} + \frac{i\hat{p}}{m\omega}\right).
$$

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 + \frac{i\hat{x}\hat{p}}{m\omega} - \frac{i\hat{p}\hat{x}}{m\omega} + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2}\right).
$$

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 + \frac{i[\hat{x}, \hat{p}]}{m\omega} + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2}\right).
$$

$$
= \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 + \frac{i(i\hbar)}{m\omega} + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2}\right) = \frac{m\omega}{2\hbar}\left(\hat{x}^2 - \frac{\hbar}{m\omega} + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{m^2\omega^2}\right).
$$

$$
= \frac{m\omega\hat{x}^2}{2\hbar} - \frac{1}{2} + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m\hbar\omega}.
$$

Multiply through by $\hbar\omega$:

$$
\hbar\omega\,\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2\hat{x}^2 + \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} - \frac{\hbar\omega}{2} = \hat{H} - \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}.
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\hat{H} = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + \frac{1}{2}\right) = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{N} + \frac{1}{2}\right),}
$$

where $\hat{N} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$ is the number operator.

#### Step 4: Commutation Relations of $\hat{H}$ with $\hat{a}$ and $\hat{a}^\dagger$

$$
[\hat{H}, \hat{a}] = \hbar\omega[\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}] = \hbar\omega(\hat{a}^\dagger[\hat{a}, \hat{a}] + [\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}]\hat{a}) = \hbar\omega(0 + (-1)\hat{a}) = -\hbar\omega\,\hat{a}.
$$

$$
[\hat{H}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = \hbar\omega[\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] = \hbar\omega(\hat{a}^\dagger[\hat{a}, \hat{a}^\dagger] + [\hat{a}^\dagger, \hat{a}^\dagger]\hat{a}) = \hbar\omega(\hat{a}^\dagger\cdot 1 + 0) = +\hbar\omega\,\hat{a}^\dagger.
$$

#### Step 5: $\hat{a}^\dagger$ Raises Energy, $\hat{a}$ Lowers Energy

If $\hat{H}\vert n\rangle = E_n\vert n\rangle$, then:

$$
\hat{H}(\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle) = (\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{H} + \hbar\omega\hat{a}^\dagger)\vert n\rangle = (E_n + \hbar\omega)\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle.
$$

So $\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle$ is an eigenstate with energy $E_n + \hbar\omega$.

Similarly: $\hat{H}(\hat{a}\vert n\rangle) = (E_n - \hbar\omega)\hat{a}\vert n\rangle$.

#### Step 6: The Spectrum is Bounded Below

Since $\hat{N} = \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}$ and for any state $\vert\psi\rangle$:

$$
\langle\psi\vert\hat{N}\vert\psi\rangle = \langle\psi\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\vert\psi\rangle = \|\hat{a}\vert\psi\rangle\|^2 \geq 0.
$$

So all eigenvalues of $\hat{N}$ are non-negative. There must exist a ground state $\vert 0\rangle$ such that $\hat{a}\vert 0\rangle = 0$.

#### Step 7: Ground State Energy

$$
\hat{H}\vert 0\rangle = \hbar\omega\left(\hat{N} + \frac{1}{2}\right)\vert 0\rangle = \hbar\omega\left(0 + \frac{1}{2}\right)\vert 0\rangle = \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\vert 0\rangle.
$$

#### Step 8: Complete Spectrum by Induction

Applying $\hat{a}^\dagger$ repeatedly: $\vert n\rangle = \frac{(\hat{a}^\dagger)^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\vert 0\rangle$ (normalization proved below), with:

$$
\boxed{E_n = \hbar\omega\left(n + \frac{1}{2}\right), \quad n = 0, 1, 2, \ldots}
$$

#### Step 9: Normalization Factor

Prove $\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{n+1}\vert n+1\rangle$:

$$
\|\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle\|^2 = \langle n\vert\hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger\vert n\rangle = \langle n\vert(\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + 1)\vert n\rangle = \langle n\vert(\hat{N} + 1)\vert n\rangle = n + 1.
$$

Similarly $\hat{a}\vert n\rangle = \sqrt{n}\vert n-1\rangle$:

$$
\|\hat{a}\vert n\rangle\|^2 = \langle n\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a}\vert n\rangle = \langle n\vert\hat{N}\vert n\rangle = n. \quad \blacksquare
$$

</details>

### Example 8.2 — Coherent States of the Harmonic Oscillator

**Problem:** A coherent state $\vert\alpha\rangle$ is defined as an eigenstate of the annihilation operator: $\hat{a}\vert\alpha\rangle = \alpha\vert\alpha\rangle$ where $\alpha \in \mathbb{C}$. Expand $\vert\alpha\rangle$ in the number basis and show it is a minimum-uncertainty state.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

#### Step 1: Expand in Number Basis

Write $\vert\alpha\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n\vert n\rangle$. Apply $\hat{a}$:

$$
\hat{a}\vert\alpha\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_n\hat{a}\vert n\rangle = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n\sqrt{n}\vert n-1\rangle = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} c_{n+1}\sqrt{n+1}\vert n\rangle.
$$

Set equal to $\alpha\vert\alpha\rangle = \sum_n \alpha c_n\vert n\rangle$:

$$
c_{n+1}\sqrt{n+1} = \alpha c_n \implies c_{n+1} = \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{n+1}}c_n.
$$

#### Step 2: Solve the Recursion

$$
c_n = \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{n}}c_{n-1} = \frac{\alpha^2}{\sqrt{n}\sqrt{n-1}}c_{n-2} = \cdots = \frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}c_0.
$$

#### Step 3: Normalize

$$
\langle\alpha\vert\alpha\rangle = |c_0|^2\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!} = |c_0|^2 e^{|\alpha|^2} = 1.
$$

Therefore $c_0 = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}$ (choosing real positive).

$$
\boxed{\vert\alpha\rangle = e^{-|\alpha|^2/2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\vert n\rangle.}
$$

#### Step 4: Photon Number Distribution

The probability of finding $n$ quanta:

$$
P(n) = |c_n|^2 = e^{-|\alpha|^2}\frac{|\alpha|^{2n}}{n!}.
$$

This is a **Poisson distribution** with mean $\bar{n} = |\alpha|^2$.

#### Step 5: Expectation Values

$$
\langle\hat{x}\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\langle\alpha\vert(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)\vert\alpha\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\alpha + \alpha^*) = \sqrt{\frac{2\hbar}{m\omega}}\,\text{Re}(\alpha).
$$

$$
\langle\hat{p}\rangle = i\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}}\langle\alpha\vert(\hat{a}^\dagger - \hat{a})\vert\alpha\rangle = i\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}}(\alpha^* - \alpha) = \sqrt{2m\omega\hbar}\,\text{Im}(\alpha).
$$

#### Step 6: Variances

$$
\langle\hat{x}^2\rangle = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\langle\alpha\vert(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)^2\vert\alpha\rangle.
$$

Expand: $(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)^2 = \hat{a}^2 + \hat{a}\hat{a}^\dagger + \hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2 = \hat{a}^2 + 2\hat{a}^\dagger\hat{a} + 1 + (\hat{a}^\dagger)^2$.

$$
\langle\alpha\vert(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)^2\vert\alpha\rangle = \alpha^2 + 2|\alpha|^2 + 1 + (\alpha^*)^2 = (\alpha + \alpha^*)^2 + 1.
$$

$$
(\Delta x)^2 = \langle x^2\rangle - \langle x\rangle^2 = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}\left[(\alpha + \alpha^*)^2 + 1 - (\alpha + \alpha^*)^2\right] = \frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}.
$$

Similarly: $(\Delta p)^2 = \frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}$.

#### Step 7: Uncertainty Product

$$
\Delta x\cdot\Delta p = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{m\omega\hbar}{2}} = \frac{\hbar}{2}.
$$

Coherent states **saturate** the Heisenberg bound — they are minimum-uncertainty states with equal uncertainties in $x$ and $p$ (in natural units). $\blacksquare$

</details>

### Example 8.3 — Matrix Elements $\langle n\vert\hat{x}\vert m\rangle$ for the Harmonic Oscillator

**Problem:** Compute the position matrix elements $\langle n\vert\hat{x}\vert m\rangle$ and show that only $m = n \pm 1$ transitions are non-zero (selection rule).

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

#### Step 1: Express $\hat{x}$ in Terms of Ladder Operators

$$
\hat{x} = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger).
$$

#### Step 2: Compute the Matrix Element

$$
\langle n\vert\hat{x}\vert m\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\langle n\vert(\hat{a} + \hat{a}^\dagger)\vert m\rangle.
$$

$$
= \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\langle n\vert\hat{a}\vert m\rangle + \langle n\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\vert m\rangle\right).
$$

#### Step 3: Evaluate Each Term

$$
\hat{a}\vert m\rangle = \sqrt{m}\vert m-1\rangle \implies \langle n\vert\hat{a}\vert m\rangle = \sqrt{m}\,\delta_{n,m-1}.
$$

$$
\hat{a}^\dagger\vert m\rangle = \sqrt{m+1}\vert m+1\rangle \implies \langle n\vert\hat{a}^\dagger\vert m\rangle = \sqrt{m+1}\,\delta_{n,m+1}.
$$

#### Step 4: Final Result

$$
\boxed{\langle n\vert\hat{x}\vert m\rangle = \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\left(\sqrt{m}\,\delta_{n,m-1} + \sqrt{m+1}\,\delta_{n,m+1}\right).}
$$

**Selection rule:** $\langle n\vert\hat{x}\vert m\rangle \neq 0$ only if $n = m \pm 1$. This means electric dipole transitions in the harmonic oscillator can only change the quantum number by $\pm 1$.

#### Step 5: The Position Matrix (Infinite-Dimensional)

$$
\hat{x} \to \sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega}}\begin{pmatrix} 0 & \sqrt{1} & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\ \sqrt{1} & 0 & \sqrt{2} & 0 & \cdots \\ 0 & \sqrt{2} & 0 & \sqrt{3} & \cdots \\ 0 & 0 & \sqrt{3} & 0 & \cdots \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots \end{pmatrix}.
$$

This is a tridiagonal matrix — a hallmark of the harmonic oscillator's algebraic structure. $\blacksquare$

</details>

### Appendix 9.1 — Supersymmetric (SUSY) Ladder Structure of the Harmonic Oscillator

The harmonic oscillator possesses a hidden supersymmetric structure that explains why the ladder operator method works so elegantly. This is the simplest example of **supersymmetric quantum mechanics** (Witten, 1981).

**Setup:** Factor the Hamiltonian using "superpotential" $W(x)$:

$$
\hat{H}_- = \hat{A}^\dagger\hat{A} + E_0, \qquad \hat{H}_+ = \hat{A}\hat{A}^\dagger + E_0,
$$

where:

$$
\hat{A} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2m}}\hat{p} + iW(\hat{x}), \qquad \hat{A}^\dagger = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2m}}\hat{p} - iW(\hat{x}).
$$

**For the harmonic oscillator:** $W(x) = \frac{m\omega x}{2\hbar^{1/2}}$ (up to normalization). More precisely, define:

$$
W(x) = \frac{m\omega x}{\sqrt{2m}} = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega^2}{2}}\,x.
$$

Then:

$$
\hat{A}^\dagger\hat{A} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + W^2 - \frac{\hbar}{2}W'(x)\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m}}\cdot\sqrt{2m} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2 - \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}.
$$

Wait — let's be more careful. With $\hat{A} = \frac{\hat{p}}{\sqrt{2m}} + iW(\hat{x})$:

$$
\hat{A}^\dagger\hat{A} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + W^2 + \frac{i}{\sqrt{2m}}[\hat{p}, W(\hat{x})].
$$

Since $[\hat{p}, W(\hat{x})] = -i\hbar W'(x)$:

$$
\hat{A}^\dagger\hat{A} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + W^2 + \frac{\hbar W'}{\sqrt{2m}}\cdot\frac{1}{1}.
$$

Hmm — let's use the standard SUSY QM formulation directly. Define:

$$
\hat{A} = \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\frac{d}{dx} + W(x), \qquad \hat{A}^\dagger = -\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\frac{d}{dx} + W(x).
$$

Then:

$$
\hat{H}_- = \hat{A}^\dagger\hat{A} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + W^2 - \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}W'.
$$

$$
\hat{H}_+ = \hat{A}\hat{A}^\dagger = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + W^2 + \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}W'.
$$

For the harmonic oscillator, choose $W(x) = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega^2}{2}}\,x = \frac{m\omega x}{\sqrt{2m}}$. Then $W' = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega^2}{2}}$ and:

$$
W^2 = \frac{m\omega^2 x^2}{2}, \qquad \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}W' = \frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\cdot\frac{m\omega}{\sqrt{2m}} = \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}.
$$

So:

$$
\hat{H}_- = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2 - \frac{\hbar\omega}{2} = \hat{H}_{\text{HO}} - \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}.
$$

$$
\hat{H}_+ = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^2}{dx^2} + \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2 + \frac{\hbar\omega}{2} = \hat{H}_{\text{HO}} + \frac{\hbar\omega}{2}.
$$

**The SUSY structure:**

1. $\hat{H}_- \geq 0$ (since $\hat{A}^\dagger\hat{A}$ is positive semi-definite), so $E_{\text{HO}} \geq \hbar\omega/2$.
2. The ground state satisfies $\hat{A}\vert 0\rangle = 0$, i.e., $\left(\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\frac{d}{dx} + \sqrt{\frac{m\omega^2}{2}}x\right)\psi_0 = 0$, giving the Gaussian ground state.
3. The partner Hamiltonians $\hat{H}_\pm$ are **isospectral** (same spectrum) except possibly for the ground state of $\hat{H}_-$.

**General SUSY QM principle:** Given any solvable potential, the superpotential $W(x) = -\frac{\hbar}{\sqrt{2m}}\frac{\psi_0'}{\psi_0}$ generates a partner potential with the same spectrum shifted by one level. This is the algebraic reason behind the solvability of the harmonic oscillator, Coulomb potential, Morse potential, and Pöschl-Teller potential.

**References:** Witten, E. (1981) *Nucl. Phys. B* **188**, 513; Cooper, Khare & Sukhatme, *Supersymmetry in Quantum Mechanics* (World Scientific, 2001); Griffiths QM Problem 4.62.

### Appendix 9.2 — Transition Probabilities Between Infinite Square Well States Under a Perturbation

Consider a particle in the ground state of an infinite square well of width $L$. At $t = 0$, a weak perturbation $V_1(x) = V_0\sin(\pi x/L)$ is turned on. What is the probability of transition to the $n$-th excited state?

**First-order time-dependent perturbation theory** gives:

$$
P_{1\to n}(t) = \frac{|V_{n1}|^2}{\hbar^2}\cdot\frac{\sin^2(\omega_{n1}t/2)}{(\omega_{n1}/2)^2},
$$

where $\omega_{n1} = (E_n - E_1)/\hbar$ and the matrix element is:

$$
V_{n1} = \langle n\vert V_1\vert 1\rangle = \frac{2V_0}{L}\int_0^L \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)\sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx.
$$

$$
= \frac{2V_0}{L}\int_0^L \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\sin^2\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx.
$$

Using $\sin^2\theta = \frac{1}{2}(1 - \cos 2\theta)$:

$$
= \frac{V_0}{L}\int_0^L \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)dx - \frac{V_0}{L}\int_0^L \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\cos\left(\frac{2\pi x}{L}\right)dx.
$$

The first integral: $\frac{1 - (-1)^n}{n\pi}$. The second uses $\sin A\cos B = \frac{1}{2}[\sin(A+B) + \sin(A-B)]$.

For $n = 2$: $V_{21} = \frac{V_0}{L}\cdot\frac{2}{2\pi} - \frac{V_0}{2L}\left[\int_0^L\sin(4\pi x/L)dx + 0\right] = \frac{V_0}{\pi} - 0 = \frac{V_0}{\pi}$.

Wait — let's compute more carefully for $n = 3$ (the dominant transition):

$$
V_{31} = \frac{V_0}{L}\cdot 0 - \frac{V_0}{2L}\left[\int_0^L\sin(5\pi x/L)dx + \int_0^L\sin(\pi x/L)dx\right].
$$

$$
= -\frac{V_0}{2L}\left[\frac{L}{5\pi}(1-\cos 5\pi) + \frac{L}{\pi}(1-\cos\pi)\right] = -\frac{V_0}{2}\left[\frac{2}{5\pi} + \frac{2}{\pi}\right] = -\frac{V_0}{\pi}\cdot\frac{6}{5}.
$$

The selection rules and matrix elements determine which transitions are allowed and their relative strengths.

**References:** Griffiths QM §11.1; Sakurai §5.6.
