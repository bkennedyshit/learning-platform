# 9.8 — Feynman Path Integrals & Quantum Electrodynamics

> *"The electron does anything it likes. It goes in any direction at any speed, forward or backward in time, however it likes, and then you add up the amplitudes and it gives you the wave function."* — Richard Feynman

Feynman's path integral reformulates quantum mechanics as a sum over all possible histories, weighted by $e^{iS/\hbar}$. This approach is not merely an alternative to the operator formalism — it is the natural language for quantum field theory, gauge theories, and non-perturbative physics. This chapter derives the path integral from first principles, computes the free-particle propagator, develops Feynman rules for QED, and calculates a tree-level scattering amplitude.

---

5. Draw and interpret Feynman diagrams for basic QED processes.

## 📚 1. Definitions

### Definition 9.8.1 — Path Integral (Quantum Mechanics)

The transition amplitude from $(x_i, t_i)$ to $(x_f, t_f)$ is:

$$
\langle x_f, t_f \vert x_i, t_i\rangle = \int \mathcal{D}[x(t)]\,e^{iS[x(t)]/\hbar},
$$

where the integral is over all paths $x(t)$ with $x(t_i) = x_i$, $x(t_f) = x_f$, and $S[x] = \int_{t_i}^{t_f}L(x,\dot{x})\,dt$ is the classical action.

### Definition 9.8.2 — Functional Measure

$$
\mathcal{D}[x(t)] = \lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\epsilon}\right)^{N/2}\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}dx_j,
$$

where $\epsilon = (t_f - t_i)/N$ and $x_j = x(t_i + j\epsilon)$.

### Definition 9.8.3 — Generating Functional (QFT)

$$
Z[J] = \int\mathcal{D}\phi\,\exp\left(i\int d^4x\,[\mathcal{L}(\phi) + J(x)\phi(x)]\right).
$$

Correlation functions are obtained by functional differentiation: $\langle\hat{\phi}(x_1)\cdots\hat{\phi}(x_n)\rangle = \frac{1}{Z[0]}\frac{\delta^n Z[J]}{\delta J(x_1)\cdots\delta J(x_n)}\bigg|_{J=0}$.

### Definition 9.8.4 — S-Matrix

The **scattering matrix** connects in-states to out-states:

$$
S_{fi} = \langle f\vert\hat{S}\vert i\rangle, \quad \hat{S} = T\exp\left(-i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{H}_{\text{int}}(t)\,dt\right).
$$

### Definition 9.8.5 — QED Lagrangian

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{QED}} = \bar{\psi}(i\gamma^\mu D_\mu - m)\psi - \frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu},
$$

where $D_\mu = \partial_\mu + ieA_\mu$ (covariant derivative) and $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$.

### Definition 9.8.6 — Fine Structure Constant

$$
\alpha = \frac{e^2}{4\pi\hbar c} \approx \frac{1}{137}.
$$

This is the expansion parameter for perturbative QED.

## 📐 2. Axioms / Postulates

### Postulate 9.8.P1 — Sum Over Histories

The probability amplitude for a quantum process is the sum (integral) over all possible intermediate configurations, each weighted by $e^{iS/\hbar}$.

### Postulate 9.8.P2 — Classical Limit

In the limit $\hbar \to 0$, the path integral is dominated by the stationary-phase path — the classical trajectory satisfying $\delta S = 0$ (Euler-Lagrange equations).

### Postulate 9.8.P3 — Gauge Invariance

QED is invariant under local $U(1)$ gauge transformations: $\psi \to e^{i\alpha(x)}\psi$, $A_\mu \to A_\mu - \frac{1}{e}\partial_\mu\alpha$. This symmetry dictates the form of the interaction.

## 🛡️ 3. Lemmas

### Lemma 9.8.1 — Gaussian Path Integral

For the quadratic action $S = \int dt\,\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2$:

$$
\int\mathcal{D}[x]\,e^{iS/\hbar} = \left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar T}\right)^{1/2}e^{im(x_f-x_i)^2/(2\hbar T)},
$$

where $T = t_f - t_i$. This is the free-particle propagator.

### Lemma 9.8.2 — Wick's Theorem

For a free field, the time-ordered product of $2n$ fields equals the sum over all possible pairings (contractions):

$$
\langle 0\vert T\hat{\phi}(x_1)\cdots\hat{\phi}(x_{2n})\vert 0\rangle = \sum_{\text{pairings}}\prod_{\text{pairs}}D_F(x_i - x_j).
$$

### Lemma 9.8.3 — QED Vertex Factor

Each electron-photon vertex in a Feynman diagram contributes a factor $-ie\gamma^\mu$ (in Feynman gauge).

## 👑 4. Theorems

### Theorem 9.8.1 — Equivalence of Path Integral and Operator Formalism

The path integral reproduces the matrix elements of the time-evolution operator:

$$
\langle x_f\vert e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\vert x_i\rangle = \int_{x(0)=x_i}^{x(T)=x_f}\mathcal{D}[x]\,e^{iS[x]/\hbar}.
$$

### Theorem 9.8.2 — Feynman Rules for QED

For any QED process, the amplitude $i\mathcal{M}$ is constructed by:
1. Draw all topologically distinct connected diagrams.
2. For each internal photon line (momentum $q$): $\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{q^2 + i\epsilon}$.
3. For each internal fermion line (momentum $p$): $\frac{i(\gamma^\mu p_\mu + m)}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$.
4. For each vertex: $-ie\gamma^\mu$.
5. For external lines: $u(p)$ (incoming $e^-$), $\bar{u}(p)$ (outgoing $e^-$), $\epsilon^\mu(k)$ (photon).
6. Impose 4-momentum conservation at each vertex.
7. Integrate over undetermined loop momenta: $\int\frac{d^4\ell}{(2\pi)^4}$.
8. Include a factor $(-1)$ for each closed fermion loop.

### Theorem 9.8.3 — Optical Theorem

$$
2\,\text{Im}\,\mathcal{M}(i \to i) = \sum_f \int d\Pi_f\,|\mathcal{M}(i \to f)|^2,
$$

relating the imaginary part of the forward scattering amplitude to the total cross section.

## ✍️ 5. Proofs / Derivations

### 5.1 Derivation of the Path Integral from the Time-Evolution Operator

**Step 1:** Divide the time interval $[0, T]$ into $N$ slices of width $\epsilon = T/N$.

$$
\langle x_f\vert e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\vert x_i\rangle = \langle x_f\vert(e^{-i\hat{H}\epsilon/\hbar})^N\vert x_i\rangle.
$$

**Step 2:** Insert $N-1$ complete sets of position states $\hat{I} = \int dx_j\vert x_j\rangle\langle x_j\vert$:

$$
= \int\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}dx_j\,\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\langle x_{j+1}\vert e^{-i\hat{H}\epsilon/\hbar}\vert x_j\rangle.
$$

**Step 3:** For small $\epsilon$, with $\hat{H} = \frac{\hat{p}^2}{2m} + V(\hat{x})$:

$$
\langle x_{j+1}\vert e^{-i\hat{H}\epsilon/\hbar}\vert x_j\rangle \approx \int\frac{dp_j}{2\pi\hbar}\,\exp\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(p_j(x_{j+1}-x_j) - \epsilon\frac{p_j^2}{2m} - \epsilon V(x_j)\right)\right].
$$

**Step 4:** Perform the Gaussian integral over $p_j$:

$$
\int\frac{dp}{2\pi\hbar}\exp\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\left(p\Delta x - \frac{\epsilon p^2}{2m}\right)\right] = \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\epsilon}}\exp\left[\frac{im(\Delta x)^2}{2\hbar\epsilon}\right].
$$

**Step 5:** Combining all slices:

$$
\langle x_f\vert e^{-i\hat{H}T/\hbar}\vert x_i\rangle = \lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\epsilon}\right)^{N/2}\int\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}dx_j\,\exp\left[\frac{i}{\hbar}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\epsilon\left(\frac{m}{2}\left(\frac{x_{j+1}-x_j}{\epsilon}\right)^2 - V(x_j)\right)\right].
$$

In the continuum limit, the exponent becomes $\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_0^T L(x,\dot{x})\,dt = \frac{i}{\hbar}S[x]$. $\blacksquare$

### 5.2 Free-Particle Propagator via Path Integral

**Step 1:** For $V = 0$, the action is $S = \int_0^T\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2\,dt$.

**Step 2:** Write $x(t) = x_{\text{cl}}(t) + y(t)$ where $x_{\text{cl}}$ is the classical path (straight line) and $y(0) = y(T) = 0$.

The classical path: $x_{\text{cl}}(t) = x_i + (x_f - x_i)t/T$, with action:

$$
S_{\text{cl}} = \frac{m(x_f - x_i)^2}{2T}.
$$

**Step 3:** The action splits: $S[x] = S_{\text{cl}} + S[y]$ (no cross term since $\ddot{x}_{\text{cl}} = 0$).

**Step 4:** The path integral over fluctuations $y(t)$ gives a constant (independent of endpoints):

$$
K(x_f, T; x_i, 0) = C(T)\,e^{iS_{\text{cl}}/\hbar} = \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar T}}\,\exp\left[\frac{im(x_f-x_i)^2}{2\hbar T}\right].
$$

**Step 5:** Verify: this satisfies $i\hbar\frac{\partial K}{\partial T} = -\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2 K}{\partial x_f^2}$ and $K \to \delta(x_f - x_i)$ as $T \to 0$. ✓ $\blacksquare$

### 5.3 Tree-Level Amplitude for Møller Scattering

**Process:** $e^-(p_1) + e^-(p_2) \to e^-(p_3) + e^-(p_4)$.

**Step 1:** At tree level, there are two diagrams: t-channel and u-channel (exchange of virtual photon).

**Step 2:** t-channel amplitude (applying Feynman rules):

$$
i\mathcal{M}_t = [\bar{u}(p_3)(-ie\gamma^\mu)u(p_1)]\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{(p_1-p_3)^2}[\bar{u}(p_4)(-ie\gamma^\nu)u(p_2)].
$$

$$
= \frac{ie^2}{t}[\bar{u}(p_3)\gamma^\mu u(p_1)][\bar{u}(p_4)\gamma_\mu u(p_2)],
$$

where $t = (p_1 - p_3)^2 = q^2$ is the Mandelstam variable.

**Step 3:** u-channel (exchange $p_3 \leftrightarrow p_4$, with relative minus sign for identical fermions):

$$
i\mathcal{M}_u = -\frac{ie^2}{u}[\bar{u}(p_4)\gamma^\mu u(p_1)][\bar{u}(p_3)\gamma_\mu u(p_2)],
$$

where $u = (p_1 - p_4)^2$.

**Step 4:** Total amplitude:

$$
i\mathcal{M} = i\mathcal{M}_t + i\mathcal{M}_u = ie^2\left[\frac{\bar{u}_3\gamma^\mu u_1\,\bar{u}_4\gamma_\mu u_2}{t} - \frac{\bar{u}_4\gamma^\mu u_1\,\bar{u}_3\gamma_\mu u_2}{u}\right].
$$

The relative minus sign between channels is the signature of Fermi statistics. $\blacksquare$

## 🧮 6. Worked Examples

### Example 9.8.1 — Path Integral for the Harmonic Oscillator Propagator

**Problem:** Compute the propagator $K(x_f, T; x_i, 0)$ for the harmonic oscillator $V = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2x^2$.

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** The classical action for the oscillator (classical path satisfies $\ddot{x} + \omega^2 x = 0$):

$$
x_{\text{cl}}(t) = \frac{x_f\sin\omega t + x_i\sin\omega(T-t)}{\sin\omega T}.
$$

**Step 2:** The classical action evaluates to:

$$
S_{\text{cl}} = \frac{m\omega}{2\sin\omega T}\left[(x_i^2 + x_f^2)\cos\omega T - 2x_ix_f\right].
$$

**Step 3:** The fluctuation integral gives the Van Vleck determinant:

$$
\int\mathcal{D}[y]\,e^{iS_{\text{fluct}}/\hbar} = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\pi i\hbar\sin\omega T}}.
$$

**Step 4:** The full propagator:

$$
K(x_f, T; x_i, 0) = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\pi i\hbar\sin\omega T}}\exp\left[\frac{im\omega}{2\hbar\sin\omega T}\left((x_i^2+x_f^2)\cos\omega T - 2x_ix_f\right)\right].
$$

**Verification:** For $\omega \to 0$: $\sin\omega T \to \omega T$, $\cos\omega T \to 1$, and we recover the free-particle propagator. ✓

### Example 9.8.2 — Computing a QED Cross Section: $e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-$

**Problem:** Compute $|\mathcal{M}|^2$ for $e^+e^- \to \mu^+\mu^-$ at tree level, averaged over initial spins and summed over final spins.

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** Single diagram (s-channel photon exchange):

$$
i\mathcal{M} = [\bar{v}(p_2)(-ie\gamma^\mu)u(p_1)]\frac{-ig_{\mu\nu}}{s}[\bar{u}(p_3)(-ie\gamma^\nu)v(p_4)],
$$

where $s = (p_1+p_2)^2$.

**Step 2:** $|\mathcal{M}|^2 = \frac{e^4}{s^2}[\bar{v}_2\gamma^\mu u_1][\bar{u}_1\gamma^\nu v_2][\bar{u}_3\gamma_\mu v_4][\bar{v}_4\gamma_\nu u_3]$.

**Step 3:** Average over initial spins, sum over final spins using completeness:

$$
\sum_{\text{spins}}[\bar{v}_2\gamma^\mu u_1][\bar{u}_1\gamma^\nu v_2] = \text{Tr}[(\not{p}_2 - m_e)\gamma^\mu(\not{p}_1 + m_e)\gamma^\nu].
$$

**Step 4:** In the ultra-relativistic limit ($m_e, m_\mu \to 0$):

$$
\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\text{spins}}|\mathcal{M}|^2 = \frac{e^4}{s^2}\cdot\text{Tr}[\not{p}_2\gamma^\mu\not{p}_1\gamma^\nu]\cdot\text{Tr}[\not{p}_3\gamma_\mu\not{p}_4\gamma_\nu].
$$

Using trace technology: $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\alpha\gamma^\mu\gamma^\beta\gamma^\nu] = 4(g^{\alpha\mu}g^{\beta\nu} - g^{\alpha\beta}g^{\mu\nu} + g^{\alpha\nu}g^{\beta\mu})$:

$$
= \frac{8e^4}{s^2}(p_1^\mu p_2^\nu + p_1^\nu p_2^\mu - g^{\mu\nu}p_1\cdot p_2)(p_{3\mu}p_{4\nu} + p_{3\nu}p_{4\mu} - g_{\mu\nu}p_3\cdot p_4).
$$

**Step 5:** In the CM frame with scattering angle $\theta$:

$$
\frac{1}{4}\sum|\mathcal{M}|^2 = e^4(1 + \cos^2\theta) = (4\pi\alpha)^2(1+\cos^2\theta).
$$

**Step 6:** The differential cross section:

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{4s}(1 + \cos^2\theta).
$$

Integrating: $\sigma_{\text{total}} = \frac{4\pi\alpha^2}{3s}$. This is the classic QED result.

### Example 9.8.3 — Evaluating a Gaussian Functional Integral

**Problem:** Compute $Z_0 = \int\mathcal{D}\phi\,\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\int d^4x\,\phi(\Box + m^2)\phi\right]$ (Euclidean).

**Solution:**

**Step 1:** This is a functional Gaussian integral. The operator $\hat{A} = -(\Box + m^2)$ (positive in Euclidean space).

**Step 2:** By analogy with the finite-dimensional result $\int d^nx\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}x^TAx} = (2\pi)^{n/2}/\sqrt{\det A}$:

$$
Z_0 = (\det[-(\Box + m^2)])^{-1/2} = \exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}\text{Tr}\ln(-\Box - m^2)\right].
$$

**Step 3:** In momentum space: $\text{Tr}\ln(-\Box - m^2) = V_4\int\frac{d^4k_E}{(2\pi)^4}\ln(k_E^2 + m^2)$, where $V_4$ is the spacetime volume.

This is the vacuum energy — it requires regularization and renormalization (beyond tree level).

## ✍️ Additional Derivations

### 5.4 Stationary Phase Approximation and the Classical Limit

**Goal:** Show that in the limit $\hbar \to 0$, the path integral is dominated by the classical path.

**Step 1:** Write the path integral as:

$$
K = \int\mathcal{D}[x]\,e^{iS[x]/\hbar}.
$$

**Step 2:** As $\hbar \to 0$, the phase $S/\hbar$ oscillates rapidly. Paths far from the classical path have wildly oscillating phases that cancel. Only paths near the stationary point $\delta S = 0$ contribute.

**Step 3:** Expand $S[x]$ around the classical path $x_{\text{cl}}$:

$$
S[x_{\text{cl}} + y] = S[x_{\text{cl}}] + \underbrace{\delta S[x_{\text{cl}}]}_{=0} + \frac{1}{2}\delta^2 S[x_{\text{cl}}] \cdot y^2 + O(y^3).
$$

**Step 4:** The leading contribution is:

$$
K \approx e^{iS_{\text{cl}}/\hbar}\int\mathcal{D}[y]\,e^{i\delta^2S\cdot y^2/(2\hbar)} = e^{iS_{\text{cl}}/\hbar}\cdot(\text{Gaussian prefactor}).
$$

This is the **semiclassical (WKB) approximation**. The classical action determines the phase; quantum corrections come from the Gaussian fluctuation integral. $\blacksquare$

### 5.5 Derivation of Wick's Theorem (Two-Point Case)

**Goal:** Show $T\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y) = :\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y): + D_F(x-y)$.

**Step 1:** Decompose $\hat{\phi} = \hat{\phi}^+ + \hat{\phi}^-$ where $\hat{\phi}^+$ contains annihilation operators and $\hat{\phi}^-$ contains creation operators.

**Step 2:** For $x^0 > y^0$:

$$
T\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y) = \hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y) = (\hat{\phi}^+(x) + \hat{\phi}^-(x))(\hat{\phi}^+(y) + \hat{\phi}^-(y)).
$$

**Step 3:** Normal ordering moves all $\hat{\phi}^-$ to the left:

$$
:\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y): = \hat{\phi}^-(x)\hat{\phi}^-(y) + \hat{\phi}^-(x)\hat{\phi}^+(y) + \hat{\phi}^-(y)\hat{\phi}^+(x) + \hat{\phi}^+(x)\hat{\phi}^+(y).
$$

**Step 4:** The difference is:

$$
T\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y) - :\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y): = [\hat{\phi}^+(x), \hat{\phi}^-(y)] = \langle 0\vert\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)\vert 0\rangle.
$$

For $x^0 > y^0$, this equals $D^+(x-y)$. Including both time orderings gives $D_F(x-y)$. $\blacksquare$

### 5.6 One-Loop Vacuum Polarization (Conceptual)

**The electron self-energy** at one loop modifies the photon propagator:

$$
\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q) = -e^2\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\text{Tr}\left[\gamma^\mu\frac{i(\not{k}+m)}{k^2-m^2}\gamma^\nu\frac{i(\not{k}+\not{q}+m)}{(k+q)^2-m^2}\right].
$$

This integral is **divergent** — it requires regularization (dimensional regularization) and renormalization. The physical result is a running coupling constant:

$$
\alpha(q^2) = \frac{\alpha(0)}{1 - \frac{\alpha(0)}{3\pi}\ln\frac{q^2}{m_e^2}}.
$$

The fine structure constant **increases** at short distances (high $q^2$) — this is the screening of the bare charge by virtual $e^+e^-$ pairs (vacuum polarization).

### Example 9.8.4 — Counting Feynman Diagrams

**Problem:** How many distinct Feynman diagrams contribute to $e^-e^- \to e^-e^-$ at one-loop order?

**Solution:**

At tree level: 2 diagrams (t-channel and u-channel).

At one-loop: each tree diagram can have a loop inserted on:
- Each external leg (4 legs × 2 diagrams = 8 self-energy corrections)
- The internal photon line (2 vacuum polarization insertions)
- The vertex (4 vertex corrections)
- Box diagrams (2 distinct topologies)

Total one-loop diagrams: approximately 16 distinct topologies (before accounting for counterterms).

The key point: perturbation theory generates a systematic expansion in powers of $\alpha \approx 1/137$. Each loop adds a factor of $\alpha/(4\pi)$, making QED extraordinarily precise.




### 5.7 Derivation: The Photon Propagator in Feynman Gauge

**Step 1:** The free electromagnetic Lagrangian with gauge-fixing term:

$$
\mathcal{L} = -\frac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2\xi}(\partial_\mu A^\mu)^2.
$$

**Step 2:** The equation of motion becomes:

$$
\left[g^{\mu\nu}\Box - (1-\frac{1}{\xi})\partial^\mu\partial^\nu\right]A_\nu = 0.
$$

**Step 3:** The propagator is the Green's function. In momentum space:

$$
\left[-g^{\mu\nu}k^2 + (1-\frac{1}{\xi})k^\mu k^\nu\right]\tilde{D}_{\nu\rho}(k) = i\delta^\mu_\rho.
$$

**Step 4:** Ansatz: $\tilde{D}_{\nu\rho} = \frac{-i}{k^2}(Ag_{\nu\rho} + Bk_\nu k_\rho)$. Substituting and solving:

In **Feynman gauge** ($\xi = 1$): the equation simplifies to $-g^{\mu\nu}k^2\tilde{D}_{\nu\rho} = i\delta^\mu_\rho$, giving:

$$
\tilde{D}^{\mu\nu}_F(k) = \frac{-ig^{\mu\nu}}{k^2 + i\epsilon}.
$$

This is the photon propagator used in QED Feynman rules. $\blacksquare$

### 5.8 Trace Technology: Computing $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma]$

**Step 1:** Use the anticommutation relation repeatedly:

$$
\text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma] = 2g^{\mu\nu}\text{Tr}[\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma] - \text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma].
$$

**Step 2:** Using $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma] = 4g^{\rho\sigma}$:

$$
= 8g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - \text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma].
$$

**Step 3:** Apply the same trick to the second trace, anticommuting $\gamma^\mu$ past $\gamma^\rho$:

$$
\text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\mu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma] = 2g^{\mu\rho}\text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\sigma] - \text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\mu\gamma^\sigma].
$$

$$
= 8g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} - \text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\mu\gamma^\sigma].
$$

**Step 4:** One more anticommutation on the last trace:

$$
\text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\mu\gamma^\sigma] = 2g^{\mu\sigma}\text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho] - \text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^\mu].
$$

$$
= 8g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho} - \text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^\mu].
$$

**Step 5:** By the cyclic property of the trace: $\text{Tr}[\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma\gamma^\mu] = \text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma]$.

**Step 6:** Collecting everything, let $T = \text{Tr}[\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\gamma^\sigma]$:

$$
T = 8g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - (8g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} - (8g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho} - T)).
$$

$$
T = 8g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - 8g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} + 8g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho} - T.
$$

$$
2T = 8(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} + g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}).
$$

$$
T = 4(g^{\mu\nu}g^{\rho\sigma} - g^{\mu\rho}g^{\nu\sigma} + g^{\mu\sigma}g^{\nu\rho}). \quad \blacksquare
$$

This identity is used in every QED cross-section calculation.

### Example 8.1 — Free-Particle Propagator via Path Integral (Gaussian Integration)

**Problem:** Compute the quantum mechanical propagator $K(x_b, t_b; x_a, t_a)$ for a free particle using the Feynman path integral.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

#### Step 1: The Path Integral Expression

$$
K(x_b, t_b; x_a, t_a) = \int\mathcal{D}[x(t)]\;\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}\int_{t_a}^{t_b}\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2\,dt\right).
$$

#### Step 2: Discretize Time

Divide $[t_a, t_b]$ into $N$ intervals of width $\epsilon = (t_b - t_a)/N$. The path integral becomes:

$$
K = \lim_{N\to\infty}\left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\epsilon}\right)^{N/2}\int dx_1\cdots dx_{N-1}\;\exp\left(\frac{im}{2\hbar\epsilon}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(x_{j+1} - x_j)^2\right).
$$

#### Step 3: Evaluate the Gaussian Integrals Sequentially

Start with the $x_1$ integral. The exponent contains terms involving $x_1$:

$$
\frac{im}{2\hbar\epsilon}\left[(x_1 - x_0)^2 + (x_2 - x_1)^2\right] = \frac{im}{2\hbar\epsilon}\left[2x_1^2 - 2x_1(x_0 + x_2) + x_0^2 + x_2^2\right].
$$

This is a Gaussian in $x_1$ with $\alpha = \frac{im}{\hbar\epsilon}$ and $\beta = \frac{im}{\hbar\epsilon}(x_0 + x_2)$:

$$
\int dx_1\;\exp\left(-\alpha x_1^2 + \beta x_1\right) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}\exp\left(\frac{\beta^2}{4\alpha}\right) = \sqrt{\frac{\pi\hbar\epsilon}{im}}\exp\left(\frac{im}{4\hbar\epsilon}(x_0 + x_2)^2\right).
$$

After completing the square: the result of integrating over $x_1$ gives a Gaussian in $(x_2 - x_0)^2$ with coefficient $\frac{im}{2\hbar\cdot 2\epsilon}$.

#### Step 4: Pattern After $k$ Integrations

After integrating over $x_1, x_2, \ldots, x_k$, the result is:

$$
\left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\epsilon}\right)^{k/2}\cdot\left(\frac{2\pi i\hbar\epsilon}{m}\right)^{k/2}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\exp\left(\frac{im(x_{k+1} - x_0)^2}{2\hbar(k+1)\epsilon}\right).
$$

Simplifying: $\frac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\exp\left(\frac{im(x_{k+1} - x_0)^2}{2\hbar(k+1)\epsilon}\right)$.

#### Step 5: Final Result (After All $N-1$ Integrations)

Setting $k = N-1$, $x_{N} = x_b$, $x_0 = x_a$, and $N\epsilon = T = t_b - t_a$:

$$
K(x_b, t_b; x_a, t_a) = \left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\epsilon}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\exp\left(\frac{im(x_b - x_a)^2}{2\hbar N\epsilon}\right).
$$

Since $\left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar\epsilon}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}} = \left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar N\epsilon}\right)^{1/2} = \left(\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar T}\right)^{1/2}$:

$$
\boxed{K(x_b, t_b; x_a, t_a) = \sqrt{\frac{m}{2\pi i\hbar T}}\;\exp\left(\frac{im(x_b - x_a)^2}{2\hbar T}\right).}
$$

#### Step 6: Verification

This is the classical action $S_{\text{cl}} = \frac{m(x_b - x_a)^2}{2T}$ in the exponent — the path integral is dominated by the classical path (stationary phase). The prefactor $\sqrt{m/(2\pi i\hbar T)}$ gives the correct normalization: $\int K(x_b, T; x_a, 0)\,dx_b = 1$ (probability conservation). $\blacksquare$

</details>

### Example 8.2 — Harmonic Oscillator Propagator via Path Integral

**Problem:** Compute the propagator for the harmonic oscillator $L = \frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}^2 - \frac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$ using the path integral.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

#### Step 1: Decompose Paths

Write $x(t) = x_{\text{cl}}(t) + y(t)$ where $x_{\text{cl}}$ satisfies the classical EOM with boundary conditions $x_{\text{cl}}(t_a) = x_a$, $x_{\text{cl}}(t_b) = x_b$, and $y(t_a) = y(t_b) = 0$.

The action splits:

$$
S[x] = S[x_{\text{cl}}] + S_2[y],
$$

where $S_2[y] = \frac{1}{2}m\int_{t_a}^{t_b}(\dot{y}^2 - \omega^2 y^2)\,dt$ (the cross terms vanish by the classical EOM).

#### Step 2: The Classical Action

The classical path for the harmonic oscillator with $x(0) = x_a$, $x(T) = x_b$:

$$
x_{\text{cl}}(t) = \frac{x_a\sin\omega(T-t) + x_b\sin\omega t}{\sin\omega T}.
$$

The classical action:

$$
S_{\text{cl}} = \frac{m\omega}{2\sin\omega T}\left[(x_a^2 + x_b^2)\cos\omega T - 2x_a x_b\right].
$$

#### Step 3: The Fluctuation Determinant

The path integral over fluctuations $y(t)$ (with $y(0) = y(T) = 0$) gives:

$$
\int\mathcal{D}[y]\;\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}S_2[y]\right) = F(T),
$$

where $F(T)$ depends only on $T$ (not on $x_a, x_b$). This is computed via the Van Vleck determinant:

$$
F(T) = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\pi i\hbar\sin\omega T}}.
$$

(This can be derived by computing the determinant of the discretized operator $-\partial_t^2 - \omega^2$ with Dirichlet boundary conditions, giving $\det = \frac{\sin\omega T}{\omega T}\cdot(\text{free particle det})$.)

#### Step 4: The Full Propagator

$$
\boxed{K(x_b, T; x_a, 0) = \sqrt{\frac{m\omega}{2\pi i\hbar\sin\omega T}}\;\exp\left(\frac{im\omega}{2\hbar\sin\omega T}\left[(x_a^2 + x_b^2)\cos\omega T - 2x_a x_b\right]\right).}
$$

#### Step 5: Consistency Checks

- **$\omega \to 0$ limit:** $\sin\omega T \to \omega T$, $\cos\omega T \to 1$, and we recover the free-particle propagator. ✓
- **$T \to 0$ limit:** $K \to \delta(x_b - x_a)$. ✓
- **Periodicity:** $K$ diverges at $T = n\pi/\omega$ (caustics/focal points of the classical motion). At $T = \pi/\omega$ (half-period), the particle refocuses: $K \propto \delta(x_b + x_a)$.

**References:** Feynman & Hibbs, *Quantum Mechanics and Path Integrals* Ch. 3; Schulman, *Techniques and Applications of Path Integration*; Tong QFT §1. $\blacksquare$

</details>



### Example 8.3 — Anomalous Magnetic Moment: The $\alpha/(2\pi)$ Correction (Schwinger)

**Problem:** Sketch the one-loop QED vertex correction that gives the anomalous magnetic moment of the electron $a_e = (g-2)/2 = \alpha/(2\pi)$.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

#### Step 1: The QED Vertex

The tree-level electron-photon vertex is $-ie\gamma^\mu$. The full vertex $\Gamma^\mu(p', p)$ receives quantum corrections. By Lorentz covariance and the Ward identity, the most general form is:

$$
\Gamma^\mu(p', p) = F_1(q^2)\gamma^\mu + \frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu}{2m}F_2(q^2),
$$

where $q = p' - p$, $\sigma^{\mu\nu} = \frac{i}{2}[\gamma^\mu, \gamma^\nu]$, and $F_1, F_2$ are form factors.

At tree level: $F_1(0) = 1$, $F_2(0) = 0$.

The magnetic moment is $g = 2F_1(0) + 2F_2(0) = 2 + 2F_2(0)$, so $a_e = F_2(0)$.

#### Step 2: The One-Loop Vertex Diagram

The one-loop correction involves a virtual photon connecting the two fermion legs:

$$
-ie\Gamma^\mu_{(1)} = (-ie)^3\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\;\gamma^\nu\frac{i(\not{k} + \not{p}' + m)}{(k+p')^2 - m^2}\gamma^\mu\frac{i(\not{k} + \not{p} + m)}{(k+p)^2 - m^2}\gamma_\nu\frac{-i}{k^2}.
$$

(One photon propagator $1/k^2$, two fermion propagators, three vertices.)

#### Step 3: Extract $F_2(0)$ Using the Gordon Identity

The Gordon identity: $\bar{u}(p')\gamma^\mu u(p) = \bar{u}(p')\left[\frac{(p'+p)^\mu}{2m} + \frac{i\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu}{2m}\right]u(p)$.

To extract $F_2(0)$, we need the coefficient of $\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_\nu/(2m)$ in the limit $q \to 0$.

#### Step 4: Feynman Parametrization and Integration

Introduce Feynman parameters to combine denominators:

$$
\frac{1}{ABC} = 2\int_0^1 dx\,dy\,dz\;\delta(x+y+z-1)\frac{1}{[xA + yB + zC]^3}.
$$

After shifting the loop momentum $k \to l = k + xp + yp'$ and performing the $d^4l$ integral (using dimensional regularization or a Pauli-Villars cutoff):

The UV-divergent part contributes to $F_1$ (charge renormalization). The finite part gives $F_2$.

#### Step 5: The Schwinger Result

After the full calculation (Schwinger, 1948):

$$
F_2(0) = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\int_0^1 dx\,dy\,dz\;\delta(x+y+z-1)\frac{2m^2 z(1-z)}{m^2(1-z)^2} = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\int_0^1\frac{2z(1-z)}{(1-z)^2}\cdot\ldots
$$

The simplified final integral (after careful algebra — see Peskin & Schroeder §6.3):

$$
F_2(0) = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\int_0^1 dz\;\frac{2(1-z)z}{(1-z)^2 + 0} = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\int_0^1 dz\;\frac{2z}{1-z}\cdot\ldots
$$

The correct evaluation gives:

$$
\boxed{a_e = F_2(0) = \frac{\alpha}{2\pi} \approx 0.00116.}
$$

#### Step 6: Physical Significance

- This was the first successful prediction of quantum electrodynamics (Schwinger, 1948).
- The current theoretical value (computed to 5 loops, $\sim 12000$ Feynman diagrams): $a_e^{\text{theory}} = 0.001\,159\,652\,181\,643(764)$.
- Experimental value: $a_e^{\text{exp}} = 0.001\,159\,652\,180\,73(28)$.
- Agreement to **12 significant figures** — the most precise prediction in all of physics. $\blacksquare$

</details>

### Example 8.4 — Compton Scattering: Feynman Diagrams and Amplitude

**Problem:** Write down the Feynman diagrams and the invariant amplitude $\mathcal{M}$ for Compton scattering $e^-(p) + \gamma(k) \to e^-(p') + \gamma(k')$ at tree level.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

#### Step 1: Identify the Diagrams

At tree level, there are two diagrams:

**Diagram (a) — $s$-channel:** The incoming photon is absorbed first, then the outgoing photon is emitted.

$$
e^-(p) + \gamma(k) \to e^-_{\text{virtual}}(p+k) \to e^-(p') + \gamma(k').
$$

**Diagram (b) — $u$-channel:** The outgoing photon is emitted first, then the incoming photon is absorbed.

$$
e^-(p) \to e^-_{\text{virtual}}(p-k') + \gamma(k') \to e^-(p') + \gamma(k).
$$

(There is no $t$-channel diagram for Compton scattering at tree level.)

#### Step 2: Apply the QED Feynman Rules

**Diagram (a):**

$$
i\mathcal{M}_a = \bar{u}(p')(-ie\gamma^\nu)\epsilon_\nu'^*(k')\;\frac{i(\not{p} + \not{k} + m)}{(p+k)^2 - m^2}\;(-ie\gamma^\mu)\epsilon_\mu(k)\,u(p).
$$

$$
= -e^2\,\bar{u}(p')\gamma^\nu\epsilon_\nu'^*\;\frac{\not{p} + \not{k} + m}{s - m^2}\;\gamma^\mu\epsilon_\mu\,u(p),
$$

where $s = (p+k)^2$.

**Diagram (b):**

$$
i\mathcal{M}_b = \bar{u}(p')(-ie\gamma^\mu)\epsilon_\mu(k)\;\frac{i(\not{p} - \not{k}' + m)}{(p-k')^2 - m^2}\;(-ie\gamma^\nu)\epsilon_\nu'^*(k')\,u(p).
$$

$$
= -e^2\,\bar{u}(p')\gamma^\mu\epsilon_\mu\;\frac{\not{p} - \not{k}' + m}{u - m^2}\;\gamma^\nu\epsilon_\nu'^*\,u(p),
$$

where $u = (p-k')^2$.

#### Step 3: Total Amplitude

$$
\mathcal{M} = \mathcal{M}_a + \mathcal{M}_b = -e^2\,\bar{u}(p')\left[\frac{\not{\epsilon}'^*(\not{p} + \not{k} + m)\not{\epsilon}}{s - m^2} + \frac{\not{\epsilon}(\not{p} - \not{k}' + m)\not{\epsilon}'^*}{u - m^2}\right]u(p).
$$

#### Step 4: Simplify Using On-Shell Conditions

Using $(\not{p} - m)u(p) = 0$ and $\bar{u}(p')(\not{p}' - m) = 0$, plus $k^2 = k'^2 = 0$ and $p^2 = p'^2 = m^2$:

$$
s - m^2 = (p+k)^2 - m^2 = 2p\cdot k, \qquad u - m^2 = (p-k')^2 - m^2 = -2p\cdot k'.
$$

#### Step 5: The Klein-Nishina Cross Section

Squaring, averaging over initial spins/polarizations, and summing over final:

$$
\overline{|\mathcal{M}|^2} = 2e^4\left[\frac{p\cdot k'}{p\cdot k} + \frac{p\cdot k}{p\cdot k'} + 2m^2\left(\frac{1}{p\cdot k} - \frac{1}{p\cdot k'}\right) + m^4\left(\frac{1}{p\cdot k} - \frac{1}{p\cdot k'}\right)^2\right].
$$

In the lab frame ($p = (m, \mathbf{0})$), this gives the **Klein-Nishina formula** (1929):

$$
\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega} = \frac{\alpha^2}{2m^2}\left(\frac{\omega'}{\omega}\right)^2\left[\frac{\omega'}{\omega} + \frac{\omega}{\omega'} - \sin^2\theta\right],
$$

where $\omega'/\omega = [1 + (\omega/m)(1-\cos\theta)]^{-1}$ is the Compton wavelength shift.

**Low-energy limit** ($\omega \ll m$): reduces to the Thomson cross section $\sigma_T = 8\pi\alpha^2/(3m^2)$. $\blacksquare$

</details>

### Appendix 9.1 — Feynman Rules for $\phi^4$ Theory

The simplest interacting quantum field theory is the real scalar field with quartic self-interaction:

$$
\mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{2}(\partial_\mu\phi)^2 - \frac{1}{2}m^2\phi^2 - \frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^4.
$$

**Derivation of Feynman rules from the path integral:**

The generating functional is:

$$
Z[J] = \int\mathcal{D}\phi\;\exp\left(i\int d^4x\left[\mathcal{L} + J\phi\right]\right).
$$

Separate $\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_0 + \mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}$ and expand $e^{i\int\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}}$ in powers of $\lambda$:

$$
Z[J] = \exp\left(-\frac{i\lambda}{4!}\int d^4z\;\frac{\delta^4}{\delta J(z)^4}\right)Z_0[J],
$$

where $Z_0[J] = \exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\int d^4x\,d^4y\;J(x)D_F(x-y)J(y)\right)$ is the free generating functional.

**The Feynman rules in momentum space:**

1. **Propagator:** For each internal line carrying momentum $p$: $\frac{i}{p^2 - m^2 + i\epsilon}$.

2. **Vertex:** For each 4-point vertex: $-i\lambda$. (The $4!$ in the Lagrangian cancels the $4!$ ways of connecting 4 lines to the vertex.)

3. **External lines:** Factor of 1 for each external leg (amputated diagrams).

4. **Loop integration:** $\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}$ for each undetermined loop momentum.

5. **Symmetry factor:** Divide by the symmetry factor $S$ of the diagram (number of automorphisms).

6. **Momentum conservation:** $(2\pi)^4\delta^4(\sum p_i)$ at each vertex.

**Example — 2-to-2 scattering at tree level:**

The amplitude for $\phi(p_1)\phi(p_2) \to \phi(p_3)\phi(p_4)$ at lowest order has three diagrams:

- $s$-channel: propagator $\frac{i}{(p_1+p_2)^2 - m^2}$ connecting two vertices, each $-i\lambda$... 

Wait — at tree level in $\phi^4$, the 2-to-2 scattering is just a single vertex (no propagator needed):

$$
i\mathcal{M} = -i\lambda.
$$

The cross section is simply $d\sigma/d\Omega = \lambda^2/(64\pi^2 s)$ in the center-of-mass frame.

At one loop, there are three diagrams (s, t, u channels) each with one loop integral, giving corrections of order $\lambda^2$.

**References:** Peskin & Schroeder §4.4–4.6; Tong QFT §3; Srednicki Ch. 9; Zee, *QFT in a Nutshell* Ch. I.7.

### Appendix 9.2 — Vacuum Polarization: The Running of the Coupling Constant

**The physics:** A virtual electron-positron pair screens the bare electric charge. At short distances (high energies), we probe inside the screening cloud and see a larger effective charge. This is **vacuum polarization**.

**The one-loop diagram:** A photon propagator receives a correction from an electron loop:

$$
\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q) = -e^2\int\frac{d^4k}{(2\pi)^4}\;\text{Tr}\left[\gamma^\mu\frac{i(\not{k}+m)}{k^2-m^2}\gamma^\nu\frac{i(\not{k}+\not{q}+m)}{(k+q)^2-m^2}\right].
$$

By gauge invariance (Ward identity): $\Pi^{\mu\nu}(q) = (q^2 g^{\mu\nu} - q^\mu q^\nu)\Pi(q^2)$.

**The result (after dimensional regularization and renormalization):**

$$
\Pi(q^2) = -\frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\left[\ln\frac{\Lambda^2}{m^2} - \int_0^1 dx\;6x(1-x)\ln\left(1 - \frac{q^2 x(1-x)}{m^2}\right)\right].
$$

After renormalization (subtracting $\Pi(0)$):

$$
\hat{\Pi}(q^2) = \Pi(q^2) - \Pi(0) = \frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\int_0^1 dx\;6x(1-x)\ln\left(1 - \frac{q^2 x(1-x)}{m^2}\right).
$$

**The running coupling constant:**

The effective coupling at momentum transfer $q$ is:

$$
\alpha_{\text{eff}}(q^2) = \frac{\alpha}{1 - \hat{\Pi}(q^2)}.
$$

For $|q^2| \gg m^2$ (high energy):

$$
\alpha_{\text{eff}}(q^2) \approx \frac{\alpha}{1 - \frac{\alpha}{3\pi}\ln\frac{|q^2|}{m^2}}.
$$

This is the **running of the fine-structure constant**:
- At $q^2 = 0$: $\alpha \approx 1/137$ (the familiar value).
- At $q^2 = m_Z^2$ (the Z-boson mass): $\alpha(m_Z) \approx 1/128$ (measured at LEP).

**The beta function:**

$$
\beta(\alpha) = \mu\frac{d\alpha}{d\mu} = \frac{2\alpha^2}{3\pi} + \mathcal{O}(\alpha^3).
$$

The positive beta function means QED is **not** asymptotically free — the coupling grows at short distances. (In contrast, QCD has a negative beta function and is asymptotically free.)

**The Landau pole:** Extrapolating naively, $\alpha_{\text{eff}} \to \infty$ at $|q^2| \sim m^2\exp(3\pi/\alpha) \sim 10^{286}$ GeV — far beyond any accessible energy. This signals the breakdown of perturbative QED, but new physics (the electroweak unification) intervenes long before.

**References:** Peskin & Schroeder §7.5; Tong QFT §6.5; Schwartz, *QFT and the Standard Model* Ch. 16; Weinberg, *QFT* Vol. I Ch. 11.
