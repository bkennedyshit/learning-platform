# 12.6 — Principal Stresses & Mohr's Circle

> *"The graphical representation of the state of stress at a point, which I have proposed, makes it possible to determine the stresses on any plane through the point by a simple geometric construction."*
> — **Christian Otto Mohr** (1835–1918)

Mohr's Circle is a powerful graphical method for visualizing stress transformation. It reveals the principal stresses (eigenvalues of the stress tensor), maximum shear stress, and the orientations of all critical planes — all from a single circle construction. This chapter connects the algebraic eigenvalue problem ([[2.6 - Eigenvalues Eigenvectors & Diagonalization]]) to its geometric interpretation.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Construct **Mohr's Circle** from a given 2D stress state.
2. Read principal stresses $\sigma_1$, $\sigma_2$ and maximum shear $\tau_{\max}$ from the circle.
3. Determine the **principal directions** (angles $\theta_p$).
4. Derive the stress transformation equations as a **circle equation**.
5. Extend to **3D Mohr's Circles** (three circles for three principal stress pairs).
6. Connect principal stresses to the **eigenvalue problem** of the stress tensor.

## 📚 1. Definitions

### Definition 12.6.1 — Principal Stresses (2D)

The **principal stresses** are the maximum and minimum normal stresses at a point:

$$
\sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy}}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}
$$

On principal planes, shear stress is **zero**.

### Definition 12.6.2 — Principal Directions

The orientation of the principal planes:

$$
\tan 2\theta_p = \frac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}
$$

This gives two angles $\theta_p$ separated by $90°$.

### Definition 12.6.3 — Maximum In-Plane Shear Stress

$$
\tau_{\max} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_2}{2} = R
$$

It acts on planes at $45°$ to the principal directions.

### Definition 12.6.4 — Mohr's Circle Parameters

- **Center:** $C = \left(\frac{\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy}}{2},\; 0\right) = (\sigma_{\text{avg}},\; 0)$
- **Radius:** $R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}$
- **Point X** (representing the $x$-face): $(\sigma_{xx},\; \tau_{xy})$
- **Point Y** (representing the $y$-face): $(\sigma_{yy},\; -\tau_{xy})$

### Definition 12.6.5 — Stress Invariants (2D)

Under any rotation, these quantities remain constant:

$$
I_1 = \sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy} = \sigma_1 + \sigma_2 \quad \text{(trace)}
$$

$$
I_2 = \sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} - \tau_{xy}^2 = \sigma_1 \sigma_2 \quad \text{(determinant)}
$$

### Definition 12.6.6 — 3D Principal Stresses

The principal stresses in 3D are the three eigenvalues of:

$$
\det(\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \sigma\mathbf{I}) = 0
$$

Convention: $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \sigma_3$.

The **absolute maximum shear stress** in 3D:

$$
\tau_{\text{abs max}} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2}
$$

## 📐 2. Axioms / Postulates

### Postulate 12.6.P1 — Stress Tensor is Symmetric

$\sigma_{ij} = \sigma_{ji}$ (proved in Chapter 12.2). This guarantees real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors (principal directions).

### Postulate 12.6.P2 — Tensor Transformation Law

Under rotation by angle $\theta$, stress components transform as a second-order tensor:

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma}' = \mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\mathbf{Q}^T
$$

The eigenvalues (principal stresses) are **invariant** under this transformation.

### Postulate 12.6.P3 — Spectral Theorem Applies

Since $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ is real symmetric, by the Spectral Theorem ([[2.6 - Eigenvalues Eigenvectors & Diagonalization]], Theorem 2.6.3):
- All eigenvalues are real.
- Eigenvectors are mutually orthogonal.
- $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ is orthogonally diagonalizable.

## 🛡️ 3. Lemmas

### Lemma 12.6.1 — Stress Transformation as a Circle Equation

<details>
<summary>🔍 Full Derivation</summary>

From the stress transformation equations (Chapter 12.2):

$$
\sigma_{x'} = \frac{\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy}}{2} + \frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}{2}\cos 2\theta + \tau_{xy}\sin 2\theta
$$

$$
\tau_{x'y'} = -\frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}{2}\sin 2\theta + \tau_{xy}\cos 2\theta
$$

Let $C = \frac{\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy}}{2}$, $a = \frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}{2}$, $b = \tau_{xy}$.

Then:

$$
\sigma_{x'} - C = a\cos 2\theta + b\sin 2\theta
$$

$$
\tau_{x'y'} = -a\sin 2\theta + b\cos 2\theta
$$

**Square both and add:**

$$
(\sigma_{x'} - C)^2 + \tau_{x'y'}^2 = a^2\cos^2 2\theta + 2ab\cos 2\theta\sin 2\theta + b^2\sin^2 2\theta
$$

$$
+ a^2\sin^2 2\theta - 2ab\sin 2\theta\cos 2\theta + b^2\cos^2 2\theta
$$

$$
= a^2(\cos^2 2\theta + \sin^2 2\theta) + b^2(\sin^2 2\theta + \cos^2 2\theta) = a^2 + b^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
(\sigma_{x'} - C)^2 + \tau_{x'y'}^2 = R^2
$$

where $R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}$.

This is the equation of a **circle** in the $(\sigma, \tau)$ plane with center $(C, 0)$ and radius $R$. This is **Mohr's Circle**. $\blacksquare$

</details>

### Lemma 12.6.2 — Principal Stresses as Eigenvalues

<details>
<summary>🔍 Proof</summary>

The 2D stress tensor eigenvalue problem:

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\mathbf{v} = \sigma\mathbf{v} \implies (\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \sigma\mathbf{I})\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}
$$

Non-trivial solution requires:

$$
\det(\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \sigma\mathbf{I}) = \det\begin{pmatrix} \sigma_{xx} - \sigma & \tau_{xy} \\ \tau_{xy} & \sigma_{yy} - \sigma \end{pmatrix} = 0
$$

Expanding:

$$
(\sigma_{xx} - \sigma)(\sigma_{yy} - \sigma) - \tau_{xy}^2 = 0
$$

$$
\sigma^2 - (\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy})\sigma + (\sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} - \tau_{xy}^2) = 0
$$

By the quadratic formula:

$$
\sigma = \frac{(\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy}) \pm \sqrt{(\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy})^2 - 4(\sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} - \tau_{xy}^2)}}{2}
$$

Simplifying the discriminant:

$$
(\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy})^2 - 4\sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} + 4\tau_{xy}^2 = (\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy})^2 + 4\tau_{xy}^2
$$

$$
\sigma_{1,2} = \frac{\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy}}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}
$$

This is identical to the Mohr's Circle formula: $\sigma_{1,2} = C \pm R$. The principal stresses are the eigenvalues of the stress tensor. $\blacksquare$

</details>

### Lemma 12.6.3 — Angle Doubling on Mohr's Circle

A physical rotation of $\theta$ corresponds to $2\theta$ on Mohr's Circle. This is because the transformation equations involve $\cos 2\theta$ and $\sin 2\theta$.

<details>
<summary>🔍 Explanation</summary>

The parametric equations of Mohr's Circle use $2\theta$ as the parameter:

$$
\sigma_{x'} = C + R\cos(2\theta_p - 2\theta)
$$

$$
\tau_{x'y'} = R\sin(2\theta_p - 2\theta)
$$

When the physical element rotates by $\theta$, the corresponding point on Mohr's Circle rotates by $2\theta$ (in the opposite sense for the standard sign convention). This means:
- $90°$ physical rotation → $180°$ on circle (diametrically opposite = $\sigma_{yy}$ face)
- $45°$ physical rotation → $90°$ on circle (maximum shear stress plane)

$\blacksquare$

</details>

## 👑 4. Theorems

### Theorem 12.6.1 — Mohr's Circle Theorem

The locus of all possible stress states $(\sigma_{x'}, \tau_{x'y'})$ obtainable by rotating the coordinate system forms a circle in the $\sigma$-$\tau$ plane:

$$
(\sigma - \sigma_{\text{avg}})^2 + \tau^2 = R^2
$$

### Theorem 12.6.2 — Principal Stress Theorem

At every point in a stressed body, there exist at least two (2D) or three (3D) mutually perpendicular planes on which the shear stress vanishes. The normal stresses on these planes are the principal stresses.

### Theorem 12.6.3 — Maximum Shear Stress Orientation

The planes of maximum shear stress are oriented at $45°$ to the principal planes. The normal stress on maximum-shear planes equals $\sigma_{\text{avg}} = (\sigma_1 + \sigma_2)/2$.

### Theorem 12.6.4 — 3D Mohr's Circles

For a 3D stress state with principal stresses $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \sigma_3$, the stress state on any plane lies within or on the boundary of three circles:
- Circle 1: center $\frac{\sigma_1+\sigma_3}{2}$, radius $\frac{\sigma_1-\sigma_3}{2}$
- Circle 2: center $\frac{\sigma_1+\sigma_2}{2}$, radius $\frac{\sigma_1-\sigma_2}{2}$
- Circle 3: center $\frac{\sigma_2+\sigma_3}{2}$, radius $\frac{\sigma_2-\sigma_3}{2}$

## ✍️ 5. Proofs / Derivations

### 5.1 Derivation: Mohr's Circle from Rotation Matrices

<details>
<summary>🔍 Full Derivation</summary>

The 2D rotation matrix:

$$
\mathbf{Q} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}
$$

The stress tensor in the rotated frame:

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma}' = \mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{\sigma}\mathbf{Q}^T
$$

**Explicit multiplication:**

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma}' = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & \sin\theta \\ -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \sigma_{xx} & \tau_{xy} \\ \tau_{xy} & \sigma_{yy} \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix}
$$

**First product** $\mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{\sigma}$:

Row 1: $(\sigma_{xx}\cos\theta + \tau_{xy}\sin\theta,\; \tau_{xy}\cos\theta + \sigma_{yy}\sin\theta)$

Row 2: $(-\sigma_{xx}\sin\theta + \tau_{xy}\cos\theta,\; -\tau_{xy}\sin\theta + \sigma_{yy}\cos\theta)$

**Second product** $(\mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{\sigma})\mathbf{Q}^T$, entry $(1,1)$:

$$
\sigma_{x'x'} = (\sigma_{xx}\cos\theta + \tau_{xy}\sin\theta)\cos\theta + (\tau_{xy}\cos\theta + \sigma_{yy}\sin\theta)\sin\theta
$$

$$
= \sigma_{xx}\cos^2\theta + 2\tau_{xy}\sin\theta\cos\theta + \sigma_{yy}\sin^2\theta
$$

$$
= \frac{\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy}}{2} + \frac{\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy}}{2}\cos 2\theta + \tau_{xy}\sin 2\theta
$$

Entry $(1,2)$:

$$
\tau_{x'y'} = (\sigma_{xx}\cos\theta + \tau_{xy}\sin\theta)(-\sin\theta) + (\tau_{xy}\cos\theta + \sigma_{yy}\sin\theta)\cos\theta
$$

$$
= -\sigma_{xx}\sin\theta\cos\theta + \tau_{xy}\cos^2\theta - \tau_{xy}\sin^2\theta + \sigma_{yy}\sin\theta\cos\theta
$$

$$
= -\frac{\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy}}{2}\sin 2\theta + \tau_{xy}\cos 2\theta
$$

These are the standard transformation equations. Squaring and adding (as in Lemma 12.6.1) yields the circle equation. $\blacksquare$

</details>

### 5.2 Derivation: Principal Directions from Eigenvectors

<details>
<summary>🔍 Full Derivation</summary>

For principal stress $\sigma_1 = C + R$, find the eigenvector:

$$
(\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \sigma_1\mathbf{I})\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}
$$

$$
\begin{pmatrix} \sigma_{xx} - \sigma_1 & \tau_{xy} \\ \tau_{xy} & \sigma_{yy} - \sigma_1 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} v_1 \\ v_2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}
$$

From the first equation:

$$
(\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_1)v_1 + \tau_{xy}v_2 = 0
$$

$$
\frac{v_2}{v_1} = -\frac{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_1}{\tau_{xy}} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_{xx}}{\tau_{xy}}
$$

The principal direction angle:

$$
\tan\theta_p = \frac{v_2}{v_1} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_{xx}}{\tau_{xy}}
$$

**Verification:** Using $\sigma_1 = \frac{\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy}}{2} + R$:

$$
\sigma_1 - \sigma_{xx} = \frac{\sigma_{yy} - \sigma_{xx}}{2} + R
$$

And $\tan 2\theta_p = \frac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy}}$ can be verified using the double-angle formula $\tan 2\theta = \frac{2\tan\theta}{1-\tan^2\theta}$. $\blacksquare$

</details>

### 5.3 Step-by-Step Mohr's Circle Construction Procedure

<details>
<summary>🔍 Complete Procedure</summary>

**Given:** $\sigma_{xx}$, $\sigma_{yy}$, $\tau_{xy}$ (with sign convention: positive shear = CW rotation on element).

**Step 1:** Plot point $X = (\sigma_{xx}, \tau_{xy})$ — represents the $x$-face.

**Step 2:** Plot point $Y = (\sigma_{yy}, -\tau_{xy})$ — represents the $y$-face.

**Step 3:** Connect $X$ and $Y$ with a straight line. The midpoint is the center $C = \left(\frac{\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy}}{2}, 0\right)$.

**Step 4:** The radius is the distance from $C$ to $X$ (or $Y$):

$$
R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy}}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}
$$

**Step 5:** Draw the circle with center $C$ and radius $R$.

**Step 6:** Read off results:
- $\sigma_1 = C + R$ (rightmost point)
- $\sigma_2 = C - R$ (leftmost point)
- $\tau_{\max} = R$ (topmost point)
- $2\theta_p$ = angle from line $CX$ to the $\sigma$-axis (measured on circle)

**Step 7:** Physical angle $\theta_p = (2\theta_p)/2$. Rotation sense on circle = rotation sense on physical element.

</details>

## 🧮 6. Worked Examples

### Example 12.6.1 — Principal Stresses from a 2D Stress State

**Given:** At a point in a loaded member:

$$
\sigma_{xx} = 80 \text{ MPa}, \quad \sigma_{yy} = -40 \text{ MPa}, \quad \tau_{xy} = 30 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Find:** Principal stresses, maximum shear stress, and principal directions.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Step 1: Center and radius.**

$$
C = \frac{\sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy}}{2} = \frac{80 + (-40)}{2} = 20 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{80-(-40)}{2}\right)^2 + 30^2} = \sqrt{60^2 + 30^2} = \sqrt{3600 + 900} = \sqrt{4500} = 67.08 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Step 2: Principal stresses.**

$$
\sigma_1 = C + R = 20 + 67.08 = 87.08 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_2 = C - R = 20 - 67.08 = -47.08 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Step 3: Maximum in-plane shear stress.**

$$
\tau_{\max} = R = 67.08 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Step 4: Principal direction.**

$$
\tan 2\theta_p = \frac{2\tau_{xy}}{\sigma_{xx} - \sigma_{yy}} = \frac{2(30)}{80-(-40)} = \frac{60}{120} = 0.5
$$

$$
2\theta_p = \arctan(0.5) = 26.57°
$$

$$
\theta_p = 13.28° \text{ (counterclockwise from } x\text{-axis)}
$$

**Verification:** $\sigma_1 + \sigma_2 = 87.08 - 47.08 = 40 = \sigma_{xx} + \sigma_{yy}$ ✓

$\sigma_1 \cdot \sigma_2 = 87.08 \times (-47.08) = -4100 = \sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} - \tau_{xy}^2 = (80)(-40) - 900 = -4100$ ✓

</details>

---

### Example 12.6.2 — Stress on an Arbitrary Plane

**Given:** Same stress state as Example 12.6.1. Find the normal and shear stress on a plane oriented at $\theta = 30°$ from the $x$-axis.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Using transformation equations:**

$$
\sigma_{x'} = \frac{80+(-40)}{2} + \frac{80-(-40)}{2}\cos 60° + 30\sin 60°
$$

$$
= 20 + 60(0.5) + 30(0.866) = 20 + 30 + 25.98 = 75.98 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\tau_{x'y'} = -\frac{80-(-40)}{2}\sin 60° + 30\cos 60°
$$

$$
= -60(0.866) + 30(0.5) = -51.96 + 15 = -36.96 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Verification via Mohr's Circle:** On the circle, rotate $2\theta = 60°$ from point $X$. The point should be at distance $R = 67.08$ from center at angle $(2\theta_p - 60°)$ from the $\sigma$-axis.

Angle from $\sigma$-axis: $2\theta_p - 2\theta = 26.57° - 60° = -33.43°$ (below axis).

$$
\sigma_{x'} = C + R\cos(33.43°) = 20 + 67.08(0.835) = 20 + 56.0 = 76.0 \text{ MPa} \approx 75.98 \quad \checkmark
$$

$$
\tau_{x'y'} = -R\sin(33.43°) = -67.08(0.551) = -36.96 \text{ MPa} \quad \checkmark
$$

</details>

### Example 12.6.3 — 3D Principal Stresses (Eigenvalue Problem)

**Given:** The stress tensor at a point:

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma} = \begin{pmatrix} 100 & 40 & 0 \\ 40 & 50 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -20 \end{pmatrix} \text{ MPa}
$$

**Find:** All three principal stresses and the absolute maximum shear stress.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

Since $\tau_{xz} = \tau_{yz} = 0$, the $z$-direction is already a principal direction with $\sigma_3 = -20$ MPa.

The remaining 2D problem in the $xy$-plane:

$$
C = \frac{100 + 50}{2} = 75 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{100-50}{2}\right)^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{25^2 + 40^2} = \sqrt{625 + 1600} = \sqrt{2225} = 47.17 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_1 = 75 + 47.17 = 122.17 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_2 = 75 - 47.17 = 27.83 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_3 = -20 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Ordering:** $\sigma_1 = 122.17 \gt  \sigma_2 = 27.83 \gt  \sigma_3 = -20$ MPa.

**Absolute maximum shear stress:**

$$
\tau_{\text{abs max}} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2} = \frac{122.17 - (-20)}{2} = \frac{142.17}{2} = 71.1 \text{ MPa}
$$

Note: This is larger than the in-plane maximum $\tau_{\max} = R = 47.17$ MPa because the out-of-plane principal stress $\sigma_3 = -20$ MPa creates a larger difference.

</details>

### Example 12.6.4 — Combined Loading: Shaft Under Bending and Torsion

**Given:** A circular shaft ($d = 50$ mm) is subjected to bending moment $M = 2$ kN·m and torque $T = 1.5$ kN·m simultaneously.

**Find:** Principal stresses and maximum shear stress at the critical point (top of shaft).

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Step 1: Section properties.**

$$
I = \frac{\pi d^4}{64} = \frac{\pi(0.05)^4}{64} = 3.068 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}^4
$$

$$
J = 2I = 6.136 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}^4
$$

**Step 2: Stresses at the top of the shaft.**

Bending stress (maximum at top, $y = c = d/2 = 25$ mm):

$$
\sigma_{xx} = \frac{Mc}{I} = \frac{2000 \times 0.025}{3.068 \times 10^{-7}} = \frac{50}{3.068 \times 10^{-7}} = 163.0 \text{ MPa}
$$

Torsional shear stress at the surface:

$$
\tau_{xy} = \frac{Tc}{J} = \frac{1500 \times 0.025}{6.136 \times 10^{-7}} = \frac{37.5}{6.136 \times 10^{-7}} = 61.1 \text{ MPa}
$$

At the top: $\sigma_{yy} = 0$ (no transverse normal stress).

**Step 3: Mohr's Circle.**

$$
C = \frac{163.0 + 0}{2} = 81.5 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
R = \sqrt{81.5^2 + 61.1^2} = \sqrt{6642 + 3733} = \sqrt{10375} = 101.9 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Step 4: Results.**

$$
\sigma_1 = 81.5 + 101.9 = 183.4 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_2 = 81.5 - 101.9 = -20.4 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\tau_{\max} = R = 101.9 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\theta_p = \frac{1}{2}\arctan\frac{2(61.1)}{163-0} = \frac{1}{2}\arctan(0.750) = \frac{36.87°}{2} = 18.4°
$$

</details>

## 📎 Appendix: Additional Theory

### A.1 Mohr's Circle Sign Convention Summary

| Convention | Positive $\tau$ | Circle Rotation |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Engineering (Hibbeler)** | CW on element → plotted upward | CCW on element = CW on circle |
| **Mechanics of Materials** | CCW on element → plotted upward | Opposite sense |

**Recommendation:** Always plot $X = (\sigma_{xx}, \tau_{xy}^{\text{on x-face}})$ and $Y = (\sigma_{yy}, -\tau_{xy}^{\text{on x-face}})$. The line $XY$ is the diameter.

### A.2 Mohr's Circle for Strain

The same construction applies to the strain tensor. Replace:
- $\sigma \to \varepsilon$ (normal strain on horizontal axis)
- $\tau \to \gamma/2$ (half the engineering shear strain on vertical axis)

$$
\varepsilon_{1,2} = \frac{\varepsilon_{xx}+\varepsilon_{yy}}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{xx}-\varepsilon_{yy}}{2}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\gamma_{xy}}{2}\right)^2}
$$

### A.3 Absolute Maximum Shear Stress (3D)

For plane stress ($\sigma_3 = 0$), the absolute maximum shear stress depends on the signs of $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$:

**Case 1:** $\sigma_1 > 0$, $\sigma_2 > 0$ (both tension):

$$
\tau_{\text{abs max}} = \frac{\sigma_1}{2} \quad \text{(on plane between } \sigma_1 \text{ and } \sigma_3 = 0\text{)}
$$

**Case 2:** $\sigma_1 > 0$, $\sigma_2 < 0$ (tension + compression):

$$
\tau_{\text{abs max}} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_2}{2} = R \quad \text{(in-plane maximum)}
$$

**Case 3:** $\sigma_1 < 0$, $\sigma_2 < 0$ (both compression):

$$
\tau_{\text{abs max}} = \frac{|\sigma_2|}{2} \quad \text{(on plane between } \sigma_3 = 0 \text{ and } \sigma_2\text{)}
$$

### A.4 Example: Strain Gauge Rosette Analysis

**Given:** A 45° strain gauge rosette measures:
- $\varepsilon_a = 400\,\mu\varepsilon$ (at $0°$)
- $\varepsilon_b = 200\,\mu\varepsilon$ (at $45°$)
- $\varepsilon_c = -100\,\mu\varepsilon$ (at $90°$)

**Find:** Principal strains and their directions.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

For a 45° rosette:

$$
\varepsilon_{xx} = \varepsilon_a = 400\,\mu\varepsilon
$$

$$
\varepsilon_{yy} = \varepsilon_c = -100\,\mu\varepsilon
$$

$$
\gamma_{xy} = 2\varepsilon_b - \varepsilon_a - \varepsilon_c = 2(200) - 400 - (-100) = 100\,\mu\varepsilon
$$

**Principal strains:**

$$

$$

## 📘 9. Appendix: Extended Derivations & Special Cases

### Appendix 9.1 — Derivation of Mohr's Circle from the Transformation Equations

**Goal:** Show that the 2D stress transformation equations describe a circle in $(\sigma, \tau)$ space.

The transformation equations for a plane rotated by angle $\theta$:

$$
\sigma_{x'} = \frac{\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy}}{2} + \frac{\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy}}{2}\cos 2\theta + \tau_{xy}\sin 2\theta
$$

$$
\tau_{x'y'} = -\frac{\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy}}{2}\sin 2\theta + \tau_{xy}\cos 2\theta
$$

Define: $C = \frac{\sigma_{xx}+\sigma_{yy}}{2}$, $a = \frac{\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy}}{2}$, $b = \tau_{xy}$.

Then:

$$
\sigma_{x'} - C = a\cos 2\theta + b\sin 2\theta \tag{1}
$$

$$
\tau_{x'y'} = -a\sin 2\theta + b\cos 2\theta \tag{2}
$$

**Square and add (1)² + (2)²:**

$$
(\sigma_{x'} - C)^2 + \tau_{x'y'}^2 = a^2\cos^2 2\theta + 2ab\sin 2\theta\cos 2\theta + b^2\sin^2 2\theta
$$

$$
\quad + a^2\sin^2 2\theta - 2ab\sin 2\theta\cos 2\theta + b^2\cos^2 2\theta
$$

$$
= a^2(\cos^2 2\theta + \sin^2 2\theta) + b^2(\sin^2 2\theta + \cos^2 2\theta)
$$

$$
= a^2 + b^2
$$

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{(\sigma_{x'} - C)^2 + \tau_{x'y'}^2 = R^2}
$$

where $R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_{xx}-\sigma_{yy}}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2}$.

This is the equation of a **circle** centered at $(C, 0)$ with radius $R$ in the $(\sigma, \tau)$ plane. $\blacksquare$

**Geometric interpretation:**
- As $\theta$ varies from $0$ to $180°$, the parameter $2\theta$ sweeps $0$ to $360°$ — one full trip around the circle.
- The angle on Mohr's circle is **twice** the physical rotation angle.
- Points diametrically opposite on the circle correspond to planes $90°$ apart physically.

*Reference: Hibbeler, Mechanics of Materials, 10th ed., §9.4; Gere, §7.4.*

### Appendix 9.2 — Principal Stresses as Eigenvalues: The Spectral Theorem Connection

**Claim:** The principal stresses are the eigenvalues of the stress tensor $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$, and the principal directions are the corresponding eigenvectors.

**Proof:** A principal plane is defined as one on which the traction vector is purely normal (no shear):

$$
\mathbf{t}^{(n)} = \sigma_n \hat{\mathbf{n}}
$$

By Cauchy's formula: $\mathbf{t}^{(n)} = \boldsymbol{\sigma}\hat{\mathbf{n}}$. Therefore:

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \sigma_n\hat{\mathbf{n}}
$$

$$
(\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \sigma_n\mathbf{I})\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \mathbf{0}
$$

This is the standard eigenvalue problem. Non-trivial solutions exist when:

$$
\det(\boldsymbol{\sigma} - \sigma_n\mathbf{I}) = 0
$$

which gives the characteristic cubic equation. Since $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ is real and symmetric (proved in Appendix 9.1 of Chapter 12.2), the **Spectral Theorem** guarantees:

1. All three eigenvalues $\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3$ are **real**.
2. Eigenvectors corresponding to distinct eigenvalues are **mutually orthogonal**.
3. The tensor can be diagonalized: $\boldsymbol{\sigma} = \mathbf{Q}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}\mathbf{Q}^T$ where $\boldsymbol{\Lambda} = \text{diag}(\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3)$ and $\mathbf{Q}$ is orthogonal.

**Physical meaning:** There always exists an orientation of coordinate axes (the principal axes) in which the stress tensor is diagonal — all shear stresses vanish. The material element in this orientation experiences only normal stresses on its faces.

**Connection to Mohr's circle:** In 2D, the eigenvalue problem reduces to a quadratic, and the two eigenvalues are exactly the points where the circle crosses the $\sigma$-axis (where $\tau = 0$).

*Reference: [[2.6 - Eigenvalues Eigenvectors & Diagonalization]]; Malvern, Mechanics of a Continuous Medium, §4.5.*

### Appendix 9.3 — Three-Dimensional Mohr's Circles

In 3D, the stress state on all possible planes through a point is bounded by **three Mohr's circles** (one for each pair of principal stresses):

- Circle 1-2: center $(\sigma_1+\sigma_2)/2$, radius $(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)/2$
- Circle 2-3: center $(\sigma_2+\sigma_3)/2$, radius $(\sigma_2-\sigma_3)/2$
- Circle 1-3: center $(\sigma_1+\sigma_3)/2$, radius $(\sigma_1-\sigma_3)/2$ (the largest)

**All possible $(\sigma_n, \tau)$ pairs lie in the region between the smallest circle and the largest circle** (the shaded "crescent" region).

The absolute maximum shear stress is the radius of the largest circle:

$$
\tau_{\text{abs max}} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2}
$$

and it acts on a plane bisecting the angle between the $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_3$ principal directions.

**For plane stress** ($\sigma_3 = 0$): the third circle has center $\sigma_1/2$ and radius $\sigma_1/2$ (if both $\sigma_1, \sigma_2 > 0$), which may be larger than the in-plane circle. This is why the absolute maximum shear stress can exceed the in-plane maximum — a common exam pitfall.

*Reference: Boresi & Schmidt, Advanced Mechanics of Materials, §2.6; StructureFree YouTube.*
