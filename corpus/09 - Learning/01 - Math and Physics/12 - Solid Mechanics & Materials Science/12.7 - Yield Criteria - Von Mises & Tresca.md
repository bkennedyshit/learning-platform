# 12.7 — Yield Criteria: Von Mises & Tresca

> *"The condition for the onset of yielding is that the elastic energy of distortion reaches a critical value."*
> — **Richard von Mises** (1913), *Mechanik der festen Körper im plastisch-deformablen Zustand*

When does a material begin to yield (permanently deform) under a complex, multi-axial stress state? Simple uniaxial tensile tests give us $\sigma_Y$, but real components experience combinations of normal and shear stresses. **Yield criteria** provide the bridge: they define a surface in principal stress space that separates elastic from plastic behavior.

## 📚 1. Definitions

### Definition 12.7.1 — Yield Strength

The **yield strength** $\sigma_Y$ is the uniaxial stress at which a material transitions from elastic to plastic behavior (typically defined by the 0.2% offset method).

### Definition 12.7.2 — Von Mises Stress (Equivalent Stress)

The **Von Mises stress** $\sigma_{\text{VM}}$ is a scalar measure of the stress state that predicts yielding:

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]}
$$

In terms of general stress components:

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{xx}^2 + \sigma_{yy}^2 + \sigma_{zz}^2 - \sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} - \sigma_{yy}\sigma_{zz} - \sigma_{zz}\sigma_{xx} + 3(\tau_{xy}^2 + \tau_{yz}^2 + \tau_{zx}^2)}
$$

**Yield condition:** $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sigma_Y$.

### Definition 12.7.3 — Tresca Stress

The **Tresca stress** is twice the maximum shear stress:

$$
\sigma_{\text{Tresca}} = \sigma_1 - \sigma_3 = 2\tau_{\max}
$$

(with $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq \sigma_3$).

**Yield condition:** $\sigma_1 - \sigma_3 = \sigma_Y$.

### Definition 12.7.4 — Factor of Safety

The **factor of safety** against yielding:

$$
n = \frac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_{\text{VM}}} \quad \text{(Von Mises)}
$$

$$
n = \frac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3} \quad \text{(Tresca)}
$$

Design requirement: $n > 1$ (typically $n = 1.5$ to $3$ depending on application).

### Definition 12.7.5 — Deviatoric Stress Invariants

The second invariant of the deviatoric stress tensor:

$$
J_2 = \frac{1}{2}s_{ij}s_{ij} = \frac{1}{6}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]
$$

The Von Mises criterion in terms of $J_2$:

$$
J_2 = \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{3} \quad \text{(at yield)}
$$

### Definition 12.7.6 — Distortion Energy

The **distortion energy density** (energy associated with shape change, not volume change):

$$
u_d = \frac{1}{12G}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right] = \frac{J_2}{G} = \frac{\sigma_{\text{VM}}^2}{6G}
$$

## 📐 2. Axioms / Postulates

### Postulate 12.7.P1 — Yielding is Independent of Hydrostatic Stress

For ductile metals, yielding depends only on the **deviatoric** (shape-changing) part of the stress tensor, not the hydrostatic (volume-changing) part. Experimental evidence: metals can withstand enormous hydrostatic pressures without yielding.

### Postulate 12.7.P2 — Isotropy of Yield Behavior

The yield criterion is the same in all directions (isotropic material). The yield surface has the symmetry of the material.

### Postulate 12.7.P3 — Convexity of the Yield Surface

The yield surface in stress space is **convex** — any line segment connecting two points inside the surface lies entirely inside. This is required by Drucker's stability postulate.

## 🛡️ 3. Lemmas

### Lemma 12.7.1 — Von Mises Criterion from Distortion Energy

<details>
<summary>🔍 Derivation</summary>

**Total strain energy density:**

$$
u = \frac{1}{2E}\left[\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2 + \sigma_3^2 - 2\nu(\sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2\sigma_3 + \sigma_3\sigma_1)\right]
$$

**Volumetric (hydrostatic) strain energy:**

$$
u_v = \frac{3(1-2\nu)}{2E}\sigma_m^2 = \frac{1-2\nu}{6E}(\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3)^2
$$

**Distortion energy** $u_d = u - u_v$:

$$
u_d = \frac{1+\nu}{6E}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]
$$

**At yield in uniaxial tension** ($\sigma_1 = \sigma_Y$, $\sigma_2 = \sigma_3 = 0$):

$$
u_{d,Y} = \frac{1+\nu}{6E}\left[\sigma_Y^2 + \sigma_Y^2 + 0\right] = \frac{(1+\nu)\sigma_Y^2}{3E}
$$

**Yield criterion:** $u_d = u_{d,Y}$:

$$
\frac{1+\nu}{6E}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right] = \frac{(1+\nu)\sigma_Y^2}{3E}
$$

Cancel $\frac{1+\nu}{E}$:

$$
\frac{1}{6}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right] = \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{3}
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right] = \sigma_Y^2
$$

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]} = \sigma_Y
$$

$\blacksquare$

</details>

### Lemma 12.7.2 — Von Mises from $J_2$ (Deviatoric Stress Invariant)

<details>
<summary>🔍 Derivation</summary>

The deviatoric stress tensor: $s_{ij} = \sigma_{ij} - \sigma_m\delta_{ij}$.

Its second invariant:

$$
J_2 = \frac{1}{2}s_{ij}s_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}\text{tr}(\mathbf{s}^2)
$$

In terms of principal stresses ($s_i = \sigma_i - \sigma_m$):

$$
J_2 = \frac{1}{2}(s_1^2 + s_2^2 + s_3^2)
$$

Since $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 0$, we can show:

$$
s_1^2 + s_2^2 + s_3^2 = \frac{1}{3}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]
$$

Therefore:

$$
J_2 = \frac{1}{6}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]
$$

And $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{3J_2}$.

The Von Mises yield criterion: $J_2 = k^2$ where $k = \sigma_Y/\sqrt{3}$ is the **yield stress in pure shear**. $\blacksquare$

</details>

### Lemma 12.7.3 — Yield in Pure Shear: Von Mises vs Tresca

**Von Mises:** For pure shear $\tau$: $\sigma_1 = \tau$, $\sigma_2 = 0$, $\sigma_3 = -\tau$.

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\tau^2 + \tau^2 + 4\tau^2}/\sqrt{2} = \sqrt{3}\tau
$$

Yield: $\sqrt{3}\tau_Y = \sigma_Y \implies \tau_Y = \frac{\sigma_Y}{\sqrt{3}} = 0.577\sigma_Y$

**Tresca:** $\sigma_1 - \sigma_3 = 2\tau = \sigma_Y \implies \tau_Y = \frac{\sigma_Y}{2} = 0.5\sigma_Y$

The Von Mises criterion predicts a **15.5% higher** shear yield strength than Tresca. Experiments on ductile metals consistently agree better with Von Mises.

## 👑 4. Theorems

### Theorem 12.7.1 — Von Mises Yield Criterion

A material yields when:

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}{2}} = \sigma_Y
$$

Equivalently: $J_2 = \sigma_Y^2/3$.

In principal stress space, this is a **cylinder** of radius $R = \sqrt{2/3}\,\sigma_Y$ with axis along the hydrostatic line $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = \sigma_3$.

### Theorem 12.7.2 — Tresca Yield Criterion

A material yields when:

$$
\max(|\sigma_1-\sigma_2|,\; |\sigma_2-\sigma_3|,\; |\sigma_3-\sigma_1|) = \sigma_Y
$$

In principal stress space, this is a **hexagonal prism** inscribed within the Von Mises cylinder.

### Theorem 12.7.3 — Tresca is Always Conservative

The Tresca yield surface is inscribed within the Von Mises surface. Therefore:
- If a stress state is safe by Tresca, it is safe by Von Mises.
- The maximum difference between the two predictions is **15.5%** (occurring in pure shear).

### Theorem 12.7.4 — Plane Stress Von Mises Ellipse

For plane stress ($\sigma_3 = 0$), the Von Mises criterion becomes:

$$
\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2 = \sigma_Y^2
$$

This is an **ellipse** in the $\sigma_1$-$\sigma_2$ plane, rotated $45°$ from the axes.

## ✍️ 5. Proofs / Derivations

### 5.1 Derivation: Von Mises Yield Surface from Deviatoric Stress

<details>
<summary>🔍 Full Derivation</summary>

**Step 1:** Decompose stress into hydrostatic and deviatoric parts:

$$
\sigma_{ij} = \sigma_m\delta_{ij} + s_{ij}, \quad \sigma_m = \frac{I_1}{3}
$$

**Step 2:** The yield criterion must be independent of $\sigma_m$ (Postulate 12.7.P1). Therefore it can only depend on the invariants of $\mathbf{s}$.

The first invariant of $\mathbf{s}$: $J_1 = s_{kk} = 0$ (always zero by construction).

The simplest non-trivial invariant: $J_2 = \frac{1}{2}s_{ij}s_{ij}$.

**Step 3:** The yield criterion takes the form $f(J_2) = 0$, or simply:

$$
J_2 = k^2
$$

for some material constant $k$.

**Step 4:** Calibrate $k$ from uniaxial tension ($\sigma_1 = \sigma_Y$, $\sigma_2 = \sigma_3 = 0$):

$$
\sigma_m = \sigma_Y/3
$$

$$
s_1 = \sigma_Y - \sigma_Y/3 = 2\sigma_Y/3
$$

$$
s_2 = 0 - \sigma_Y/3 = -\sigma_Y/3
$$

$$
s_3 = 0 - \sigma_Y/3 = -\sigma_Y/3
$$

$$
J_2 = \frac{1}{2}\left[\left(\frac{2\sigma_Y}{3}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\sigma_Y}{3}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{\sigma_Y}{3}\right)^2\right] = \frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{4\sigma_Y^2}{9} + \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{9} + \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{9}\right] = \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{3}
$$

Therefore $k^2 = \sigma_Y^2/3$, and the yield criterion is:

$$
J_2 = \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{3} \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad \sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{3J_2} = \sigma_Y
$$

$\blacksquare$

</details>

### 5.2 Derivation: Plane Stress Von Mises Ellipse

<details>
<summary>🔍 Full Derivation</summary>

Set $\sigma_3 = 0$ in the Von Mises criterion:

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}}^2 = \frac{1}{2}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + \sigma_2^2 + \sigma_1^2\right] = \sigma_Y^2
$$

Expand:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left[\sigma_1^2 - 2\sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2 + \sigma_2^2 + \sigma_1^2\right] = \sigma_Y^2
$$

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left[2\sigma_1^2 - 2\sigma_1\sigma_2 + 2\sigma_2^2\right] = \sigma_Y^2
$$

$$
\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2 = \sigma_Y^2
$$

This is a **conic section**. The discriminant $B^2 - 4AC = 1 - 4(1)(1) = -3 \lt  0$, confirming it's an ellipse.

The ellipse is rotated $45°$ from the $\sigma_1$, $\sigma_2$ axes. Its semi-axes (in rotated coordinates $\sigma_1' = (\sigma_1+\sigma_2)/\sqrt{2}$, $\sigma_2' = (\sigma_1-\sigma_2)/\sqrt{2}$):

Along $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2$: semi-axis $= \sigma_Y\sqrt{2}$

Along $\sigma_1 = -\sigma_2$: semi-axis $= \sigma_Y\sqrt{2/3}$

$\blacksquare$

</details>

## 🧮 6. Worked Examples

### Example 12.7.1 — Factor of Safety: Von Mises vs Tresca

**Given:** A steel component ($\sigma_Y = 250$ MPa) has principal stresses:

$$
\sigma_1 = 180 \text{ MPa}, \quad \sigma_2 = 60 \text{ MPa}, \quad \sigma_3 = -30 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Find:** Factor of safety using both criteria.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Von Mises:**

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\frac{(180-60)^2 + (60-(-30))^2 + (-30-180)^2}{2}}
$$

$$
= \sqrt{\frac{120^2 + 90^2 + (-210)^2}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{14400 + 8100 + 44100}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{66600}{2}} = \sqrt{33300} = 182.5 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
n_{\text{VM}} = \frac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_{\text{VM}}} = \frac{250}{182.5} = 1.37
$$

**Tresca:**

$$
\sigma_{\text{Tresca}} = \sigma_1 - \sigma_3 = 180 - (-30) = 210 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
n_{\text{Tresca}} = \frac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3} = \frac{250}{210} = 1.19
$$

**Comparison:** Tresca is more conservative ($n_{\text{Tresca}} \lt  n_{\text{VM}}$), as expected.

</details>

---

### Example 12.7.2 — Thin-Walled Pressure Vessel

**Given:** A thin-walled cylindrical pressure vessel (steel, $\sigma_Y = 300$ MPa) with:
- Hoop stress: $\sigma_1 = 150$ MPa
- Axial stress: $\sigma_2 = 75$ MPa
- Radial stress: $\sigma_3 \approx 0$ (thin wall)

**Find:** Factor of safety against yielding.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Von Mises (plane stress form):**

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2} = \sqrt{150^2 - 150(75) + 75^2}
$$

$$
= \sqrt{22500 - 11250 + 5625} = \sqrt{16875} = 129.9 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
n_{\text{VM}} = \frac{300}{129.9} = 2.31
$$

**Tresca:**

$$
n_{\text{Tresca}} = \frac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3} = \frac{300}{150 - 0} = 2.0
$$

Note: For biaxial tension (both positive), Tresca uses $\sigma_1 - 0 = 150$ since $\sigma_3 = 0$.

</details>

---

### Example 12.7.3 — Combined Bending and Torsion

**Given:** A shaft ($\sigma_Y = 350$ MPa) has at its critical point:

$$
\sigma_{xx} = 200 \text{ MPa}, \quad \sigma_{yy} = 0, \quad \tau_{xy} = 100 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Find:** Will the shaft yield? Compute factor of safety.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Step 1: Von Mises stress (direct formula for 2D).**

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\sigma_{xx}^2 - \sigma_{xx}\sigma_{yy} + \sigma_{yy}^2 + 3\tau_{xy}^2}
$$

$$
= \sqrt{200^2 - 0 + 0 + 3(100)^2} = \sqrt{40000 + 30000} = \sqrt{70000} = 264.6 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Step 2: Check yield.**

$\sigma_{\text{VM}} = 264.6 \lt  \sigma_Y = 350$ → **No yielding**.

$$
n_{\text{VM}} = \frac{350}{264.6} = 1.32
$$

**Step 3: Tresca check.**

Principal stresses:

$$
\sigma_{1,2} = \frac{200}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{200}{2}\right)^2 + 100^2} = 100 \pm \sqrt{10000 + 10000} = 100 \pm 141.4
$$

$$
\sigma_1 = 241.4 \text{ MPa}, \quad \sigma_2 = -41.4 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
n_{\text{Tresca}} = \frac{350}{241.4 - (-41.4)} = \frac{350}{282.8} = 1.24
$$

</details>

---

### Example 12.7.4 — Design Problem: Maximum Allowable Torque

**Given:** A solid shaft of diameter $d = 40$ mm, made of steel ($\sigma_Y = 280$ MPa). The shaft carries a bending moment $M = 500$ N·m. Required factor of safety $n = 2.0$ (Von Mises).

**Find:** Maximum allowable torque $T$.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Step 1: Section properties.**

$$
c = 20 \text{ mm} = 0.02 \text{ m}
$$

$$
I = \frac{\pi c^4}{4} = \frac{\pi(0.02)^4}{4} = 1.257 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}^4
$$

$$
J = 2I = 2.513 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m}^4
$$

**Step 2: Stresses at critical point (surface).**

$$
\sigma = \frac{Mc}{I} = \frac{500 \times 0.02}{1.257 \times 10^{-7}} = 79.6 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\tau = \frac{Tc}{J} = \frac{T \times 0.02}{2.513 \times 10^{-7}} = 79{,}585\,T \text{ (Pa, with } T \text{ in N·m)}
$$

**Step 3: Von Mises criterion with factor of safety.**

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \frac{\sigma_Y}{n} = \frac{280}{2} = 140 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\sigma^2 + 3\tau^2} = 140
$$

$$
79.6^2 + 3\tau^2 = 140^2
$$

$$
6336 + 3\tau^2 = 19600
$$

$$
\tau^2 = \frac{13264}{3} = 4421
$$

$$
\tau = 66.5 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Step 4: Solve for $T$.**

$$
T = \frac{\tau \cdot J}{c} = \frac{66.5 \times 10^6 \times 2.513 \times 10^{-7}}{0.02} = \frac{16.71}{0.02} = 835 \text{ N·m}
$$

**Maximum allowable torque:** $T = 835$ N·m.

</details>

### Example 12.7.5 — Comparison Table: When Do Criteria Differ Most?

| Stress State | $\sigma_{\text{VM}}/\sigma_Y$ | $(\sigma_1-\sigma_3)/\sigma_Y$ | Difference |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Uniaxial tension | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0% |
| Equal biaxial | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0% |
| Pure shear | $1/\sqrt{3} = 0.577$ | $0.5$ | 15.5% |
| Plane strain tension | $\sqrt{3}/2 = 0.866$ | $1.0$ | 15.5% |

The maximum discrepancy is always **15.5%**, occurring when $\sigma_2 = (\sigma_1+\sigma_3)/2$.

## 📎 Appendix: Additional Theory

### A.1 Other Yield/Failure Criteria

| Criterion | Formula | Best For |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Von Mises** | $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sigma_Y$ | Ductile metals (general) |
| **Tresca** | $\sigma_1 - \sigma_3 = \sigma_Y$ | Ductile metals (conservative) |
| **Rankine** (Max Normal Stress) | $\max(|\sigma_1|, |\sigma_3|) = \sigma_{\text{ult}}$ | Brittle materials |
| **Mohr-Coulomb** | $\frac{\sigma_1}{\sigma_{ut}} - \frac{\sigma_3}{\sigma_{uc}} = 1$ | Brittle with unequal tension/compression |
| **Drucker-Prager** | $\alpha I_1 + \sqrt{J_2} = k$ | Soils, concrete, pressure-dependent |

### A.2 Von Mises in Terms of Octahedral Shear Stress

The **octahedral shear stress** acts on planes equally inclined to all three principal axes:

$$
\tau_{\text{oct}} = \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\sigma_{\text{VM}}
$$

The Von Mises criterion is equivalent to: $\tau_{\text{oct}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\sigma_Y$.

### A.3 Yield Surface in the π-Plane (Deviatoric Plane)

The **π-plane** (or deviatoric plane) is perpendicular to the hydrostatic axis $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = \sigma_3$ in principal stress space.

- **Von Mises:** Projects as a **circle** of radius $R = \sqrt{2/3}\,\sigma_Y$ in the π-plane.
- **Tresca:** Projects as a **regular hexagon** inscribed in the Von Mises circle.

The hexagon touches the circle at 6 points corresponding to uniaxial tension/compression states.

### A.4 Derivation: Tresca Yield Surface Equations (Plane Stress)

For plane stress ($\sigma_3 = 0$), the Tresca criterion has three cases:

**Case 1:** $\sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2 \geq 0$: $\sigma_1 = \sigma_Y$

**Case 2:** $\sigma_1 \geq 0 \geq \sigma_2$: $\sigma_1 - \sigma_2 = \sigma_Y$

**Case 3:** $0 \geq \sigma_1 \geq \sigma_2$: $-\sigma_2 = \sigma_Y$ (i.e., $\sigma_2 = -\sigma_Y$)

These define the six sides of the hexagon in the $\sigma_1$-$\sigma_2$ plane.

### A.5 Example: Thick-Walled Pressure Vessel (Lamé Equations)

**Given:** A thick-walled cylinder with inner radius $a = 50$ mm, outer radius $b = 100$ mm, internal pressure $p_i = 80$ MPa, external pressure $p_o = 0$. Material: $\sigma_Y = 300$ MPa.

**Find:** Factor of safety at the inner surface using Von Mises.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Lamé equations** for radial and hoop stress:

$$
\sigma_r = \frac{p_i a^2 - p_o b^2}{b^2 - a^2} - \frac{(p_i - p_o)a^2 b^2}{(b^2 - a^2)r^2}
$$

$$
\sigma_\theta = \frac{p_i a^2 - p_o b^2}{b^2 - a^2} + \frac{(p_i - p_o)a^2 b^2}{(b^2 - a^2)r^2}
$$

With $p_o = 0$, $k = b/a = 2$:

$$
\sigma_r(a) = -p_i = -80 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_\theta(a) = p_i\frac{a^2 + b^2}{b^2 - a^2} = 80 \cdot \frac{50^2 + 100^2}{100^2 - 50^2} = 80 \cdot \frac{12500}{7500} = 133.3 \text{ MPa}
$$

Axial stress (closed ends): $\sigma_z = \frac{p_i a^2}{b^2 - a^2} = \frac{80 \times 2500}{7500} = 26.7$ MPa

**Principal stresses at inner surface:**

$$
\sigma_1 = 133.3, \quad \sigma_2 = 26.7, \quad \sigma_3 = -80 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Von Mises:**

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\frac{(133.3-26.7)^2 + (26.7-(-80))^2 + (-80-133.3)^2}{2}}
$$

$$
= \sqrt{\frac{106.6^2 + 106.7^2 + 213.3^2}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{11363 + 11385 + 45497}{2}} = \sqrt{34122} = 184.7 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
n = \frac{300}{184.7} = 1.62
$$

</details>

### A.6 Experimental Validation

Extensive testing on ductile metals (Taylor & Quinney, 1931; Lode, 1926) confirms:
- Von Mises predicts yield within **±5%** for most ductile metals.
- Tresca underpredicts yield stress in pure shear by ~15%.
- For aluminum alloys, Von Mises is particularly accurate.
- For mild steel with a pronounced yield point, both criteria work well in uniaxial states.

The Von Mises criterion is preferred in modern engineering practice and is the default in FEA software (ANSYS, Abaqus, NASTRAN).

### Example 8.1 — Factor of Safety Comparison: Von Mises vs. Tresca for Various Stress States

**Given:** A ductile steel component ($\sigma_Y = 250$ MPa) is analyzed at four critical points with the following stress states (all in MPa):

| Point | $\sigma_{xx}$ | $\sigma_{yy}$ | $\tau_{xy}$ |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| A | 200 | 0 | 0 |
| B | 100 | 100 | 0 |
| C | 0 | 0 | 120 |
| D | 150 | -50 | 60 |

**Find:** Factor of safety at each point using both Von Mises and Tresca criteria. Identify where the criteria differ most.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Point A: Uniaxial tension ($\sigma_{xx} = 200$, rest zero).**

Principal stresses: $\sigma_1 = 200$, $\sigma_2 = 0$, $\sigma_3 = 0$.

Von Mises: $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{200^2 - 200(0) + 0^2} = 200$ MPa

$$
n_{\text{VM}} = \frac{250}{200} = 1.25
$$

Tresca: $\tau_{\max} = \frac{200 - 0}{2} = 100$ MPa. Yield when $\tau_{\max} = \sigma_Y/2 = 125$ MPa.

$$
n_{\text{Tr}} = \frac{125}{100} = 1.25
$$

**Difference: 0%** (uniaxial tension — both criteria agree exactly).

**Point B: Equal biaxial tension ($\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = 100$, $\sigma_3 = 0$).**

Von Mises: $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{100^2 - 100(100) + 100^2} = \sqrt{10000} = 100$ MPa

$$
n_{\text{VM}} = \frac{250}{100} = 2.50
$$

Tresca: $\tau_{\max} = \frac{\max(\sigma_1, \sigma_2) - \sigma_3}{2} = \frac{100 - 0}{2} = 50$ MPa

$$
n_{\text{Tr}} = \frac{125}{50} = 2.50
$$

**Difference: 0%** (equal biaxial — both agree).

**Point C: Pure shear ($\tau_{xy} = 120$, all normal stresses zero).**

Principal stresses: $\sigma_1 = 120$, $\sigma_2 = -120$, $\sigma_3 = 0$.

Von Mises: $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{0 + 0 + 3(120)^2} = 120\sqrt{3} = 207.8$ MPa

$$
n_{\text{VM}} = \frac{250}{207.8} = 1.203
$$

Tresca: $\tau_{\max} = \frac{120 - (-120)}{2} = 120$ MPa

$$
n_{\text{Tr}} = \frac{125}{120} = 1.042
$$

**Difference: 15.5%** — Tresca is more conservative in pure shear.

**Point D: Combined state ($\sigma_{xx} = 150$, $\sigma_{yy} = -50$, $\tau_{xy} = 60$).**

Principal stresses:

$$
\sigma_{1,2} = \frac{150+(-50)}{2} \pm \sqrt{\left(\frac{150-(-50)}{2}\right)^2 + 60^2} = 50 \pm \sqrt{100^2 + 60^2} = 50 \pm 116.6
$$

$$
\sigma_1 = 166.6, \quad \sigma_2 = -66.6, \quad \sigma_3 = 0
$$

Von Mises: $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{166.6^2 - 166.6(-66.6) + (-66.6)^2}$

$$
= \sqrt{27756 + 11096 + 4436} = \sqrt{43288} = 208.1 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
n_{\text{VM}} = \frac{250}{208.1} = 1.201
$$

Tresca: $\tau_{\max} = \frac{166.6 - (-66.6)}{2} = 116.6$ MPa

$$
n_{\text{Tr}} = \frac{125}{116.6} = 1.072
$$

**Difference: 12.0%**

**Summary Table:**

| Point | $n_{\text{VM}}$ | $n_{\text{Tr}}$ | Difference |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| A (uniaxial) | 1.25 | 1.25 | 0% |
| B (biaxial) | 2.50 | 2.50 | 0% |
| C (pure shear) | 1.203 | 1.042 | 15.5% |
| D (combined) | 1.201 | 1.072 | 12.0% |

**Conclusion:** The maximum discrepancy (15.5%) occurs in pure shear. Tresca is always equal to or more conservative than Von Mises. For design, Tresca gives a built-in safety margin of up to 15.5%.

*Reference: Dowling, Mechanical Behavior of Materials, §7.4; Hibbeler, §10.7.*

### Example 8.2 — Pressure Vessel Design Using Yield Criteria

**Given:** A thin-walled cylindrical pressure vessel (radius $r = 400$ mm, wall thickness $t$) must withstand internal pressure $p = 5$ MPa with a factor of safety $n = 2.5$ against yielding. Material: stainless steel ($\sigma_Y = 290$ MPa).

**Find:** (a) Required wall thickness using Von Mises. (b) Required wall thickness using Tresca. (c) Compare.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Step 1: Stress state in the vessel wall.**

$$
\sigma_1 = \sigma_{\text{hoop}} = \frac{pr}{t}, \quad \sigma_2 = \sigma_{\text{axial}} = \frac{pr}{2t}, \quad \sigma_3 \approx 0
$$

Note: $\sigma_1 = 2\sigma_2$ always for a cylinder.

**Step 2: Von Mises criterion.**

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2}
$$

With $\sigma_2 = \sigma_1/2$:

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1(\sigma_1/2) + (\sigma_1/2)^2} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1^2/2 + \sigma_1^2/4} = \sigma_1\sqrt{3/4} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sigma_1
$$

Yield condition with safety factor:

$$
\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\sigma_1 = \frac{\sigma_Y}{n} = \frac{290}{2.5} = 116 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
\sigma_1 = \frac{2 \times 116}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{232}{1.732} = 134.0 \text{ MPa}
$$

$$
t_{\text{VM}} = \frac{pr}{\sigma_1} = \frac{5 \times 400}{134.0} = 14.9 \text{ mm}
$$

**Step 3: Tresca criterion.**

For this stress state ($\sigma_1 \gt  \sigma_2 \gt  0 = \sigma_3$):

$$
\tau_{\max} = \frac{\sigma_1 - 0}{2} = \frac{\sigma_1}{2}
$$

Yield: $\sigma_1/2 = \sigma_Y/(2n)$, so $\sigma_1 = \sigma_Y/n = 290/2.5 = 116$ MPa.

$$
t_{\text{Tr}} = \frac{pr}{\sigma_1} = \frac{5 \times 400}{116} = 17.2 \text{ mm}
$$

**Step 4: Comparison.**

$$
\frac{t_{\text{Tr}}}{t_{\text{VM}}} = \frac{17.2}{14.9} = 1.155
$$

Tresca requires **15.5% more material** — exactly the maximum discrepancy factor $2/\sqrt{3} = 1.155$.

**Design decision:** Use $t = 18$ mm (round up to standard plate thickness) if using Tresca, or $t = 16$ mm if using Von Mises with confidence in the material data.

*Reference: Hibbeler, Mechanics of Materials, §10.7; ASME Boiler & Pressure Vessel Code.*

</details>


### Example 8.3 — Octahedral Shear Stress Calculation

**Given:** Principal stresses at a point: $\sigma_1 = 180$ MPa, $\sigma_2 = 60$ MPa, $\sigma_3 = -40$ MPa. Material yield strength $\sigma_Y = 300$ MPa.

**Find:** (a) Octahedral normal and shear stresses. (b) Verify that the Von Mises criterion can be expressed in terms of $\tau_{\text{oct}}$. (c) Factor of safety.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Part (a): Octahedral stresses.**

The octahedral plane is equally inclined to all three principal axes: $\hat{\mathbf{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(1, 1, 1)$.

Octahedral normal stress:

$$
\sigma_{\text{oct}} = \frac{\sigma_1 + \sigma_2 + \sigma_3}{3} = \frac{180 + 60 + (-40)}{3} = \frac{200}{3} = 66.67 \text{ MPa}
$$

Octahedral shear stress:

$$
\tau_{\text{oct}} = \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{(180-60)^2 + (60-(-40))^2 + (-40-180)^2}
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{120^2 + 100^2 + 220^2} = \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{14400 + 10000 + 48400}
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{3}\sqrt{72800} = \frac{269.8}{3} = 89.9 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Part (b): Connection to Von Mises.**

Von Mises stress:

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\frac{(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2}{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{72800}{2}} = \sqrt{36400} = 190.8 \text{ MPa}
$$

Relationship:

$$
\tau_{\text{oct}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \frac{1.414}{3}(190.8) = 89.9 \text{ MPa} \quad \checkmark
$$

The Von Mises criterion $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sigma_Y$ is equivalent to:

$$
\tau_{\text{oct}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}\sigma_Y = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3}(300) = 141.4 \text{ MPa}
$$

**Part (c): Factor of safety.**

$$
n = \frac{\sigma_Y}{\sigma_{\text{VM}}} = \frac{300}{190.8} = 1.57
$$

Or equivalently: $n = \frac{141.4}{89.9} = 1.57$ ✓

*Reference: Chakrabarty, Theory of Plasticity, §1.5; MIT OCW 3.032.*

</details>

### Example 8.4 — Plane-Stress Yield Locus: Numerical Verification

**Given:** A material with $\sigma_Y = 400$ MPa is subjected to plane stress ($\sigma_3 = 0$). Consider the stress state $\sigma_1 = 300$ MPa, $\sigma_2 = -200$ MPa.

**Find:** (a) Is the material yielded according to Von Mises? Tresca? (b) What is the maximum $\sigma_1$ (keeping $\sigma_2 = -200$ MPa) before yield by each criterion? (c) Plot the point relative to both yield surfaces.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Part (a): Check yield.**

Von Mises (plane stress, $\sigma_3 = 0$):

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2} = \sqrt{300^2 - 300(-200) + (-200)^2}
$$

$$
= \sqrt{90000 + 60000 + 40000} = \sqrt{190000} = 435.9 \text{ MPa}
$$

Since $435.9 \gt  400$: **YIELDED** by Von Mises. ✗

Tresca: $\tau_{\max} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2}$. With $\sigma_1 = 300 \gt  0 \gt  \sigma_2 = -200$, and $\sigma_3 = 0$ is between them... Wait: order the principal stresses: $\sigma_1 = 300$, $\sigma_2 = 0$ (the out-of-plane), $\sigma_3 = -200$.

$$
\tau_{\max} = \frac{300 - (-200)}{2} = 250 \text{ MPa}
$$

Yield when $\tau_{\max} = \sigma_Y/2 = 200$ MPa. Since $250 \gt  200$: **YIELDED** by Tresca. ✗

**Part (b): Maximum $\sigma_1$ at yield onset.**

Von Mises: $\sigma_1^2 + 200\sigma_1 + 40000 = 160000$

$$
\sigma_1^2 + 200\sigma_1 - 120000 = 0
$$

$$
\sigma_1 = \frac{-200 + \sqrt{40000 + 480000}}{2} = \frac{-200 + 721.1}{2} = 260.6 \text{ MPa}
$$

Tresca: $\sigma_1 - (-200) = 400 \implies \sigma_1 = 200$ MPa

**Part (c): Geometric interpretation.**

The Von Mises yield surface in the $\sigma_1$-$\sigma_2$ plane is an ellipse:

$$
\sigma_1^2 - \sigma_1\sigma_2 + \sigma_2^2 = \sigma_Y^2
$$

The Tresca yield surface is a hexagon inscribed within this ellipse. The point $(300, -200)$ lies **outside** both surfaces, confirming yielding. The Von Mises boundary at $\sigma_2 = -200$ is at $\sigma_1 = 260.6$; the Tresca boundary is at $\sigma_1 = 200$.

The ratio of allowable loads: $260.6/200 = 1.30$ — Von Mises allows 30% more load in this particular stress state (which is close to pure shear character).

*Reference: Dowling, Mechanical Behavior of Materials, §7.5; Hibbeler, §10.7.*

</details>

## 📘 9. Appendix: Extended Derivations & Special Cases

### Appendix 9.1 — Derivation of the Von Mises Criterion from the $J_2$ Deviatoric Invariant

**Goal:** Show that the Von Mises criterion is equivalent to a critical value of the second invariant of the deviatoric stress tensor.

**Step 1: Deviatoric stress tensor.**

$$
s_{ij} = \sigma_{ij} - \frac{1}{3}\sigma_{kk}\delta_{ij}
$$

The second invariant of $\mathbf{s}$:

$$
J_2 = \frac{1}{2}s_{ij}s_{ij} = \frac{1}{2}\text{tr}(\mathbf{s}^2)
$$

**Step 2: Express $J_2$ in terms of principal stresses.**

In principal coordinates, $s_1 = \sigma_1 - \sigma_m$, $s_2 = \sigma_2 - \sigma_m$, $s_3 = \sigma_3 - \sigma_m$ where $\sigma_m = (\sigma_1+\sigma_2+\sigma_3)/3$.

$$
J_2 = \frac{1}{2}(s_1^2 + s_2^2 + s_3^2)
$$

Using $s_1 + s_2 + s_3 = 0$, we can show:

$$
s_1^2 + s_2^2 + s_3^2 = \frac{1}{3}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]
$$

**Proof of this identity:** Expand $s_i = \sigma_i - \sigma_m$:

$$
\sum s_i^2 = \sum(\sigma_i - \sigma_m)^2 = \sum\sigma_i^2 - 2\sigma_m\sum\sigma_i + 3\sigma_m^2
$$

$$
= \sum\sigma_i^2 - 2\sigma_m(3\sigma_m) + 3\sigma_m^2 = \sum\sigma_i^2 - 3\sigma_m^2
$$

Also:

$$
\frac{1}{3}\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_i - \sigma_j)^2 = \frac{1}{3}\left[2\sum\sigma_i^2 - 2\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i\sigma_j\right]
$$

And $\sum\sigma_i^2 - 3\sigma_m^2 = \sum\sigma_i^2 - \frac{(\sum\sigma_i)^2}{3} = \frac{3\sum\sigma_i^2 - (\sum\sigma_i)^2}{3} = \frac{2\sum\sigma_i^2 - 2\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i\sigma_j}{3}$

These are equal. ✓

Therefore:

$$
J_2 = \frac{1}{6}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]
$$

**Step 3: The Von Mises criterion.**

$$
\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{3J_2} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\left[(\sigma_1-\sigma_2)^2 + (\sigma_2-\sigma_3)^2 + (\sigma_3-\sigma_1)^2\right]}
$$

Yielding occurs when $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sigma_Y$, i.e.:

$$
\boxed{J_2 = \frac{\sigma_Y^2}{3} = k^2}
$$

where $k = \sigma_Y/\sqrt{3}$ is the **yield stress in pure shear** (since for pure shear $\sigma_1 = -\sigma_3 = \tau$, $\sigma_2 = 0$: $J_2 = \tau^2$, so yield at $\tau = k = \sigma_Y/\sqrt{3}$).

**Physical interpretation:** Von Mises yield depends only on the **deviatoric** (shape-changing) part of the stress — hydrostatic pressure does not cause yielding in metals. This is experimentally confirmed: metals can withstand enormous hydrostatic pressures without yielding.

$\blacksquare$

*Reference: Chakrabarty, Theory of Plasticity, §1.3; Hill, The Mathematical Theory of Plasticity, §2.2.*

### Appendix 9.2 — Derivation of the Tresca Criterion and Its Relation to Von Mises

**The Tresca (Maximum Shear Stress) Criterion:**

Yielding occurs when the maximum shear stress reaches the shear yield strength:

$$
\tau_{\max} = \frac{\sigma_1 - \sigma_3}{2} = \frac{\sigma_Y}{2} = k_T
$$

where $k_T = \sigma_Y/2$ is the Tresca shear yield strength.

**Derivation from physical observation:**

In a uniaxial tensile test ($\sigma_1 = \sigma_Y$, $\sigma_2 = \sigma_3 = 0$):

$$
\tau_{\max} = \frac{\sigma_Y - 0}{2} = \frac{\sigma_Y}{2}
$$

Tresca postulates that this critical shear stress value is the universal yield condition regardless of the stress state. Slip lines in single crystals (observed at $45°$ to the tensile axis) support this — yielding initiates on planes of maximum shear.

**Comparison with Von Mises in pure shear:**

For pure shear: $\sigma_1 = \tau$, $\sigma_2 = 0$, $\sigma_3 = -\tau$.

- Tresca: $\tau_{\max} = \tau = \sigma_Y/2 \implies \tau_Y^{\text{Tresca}} = \sigma_Y/2$
- Von Mises: $\sigma_{\text{VM}} = \sqrt{3}\tau = \sigma_Y \implies \tau_Y^{\text{VM}} = \sigma_Y/\sqrt{3}$

Ratio: $\frac{\tau_Y^{\text{VM}}}{\tau_Y^{\text{Tresca}}} = \frac{\sigma_Y/\sqrt{3}}{\sigma_Y/2} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = 1.155$

Von Mises predicts 15.5% higher shear yield strength than Tresca. Experiments (Taylor & Quinney, 1931) show the actual value is closer to Von Mises.

**Yield surface geometry:**

In the $\pi$-plane (deviatoric plane perpendicular to the hydrostatic axis $\sigma_1 = \sigma_2 = \sigma_3$):
- Von Mises: a **circle** of radius $R = \sqrt{2/3}\,\sigma_Y$
- Tresca: a **regular hexagon** inscribed in the Von Mises circle

The hexagon touches the circle at 6 vertices corresponding to uniaxial stress states. Between vertices, the hexagon lies inside the circle — Tresca is always more conservative.

*Reference: Hill, Mathematical Theory of Plasticity, §2.1; Lubliner, Plasticity Theory, §3.3.*

### Appendix 9.3 — Drucker's Stability Postulate and Convexity of Yield Surfaces

**Drucker's Postulate (1951):** For a stable (work-hardening) material, the work done by an external agency during a loading cycle that starts and ends at the current stress state is non-negative:

$$
\oint d\sigma_{ij}\,d\varepsilon_{ij}^p \geq 0
$$

**Consequences:**

1. **Convexity:** The yield surface must be **convex** in stress space. No "dents" or concavities are allowed. Both Von Mises (circle) and Tresca (hexagon) satisfy this.

2. **Normality rule:** The plastic strain increment vector $d\varepsilon_{ij}^p$ must be **normal** to the yield surface at the current stress point (associated flow rule):

$$
d\varepsilon_{ij}^p = d\lambda \frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma_{ij}}
$$

where $f(\sigma_{ij}) = 0$ is the yield surface equation and $d\lambda \geq 0$ is a scalar multiplier.

3. **Uniqueness:** The stress-strain response is unique (no bifurcation in the hardening regime).

**For Von Mises** ($f = J_2 - k^2 = 0$):

$$
d\varepsilon_{ij}^p = d\lambda \frac{\partial J_2}{\partial \sigma_{ij}} = d\lambda \cdot s_{ij}
$$

This means plastic strain increments are proportional to the deviatoric stress — the **Prandtl-Reuss equations**. Physically: plastic flow occurs in the direction of the deviatoric stress (shape change), with no plastic volume change ($d\varepsilon_{kk}^p = d\lambda \cdot s_{kk} = 0$).

**For Tresca:** The normality rule creates difficulties at the hexagon corners (vertices), where the normal is not unique. At corners, the plastic strain increment can be any vector in the "cone" of normals to the adjacent faces. This mathematical inconvenience is one reason Von Mises is preferred in computational plasticity (FEA).

**Materials that violate Drucker's postulate:** Soils, concrete, and granular materials exhibit pressure-dependent yielding and non-associated flow (dilation angle ≠ friction angle). For these, the Drucker-Prager or Mohr-Coulomb criteria are used instead.

*Reference: Drucker, D.C. (1951), Quarterly of Applied Mathematics; Lubliner, Plasticity Theory, §3.5; de Souza Neto et al., Computational Methods for Plasticity, §3.4.*
