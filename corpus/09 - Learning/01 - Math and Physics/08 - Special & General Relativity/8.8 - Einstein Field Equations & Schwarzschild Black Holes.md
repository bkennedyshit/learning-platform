# 8.8 — Einstein Field Equations & Schwarzschild Black Holes

> *"The Einstein field equations are the most beautiful equations in all of physics."*
> — Charles W. Misner, Kip S. Thorne & John Archibald Wheeler, *Gravitation* (1973)

We have assembled all the mathematical machinery: manifolds, metrics, connections, and curvature tensors. Now we write the crown jewel of classical physics — **Einstein's field equations** — the dynamical equations that determine how matter curves spacetime. We then solve them for the simplest non-trivial case: a static, spherically symmetric vacuum, obtaining the **Schwarzschild solution** — the geometry of non-rotating black holes.

7. Compute the perihelion precession of Mercury and the deflection of light.
8. Discuss the Friedmann equations as the EFE applied to cosmology.

## 📚 1. Definitions

### Definition 8.8.1 — Einstein Field Equations

The **Einstein field equations** (EFE) relate the geometry of spacetime to the distribution of matter and energy:

$$
G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4} T_{\mu\nu}
$$

where:
- $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R$ is the **Einstein tensor**
- $\Lambda$ is the **cosmological constant**
- $T_{\mu\nu}$ is the **stress-energy tensor**
- $G$ is Newton's gravitational constant
- The factor $8\pi G/c^4$ is fixed by requiring the Newtonian limit

### Definition 8.8.2 — Stress-Energy Tensor for a Perfect Fluid

$$
T_{\mu\nu} = \left(\rho + \frac{p}{c^2}\right)U_\mu U_\nu + p\, g_{\mu\nu}
$$

where $\rho$ is the energy density, $p$ is the pressure, and $U^\mu$ is the fluid 4-velocity.

### Definition 8.8.3 — The Einstein-Hilbert Action

The action whose variation yields the EFE:

$$
S = \frac{c^4}{16\pi G}\int (R - 2\Lambda)\sqrt{-g}\, d^4x + S_{\text{matter}}
$$

Varying with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$ and setting $\delta S = 0$ yields the EFE.

### Definition 8.8.4 — Schwarzschild Solution

The unique spherically symmetric vacuum ($T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, $\Lambda = 0$) solution to the EFE (Birkhoff's theorem):

$$
ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)c^2 dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2
$$

where $r_s = 2GM/c^2$ and $d\Omega^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2$.

### Definition 8.8.5 — Event Horizon

The **event horizon** is the surface $r = r_s$ where $g_{00} = 0$ and $g_{11} \to \infty$. It is a **null hypersurface** — a one-way membrane through which particles and light can enter but never escape.

### Definition 8.8.6 — Key Schwarzschild Radii

- **Schwarzschild radius (event horizon):** $r_s = 2GM/c^2$
- **Photon sphere:** $r = 3GM/c^2 = 3r_s/2$ (unstable circular photon orbits)
- **ISCO (Innermost Stable Circular Orbit):** $r = 6GM/c^2 = 3r_s$ (last stable orbit for massive particles)

## 📐 2. Axioms / Postulates

### Postulate 8.8.1 — The EFE as the Simplest Consistent Theory

The Einstein field equations are the unique second-order partial differential equations for the metric that:
1. Are generally covariant (tensor equations)
2. Reduce to Newtonian gravity in the weak-field limit
3. Satisfy automatic energy-momentum conservation ($\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ via the Bianchi identity)
4. Involve at most second derivatives of the metric

(Lovelock's theorem makes this precise in 4D.)

### Postulate 8.8.2 — Birkhoff's Theorem

Any spherically symmetric vacuum solution to the EFE is necessarily **static** and is the Schwarzschild solution. This means: a pulsating spherical star (as long as it remains spherical) produces the same external gravitational field as a static star of the same mass.

## 🛡️ 3. Lemmas

### Lemma 8.8.1 — Trace of the Einstein Equations

**Statement:** Taking the trace of the EFE gives:

$$
R = -\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T + 4\Lambda
$$

where $T = g^{\mu\nu}T_{\mu\nu}$ is the trace of the stress-energy tensor.

**Proof:** Contract $G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = (8\pi G/c^4)T_{\mu\nu}$ with $g^{\mu\nu}$:

$$
g^{\mu\nu}G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g^{\mu\nu}g_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T
$$

$$
(R - \frac{1}{2}\cdot 4 \cdot R) + 4\Lambda = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T
$$

$$
-R + 4\Lambda = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T \implies R = -\frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T + 4\Lambda \qquad \blacksquare
$$

### Lemma 8.8.2 — Trace-Reversed Form of the EFE

**Statement:** The EFE can be written equivalently as:

$$
R_{\mu\nu} = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}\left(T_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}T\right) + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
$$

In vacuum ($T_{\mu\nu} = 0$, $\Lambda = 0$): $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$.

## 👑 4. Theorems

### Theorem 8.8.1 — Derivation of the Schwarzschild Solution

**Statement:** The most general static, spherically symmetric metric can be written:

$$
ds^2 = -e^{2\alpha(r)}c^2 dt^2 + e^{2\beta(r)}dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2
$$

The vacuum EFE ($R_{\mu\nu} = 0$) uniquely determine $\alpha$ and $\beta$, yielding the Schwarzschild metric.

### Theorem 8.8.2 — Effective Potential for Schwarzschild Geodesics

**Statement:** The radial motion of a test particle (mass $m$, energy $E$, angular momentum $L$) in Schwarzschild spacetime is governed by:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2 + V_{\text{eff}}(r) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{E^2}{m^2c^4} - 1\right)c^2
$$

where the effective potential is:

$$
V_{\text{eff}}(r) = -\frac{GM}{r} + \frac{L^2}{2m^2r^2} - \frac{GML^2}{m^2c^2r^3}
$$

The last term ($\propto 1/r^3$) is the **GR correction** absent in Newtonian gravity — it causes perihelion precession and the existence of an ISCO.

### Theorem 8.8.3 — Perihelion Precession of Mercury

**Statement:** A planet in a nearly circular orbit at radius $r$ precesses by:

$$
\Delta\phi = \frac{6\pi GM}{c^2 a(1-e^2)}
$$

per orbit, where $a$ is the semi-major axis and $e$ is the eccentricity. For Mercury: $\Delta\phi = 42.98''$/century.

### Theorem 8.8.4 — Gravitational Light Deflection (Full GR)

**Statement:** A photon passing a mass $M$ at closest approach $r_0$ is deflected by:

$$
\delta\phi = \frac{4GM}{c^2 r_0}
$$

This is **twice** the Newtonian/EP prediction — the extra factor comes from spatial curvature. Confirmed by Eddington's 1919 solar eclipse expedition.

## ✍️ 5. Proofs / Derivations

### Proof 8.8.1 — Deriving the Schwarzschild Metric from $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$

**Setup:** Ansatz for a static, spherically symmetric metric:

$$
ds^2 = -e^{2\alpha(r)}c^2 dt^2 + e^{2\beta(r)}dr^2 + r^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2)
$$

**Step 1:** Compute the non-zero Christoffel symbols (using the diagonal metric):

$$
\Gamma^0_{01} = \alpha', \quad \Gamma^1_{00} = \alpha' e^{2(\alpha-\beta)}c^2, \quad \Gamma^1_{11} = \beta'
$$

$$
\Gamma^1_{22} = -re^{-2\beta}, \quad \Gamma^1_{33} = -r\sin^2\theta\, e^{-2\beta}
$$

$$
\Gamma^2_{12} = \frac{1}{r}, \quad \Gamma^2_{33} = -\sin\theta\cos\theta, \quad \Gamma^3_{13} = \frac{1}{r}, \quad \Gamma^3_{23} = \cot\theta
$$

where primes denote $d/dr$.

**Step 2:** Compute $R_{00}$:

$$
R_{00} = e^{2(\alpha-\beta)}\left[\alpha'' + (\alpha')^2 - \alpha'\beta' + \frac{2\alpha'}{r}\right]c^2
$$

Setting $R_{00} = 0$:

$$
\alpha'' + (\alpha')^2 - \alpha'\beta' + \frac{2\alpha'}{r} = 0 \tag{A}
$$

**Step 3:** Compute $R_{11}$:

$$
R_{11} = -\alpha'' - (\alpha')^2 + \alpha'\beta' + \frac{2\beta'}{r}
$$

Setting $R_{11} = 0$:

$$
-\alpha'' - (\alpha')^2 + \alpha'\beta' + \frac{2\beta'}{r} = 0 \tag{B}
$$

**Step 4:** Add equations (A) and (B):

$$
\frac{2\alpha'}{r} + \frac{2\beta'}{r} = 0 \implies \alpha' + \beta' = 0 \implies \alpha + \beta = \text{const}
$$

**Step 5:** Boundary condition: as $r \to \infty$, the metric must approach Minkowski, so $\alpha \to 0$ and $\beta \to 0$. Therefore:

$$
\alpha = -\beta
$$

**Step 6:** Compute $R_{22}$:

$$
R_{22} = e^{-2\beta}[r(\alpha' - \beta') + 1] - 1 = e^{-2\beta}[2r\alpha' + 1] - 1
$$

Setting $R_{22} = 0$:

$$
e^{2\alpha}(1 + 2r\alpha') = 1
$$

$$
\frac{d}{dr}(re^{2\alpha}) = e^{2\alpha} + 2r\alpha' e^{2\alpha} = e^{2\alpha}(1 + 2r\alpha') = 1
$$

**Step 7:** Integrate:

$$
re^{2\alpha} = r + C
$$

where $C$ is an integration constant. Therefore:

$$
e^{2\alpha} = 1 + \frac{C}{r}
$$

**Step 8:** Match to Newtonian limit. For $r \to \infty$: $g_{00} \approx -(1 + 2\Phi/c^2)$ where $\Phi = -GM/r$. So:

$$
e^{2\alpha} = 1 - \frac{2GM}{c^2 r} = 1 - \frac{r_s}{r}
$$

Therefore $C = -r_s$.

**Step 9:** The complete solution:

$$
\boxed{ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)c^2 dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2} \qquad \blacksquare
$$

---

### Proof 8.8.2 — Effective Potential and Orbital Equation

**Setup:** A particle of mass $m$ moves in the Schwarzschild geometry. By the Killing symmetries ($\partial_t$ and $\partial_\phi$), we have conserved quantities:

$$
\tilde{E} = \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)\frac{dt}{d\tau}, \qquad \tilde{L} = r^2\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}
$$

(using $c = 1$ units for clarity).

**Step 1:** The normalization condition $g_{\mu\nu}U^\mu U^\nu = -1$ (for massive particles):

$$
-\left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right)\left(\frac{dt}{d\tau}\right)^2 + \left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2 + r^2\left(\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}\right)^2 = -1
$$

(restricting to equatorial motion $\theta = \pi/2$, $d\theta = 0$).

**Step 2:** Substitute the conserved quantities:

$$
-\frac{\tilde{E}^2}{1-r_s/r} + \frac{1}{1-r_s/r}\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2 + \frac{\tilde{L}^2}{r^2} = -1
$$

Multiply by $(1-r_s/r)$:

$$
-\tilde{E}^2 + \left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2 + \left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right)\frac{\tilde{L}^2}{r^2} + \left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right) = 0
$$

**Step 3:** Rearrange:

$$
\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2 = \tilde{E}^2 - \left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right)\left(1 + \frac{\tilde{L}^2}{r^2}\right)
$$

**Step 4:** Define the effective potential:

$$
V_{\text{eff}}(r) = \left(1-\frac{r_s}{r}\right)\left(1 + \frac{\tilde{L}^2}{r^2}\right) = 1 - \frac{r_s}{r} + \frac{\tilde{L}^2}{r^2} - \frac{r_s\tilde{L}^2}{r^3}
$$

The equation of motion becomes:

$$
\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2 = \tilde{E}^2 - V_{\text{eff}}(r)
$$

Restoring $c$ and $G$: the GR correction term is $-GML^2/(m^2c^2r^3)$, absent in Newtonian gravity. $\blacksquare$

---

### Proof 8.8.3 — Perihelion Precession

**Setup:** For a nearly circular orbit, expand around the minimum of $V_{\text{eff}}$.

**Step 1:** The orbital equation (using $u = 1/r$, $\phi$ as parameter):

$$
\frac{d^2u}{d\phi^2} + u = \frac{GM}{L^2/m^2} + \frac{3GM}{c^2}u^2
$$

The last term is the GR correction.

**Step 2:** For a Newtonian orbit: $u_0 = \frac{GM m^2}{L^2}(1 + e\cos\phi)$.

**Step 3:** Perturbation: substitute $u = u_0 + \delta u$ and linearize. The correction causes the argument of the cosine to shift:

$$
u \approx \frac{GMm^2}{L^2}(1 + e\cos((1-\epsilon)\phi))
$$

where $\epsilon = 3G^2M^2m^2/(L^2c^2)$.

**Step 4:** The orbit closes after $\phi = 2\pi/(1-\epsilon) \approx 2\pi(1+\epsilon)$. The precession per orbit:

$$
\Delta\phi = 2\pi\epsilon = \frac{6\pi G^2M^2m^2}{L^2c^2}
$$

Using $L^2/m^2 = GMa(1-e^2)$ for a Keplerian orbit:

$$
\boxed{\Delta\phi = \frac{6\pi GM}{c^2 a(1-e^2)}} \qquad \blacksquare
$$

For Mercury: $a = 5.79\times10^{10}$ m, $e = 0.2056$, $M = M_\odot$:

$$
\Delta\phi = \frac{6\pi \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 1.989\times10^{30}}{(3\times10^8)^2 \times 5.79\times10^{10} \times (1-0.0423)} = 5.02\times10^{-7}\,\text{rad/orbit}
$$

Mercury's orbital period is 87.97 days → 415 orbits/century:

$$
\Delta\phi_{\text{century}} = 5.02\times10^{-7} \times 415 = 2.08\times10^{-4}\,\text{rad} = 42.98''
$$

This matches observation perfectly — one of the first confirmations of GR.

## 🧮 6. Worked Examples

### Example 8.8.1 — Schwarzschild Radius of Common Objects

**Problem:** Compute the Schwarzschild radius for (a) Earth, (b) the Sun, (c) a supermassive black hole of $4\times10^6 M_\odot$ (Sgr A*).

**Solution:**

$$
r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2}
$$

**(a) Earth** ($M = 5.972\times10^{24}$ kg):

$$
r_s = \frac{2 \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 5.972\times10^{24}}{(3\times10^8)^2} = \frac{7.97\times10^{14}}{9\times10^{16}} = 8.87\times10^{-3}\,\text{m} \approx 8.9\,\text{mm}
$$

**(b) Sun** ($M = 1.989\times10^{30}$ kg):

$$
r_s = \frac{2 \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 1.989\times10^{30}}{9\times10^{16}} = 2.95\,\text{km}
$$

**(c) Sgr A*** ($M = 4\times10^6 \times 1.989\times10^{30} = 7.96\times10^{36}$ kg):

$$
r_s = 2.95\,\text{km} \times 4\times10^6 = 1.18\times10^7\,\text{km} \approx 0.08\,\text{AU}
$$

(About 17 solar radii — the Event Horizon Telescope imaged this in 2019.)

---

### Example 8.8.2 — Radial Free Fall into a Black Hole

**Problem:** A particle falls radially from rest at $r = r_0$ into a Schwarzschild black hole. Find the proper time to reach the singularity.

**Solution:**

For radial infall ($L = 0$) from rest at $r_0$ (so $\tilde{E}^2 = 1 - r_s/r_0$):

$$
\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2 = \frac{r_s}{r} - \frac{r_s}{r_0}
$$

For infall from rest at infinity ($r_0 \to \infty$, $\tilde{E} = 1$):

$$
\frac{dr}{d\tau} = -\sqrt{\frac{r_s}{r}} = -c\sqrt{\frac{r_s}{r}}
$$

(negative for infall). Separate variables:

$$
d\tau = -\frac{\sqrt{r}\, dr}{c\sqrt{r_s}}
$$

Integrate from $r = r_s$ to $r = 0$:

$$
\Delta\tau = \frac{1}{c\sqrt{r_s}}\int_0^{r_s}\sqrt{r}\, dr = \frac{1}{c\sqrt{r_s}}\left[\frac{2}{3}r^{3/2}\right]_0^{r_s} = \frac{2r_s}{3c}
$$

Restoring units: $\Delta\tau = \frac{2r_s}{3c} = \frac{4GM}{3c^3}$.

For a solar-mass black hole: $\Delta\tau = 4 \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 1.989\times10^{30}/(3 \times (3\times10^8)^3) = 6.6\,\mu\text{s}$.

For Sgr A*: $\Delta\tau \approx 26$ seconds.

---

### Example 8.8.3 — Light Deflection by the Sun

**Problem:** Compute the deflection angle for starlight grazing the Sun's limb.

**Solution:**

$$
\delta\phi = \frac{4GM_\odot}{c^2 R_\odot} = \frac{4 \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 1.989\times10^{30}}{(3\times10^8)^2 \times 6.96\times10^8}
$$

$$
= \frac{5.31\times10^{20}}{6.26\times10^{25}} = 8.48\times10^{-6}\,\text{rad} = 1.75''
$$

This was confirmed by Eddington's 1919 eclipse expedition (measured $1.61'' \pm 0.30''$, consistent with GR's prediction of $1.75''$ and inconsistent with the Newtonian prediction of $0.87''$).

---

### Example 8.8.4 — Friedmann Equation from the EFE

**Problem:** Apply the EFE to the flat FLRW metric with a perfect fluid to derive the Friedmann equation.

**Solution:**

From Example 8.7.3, the Ricci components for flat FLRW are:

$$
R_{00} = -\frac{3\ddot{a}}{a}, \qquad R_{ij} = \frac{1}{c^2}(a\ddot{a} + 2\dot{a}^2)\delta_{ij}
$$

The stress-energy tensor for a perfect fluid at rest in comoving coordinates:

$$
T_{00} = \rho c^2, \qquad T_{ij} = p\, a^2\delta_{ij}
$$

**The (00) component of the EFE** ($G_{00} = 8\pi G T_{00}/c^4$):

$$
G_{00} = R_{00} - \frac{1}{2}g_{00}R = -\frac{3\ddot{a}}{a} - \frac{1}{2}(-c^2)\frac{6}{c^2}\left(\frac{\ddot{a}}{a} + \frac{\dot{a}^2}{a^2}\right)
$$

$$
= -\frac{3\ddot{a}}{a} + 3\frac{\ddot{a}}{a} + 3\frac{\dot{a}^2}{a^2} = \frac{3\dot{a}^2}{a^2}
$$

Setting $G_{00} = 8\pi G\rho c^2/c^4 = 8\pi G\rho/c^2$:

$$
\frac{3\dot{a}^2}{a^2} = \frac{8\pi G\rho}{c^2}
$$

$$
\boxed{\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^2 = H^2 = \frac{8\pi G\rho}{3c^2}}
$$

This is the **Friedmann equation** — the foundation of modern cosmology.

## 🧠 8. Extended Worked Examples & Deep Dives

### Example 8.8.E1 — Einstein Field Equations from the Hilbert Action (Variational Derivation)

**Problem:** Derive the vacuum Einstein field equations $R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R = 0$ by varying the Einstein-Hilbert action $S = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int R\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x$ with respect to the metric $g^{\mu\nu}$.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Step 1: The action.**

$$
S_{\text{EH}} = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int R\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x
$$

We vary with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$: $g^{\mu\nu} \to g^{\mu\nu} + \delta g^{\mu\nu}$.

**Step 2: Variation of the integrand.**

$$
\delta(R\sqrt{-g}) = \delta(g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g})
$$

$$
= (\delta g^{\mu\nu})R_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g} + g^{\mu\nu}(\delta R_{\mu\nu})\sqrt{-g} + g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}\delta(\sqrt{-g})
$$

We need three pieces: $\delta R_{\mu\nu}$, $\delta\sqrt{-g}$, and the first term is already done.

**Step 3: Variation of $\sqrt{-g}$.**

Using the matrix identity $\delta\ln\det M = \text{tr}(M^{-1}\delta M)$:

$$
\delta g = \delta\det(g_{\mu\nu}) = g\, g^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu}
$$

Since $g^{\mu\nu}\delta g_{\mu\nu} = -g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ (from $\delta(g^{\mu\alpha}g_{\alpha\nu}) = \delta(\delta^\mu_\nu) = 0$):

$$
\delta g = -g\, g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}
$$

$$
\delta\sqrt{-g} = \frac{-\delta g}{2\sqrt{-g}} = \frac{g\, g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}}{2\sqrt{-g}} = -\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}
$$

**Step 4: Variation of the Ricci tensor (Palatini identity).**

The key result (proved separately):

$$
\delta R_{\mu\nu} = \nabla_\rho(\delta\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu}) - \nabla_\nu(\delta\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\rho})
$$

This is a total covariant divergence! Therefore:

$$
g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g} = \nabla_\rho V^\rho\sqrt{-g} = \partial_\rho(V^\rho\sqrt{-g})
$$

where $V^\rho = g^{\mu\nu}\delta\Gamma^\rho_{\mu\nu} - g^{\mu\rho}\delta\Gamma^\sigma_{\mu\sigma}$.

This is a total divergence — it integrates to a boundary term by Stokes' theorem:

$$
\int g^{\mu\nu}(\delta R_{\mu\nu})\sqrt{-g}\,d^4x = \oint_{\partial\mathcal{M}} V^\rho\,d\Sigma_\rho = 0
$$

(vanishes for variations that vanish on the boundary).

**Step 5: Combine.**

$$
\delta S = \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int\left[R_{\mu\nu}\sqrt{-g} + 0 - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R\sqrt{-g}\right]\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,d^4x
$$

$$
= \frac{1}{16\pi G}\int\left(R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R\right)\sqrt{-g}\,\delta g^{\mu\nu}\,d^4x
$$

**Step 6: Stationarity condition $\delta S = 0$ for arbitrary $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$:**

$$
\boxed{R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R = 0 \quad \text{(Vacuum Einstein equations)}}
$$

**Step 7: With matter.** Adding the matter action $S_{\text{matter}}$, define the stress-energy tensor:

$$
T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_{\text{matter}}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}
$$

The full equations become:

$$
G_{\mu\nu} \equiv R_{\mu\nu} - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R = \frac{8\pi G}{c^4}T_{\mu\nu}
$$

The factor $8\pi G/c^4$ is fixed by requiring the Newtonian limit to reproduce Poisson's equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$.

</details>

### Example 8.8.E2 — Schwarzschild Radius: Sun, Earth, and ISS

**Problem:** Compute the Schwarzschild radius $r_s = 2GM/c^2$ for (a) the Sun, (b) Earth, (c) the International Space Station ($m = 420{,}000$ kg). Discuss the physical meaning.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**The formula:**

$$
r_s = \frac{2GM}{c^2}
$$

**Constants:** $G = 6.674\times10^{-11}$ N·m²/kg², $c = 2.998\times10^8$ m/s.

**(a) Sun:** $M_\odot = 1.989\times10^{30}$ kg.

$$
r_s = \frac{2 \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 1.989\times10^{30}}{(2.998\times10^8)^2}
$$

$$
= \frac{2.654\times10^{20}}{8.988\times10^{16}} = 2953\,\text{m} \approx 2.95\,\text{km}
$$

The Sun's actual radius is $R_\odot = 696{,}000$ km, so $r_s/R_\odot = 4.2\times10^{-6}$. The Sun is far from being a black hole.

**(b) Earth:** $M_\oplus = 5.972\times10^{24}$ kg.

$$
r_s = \frac{2 \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 5.972\times10^{24}}{8.988\times10^{16}} = \frac{7.972\times10^{14}}{8.988\times10^{16}} = 8.87\times10^{-3}\,\text{m} \approx 8.87\,\text{mm}
$$

To make Earth a black hole, you'd need to compress it to less than a marble's size.

**(c) ISS:** $m = 4.2\times10^5$ kg.

$$
r_s = \frac{2 \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 4.2\times10^5}{8.988\times10^{16}} = \frac{5.606\times10^{-5}}{8.988\times10^{16}} = 6.24\times10^{-22}\,\text{m}
$$

This is about $4\times10^{-7}$ times the proton radius ($\sim 10^{-15}$ m) — far below the Planck length ($1.6\times10^{-35}$ m is even smaller, so actually $r_s \approx 6\times10^{-22}$ m is much larger than the Planck length but far smaller than any atomic scale).

**Summary table:**

| Object | Mass | $r_s$ | Actual radius | $r_s/R$ |
|--------|------|--------|---------------|----------|
| Sun | $2.0\times10^{30}$ kg | 2.95 km | 696,000 km | $4.2\times10^{-6}$ |
| Earth | $6.0\times10^{24}$ kg | 8.87 mm | 6,371 km | $1.4\times10^{-9}$ |
| ISS | $4.2\times10^5$ kg | $6.2\times10^{-22}$ m | ~100 m | $6\times10^{-24}$ |

**Physical meaning:** The ratio $r_s/R$ measures "how relativistic" the gravitational field is at the surface. For the Sun, GR corrections are parts per million. For a neutron star ($r_s/R \sim 0.4$), GR is essential. For a black hole ($r_s/R = 1$), the surface IS the horizon.

</details>

### Example 8.8.E3 — ISCO (Innermost Stable Circular Orbit) Derivation

**Problem:** For the Schwarzschild metric, find the radius of the innermost stable circular orbit (ISCO) for a massive test particle.

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Step 1: Effective potential.**

From the geodesic equation with conserved energy $\tilde{E} = E/(mc^2)$ and angular momentum $\tilde{L} = L/(mc)$, the radial equation is:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^2 + V_{\text{eff}}(r) = \frac{1}{2}(\tilde{E}^2 - 1)
$$

where (in geometric units $G = c = 1$):

$$
V_{\text{eff}}(r) = -\frac{M}{r} + \frac{\tilde{L}^2}{2r^2} - \frac{M\tilde{L}^2}{r^3}
$$

The last term $-M\tilde{L}^2/r^3$ is the GR correction (absent in Newtonian gravity).

**Step 2: Circular orbit conditions.**

A circular orbit at radius $r_0$ requires:

$$
V'_{\text{eff}}(r_0) = 0 \quad \text{(extremum)}
$$

$$
\frac{dV_{\text{eff}}}{dr} = \frac{M}{r^2} - \frac{\tilde{L}^2}{r^3} + \frac{3M\tilde{L}^2}{r^4} = 0
$$

Multiply by $r^4/M$:

$$
r^2 - \frac{\tilde{L}^2}{M}r + 3\tilde{L}^2 = 0
$$

This gives $r$ as a function of $\tilde{L}$.

**Step 3: Stability condition.**

A stable orbit requires $V''_{\text{eff}}(r_0) \gt  0$ (minimum, not maximum). The ISCO is the marginally stable orbit where:

$$
V''_{\text{eff}}(r_{\text{ISCO}}) = 0
$$

Compute:

$$
V''_{\text{eff}} = -\frac{2M}{r^3} + \frac{3\tilde{L}^2}{r^4} - \frac{12M\tilde{L}^2}{r^5}
$$

Set $V''_{\text{eff}} = 0$:

$$
-\frac{2M}{r^3} + \frac{3\tilde{L}^2}{r^4} - \frac{12M\tilde{L}^2}{r^5} = 0
$$

Multiply by $r^5/(M)$:

$$
-2r^2 + \frac{3\tilde{L}^2}{M}r - 12\tilde{L}^2 = 0 \tag{*}
$$

**Step 4: Solve simultaneously with $V' = 0$.**

From $V' = 0$: $r^2 - \frac{\tilde{L}^2}{M}r + 3\tilde{L}^2 = 0$, so $\frac{\tilde{L}^2}{M} = \frac{r^2}{r - 3M}$ (solving for $\tilde{L}^2/M$ from $Mr^2 = \tilde{L}^2 r - 3M\tilde{L}^2$, i.e., $\tilde{L}^2 = \frac{Mr^2}{r - 3M}$).

Substitute into (*):

$$
-2r^2 + 3\cdot\frac{Mr}{r-3M}\cdot r - 12\cdot\frac{Mr^2}{r-3M} = 0
$$

$$
-2r^2 + \frac{3Mr^2}{r-3M} - \frac{12Mr^2}{r-3M} = 0
$$

Divide by $r^2$:

$$
-2 + \frac{3M - 12M}{r - 3M} = -2 - \frac{9M}{r-3M} = 0
$$

$$
-2(r - 3M) - 9M = 0
$$

$$
-2r + 6M - 9M = 0
$$

$$
-2r - 3M = 0
$$

Hmm, that gives a negative $r$. Let me redo more carefully.

From $V' = 0$: $M r^2 - \tilde{L}^2 r + 3M\tilde{L}^2 = 0$ (multiplied through by $r^4$ and rearranged). Solving for $\tilde{L}^2$:

$$
\tilde{L}^2(r - 3M) = Mr^2 \implies \tilde{L}^2 = \frac{Mr^2}{r - 3M}
$$

From $V'' = 0$: $-2Mr^2 + 3\tilde{L}^2 r - 12M\tilde{L}^2 = 0$ (multiplied by $r^5$). Substitute $\tilde{L}^2$:

$$
-2Mr^2 + \frac{3Mr^2}{r-3M}\cdot r - \frac{12M \cdot Mr^2}{r-3M} = 0
$$

Divide by $Mr^2$:

$$
-2 + \frac{3r}{r-3M} - \frac{12M}{r-3M} = 0
$$

$$
-2 + \frac{3r - 12M}{r - 3M} = 0
$$

$$
\frac{3r - 12M}{r - 3M} = 2
$$

$$
3r - 12M = 2(r - 3M) = 2r - 6M
$$

$$
r = 6M
$$

$$
\boxed{r_{\text{ISCO}} = 6M = 3r_s}
$$

**Step 5: Physical values.**

Restoring units: $r_{\text{ISCO}} = 6GM/c^2 = 3r_s$.

For a solar-mass black hole: $r_{\text{ISCO}} = 3 \times 2.95\,\text{km} = 8.85\,\text{km}$.

For Sgr A* ($M \approx 4\times10^6 M_\odot$): $r_{\text{ISCO}} = 3 \times 2.95 \times 4\times10^6\,\text{km} = 3.5\times10^7\,\text{km} \approx 0.24\,\text{AU}$.

**Step 6: Angular momentum and energy at ISCO.**

$$
\tilde{L}_{\text{ISCO}}^2 = \frac{M(6M)^2}{6M - 3M} = \frac{36M^3}{3M} = 12M^2
$$

$$
\tilde{L}_{\text{ISCO}} = 2\sqrt{3}\,M
$$

The binding energy: $\tilde{E}_{\text{ISCO}} = \sqrt{1 - 2M/r_{\text{ISCO}}} \cdot (1 + \tilde{L}^2/r^2 - ...)$. The exact result is:

$$
\tilde{E}_{\text{ISCO}} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{3} \approx 0.9428
$$

The binding energy is $1 - \tilde{E}_{\text{ISCO}} = 1 - 2\sqrt{2}/3 \approx 5.72\%$ of the rest mass. This is the maximum energy extractable from matter falling into a non-rotating black hole — far more efficient than nuclear fusion (~0.7%).

</details>

### Example 8.8.E4 — Light Deflection by the Sun

**Problem:** Derive the angle of deflection of a light ray passing near the Sun at closest approach distance $b$ (impact parameter).

<details>
<summary>🔍 View Step-by-Step Solution</summary>

**Step 1: Null geodesic equation.**

For a photon ($ds^2 = 0$) in the Schwarzschild metric (equatorial plane, $\theta = \pi/2$), using conserved quantities $E$ (energy) and $L$ (angular momentum):

$$
\left(\frac{dr}{d\lambda}\right)^2 = E^2 - \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)\frac{L^2}{r^2}
$$

Define the impact parameter $b = L/E$ and use $u = 1/r$:

$$
\left(\frac{du}{d\phi}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{b^2} - u^2 + r_s u^3
$$

**Step 2: Differentiate to get the orbit equation.**

$$
\frac{d^2u}{d\phi^2} + u = \frac{3}{2}r_s u^2
$$

The right side is the GR correction. Without it ($r_s = 0$): $u = \sin\phi/b$ — a straight line.

**Step 3: Perturbative solution.**

Write $u = u_0 + u_1$ where $u_0 = \sin\phi/b$ (zeroth order) and $u_1$ is the first-order correction.

Substitute into the orbit equation:

$$
\frac{d^2 u_1}{d\phi^2} + u_1 = \frac{3r_s}{2}u_0^2 = \frac{3r_s}{2b^2}\sin^2\phi = \frac{3r_s}{4b^2}(1 - \cos 2\phi)
$$

**Step 4: Solve the inhomogeneous equation.**

Particular solution: try $u_1 = A + B\cos 2\phi$.

$$
-4B\cos 2\phi + A + B\cos 2\phi = \frac{3r_s}{4b^2}(1 - \cos 2\phi)
$$

$$
A - 3B\cos 2\phi = \frac{3r_s}{4b^2} - \frac{3r_s}{4b^2}\cos 2\phi
$$

Matching: $A = \frac{3r_s}{4b^2}$ and $-3B = -\frac{3r_s}{4b^2}$, so $B = \frac{r_s}{4b^2}$.

$$
u_1 = \frac{3r_s}{4b^2} + \frac{r_s}{4b^2}\cos 2\phi = \frac{r_s}{4b^2}(3 + \cos 2\phi) = \frac{r_s}{2b^2}(1 + \cos^2\phi)
$$

(using $3 + \cos 2\phi = 3 + 2\cos^2\phi - 1 = 2 + 2\cos^2\phi = 2(1+\cos^2\phi)$).

**Step 5: Full solution.**

$$
u = \frac{\sin\phi}{b} + \frac{r_s}{2b^2}(1 + \cos^2\phi)
$$

**Step 6: Find the deflection angle.**

The light ray comes from $r = \infty$ ($u = 0$) at some angle $\phi_1$ and goes to $r = \infty$ at angle $\phi_2$. Set $u = 0$:

$$
\frac{\sin\phi}{b} + \frac{r_s}{2b^2}(1 + \cos^2\phi) = 0
$$

For small deflection ($r_s/b \ll 1$), the angles are near $\phi = 0$ and $\phi = \pi$ (straight line). Write $\phi_1 = -\epsilon_1$ (small) and $\phi_2 = \pi + \epsilon_2$ (small):

At $\phi = -\epsilon_1$: $\sin(-\epsilon_1)/b + r_s/(2b^2)(1 + \cos^2\epsilon_1) \approx -\epsilon_1/b + r_s/b^2 = 0$

$$
\epsilon_1 = r_s/b
$$

At $\phi = \pi + \epsilon_2$: $\sin(\pi + \epsilon_2)/b + r_s/(2b^2)(1 + \cos^2(\pi+\epsilon_2)) \approx -\epsilon_2/b + r_s/b^2 = 0$

$$
\epsilon_2 = r_s/b
$$

**Total deflection angle:**

$$
\delta = \epsilon_1 + \epsilon_2 = \frac{2r_s}{b} = \frac{4GM}{c^2 b}
$$

$$
\boxed{\delta = \frac{4GM}{c^2 b}}
$$

**Step 7: Numerical value for the Sun.**

At the solar limb: $b = R_\odot = 6.96\times10^8$ m.

$$
\delta = \frac{4 \times 6.674\times10^{-11} \times 1.989\times10^{30}}{(3\times10^8)^2 \times 6.96\times10^8}
$$

$$
= \frac{5.31\times10^{20}}{6.26\times10^{25}} = 8.48\times10^{-6}\,\text{rad} = 1.75''
$$

This is exactly twice the Newtonian prediction ($\delta_N = 2GM/(c^2 b) = 0.87''$). The factor of 2 comes from the spatial curvature contribution (the $g_{rr}$ component), which has no Newtonian analogue.

**Historical note:** Eddington's 1919 solar eclipse expedition measured $\delta = 1.98'' \pm 0.16''$, confirming Einstein's prediction over Newton's. Modern VLBI measurements confirm GR to 0.01% precision.

</details>

## 📘 9. Appendix: Extended Derivations & Special Cases

### Appendix 9.1 — Birkhoff's Theorem

**Theorem (Birkhoff, 1923).** Any spherically symmetric solution of the vacuum Einstein equations $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$ is necessarily static and is given by the Schwarzschild metric.

**Significance:** This means:
1. A pulsating spherically symmetric star (e.g., a radially oscillating star) produces NO gravitational waves — the exterior metric is always Schwarzschild.
2. The Schwarzschild solution is unique for any spherically symmetric vacuum (analogous to Newton's shell theorem).
3. The metric outside a collapsing star is Schwarzschild even during the collapse.

**Proof sketch:**

**Step 1:** The most general spherically symmetric metric can be written (by coordinate choice) as:

$$
ds^2 = -e^{2\alpha(t,r)}c^2\,dt^2 + e^{2\beta(t,r)}dr^2 + r^2\,d\Omega^2
$$

where $d\Omega^2 = d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\,d\phi^2$ and we have used the "area radius" $r$ (defined so that spheres at constant $r$ have area $4\pi r^2$).

**Step 2:** Compute the Einstein tensor $G_{\mu\nu}$ for this metric. The vacuum equations $G_{\mu\nu} = 0$ give:

From $G_{01} = 0$: $\partial_t\beta = 0$, so $\beta = \beta(r)$ only.

From $G_{00} = 0$: $\frac{1}{r^2}\frac{d}{dr}[r(1 - e^{-2\beta})] = 0$, giving $e^{-2\beta} = 1 - r_s/r$ for some constant $r_s$.

From $G_{11} = 0$: $\partial_r\alpha = -\partial_r\beta$, so $\alpha = -\beta + f(t)$ for some function $f(t)$.

**Step 3:** The function $f(t)$ can be absorbed by redefining the time coordinate $t \to t'$ with $dt' = e^{f(t)}dt$. After this redefinition:

$$
e^{2\alpha} = e^{-2\beta} = 1 - \frac{r_s}{r}
$$

This is the Schwarzschild metric — and it is **independent of $t$** (static). $\blacksquare$

**Contrast with electromagnetism:** A pulsating charged sphere DOES radiate (electromagnetic waves). The difference is that EM radiation is dipolar (and higher), while gravitational radiation requires at least quadrupolar asymmetry. Spherical symmetry eliminates all multipoles, so no gravitational radiation is possible.

### Appendix 9.2 — Kruskal-Szekeres Coordinates: The Maximal Extension of Schwarzschild

**The problem with Schwarzschild coordinates:** At $r = r_s$, the metric components $g_{00} \to 0$ and $g_{11} \to \infty$. But the Kretschner scalar $K = 48M^2/r^6$ is finite at $r = r_s$ — this is a **coordinate singularity**, not a physical one. We need coordinates that are regular at the horizon.

**Step 1: Tortoise coordinate.**

Define $r^* = r + r_s\ln|r/r_s - 1|$, so $dr^* = dr/(1 - r_s/r)$. The metric becomes:

$$
ds^2 = \left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)(-c^2\,dt^2 + dr^{*2}) + r^2\,d\Omega^2
$$

**Step 2: Null coordinates.**

Define $u = ct - r^*$ (outgoing null) and $v = ct + r^*$ (ingoing null):

$$
ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{r_s}{r}\right)du\,dv + r^2\,d\Omega^2
$$

Still singular at $r = r_s$ (the prefactor vanishes).

**Step 3: Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates.**

Define:

$$
U = -e^{-u/(2r_s)} = -e^{-(ct-r^*)/(2r_s)}, \qquad V = e^{v/(2r_s)} = e^{(ct+r^*)/(2r_s)}
$$

Then:

$$
UV = -e^{(r^* - r^* + 2r^*... )} 
$$

Actually, let's compute $UV$:

$$
UV = -e^{-u/(2r_s)}\cdot e^{v/(2r_s)} = -e^{(v-u)/(2r_s)} = -e^{2r^*/(2r_s)} = -e^{r^*/r_s}
$$

Using $r^* = r + r_s\ln|r/r_s - 1|$:

$$
e^{r^*/r_s} = e^{r/r_s}\cdot|r/r_s - 1| = e^{r/r_s}\cdot\frac{|r - r_s|}{r_s}
$$

So:

$$
UV = -\frac{r - r_s}{r_s}e^{r/r_s} \quad \text{(for } r > r_s\text{)}
$$

**Step 4: The metric in Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates.**

$$
ds^2 = -\frac{4r_s^3}{r}e^{-r/r_s}\,dU\,dV + r^2\,d\Omega^2
$$

where $r$ is implicitly defined by $UV = -(r/r_s - 1)e^{r/r_s}$.

**Key properties:**
- The metric is **perfectly regular** at $r = r_s$ (where $UV = 0$).
- The true singularity $r = 0$ corresponds to $UV = e$ (a spacelike hyperbola).
- The horizon $r = r_s$ corresponds to $U = 0$ or $V = 0$ — null surfaces.
- Light cones are at $45°$ everywhere (since $ds^2 = 0$ gives $dU\,dV = 0$, i.e., $U = \text{const}$ or $V = \text{const}$).

**The four regions of the maximal extension:**

| Region | $U$ | $V$ | Physical interpretation |
|--------|-----|-----|------------------------|
| I | $U < 0$ | $V > 0$ | Exterior (our universe) |
| II | $U > 0$ | $V > 0$ | Black hole interior (future singularity) |
| III | $U > 0$ | $V < 0$ | Parallel exterior universe |
| IV | $U < 0$ | $V < 0$ | White hole interior (past singularity) |

**Physical interpretation:**
- Region II is the black hole interior: once inside ($r < r_s$), all future-directed paths lead to the singularity $r = 0$. The singularity is in the **future**, not at a spatial location — you can't avoid it by moving in any direction.
- Region IV (white hole) is the time-reverse of a black hole: nothing can enter, everything exits. It is not believed to exist in nature.
- Region III is a second asymptotically flat universe, connected to ours by the Einstein-Rosen bridge (wormhole) — but it is not traversable (the bridge pinches off before anything can cross).

**References:** Carroll §5.7; MTW §31; Wald §6.4; Hartle Ch. 12.
