## 🐕 2. Quadrupeds & Drones

### Quads
- **Boston Dynamics Spot** — industrial inspection, deployed widely.
- **ANYbotics ANYmal** — outdoor / industrial.
- **Unitree Go2 / B2** — research + recreational.

### Drones
- Stack: airframe → motors + ESC → flight controller (Pixhawk / Cube / FMU) → autopilot (PX4 or ArduPilot) → companion computer (Jetson / Pi 5) → ROS 2 / MAVLink / DroneCAN (Cyphal) bridge → mission planner (QGroundControl / MAVSDK).
- 2026 trends: VTOL fixed-wing for mid-range delivery; LLM-augmented mission planning; ANR (anti-drone) market growth.

## ⚔️ 4. The Platform War — ROS 2 vs Proprietary "Physical AI"

The 2026 split (rephrased from [TechTimes — Robotics Summit 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317154/20260525/humanoid-robots-reach-production-scale-robotics-summit-opens-ros-vs-proprietary-physical-ai.htm) for compliance):

| Camp | Position | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ROS 2 + open stacks** | Vendor-agnostic; modular; community innovation | Most academia, many startups, Boston Dynamics-friendly tools |
| **NVIDIA Isaac (Sim + Lab + ROS bridge + GR00T)** | "GPU-accelerated open stack" | NVIDIA-aligned ecosystem |
| **Closed VLA platforms** (Figure Helix, Tesla Optimus, Skild AI, pi-0) | End-to-end vertically integrated | Figure, Tesla, Skild, pi |
| **Hybrid / pragmatic** | ROS 2 below + VLA above | Most practical 2026 deployments |

The question of who controls the **software layer** of robotics is the trillion-dollar one — and it's genuinely undecided as of May 2026.

## 💼 5. Where the Money Will Be

For a software-forward engineer in 2026, the most defensible niches are:

1. **Vertical software** — pick a domain (warehouse / construction / surgery / agriculture) and own its full stack on top of generic hardware.
2. **Simulation + data services** — generate synthetic training data + benchmarks for VLA models.
3. **Fleet / ops platforms** — managing a fleet of humanoids (or any robots) at scale.
4. **Perception models** — task-specific vision/depth/segmentation models, sold as services.
5. **Teleoperation rigs + data collection** — high-quality demonstration data is the bottleneck for VLA training.
6. **Robot-as-a-Service** — lease + integrate other people's hardware; capture ops margin.

## 🛠️ 6. Worked Example (skeleton) — Spec a 2026 Hobby Humanoid

Goal: a research-grade adult-sized bipedal humanoid for ~$25K parts budget.

1. **Skeleton** — aluminum-extrusion + 3D-printed parts; total ~30–40 kg.
2. **Joints** — 22–28 DOFs; quasi-direct-drive BLDC modules (e.g., Unitree-style or open-source clones); ODrive-controlled.
3. **Compute** — Jetson Orin NX (16 GB) + co-processor STM32 for real-time control.
4. **Sensors** — IMU (BNO086), 2× RealSense or OAK-D, optional 2D LiDAR.
5. **Software** — Ubuntu 32.04 + ROS 2 Humble + Drake / MuJoCo for sim + WBC controller + open VLA model for high-level behavior.
6. **Targets** — stand, walk, manipulate cup-sized objects, recover from a small push.

## ⚠️ 8. Common Misconceptions

- **"Humanoids are 5+ years away from production."** They are **shipping** now. Not at scale yet, but the demos-to-deployment line was crossed in 2024–2025.
- **"VLA models will replace classical robotics."** They sit on top — perception + planning + low-level control still apply. The integration matters.
- **"Pick one platform and bet."** The 2026 platform war is genuinely undecided; build vendor-agnostic where possible.
- **"Hardware is the bottleneck."** Increasingly the bottleneck is data + software + foundations. Pure hardware companies are commoditizing.
