# 20.5 — SLOs, SLAs, Error Budgets & Incident Response

> *"100% is the wrong reliability target for basically everything."* — Google SRE Book (paraphrased; the entire error-budget framework grows out of this single observation; rephrased for compliance from [sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/))

This is the **highest-leverage chapter in the track**. Everything else (CI/CD, IaC, containers, observability, chaos, deployment strategies, FinOps) becomes coherent only once you have an explicit reliability target and a written policy for what to do when you exceed it. This chapter is built from the [Google SRE Book](https://sre.google/sre-book/table-of-contents/) and [SRE Workbook](https://sre.google/workbook/table-of-contents/) — both free.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

1. Define **SLI**, **SLO**, **SLA**, and **error budget** crisply, with units.
2. Pick SLIs that are **user-perceptible**, not vanity metrics.
3. Compute an error budget and a **burn rate**.
4. Author **multi-window, multi-burn-rate alerts** instead of static thresholds.
5. Write an **error-budget policy** in plain English.
6. Run an **on-call rotation** that doesn't burn out the team.
7. Run a **blameless postmortem** that produces real action items.

## 📚 1. The Vocabulary, Crisply

### Definition 20.5.1 — SLI (Service Level Indicator)
A **measurement** of one aspect of service performance. Concrete, with units.
- *Good SLI:* "fraction of HTTP requests that return < 300 ms with status < 500."
- *Bad SLI:* "system health score" (vague, no units).

### Definition 20.5.2 — SLO (Service Level Objective)
A **target** for an SLI over a **rolling window**.
- *Example:* "99.9% of HTTP requests in any rolling 30-day window return < 300 ms with status < 500."
- The window matters as much as the percentage. 99.9% over 30 days is very different from 99.9% over 1 hour.

### Definition 20.5.3 — SLA (Service Level Agreement)
An **external contract** with consequences (refunds, credits) when violated. Usually looser than the SLO — operate to the SLO; sign an SLA at a less-strict level.

### Definition 20.5.4 — Error Budget
The complement of the SLO over a window:
$$
\text{error budget} = (1 - \text{SLO}) \times \text{requests in window}
$$

For a 99.9% SLO, the budget is **0.1%** of requests. (paraphrased example from the [Google SRE Workbook — Implementing SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/implementing-slos/), rephrased for compliance)

### Definition 20.5.5 — Burn Rate
The ratio at which you are consuming the budget, normalized so 1.0 = "exactly on pace to burn the whole budget at the end of the window." A burn rate of 14.4 over 1 hour = burning the entire 30-day budget in 1 hour.

## 🔥 2. Multi-Window, Multi-Burn-Rate Alerts

The SRE Workbook's single most useful pattern. Instead of static thresholds, you alert on burn rate over multiple time windows — short windows catch fast burns; long windows confirm sustained burns.

| Severity | Burn rate | Long window | Short window | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Page (SEV-1)** | ≥ 14.4 | 1 h | 5 min | At this rate, the budget burns in ~2 days; act now |
| **Page (SEV-2)** | ≥ 6   | 6 h | 30 min | Burn budget in ~5 days |
| **Ticket** | ≥ 1   | 3 d | 6 h | Slow burn; investigate during day |

The double-window guard prevents a 5-minute spike from paging without confirmation. (paraphrased from [Google SRE Workbook — Alerting on SLOs](https://sre.google/workbook/alerting-on-slos/), rephrased for compliance)

### PromQL sketch

```promql
# Burn rate over 1h window (using the recording rule from 20.4)
(1 - orders_api:availability:ratio_rate1h) / 0.001 > 14.4
and
(1 - orders_api:availability:ratio_rate5m) / 0.001 > 14.4
```

## 📝 3. The Error-Budget Policy (Written, Signed)

An error-budget policy says, in plain English, *what we do* when the budget runs out. Without one, "we have an SLO" is a wallpaper. The policy is signed by both engineering and product. Skeleton (paraphrased from the [Google SRE Workbook — Error Budget Policy template](https://sre.google/workbook/error-budget-policy/), rephrased for compliance):

> ## Error Budget Policy — Orders API
> **SLO:** 99.9% of requests in 30 days under 300 ms with non-5xx response.
>
> **When budget burn ≥ 100% (budget exhausted):**
> 1. Feature freeze on Orders API. Only reliability fixes ship.
> 2. Daily 15-minute reliability standup until budget is back above 50%.
> 3. Postmortem identifies root cause within 72 hours.
>
> **When budget burn ≥ 50%:**
> 1. PR-level review is escalated to senior engineer for risk-bearing changes.
> 2. Deploys are gated to business hours.
>
> **When budget is healthy (< 25% burn):**
> 1. Team is encouraged to *spend* the remaining budget on faster experimentation, riskier launches, or chaos engineering.
>
> **Signed:** Eng Lead · PM Lead · Date.

The third clause is the secret. A healthy budget that *isn't* spent is wasted. SRE pushes you to spend it deliberately.

## 📞 4. On-Call That Doesn't Burn People Out

Principles (paraphrased from [Google SRE Book — Being On-Call](https://sre.google/sre-book/being-on-call/), rephrased for compliance):

1. **Rotation size ≥ 6 people** so each person is on-call < ~1 week / 6 weeks.
2. **Cap pages** — > 2 pages in 24h is a sign of broken alerting, not heroism.
3. **Comp on-call hours** explicitly (TOIL is real work).
4. **No auto-paging on warnings** — a page implies "human action needed in minutes."
5. **Run-books per page** — every alert has a linked run-book; no run-book → not a page.
6. **Hand-off ritual** — a written summary at every shift change.

Tooling: PagerDuty, Opsgenie (Atlassian), Squadcast, Rootly, FireHydrant, incident.io. The tool is less important than the policy on top of it.

## 🧯 5. Incident Response — IMS Roles

Borrowed from emergency-management Incident Command System:

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Incident Commander (IC)** | Owns the incident; doesn't dive into code |
| **Operations Lead** | Executes mitigations |
| **Communications Lead** | Updates stakeholders / status page |
| **Scribe** | Live timeline in the channel |

Severity levels are simple:
- **SEV-1**: customer impact, all-hands.
- **SEV-2**: customer impact, contained.
- **SEV-3**: internal impact only.

A status page (Statuspage.io, Better Stack, incident.io) is a non-negotiable — silence breeds panic.

## 🪞 6. Blameless Postmortems

A blameless postmortem assumes everyone acted reasonably given what they knew. The goal is not "who messed up" but **what conditions made the mistake easy to make**.

Standard sections:
1. Summary (what happened, in two sentences, customer-impact framed).
2. Timeline (UTC, every event with a source — log, page, person).
3. Root cause (use 5-Whys; one cause is rare).
4. Detection (how did we find out? alert? customer? CEO?).
5. Mitigation (what we did to stop the bleeding).
6. Action items (each with an owner + ticket + due date — and *each is treated like real work*).
7. Lessons learned.

(structure paraphrased from the [Google SRE Book — Postmortem Culture chapter](https://sre.google/sre-book/postmortem-culture/), rephrased for compliance)

> **Key practice:** publish the postmortem internally. The act of writing it is half the value; the second half is everyone else reading it.

## 🛠️ 7. Worked Example — Stand Up an SLO in One Afternoon

1. Pick the SLI: HTTP 200 + < 300 ms / total non-5xx requests for `orders-api`.
2. Pick the window: 30 days rolling.
3. Pick the target: 99.9%.
4. Write the recording rule (from [[20.4 - Observability - Logs, Metrics, Traces]]).
5. Write multi-window burn-rate alerts (PromQL above).
6. Pipe alerts to PagerDuty (SEV-1) and Slack (ticket).
7. Draft the error-budget policy (above), get sign-off.
8. Add a Grafana dashboard panel showing **remaining budget** and **30-day burn**.

You now have what most teams claim to have but don't.

## ⚠️ 9. Common Misconceptions

- **"100% reliability is the goal."** It isn't. It's expensive, slows shipping, and you can't observe perfection anyway. Pick a target slightly worse than your *user-perceptible* threshold. (paraphrased from [Google SRE Book — Embracing Risk](https://sre.google/sre-book/embracing-risk/), rephrased for compliance)
- **"SLA = SLO."** SLA is the contract; SLO is operational. Operate stricter than you sign.
- **"More alerts = better coverage."** More alerts = page fatigue → real pages get missed. Fewer, sharper, burn-rate-based alerts win.
- **"Postmortems should name names."** They shouldn't. Blameless postmortems generate honesty; blame-laden ones generate cover-ups.
- **"On-call is engineering glory."** It is **work**. Rotate it, cap it, comp it.
