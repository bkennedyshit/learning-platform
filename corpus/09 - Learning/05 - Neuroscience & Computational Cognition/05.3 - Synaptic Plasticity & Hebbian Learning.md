# 05.3 — Synaptic Plasticity & Hebbian Learning

> *"When an axon of cell A is near enough to excite cell B and repeatedly or persistently takes part in firing it, some growth process or metabolic change takes place in one or both cells such that A's efficiency, as one of the cells firing B, is increased."*
> — **Donald O. Hebb**, *The Organization of Behavior* (1949)

Synaptic plasticity is the biological mechanism of learning and memory. This chapter derives the mathematical rules governing how synaptic weights change — from Hebb's original postulate through modern spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) — and draws the critical comparison to gradient-descent learning in artificial neural networks.

## 📚 1. Definitions

### Definition 05.3.1 — Synapse

A **synapse** is the junction between two neurons where signal transmission occurs. Components:
- **Presynaptic terminal** (bouton): Contains synaptic vesicles filled with neurotransmitter (~5,000 molecules/vesicle for glutamate)
- **Synaptic cleft**: 20–40 nm gap between pre- and postsynaptic membranes
- **Postsynaptic density** (PSD): Protein-rich region containing receptors, scaffolding proteins (PSD-95), and signaling molecules

### Definition 05.3.2 — Synaptic Weight

The **synaptic weight** $w_{ij}$ quantifies the efficacy of the connection from neuron $j$ (presynaptic) to neuron $i$ (postsynaptic). Biologically determined by:
- Number of release sites (active zones)
- Probability of vesicle release ($p_r$, typically 0.1–0.9)
- Quantal size (postsynaptic response to one vesicle)
- Number and conductance of postsynaptic receptors

$$
w_{ij} \propto N_{\text{sites}} \times p_r \times q
$$

### Definition 05.3.3 — Long-Term Potentiation (LTP)

**LTP** is a persistent increase in synaptic strength lasting hours to years, induced by high-frequency stimulation (typically >100 Hz tetanus or theta-burst). First demonstrated by Bliss & Lømo (1973) in hippocampal CA1.

**Induction requirements:**
1. Strong postsynaptic depolarization (to relieve Mg²⁺ block of NMDA receptors)
2. Presynaptic glutamate release (to bind NMDA receptors)
3. Temporal coincidence of 1 and 2 (within ~10–20 ms)

### Definition 05.3.4 — Long-Term Depression (LTD)

**LTD** is a persistent decrease in synaptic strength, induced by:
- Low-frequency stimulation (1–5 Hz for 10–15 minutes) — homosynaptic LTD
- Postsynaptic firing before presynaptic (anti-causal timing) — STDP-based LTD
- Moderate Ca²⁺ influx (below LTP threshold but above baseline)

### Definition 05.3.5 — Hebb's Rule (Mathematical Formulation)

The **Hebbian learning rule** in its simplest form:

$$
\Delta w_{ij} = \eta \cdot x_i \cdot x_j
$$

where:
- $\Delta w_{ij}$ = change in synaptic weight from neuron $j$ to neuron $i$
- $\eta$ = learning rate
- $x_i$ = postsynaptic activity
- $x_j$ = presynaptic activity

**Problem:** Pure Hebbian learning is unstable — weights grow without bound. This necessitates normalization rules (Oja's rule, BCM theory).

### Definition 05.3.6 — NMDA Receptor as Coincidence Detector

![[neuro-05__fig4.svg|697]]

The **NMDA receptor** (N-methyl-D-aspartate) is a ligand-gated ion channel that requires BOTH:
1. Glutamate binding (presynaptic activity)
2. Postsynaptic depolarization (to expel Mg²⁺ block from the channel pore)

This dual requirement makes it a biological AND-gate — the molecular implementation of Hebb's rule:

$$
I_{\text{NMDA}} = \bar{g}_{\text{NMDA}} \cdot B(V) \cdot s \cdot (V - E_{\text{NMDA}})
$$

where the Mg²⁺ block function:

$$
B(V) = \frac{1}{1 + [\text{Mg}^{2+}]_o \cdot e^{-0.062V} / 3.57}
$$

### Definition 05.3.7 — Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP)

**STDP** is the experimentally observed rule (Markram et al., 1997; Bi & Poo, 1998) where the sign and magnitude of synaptic change depends on the precise timing between pre- and postsynaptic spikes:

$$
\Delta w = \begin{cases} A_+ \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_+}\right) & \text{if } \Delta t > 0 \text{ (pre before post → LTP)} \\ -A_- \exp\left(\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_-}\right) & \text{if } \Delta t < 0 \text{ (post before pre → LTD)} \end{cases}
$$

where $\Delta t = t_{\text{post}} - t_{\text{pre}}$, $A_+ \approx 0.01$, $A_- \approx 0.012$, $\tau_+ \approx 20$ ms, $\tau_- \approx 20$ ms.

### Definition 05.3.8 — BCM Theory (Bienenstock-Cooper-Munro)

The **BCM rule** (1982) introduces a sliding modification threshold $\theta_M$ that separates LTP from LTD:

$$
\Delta w = \eta \cdot x_{\text{pre}} \cdot \phi(x_{\text{post}}, \theta_M)
$$

where $\phi(x, \theta) = x(x - \theta)$ and $\theta_M$ slides with the time-averaged postsynaptic activity:

$$
\theta_M = \langle x_{\text{post}}^2 \rangle
$$

This creates **metaplasticity**: highly active synapses raise the threshold, making further LTP harder (homeostatic regulation).

## 🔬 2. Biological Mechanisms

### 2.1 — LTP Induction Cascade (Early-Phase, E-LTP)

The complete molecular pathway for NMDA-dependent LTP at a glutamatergic synapse:

$$
\text{High-freq presynaptic firing} \rightarrow \text{Glutamate release} \rightarrow \text{AMPA-R activation} \rightarrow \text{Postsynaptic depolarization}
$$

$$
\rightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+} \text{ expelled from NMDA-R} \rightarrow \text{Ca}^{2+} \text{ influx through NMDA-R}
$$

$$
\rightarrow [\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i \uparrow\uparrow \rightarrow \text{CaMKII activation (autophosphorylation)}
$$

$$
\rightarrow \text{AMPA-R phosphorylation (increased conductance)} + \text{AMPA-R trafficking to membrane}
$$

**Temporal requirements:**
- Presynaptic glutamate must be present while postsynaptic membrane is depolarized
- Ca²⁺ must exceed ~500 nM threshold (vs. ~100 nM at rest)
- CaMKII autophosphorylation creates a molecular "memory switch" (bistable)

### 2.2 — Late-Phase LTP (L-LTP) — Protein Synthesis Dependent

For memories lasting >3 hours, additional cascades are required:

$$
\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{CaMKII} \rightarrow \text{CREB phosphorylation} \rightarrow \text{Gene transcription (nucleus)}
$$

$$
\rightarrow \text{New protein synthesis} \rightarrow \text{Structural changes:}
$$

- New dendritic spine formation
- Spine enlargement (thin → mushroom morphology)
- Increased PSD-95 scaffolding
- New AMPA receptor insertion

**Synaptic tagging hypothesis:** Active synapses are "tagged" so that newly synthesized proteins are captured specifically at potentiated synapses (not all synapses on the neuron).

### 2.3 — LTD Induction (NMDA-Dependent)

LTD requires moderate Ca²⁺ elevation (lower than LTP threshold):

$$
\text{Low-freq stimulation (1 Hz)} \rightarrow \text{Modest Ca}^{2+} \text{ influx} \rightarrow [\text{Ca}^{2+}]_i \approx 200\text{–}500 \text{ nM}
$$

$$
\rightarrow \text{Calcineurin (PP2B) activation} \rightarrow \text{Protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) activation}
$$

$$
\rightarrow \text{AMPA-R dephosphorylation} \rightarrow \text{AMPA-R endocytosis (internalization)}
$$

**The Ca²⁺ hypothesis of bidirectional plasticity:**
- Low Ca²⁺ (~200–500 nM) → phosphatases dominate → LTD
- High Ca²⁺ (>500 nM) → kinases dominate → LTP
- Baseline Ca²⁺ (~100 nM) → no change

### 2.4 — STDP Molecular Mechanism

The timing dependence arises from the interaction between:
- **Back-propagating action potential (bAP):** AP propagates backward into dendrites, providing the postsynaptic depolarization signal
- **NMDA receptor kinetics:** Glutamate binding opens the channel for ~50–100 ms

**Pre-before-post (Δt > 0, LTP):**
Glutamate binds NMDA-R → bAP arrives within 20 ms → Mg²⁺ block relieved while glutamate still bound → maximal Ca²⁺ influx → CaMKII → LTP

**Post-before-pre (Δt < 0, LTD):**
bAP arrives first → NMDA-R not yet activated → when glutamate arrives, bAP has decayed → partial depolarization → moderate Ca²⁺ → calcineurin → LTD

### 2.5 — Metaplasticity and the BCM Sliding Threshold

The BCM threshold $\theta_M$ has a biological correlate: the **ratio of NR2A to NR2B NMDA receptor subunits**.

- High activity history → more NR2A (shorter Ca²⁺ transients) → higher LTP threshold
- Low activity history → more NR2B (longer Ca²⁺ transients) → lower LTP threshold

This is the mechanism by which 5-HT2A agonism may reopen plasticity: by shifting the NR2A/NR2B ratio back toward the juvenile (NR2B-dominant) state, effectively lowering $\theta_M$ and making LTP easier to induce.

## 📐 3. Mathematical Models

### 3.1 — Classical Hebbian Rule and Instability

The basic Hebbian update:

$$
\Delta w_{ij} = \eta \cdot y_i \cdot x_j
$$

where $y_i = \sum_j w_{ij} x_j$ is the postsynaptic output. In matrix form for weight vector $\mathbf{w}$:

$$
\Delta \mathbf{w} = \eta \cdot y \cdot \mathbf{x} = \eta \cdot (\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{x}) \cdot \mathbf{x}
$$

**Instability proof:** If inputs have covariance matrix $\mathbf{C} = \langle \mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^T \rangle$, then:

$$
\langle \Delta \mathbf{w} \rangle = \eta \cdot \mathbf{C} \cdot \mathbf{w}
$$

This is a linear ODE $\dot{\mathbf{w}} = \eta \mathbf{C} \mathbf{w}$ whose solution is:

$$
\mathbf{w}(t) = \sum_k c_k e^{\eta \lambda_k t} \mathbf{e}_k
$$

where $\lambda_k$ are eigenvalues of $\mathbf{C}$ (all positive since $\mathbf{C}$ is positive semi-definite). Therefore $\|\mathbf{w}\| \rightarrow \infty$ — **unbounded growth**.

### 3.2 — Oja's Rule (Normalized Hebbian Learning)

Oja (1982) added a weight decay term that constrains $\|\mathbf{w}\| = 1$:

$$
\Delta w_{ij} = \eta \cdot y_i (x_j - y_i \cdot w_{ij})
$$

In vector form:

$$
\Delta \mathbf{w} = \eta \cdot y(\mathbf{x} - y\mathbf{w})
$$

**Convergence:** $\mathbf{w}$ converges to the **principal eigenvector** of the input covariance matrix $\mathbf{C}$ — i.e., Oja's rule performs online PCA (see [[2.6 - Eigenvalues Eigenvectors & Diagonalization]]).

**Proof sketch:** At equilibrium, $\langle \Delta \mathbf{w} \rangle = 0$:

$$
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{w} - (\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{C} \mathbf{w})\mathbf{w} = 0 \implies \mathbf{C}\mathbf{w} = \lambda \mathbf{w}
$$

where $\lambda = \mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{C} \mathbf{w}$. This is the eigenvalue equation. The largest eigenvalue is selected because it's the only stable fixed point.

### 3.3 — STDP as a Mathematical Learning Rule

The STDP weight update for a pair of pre/post spikes:

$$
\Delta w = \begin{cases} A_+ \exp\left(-\frac{|t_{\text{post}} - t_{\text{pre}}|}{\tau_+}\right) & \text{if } t_{\text{post}} > t_{\text{pre}} \\ -A_- \exp\left(-\frac{|t_{\text{pre}} - t_{\text{post}}|}{\tau_-}\right) & \text{if } t_{\text{pre}} > t_{\text{post}} \end{cases}
$$

For multiple spike pairs, the total weight change integrates over all pairs:

$$
\Delta w = \sum_{\text{pre spikes } t_j} \sum_{\text{post spikes } t_i} W(t_i - t_j)
$$

where $W(\Delta t)$ is the STDP kernel function.

**Net effect for Poisson spike trains** at rates $r_{\text{pre}}$ and $r_{\text{post}}$:

$$
\langle \Delta w \rangle = r_{\text{pre}} \cdot r_{\text{post}} \left( A_+ \tau_+ - A_- \tau_- \right)
$$

If $A_+ \tau_+ > A_- \tau_-$: net potentiation (correlated firing strengthens connections).
If $A_+ \tau_+ < A_- \tau_-$: net depression (prevents runaway excitation).

### 3.4 — BCM Theory (Full Derivation)

The BCM modification function:

$$
\phi(y, \theta_M) = y(y - \theta_M)
$$

Weight update:

$$
\frac{dw}{dt} = \eta \cdot x \cdot \phi(y, \theta_M) = \eta \cdot x \cdot y(y - \theta_M)
$$

Sliding threshold dynamics:

$$
\tau_\theta \frac{d\theta_M}{dt} = -\theta_M + \langle y^2 \rangle
$$

**Fixed-point analysis:** At equilibrium with input statistics:
- If $y < \theta_M$: $\phi < 0$ → LTD (weakening)
- If $y > \theta_M$: $\phi > 0$ → LTP (strengthening)
- If $y = 0$ or $y = \theta_M$: no change

The sliding threshold creates **selectivity**: neurons become tuned to specific input patterns because:
1. Strongly driven inputs push $y > \theta_M$ → LTP
2. Weakly driven inputs have $y < \theta_M$ → LTD
3. As selectivity increases, $\theta_M$ adjusts to maintain homeostasis

### 3.5 — NMDA Receptor Voltage Dependence (Mg²⁺ Block)

The Jahr-Stevens (1990) model of the Mg²⁺ block:

$$
B(V) = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{[\text{Mg}^{2+}]_o}{3.57} \exp(-0.062 V)}
$$

At $[\text{Mg}^{2+}]_o = 1$ mM:
- $B(-70 \text{ mV}) = \frac{1}{1 + 0.28 \cdot e^{4.34}} = \frac{1}{1 + 21.4} = 0.045$ (95.5% blocked)
- $B(-30 \text{ mV}) = \frac{1}{1 + 0.28 \cdot e^{1.86}} = \frac{1}{1 + 1.80} = 0.357$ (64% blocked)
- $B(0 \text{ mV}) = \frac{1}{1 + 0.28} = 0.781$ (22% blocked)
- $B(+20 \text{ mV}) = \frac{1}{1 + 0.28 \cdot e^{-1.24}} = \frac{1}{1 + 0.081} = 0.925$ (7.5% blocked)

This steep voltage dependence is what makes the NMDA receptor a coincidence detector.

## ✍️ 4. Derivations & Worked Calculations

<details>
<summary>🔍 Worked Example 05.3.1 — STDP Weight Update for a Spike Pair</summary>

**Problem:** A presynaptic spike occurs at $t_{\text{pre}} = 100$ ms and a postsynaptic spike at $t_{\text{post}} = 110$ ms. Parameters: $A_+ = 0.01$, $A_- = 0.012$, $\tau_+ = 20$ ms, $\tau_- = 20$ ms, initial weight $w = 0.5$. Calculate the new weight.

**Step 1:** Compute timing difference:

$$
\Delta t = t_{\text{post}} - t_{\text{pre}} = 110 - 100 = +10 \text{ ms}
$$

Since $\Delta t \gt  0$ (pre before post), this is the **LTP** case.

**Step 2:** Apply STDP rule:

$$
\Delta w = A_+ \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau_+}\right) = 0.01 \exp\left(-\frac{10}{20}\right) = 0.01 \cdot e^{-0.5}
$$

**Step 3:** Compute:

$$
\Delta w = 0.01 \times 0.6065 = 0.006065
$$

**Step 4:** New weight:

$$
w_{\text{new}} = w + \Delta w = 0.5 + 0.006065 = 0.506065
$$

**Interpretation:** The synapse is strengthened by ~1.2% because the presynaptic neuron fired 10 ms before the postsynaptic neuron — a causal relationship that STDP reinforces.

</details>

<details>
<summary>🔍 Worked Example 05.3.2 — Oja's Rule Convergence to Principal Eigenvector</summary>

**Problem:** Input data has covariance matrix $\mathbf{C} = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$. Starting from $\mathbf{w}_0 = (0.6, 0.8)^T$, perform 3 iterations of Oja's rule with $\eta = 0.1$ using the expected update $\langle \Delta \mathbf{w} \rangle = \eta[\mathbf{C}\mathbf{w} - (\mathbf{w}^T\mathbf{C}\mathbf{w})\mathbf{w}]$.

**Step 1:** Iteration 1. Compute $\mathbf{C}\mathbf{w}_0$:

$$
\mathbf{C}\mathbf{w}_0 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 3 \end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix} 0.6 \\ 0.8 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 2.0 \\ 3.0 \end{pmatrix}
$$

Compute $\mathbf{w}_0^T \mathbf{C} \mathbf{w}_0 = (0.6)(2.0) + (0.8)(3.0) = 1.2 + 2.4 = 3.6$

Update: $\Delta \mathbf{w} = 0.1[(2.0, 3.0)^T - 3.6(0.6, 0.8)^T] = 0.1[(2.0, 3.0)^T - (2.16, 2.88)^T] = 0.1(-0.16, 0.12)^T = (-0.016, 0.012)^T$

$$
\mathbf{w}_1 = (0.584, 0.812)^T, \quad \|\mathbf{w}_1\| = \sqrt{0.341 + 0.659} = 1.0
$$

**Step 2:** The true eigenvectors of $\mathbf{C}$ are found from $\det(\mathbf{C} - \lambda \mathbf{I}) = 0$:

$$
(2-\lambda)(3-\lambda) - 1 = \lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 5 = 0 \implies \lambda = \frac{5 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}
$$

$\lambda_1 = 3.618$ (principal), $\lambda_2 = 1.382$.

Principal eigenvector: $(\mathbf{C} - 3.618\mathbf{I})\mathbf{v} = 0 \implies \mathbf{v}_1 \propto (0.526, 0.851)^T$ (normalized).

**Step 3:** After many iterations, $\mathbf{w} \rightarrow (0.526, 0.851)^T$ — Oja's rule extracts the principal component. The weight vector is already rotating toward this direction.

</details>

<details>
<summary>🔍 Worked Example 05.3.3 — BCM Threshold Dynamics</summary>

**Problem:** A neuron receives input that produces postsynaptic activity $y = 5$ Hz. The current BCM threshold is $\theta_M = 3$ Hz. The time-averaged activity is $\langle y^2 \rangle = 20$ Hz². With $\tau_\theta = 1000$ ms, compute: (a) whether this synapse undergoes LTP or LTD, (b) the rate of threshold change.

**Step 1:** BCM modification function:

$$
\phi(y, \theta_M) = y(y - \theta_M) = 5(5 - 3) = 5 \times 2 = 10 \gt  0
$$

Since $\phi \gt  0$, this synapse undergoes **LTP**. ✓

**Step 2:** Threshold dynamics:

$$
\frac{d\theta_M}{dt} = \frac{-\theta_M + \langle y^2 \rangle}{\tau_\theta} = \frac{-3 + 20}{1000} = \frac{17}{1000} = 0.017 \text{ Hz/ms}
$$

The threshold is **increasing** because the neuron is highly active. Eventually $\theta_M$ will rise above $y = 5$, converting this synapse from LTP to LTD — homeostatic regulation.

**Step 3:** Equilibrium threshold: $\theta_M^* = \langle y^2 \rangle = 20$ Hz². At this point, only inputs driving $y \gt  \sqrt{20} \approx 4.47$ Hz will undergo LTP — creating selectivity.

</details>

<details>
<summary>🔍 Worked Example 05.3.4 — NMDA Mg²⁺ Block at Different Voltages</summary>

**Problem:** Calculate the fraction of NMDA current available (Mg²⁺ unblock) at membrane potentials of −70, −40, −20, and 0 mV. Use $[\text{Mg}^{2+}]_o = 1.0$ mM.

**Step 1:** Apply the Jahr-Stevens equation at each voltage:

$$
B(V) = \frac{1}{1 + \frac{1.0}{3.57} \exp(-0.062 V)} = \frac{1}{1 + 0.280 \exp(-0.062 V)}
$$

**Step 2:** Calculate:

At $V = -70$ mV:

$$
B(-70) = \frac{1}{1 + 0.280 \cdot e^{4.34}} = \frac{1}{1 + 0.280 \times 76.7} = \frac{1}{1 + 21.5} = 0.044
$$

At $V = -40$ mV:

$$
B(-40) = \frac{1}{1 + 0.280 \cdot e^{2.48}} = \frac{1}{1 + 0.280 \times 11.9} = \frac{1}{1 + 3.34} = 0.230
$$

At $V = -20$ mV:

$$
B(-20) = \frac{1}{1 + 0.280 \cdot e^{1.24}} = \frac{1}{1 + 0.280 \times 3.46} = \frac{1}{1 + 0.968} = 0.508
$$

At $V = 0$ mV:

$$
B(0) = \frac{1}{1 + 0.280 \cdot e^{0}} = \frac{1}{1.280} = 0.781
$$

**Summary table:**

| $V_m$ (mV) | $B(V)$ | % NMDA current available |
|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| −70 | 0.044 | 4.4% |
| −40 | 0.230 | 23.0% |
| −20 | 0.508 | 50.8% |
| 0 | 0.781 | 78.1% |

**Interpretation:** At resting potential (−70 mV), NMDA receptors are 95.6% blocked — essentially non-functional. Significant Ca²⁺ influx only occurs when the postsynaptic membrane is depolarized to at least −40 mV (by AMPA receptor activation or back-propagating APs). This is the biophysical basis of the coincidence detection requirement.

</details>

<details>
<summary>🔍 Worked Example 05.3.5 — Net STDP Effect for Correlated Poisson Spike Trains</summary>

**Problem:** Two neurons fire as Poisson processes at $r_{\text{pre}} = 20$ Hz and $r_{\text{post}} = 30$ Hz with a cross-correlation peak at $\Delta t = +5$ ms (pre tends to fire 5 ms before post). STDP parameters: $A_+ = 0.005$, $A_- = 0.005$, $\tau_+ = \tau_- = 20$ ms. Estimate the net weight change per second.

**Step 1:** For uncorrelated Poisson trains, the net drift is:

$$
\langle \Delta w \rangle_{\text{uncorr}} = r_{\text{pre}} \cdot r_{\text{post}} \cdot (A_+ \tau_+ - A_- \tau_-)
$$

Since $A_+ \tau_+ = A_- \tau_- = 0.1$ ms, the uncorrelated contribution is **zero**.

**Step 2:** The correlated component. With a correlation peak at $\Delta t_0 = +5$ ms and correlation strength $c = 0.3$ (30% of post spikes are causally driven by pre spikes):

$$
\langle \Delta w \rangle_{\text{corr}} = c \cdot r_{\text{pre}} \cdot A_+ \exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t_0}{\tau_+}\right)
$$

$$
= 0.3 \times 20 \times 0.005 \times \exp(-5/20) = 0.03 \times 0.7788 = 0.0234 \text{ per second}
$$

**Step 3:** Total weight change per second:

$$
\frac{dw}{dt} \approx 0 + 0.0234 = 0.0234 \text{ s}^{-1}
$$

**Interpretation:** The synapse strengthens at ~2.3% per second due to the causal correlation. After ~43 seconds of correlated activity, the weight would double (absent saturation bounds). This demonstrates how STDP selectively strengthens causally predictive connections.

</details>

## 🤖 5. AI/ML Translation

### 5.1 — Hebbian Learning vs. Backpropagation: The Fundamental Divide

| Feature | Biological (Hebbian/STDP) | Artificial (Backpropagation) |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Locality** | Purely local: only pre/post activity needed | Global: requires error signal from output layer |
| **Credit assignment** | Temporal (STDP timing) | Spatial (chain rule through layers) |
| **Update rule** | $\Delta w \propto f(x_{\text{pre}}, x_{\text{post}}, \Delta t)$ | $\Delta w = -\eta \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial w}$ |
| **Supervision** | Unsupervised (self-organizing) | Supervised (requires labels) |
| **Symmetry** | Asymmetric (no weight transport) | Requires symmetric feedback weights |
| **Biological plausibility** | ✓ | ✗ (weight transport problem) |
| **Scalability** | Proven (16B neurons) | Proven (trillions of parameters) |

### 5.2 — The Weight Transport Problem

Backpropagation requires that feedback weights equal the transpose of forward weights ($W^T$). This is biologically implausible because:
1. Synapses are unidirectional
2. No known mechanism copies weights between forward and backward pathways
3. Feedback connections in cortex have different laminar patterns than feedforward

**Proposed biological solutions:**
- **Feedback alignment** (Lillicrap et al., 2016): Random fixed feedback weights still enable learning
- **Predictive coding** (Rao & Ballard, 1999): Each layer predicts the layer below; prediction errors propagate locally
- **Equilibrium propagation** (Scellier & Bengio, 2017): Contrastive Hebbian learning in energy-based models

### 5.3 — STDP in Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs)

Modern neuromorphic hardware (Intel Loihi, IBM TrueNorth) implements STDP directly:
- **Temporal coding:** Information in spike timing, not rate
- **Event-driven computation:** Energy-efficient (only compute on spikes)
- **Online learning:** No separate training/inference phases

STDP naturally performs:
- **Temporal sequence learning:** Strengthens connections that predict future spikes
- **Input correlation detection:** Equivalent to online PCA (via Oja's rule connection)
- **Competitive learning:** Winner-take-all dynamics emerge from lateral inhibition + STDP

### 5.4 — Metaplasticity → Learning Rate Scheduling

The BCM sliding threshold has a direct AI analog:

| BCM Mechanism | AI/ML Equivalent |
|:---|:---|
| Sliding threshold $\theta_M$ | Adaptive learning rate (Adam optimizer) |
| $\theta_M$ increases with activity | Learning rate decay with training progress |
| Low $\theta_M$ (high plasticity) | Warm-up phase / high initial learning rate |
| High $\theta_M$ (stability) | Fine-tuning with low learning rate |
| 5-HT2A lowering $\theta_M$ | Learning rate restart (cosine annealing with warm restarts) |

### 5.5 — What AI Currently Ignores

1. **Dendritic compartmentalization:** Different synapses on the same neuron can undergo independent plasticity based on local dendritic voltage. ANNs treat all inputs to a neuron identically.
2. **Neuromodulatory gating:** Dopamine, acetylcholine, and serotonin gate whether plasticity occurs at all (three-factor learning rules). Standard backprop has no equivalent of "this experience should/shouldn't be learned."
3. **Structural plasticity:** Biology creates and eliminates entire synapses. ANNs only modify existing weight values (though pruning/growing architectures are emerging).
4. **Sleep consolidation:** Offline replay and synaptic downscaling during sleep. No standard ANN equivalent (though experience replay in RL is analogous).

## 🧬 6. Personal Context

### Critical Period Reopening via 5-HT2A Agonism

The BCM framework provides a quantitative model for how psychoplastogens may reopen critical-period plasticity:

1. **Baseline state (adult cortex):** High $\theta_M$ due to years of activity-dependent threshold elevation. Existing neural circuits are stable but resistant to reorganization.

2. **5-HT2A activation:** Triggers intracellular cascades that:
   - Increase BDNF/TrkB signaling → promotes dendritic spine growth (structural plasticity)
   - Shift NMDA receptor subunit composition toward NR2B → longer Ca²⁺ transients → effectively lowers $\theta_M$
   - Reduce perineuronal net (PNN) density → removes physical barriers to synaptic remodeling

3. **Plasticity window (hours to days post-administration):**
   - $\theta_M$ is temporarily lowered → previously sub-threshold inputs can now induce LTP
   - New dendritic spines form (Ly et al., 2018: +10% spine density in 24h)
   - Existing maladaptive circuits become modifiable

4. **Consolidation:** New learning during this window is consolidated via standard L-LTP mechanisms (CREB, protein synthesis). The therapeutic implication: pairing the plasticity window with targeted experience (therapy, skill training) can restructure trauma-adapted circuits.

### Bilateral Processing and Hebbian Learning

In subjects with enhanced bilateral processing, the corpus callosum provides additional pathways for Hebbian association between hemispheres. Callosal synapses undergo the same STDP rules, meaning:
- Temporally correlated bilateral activity strengthens interhemispheric connections
- The ~10–15 ms interhemispheric transfer time falls within the STDP window ($\tau_+ = 20$ ms)
- This predicts that bilateral processors develop stronger callosal synapses through activity-dependent STDP during development
