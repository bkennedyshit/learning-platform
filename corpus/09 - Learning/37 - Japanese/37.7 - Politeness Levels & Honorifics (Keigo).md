# 37.7 — Politeness Levels & Honorifics (Keigo)

> *"In English, politeness is about word choice. In Japanese, politeness is baked into the grammar itself — and using the wrong level is like wearing a tuxedo to a beach or jeans to a wedding."*

Keigo (敬語) is the Japanese system of formal and respectful speech. It operates on three levels that encode social hierarchy directly into verb forms, vocabulary, and honorific prefixes. Without teineigo (the basic polite level), you cannot function in most Japanese contexts outside of close friendships. This chapter teaches all three levels and — critically — when to use each.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Use **teineigo** (丁寧語 — polite language) consistently as your default register.
2. Recognize and produce key **sonkeigo** (尊敬語 — respectful language) forms for the listener's actions.
3. Recognize and produce key **kenjougo** (謙譲語 — humble language) forms for your own actions.
4. Choose the appropriate level for: friends, colleagues, teachers, customers, and strangers.
5. Use common honorific vocabulary (special words that replace standard words in formal contexts).

---

## 📚 1. The Three Levels of Keigo

| Level | Japanese | Purpose | Who uses it for whom |
|-------|---------|---------|---------------------|
| **Teineigo** | 丁寧語 | Polite, neutral formal speech | You, to strangers, colleagues, anyone you're not close to |
| **Sonkeigo** | 尊敬語 | Elevates the listener's actions | You speak ABOUT or TO the respected person's actions |
| **Kenjougo** | 謙譲語 | Humbles your own actions (lowers yourself) | You speak about YOUR OWN actions toward a respected person |

> **The key insight:** Keigo is about the **direction of the action**, not just the politeness level. Sonkeigo lifts the *other person* up. Kenjougo brings *yourself* down. Using sonkeigo for your own actions or kenjougo for someone else's is a significant error.

---

## 📚 2. Teineigo — Your Default Register

Teineigo = ます/です form speech. Everything covered in chapters 18.4–37.6 is teineigo.

**Core features:**
- Verbs in ます-form: *食べます, 飲みます, 行きます*
- Copula as です: *学生です, きれいです*
- Polite question marker か: *どこへ行きますか？*

**When to use teineigo:**
- With strangers
- In shops, restaurants, public services
- With teachers, professors, bosses (as a baseline)
- In formal writing (business emails, reports)
- Default for all adult interactions until explicitly shifted to casual

**When to shift to plain form (casual):**
- With close friends and family
- With people explicitly younger who you know well
- In your own diary / internal monologue

## 📚 3. Sonkeigo — Respectful Language (for the other person)

Sonkeigo is used when describing actions performed by someone of higher status — your teacher, customer, boss, or anyone you want to show deference toward.

### Special sonkeigo verb forms

| Standard verb | Sonkeigo equivalent | Meaning |
|--------------|---------------------|---------|
| いる (to be/exist) | いらっしゃる | (respected person) is/exists |
| 行く (to go) | いらっしゃる | (respected person) goes |
| 来る (to come) | いらっしゃる / おいでになる | (respected person) comes |
| 言う (to say) | おっしゃる | (respected person) says |
| する (to do) | なさる | (respected person) does |
| 食べる (to eat) | 召し上がる | (respected person) eats |
| 飲む (to drink) | 召し上がる | (respected person) drinks |
| もらう (to receive) | お受け取りになる | (respected person) receives |
| 知っている (to know) | ご存知 (ごぞんじ) | (respected person) knows |
| 見る (to see) | ご覧になる | (respected person) sees |
| くれる (to give [to me]) | くださる | (respected person) gives [to me] |
| 読む (to read) | お読みになる | (respected person) reads |

### The お/ご + verb stem + になる pattern

When no special sonkeigo verb exists, use: **お + verb-stem + になる**

*お読みになる* (to read — respectful)
*お帰りになる* (to return home — respectful)
*お使いになる* (to use — respectful)

> **お vs. ご:** お is used with native Japanese words; ご is used with Chinese-origin (Sino-Japanese) words. *お名前* (onamae = name), *ご住所* (gojuusho = address).

### Example sentences (sonkeigo)

*先生はもうお帰りになりましたか？*
(Has the teacher already gone home?)

*社長はこの報告書をお読みになりましたか？*
(Has the president read this report?)

*田中様はいらっしゃいますか？*
(Is Mr. Tanaka here? — standard telephone/reception usage)

## 📚 4. Kenjougo — Humble Language (for your own actions)

Kenjougo is used when you describe your own actions (or your in-group's actions) directed toward someone of higher status. You lower yourself to raise them up.

### Special kenjougo verb forms

| Standard verb | Kenjougo equivalent | Meaning |
|--------------|---------------------|---------|
| いる (to be/exist) | おる | I/we am/is (humble) |
| 行く (to go) | まいる / うかがう | I go (humble) |
| 来る (to come) | まいる / うかがう | I come (humble) |
| 言う (to say) | もうす / もうしあげる | I say (humble) |
| する (to do) | いたす | I do (humble) |
| 食べる/飲む (eat/drink) | いただく | I eat/drink/receive (humble) |
| もらう (to receive) | いただく | I receive (humble) |
| あげる (to give [to others]) | さしあげる | I give (humble) |
| 見る (to see) | はいけんする | I see (humble) |
| 知っている (to know) | ぞんじております | I know (humble) |
| 訪ねる/行く (visit) | うかがう | I visit/go (humble) |
| 読む (to read) | はいどく (less common) / 読ませていただく | I read (humble) |

### The お/ご + verb stem + する pattern

**お + verb-stem + する/いたす** — humble form of your own actions:

*お持ちします。* (I will carry/hold [it for you].)
*ご説明いたします。* (I will explain.)
*お電話いたします。* (I will call you.)

### Example sentences (kenjougo)

*私が参ります。*
(I will go / I'll come — humble)

*明日、うかがいます。*
(I will visit [you] tomorrow. — humble)

*はい、田中と申します。*
(Yes, my name is Tanaka. — humble self-introduction)

*いただきます。*
(I humbly receive — said before eating meals. Kenjougo of eating.)

## 📚 5. Honorific Prefixes — お and ご

Attaching お or ご to nouns makes them more polite/respectful:

| Plain | Honorific | Notes |
|-------|----------|-------|
| 名前 (namae) | お名前 (onamae) | name |
| 金 (kane) | お金 (okane) | money — so ubiquitous the お is considered standard |
| 茶 (cha) | お茶 (ocha) | tea |
| 水 (mizu) | お水 (omizu) | water |
| 時間 (jikan) | お時間 (ojikan) | time (your time) |
| 住所 (juusho) | ご住所 (gojuusho) | address |
| 家族 (kazoku) | ご家族 (gokazoku) | (your) family |
| 連絡 (renraku) | ご連絡 (gorenraku) | contact/communication |
| 説明 (setsumei) | ご説明 (gosetsumei) | explanation |
| 利用 (riyou) | ご利用 (goriyou) | use/usage |

> Some words have become so fixed with お that the plain form sounds rude: *お金, お茶, お水, おトイレ* — always use the お version in polite speech.

## 📚 6. When to Use Which Level — Social Context Map

| Context | Level | Notes |
|---------|-------|-------|
| Close friends, family | Plain form | Teineigo with close friends sounds distant |
| Classmates (similar age) | Teineigo or plain | Start with teineigo; shift when appropriate |
| Teacher / professor | Teineigo + some sonkeigo for their actions | *先生がおっしゃるとおり…* |
| Boss / supervisor | Teineigo + kenjougo for your actions | Business standard |
| Customer service (you as service provider) | Full keigo (teineigo + both sonkeigo & kenjougo) | Japanese customer service is famous for this |
| Strangers | Teineigo | Safe default always |
| Written business communication | Full formal keigo | See standard opening/closing phrases below |

## 📚 7. Business Email Phrases (Standard Openings/Closings)

Japanese business emails follow rigid formulas:

**Opening:**
*いつもお世話になっております。* (Thank you for your continued support / I'm always in your debt — standard business opener, even when you've never met)

**Closing:**
*よろしくお願いいたします。* (I humbly request your favorable consideration — the universal closer)
*どうぞよろしくお願い申し上げます。* (Very formal version)

**Self-introduction:**
*〇〇会社の田中と申します。* (I am Tanaka from [Company] — kenjougo)

## ⚠️ 8. Common Errors

| Error | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Using sonkeigo for your own actions | *私がおっしゃいます* ❌ — おっしゃる is for the other person's speech, not your own. Use もうします. |
| Using kenjougo for the other person | *先生がまいります* ❌ — まいる is humble (your own movement). The teacher would いらっしゃる. |
| いただきます as only before food | It also means "I humbly receive" in business: *拝受いたします* or *いただきます* for receiving a business card, document, etc. |
| Skipping keigo entirely with strangers | Plain form with a stranger or authority figure is rude and jarring. Always default to teineigo. |
