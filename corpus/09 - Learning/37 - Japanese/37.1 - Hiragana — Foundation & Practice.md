# 37.1 — Hiragana: Foundation & Practice

> *"Hiragana is not a step toward reading Japanese. It IS reading Japanese. Every other skill builds on top of it."*

Hiragana is the first writing system. It is a syllabary — each character represents a syllable (consonant + vowel, or vowel alone), not a single sound or a meaning. Japanese children learn it before kanji. You must too. The goal of this chapter is not "familiar with hiragana" — it is **automatic recognition at reading speed**. Nothing in this track proceeds without that.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Read and write all 46 base hiragana characters with 100% accuracy.
2. Read and produce the voiced variants (dakuten) and semi-voiced variants (handakuten).
3. Read and produce youon (combined characters with small や, ゆ, よ).
4. Read the long vowel mark (ー in katakana; doubled vowel in hiragana).
5. Read the small っ (double consonant marker).
6. Recognize hiragana at natural reading speed (≤1 second per character, target: fluent).

## 📚 1. The Hiragana System — Overview

Hiragana has **46 base characters** arranged in a grid called the **gojūon** (50-sound table). Each character = one mora (a unit of sound). The grid is organized by vowel column (a, i, u, e, o) and consonant row.

### The Gojūon Table

| | a | i | u | e | o |
|--|---|---|---|---|---|
| **∅** | あ | い | う | え | お |
| **k** | か | き | く | け | こ |
| **s** | さ | し | す | せ | そ |
| **t** | た | ち | つ | て | と |
| **n** | な | に | ぬ | ね | の |
| **h** | は | ひ | ふ | へ | ほ |
| **m** | ま | み | む | め | も |
| **y** | や | ー | ゆ | ー | よ |
| **r** | ら | り | る | れ | ろ |
| **w** | わ | ー | ー | ー | を |
| **n** | ん | | | | |

### Romaji Pronunciation Notes

| Hiragana | Romaji | Pronunciation trap for English speakers |
|---------|--------|----------------------------------------|
| し | shi | Not "si" — "sh" sound |
| ち | chi | Not "ti" — "ch" sound |
| つ | tsu | The "ts" cluster — practice "ca**ts**" then isolate "tsu" |
| ふ | fu | Bilabial "f" — lips together, not teeth-to-lip like English f |
| じ | ji | From the z-row with dakuten |
| ず | zu | "z" sound |
| ん | n | Syllabic n — a full mora, not attached to next syllable |

## 📚 2. Dakuten ( ゛) — Voiced Variants

Adding the two-stroke mark ( ゛) to certain base characters makes them voiced. This adds 20 characters:

| Base | + Dakuten | Sound |
|------|----------|-------|
| か か き く け こ | が ぎ ぐ げ ご | k → g |
| さ し す せ そ | ざ じ ず ぜ ぞ | s → z |
| た ち つ て と | だ ぢ づ で ど | t → d |
| は ひ ふ へ ほ | ば び ぶ べ ぼ | h → b |

> **Note:** ぢ (di) and づ (du) exist but are rare — じ (ji) and ず (zu) are used in most cases. When you see ぢ or づ, they sound identical to じ and ず in modern standard Japanese.

---

## 📚 3. Handakuten ( ゜) — Semi-Voiced Variants

The small circle mark ( ゜) applies only to the h-row, converting h → p:

| Base | + Handakuten | Sound |
|------|-------------|-------|
| は ひ ふ へ ほ | ぱ ぴ ぷ ぺ ぽ | h → p |

---

## 📚 4. Youon — Combined Characters

Small や (ゃ), ゆ (ゅ), よ (ょ) combine with i-column characters to form single-mora glides. The small kana is written smaller and does NOT represent a separate syllable.

| Base (i-col) | + ゃ | + ゅ | + ょ |
|-------------|------|------|------|
| き (ki) | きゃ kya | きゅ kyu | きょ kyo |
| し (shi) | しゃ sha | しゅ shu | しょ sho |
| ち (chi) | ちゃ cha | ちゅ chu | ちょ cho |
| に (ni) | にゃ nya | にゅ nyu | にょ nyo |
| ひ (hi) | ひゃ hya | ひゅ hyu | ひょ hyo |
| み (mi) | みゃ mya | みゅ myu | みょ myo |
| り (ri) | りゃ rya | りゅ ryu | りょ ryo |
| ぎ (gi) | ぎゃ gya | ぎゅ gyu | ぎょ gyo |
| じ (ji) | じゃ ja | じゅ ju | じょ jo |
| び (bi) | びゃ bya | びゅ byu | びょ byo |
| ぴ (pi) | ぴゃ pya | ぴゅ pyu | ぴょ pyo |

## 📚 5. Special Characters

### Small っ — Double Consonant (Geminate)
The small っ indicates that the following consonant is doubled — a brief pause/stop before the next consonant. It is one full mora of silence/tension.

*きって* (kitte) = stamp — the t is held: "kit-te"
*ざっし* (zasshi) = magazine — "zas-shi"
*いっぱい* (ippai) = full / a lot — "ip-pai"

> **English comparison:** Think of the difference between "a name" and "an name" — the doubled consonant adds a mora of duration. In English it's uncommon; in Japanese it changes meaning: *来て* (kite = come!) vs. *切手* (kitte = stamp).

### Long Vowels in Hiragana
Long vowels are written by adding the vowel again (or お + う for the o-sound):

| Long vowel | How written | Example |
|-----------|------------|---------|
| long a (ā) | ああ or あ + あ | おかあさん (okaasan = mother) |
| long i (ī) | ii | おにいさん (oniisan = older brother) |
| long u (ū) | uu | くうき (kuuki = air) |
| long e (ē) | ee or ei | おねえさん (oneesan = older sister) |
| long o (ō) | oo or ou | おとうさん (otousan = father), とおい (tooi = far) |

> The long o is often written with う (ou) — *そうです* (sou desu = that's right). The う is not pronounced "u" here — it lengthens the o.

## 🛠️ 6. Learning Strategy — How to Reach 100%

### The Tofugu Method (recommended)
[Tofugu's hiragana guide](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/learn-hiragana/) uses **mnemonics** for each character — visual associations between the character's shape and a sound. Example: あ looks like an angry person saying "Ahh!" Use their mnemonics until recognition is automatic, then let them fall away.

### Drill Sequence
1. **Learn in rows** — master vowel row (あいうえお) → k-row → s-row → etc. Don't try all 46 at once.
2. **Write by hand** — tracing the stroke order builds motor memory. Use a notebook or an app (Kanji Study, Obenkyo).
3. **Anki flashcards** — hiragana on front, romaji on back. Drill until 100%.
4. **Reading practice** — read actual Japanese words written in hiragana (no kanji) as soon as you know 2 rows.

### Practice Script
Run `18.1_hiragana_drill.py --mode recognition --rows all --count 30` to generate randomized identification exercises.

### Benchmark
You are done with this chapter when you can:
- Read any hiragana sequence at natural pace (≤0.5 sec per character)
- Write any hiragana character from memory (stroke order correct)
- Recognize all dakuten, handakuten, and youon instantly
- Read the small っ and know it marks a double consonant

**Typical timeline:** 1–2 weeks of 30 min/day practice.

## 📚 7. Hiragana Full Reference — With Stroke Order Notes

### Vowels (あ行)
- **あ** (a) — 3 strokes. Looks like an "a" crossed with a Japanese brush stroke.
- **い** (i) — 2 strokes. Two vertical lines, like the number 11.
- **う** (u) — 2 strokes. Like a small "u" with a hook.
- **え** (e) — 2 strokes. Cross-like; upper part is a horizontal stroke.
- **お** (o) — 3 strokes. The most complex vowel; practice carefully.

### K-row (か行) through W-row (わ行)
Stroke orders available at [Tofugu Hiragana Guide](https://www.tofugu.com/japanese/learn-hiragana/) and in the Kanji Study app.

> Full stroke order diagrams are better learned from a visual reference than a text file. Use the Tofugu guide or the `_practice/` writing sheets generated by `18.1_hiragana_drill.py --mode writing`.

## ⚠️ 8. Common Errors

| Error | Notes |
|-------|-------|
| Confusing は and ほ | は has a longer top arm; ほ has a loop. Drill both until instant. |
| Confusing ぬ and め | ぬ has a "2" loop; め has a fuller loop. |
| Confusing わ and ね and れ | All three share similar top structures — focus on the bottom half differences. |
| Confusing り and い | り has a connecting hook at the bottom; い has two separate strokes. |
| Reading small っ as full つ | Small っ = double consonant marker; full-size つ = "tsu" sound. Context and size distinguish them. |
| Ignoring long vowels | おかあさん is 5 morae: o-ka-a-sa-n. Missing the long vowel changes the word. |
