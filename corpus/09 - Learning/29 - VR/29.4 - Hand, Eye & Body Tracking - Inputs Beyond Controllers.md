# 29.4 — Hand, Eye & Body Tracking: Inputs Beyond Controllers

> *"Controllers were the training wheels of VR. In 2026, hands + eyes are the steering wheel."*

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

1. Use OpenXR **hand-tracking** extensions (`XR_EXT_hand_tracking`) to read 26 joint poses per hand.
2. Implement **pinch**, **grab**, **point**, **swipe**, and **gesture-recognition** with hand input.
3. Use OpenXR **eye-tracking** for foveated rendering, gaze-based selection, attention analytics.
4. Use OpenXR **body-tracking** + IMU-based IK to drive full-body avatars.
5. Recognize the trade-offs: **vision-based** (Quest 3 cameras) vs **dedicated sensor** (Vive Tracker / IMU pucks) tracking.
6. Pipe modern multimodal **VLM** scene understanding into VR interaction.

## 📚 1. Hand Tracking — 26 Joints

Standard hand model (per OpenXR `XR_EXT_hand_tracking`):
- Wrist, palm
- 5 fingers × (metacarpal, proximal, intermediate, distal, tip)
- Plus a few helper joints

Each joint has pose (position + orientation) + radius + tracking confidence.

### Common gestures
- **Pinch** — thumb tip + index tip distance.
- **Grab** — average finger tip distance to palm.
- **Point** — index extended, others curled.
- **Thumbs-up / OK / etc.** — geometric heuristics or learned classifiers.

> Source: [Meta OpenXR SDK samples](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/native/native-openxr-sdk-sample/) — content rephrased for compliance.

### Latency
Modern Quest 3 + Vision Pro keep end-to-end hand-tracking latency **under 20 ms** — fast enough to feel "your hands," not "delayed proxies."

## 👀 2. Eye Tracking

Two big use cases:

### Foveated rendering
- Reduce shader / pixel cost outside foveal region by 2–4×.
- Game-changer for high-resolution headsets (Vision Pro).

### Gaze interaction
- "**Look + pinch**" is the Vision Pro-defining gesture.
- Cursor follows gaze; pinch-to-select with hand.

### Analytics
- Heatmaps of where users look in a scene — invaluable for arch-viz client review and education research.

---

## 🧍 3. Body Tracking

| Approach | Hardware | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Inside-out camera body tracking | Quest 3 | Approx. upper body + legs (improving) |
| IMU pucks | Vive Tracker, HaritoraX | Hip + feet + chest accuracy |
| Optical mocap | OptiTrack, Vicon | Studio-grade |
| Multi-camera + ML (e.g., Mediapipe) | RGB cameras | Cheap, lower fidelity |

For arch-viz multi-user, **upper-body IK** from headset + 2 controllers + optional waist IMU is usually sufficient.

---

## 🤖 4. IK for Avatars

Common stack:
- **Final IK** (Unity), **Control Rig** (Unreal), **VRIK** — drive avatar from headset + hand poses.
- Add foot-IK on terrain or floor.
- Constraint hand to controller / hand-tracking pose.

Avatar IK fidelity is the difference between "people are present" and "creepy floating heads."

## ⚠️ 7. Common Misconceptions

- **"Hand tracking is unreliable."** It was; in 2026 it's production quality on Quest 3 / Vision Pro for most non-fast tasks.
- **"Eye tracking is creepy."** Local, on-device gaze data is now standard; just respect privacy + don't transmit.
- **"Body tracking needs trackers."** Vision-only body tracking is closing the quality gap each year.
- **"Skip controllers for everything."** For fast / precision tasks (FPS, sim, art) controllers still beat hands.
