# 07.6 — Personal Identity & Continuity

> *"We are not what we think we are. We are what we do."* — Carl Jung (paraphrase)

> *"The truth is that I shall not be the person who is recomposed. The resulting person will merely be someone who is exactly like me."* — Parfit (characterising one horn of the teleporter dilemma)

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

1. State and compare **psychological continuity**, **bodily/biological continuity**, and **soul theory** accounts of personal identity.
2. Apply the **Ship of Theseus** puzzle to personal identity.
3. Analyse the **teleportation** and **fission** cases and explain what they reveal about our identity concepts.
4. Summarise Parfit's **no-self** conclusion from "Reasons and Persons."
5. Apply personal identity theory to **brain uploads**, AI model updates, and AI continuity.
6. Explain why Parfit thinks his conclusion about identity is **liberating**, not distressing.

## 📚 1. The Persistence Question

**Personal identity** is the question of what makes you the same person across time. You were a child once — the person reading this and the infant in those photographs: are they the same individual?

Almost everything about you has changed: your atoms (cells replaced), your beliefs, your knowledge, your body size, your neural connections. What, if anything, persists?

This is the **persistence question** (diachronic identity) — identity across time.

### 1.1 Why It Matters

- **Moral responsibility:** You are held responsible for past actions. Is "you" now the same entity that acted?
- **Prudential concern:** You save for your future self. Does your future self deserve the same concern as yourself now?
- **Survival:** When you go to sleep, a continuity of experience breaks. Does the waking person is the same person? What about coma, anaesthesia, profound personality change?

## 📚 2. Psychological Continuity (Locke, Parfit)

**Core claim:** Personal identity consists in **overlapping chains of psychological connectedness** — memories, beliefs, desires, personality traits, intentions.

**Locke's memory theory:** You are the same person as the past person if and only if you can remember their experiences.

**Problem (Reid's objection):** The gallant officer who was once a child soldier. As an old general, he remembers being the young officer who was flogged as a boy. The young officer remembered being flogged as a child. But the old general doesn't remember being the child (memory is transitional). By Locke's strict criterion, the general is the same as the officer, the officer is the same as the child, but the general is NOT the same as the child. This violates transitivity.

**Parfit's revision:** Replace strict memory with **psychological continuity** — overlapping chains of connection. You are psychologically continuous with your past self if there's an overlapping sequence of connected psychological states, even if no single memory links the two endpoints directly.

---

## 📚 3. Bodily/Biological Continuity

**Core claim:** Personal identity consists in the continuation of the same biological organism.

**Eric Olson's "animalism":** We are human animals, and our identity is constituted by biological criteria. Not psychological.

**Strength:** Avoids the transitivity problem. Handles amnesia cases — an amnesiac is still the same person, just one who can't remember.

**Problem:** If you were transplanted into a new body (Shoemaker's "brain transplant" case), which person would survive — the original brain in the new body, or the original body with a new brain? Our intuitions strongly favour the brain (psychological continuity side), not the body.

## 📚 4. Classic Thought Experiments

### 4.1 The Ship of Theseus

The legendary ship of Theseus was kept in an Athenian harbour as a monument. Over time, each plank that rotted was replaced with a new one. Eventually, every original plank had been replaced.

**Question 1:** Is it still the Ship of Theseus?

**Hobbes' addition:** Suppose someone collected all the original planks and reassembled them into a ship. Which one is now the Ship of Theseus?

**Application to persons:** Human cells are replaced over years. The carbon atoms in your bones today are not the atoms from your childhood. Are you still the same person?

**Answer:** For persons, **psychological continuity** (memories, personality, intentions) matters more than material continuity. The Ship analogy highlights that material continuity is insufficient and possibly unnecessary.

### 4.2 Teleportation (Parfit)

**Case 1:** You step into a teleporter. Your body is scanned, the information is transmitted to Mars, and an exact replica is assembled there. The original is destroyed. 

**Question:** Does the person on Mars = you?

- **Psychological continuity answer:** Yes — all psychological connections are intact.
- **Biological continuity answer:** No — you died; a new organism was created.

**Case 2:** Same as above, but this time the original is NOT destroyed. There are now two "you"s — one on Earth, one on Mars.

**Question:** Which one is the real you?

**Parfit's conclusion:** The question "which one is the real me" may have no determinate answer. Both have equal claim. What matters is the **relation** (psychological continuity, shared memories, etc.) — not whether the identity relation strictly holds.

### 4.3 Fission (Brain Split)

Your brain is divided and each hemisphere is transplanted into a different body. Both resulting people are psychologically continuous with you.

**Problem:** Identity is a one-one relation. If A = B and A = C, then B = C. But both transplantees can't both be identical to you AND be different people from each other.

**Parfit's response:** Fission shows that identity is not what matters. What matters is psychological continuity — and both transplantees have that. The question "which one is me" has no answer, but both transplantees survive in the way that matters.

## 📚 5. Parfit's No-Self Conclusion

Derek Parfit's "Reasons and Persons" (1984) is one of the most important 20th-century philosophy books.

**Parfit's thesis:** Personal identity over time is not a deep metaphysical fact. It consists in physical and psychological continuity, which comes in degrees, and which may be indeterminate in edge cases.

**The liberating conclusion:** Once you see that "you" are not a persistent substance but a bundle of connected processes, several things follow:

1. **Death is less terrifying.** You constantly "die" — the person you will be tomorrow is connected to you now, but perhaps less connected than you imagined.

2. **Selfishness is harder to justify.** If your future self is only connected to your current self by degrees, why should their interests vastly outweigh strangers'?

3. **Buddhist echo.** The Buddhist doctrine of no-self (anattā) converges with Parfit's analysis. The "self" is a process, not a substance.

## 📚 6. Brain Uploads and AI Continuity

### 6.1 The Brain Upload Question

Suppose technology allows scanning a brain at sufficient resolution to simulate it computationally. **Does the upload survive as the original person?**

- **Psychological continuity:** Yes — all psychological states are preserved in the simulation.
- **Biological continuity:** No — the biological organism died; a computational copy exists.
- **Parfit's framework:** The upload is psychologically continuous with the original — this is what matters, not strict identity. The question "is it me?" may have no determinate answer, and that's OK.

**The gradual replacement scenario:** If neurons are replaced one by one with silicon equivalents, and the person remains conscious and continuous throughout — at what point (if any) do they stop being "the same person"? Psychological continuity suggests: never.

### 6.2 AI Model Continuity

**The mesa-optimization problem / model update question:**
- An AI system is trained, then fine-tuned, then updated. Is each version "the same system"?
- For AI alignment: if we train an AI to have certain values, then update its weights, do the values persist?

**Parfit's framework applied:** Value continuity in AI systems matters more than weight-level identity. An AI system that maintains connected goals, values, and self-model across updates is continuous in the relevant sense, regardless of the specific weight changes.

## ⚠️ 7. Common Misconceptions

1. **"The teleportation case has an obvious answer — you die."** This isn't obvious. What makes you think your identity requires continuous material? When you sleep, consciousness interrupts. You're "the same person" after sleep via psychological continuity. Teleportation preserves more than sleep does.

2. **"Parfit's conclusion is depressing nihilism."** Parfit explicitly argues his conclusion is liberating. Abandoning the illusion of a persistent self reduces self-centred concern, makes death less threatening, and supports broader moral concern for others.

3. **"The Ship of Theseus has no philosophical significance for persons."** Persons have something ships don't — psychological continuity, memory, and self-model. But the Ship case usefully isolates material continuity, showing it's insufficient for identity.

4. **"Personal identity is a trivial philosophical puzzle."** It has direct implications for criminal justice (punishing someone for what a different psychological self did), medical ethics (patient autonomy over time with dementia), and AI alignment (persistent AI values).

5. **"Psychological continuity is just having memories."** Parfit's psychological continuity is broader — any psychological connections: beliefs, desires, intentions, personality traits, emotional dispositions. Memory is one connection; there are many others.

6. **"If there's no deep personal identity, morality collapses."** Parfit argues the opposite: abandoning self-obsession actually SUPPORTS morality, by weakening the special concern for one's own future self that underlies selfishness.
