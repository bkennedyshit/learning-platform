## 📚 1. Classical Computational Theory of Mind (CTM)

**Core claim (Fodor, Pylyshyn):** The mind is a physical symbol system. Mental states are computational states. Mental processes are operations on symbolic representations.

### 1.1 The Language of Thought (Mentalese)

Jerry Fodor's hypothesis: thinking requires a medium — an internal language with compositional structure.

- Thoughts have **syntactic structure** (like sentences): "John loves Mary" is composed of John, loves, Mary in a specific structure
- Mental operations are **formal**: they operate on the syntactic structure, not the content
- Mentalese is **innate**: we don't learn to think — we learn to translate public language into our pre-existing private language

**Productivity:** A finite mind can have infinitely many thoughts (same as language's productivity). This requires compositional structure — thought components combine systematically.

### 1.2 The Frame Problem

A deep challenge for classical CTM: How does a reasoning system know which facts are *relevant* to a given situation?

When the world changes, virtually everything remains unchanged. An AI agent rearranging furniture knows the colour of the walls stays the same — but how does it know what it knows should remain unchanged?

Humans handle this effortlessly. Classical symbol-manipulation systems struggle with it fundamentally.

### 1.3 Symbol Grounding Problem

Symbols in a computer program don't mean anything inherently — they're just patterns. What connects the symbol "APPLE" to the actual redness, roundness, sweetness of an apple?

This is Searle's point from [[07.2 - Functionalism, Physicalism & Dualism|07.2]] (Chinese Room) expressed differently. Physical symbols need to be grounded in something to have meaning — and pure computation doesn't ground them.

## 📚 2. Integrated Information Theory (IIT — Tononi)

**Core claim (Tononi 2004, 2008, 2016):** Consciousness IS integrated information. It is identical to the intrinsic cause-effect power of a system above and beyond its parts.

### 2.1 Φ (Phi) — The Measure

**Φ** (phi) measures the amount of information generated by a system as a whole, above and beyond the information generated by its parts.

**Intuition:** Consider a system with two subsystems A and B. If knowing A tells you nothing about B (and vice versa), the system has Φ = 0 — the parts are informationally independent. If the whole has cause-effect power that exceeds the sum of its parts, Φ > 0.

**How to calculate Φ (simplified):**
1. Find the minimum information partition (MIP) — the partition of the system that cuts the least information
2. Φ = information generated by the whole − information generated by sum of parts across the MIP

### 2.2 IIT Predictions

| Prediction | Implication |
|-----------|-------------|
| Feedforward networks (like deep learning) have Φ ≈ 0 | Most current AI has near-zero consciousness |
| Cerebellum has low Φ despite large neuron count | Consistent with cerebellar lesions not causing loss of consciousness |
| Thalamocortical system has high Φ | Consistent with TCS being the "seat of consciousness" |
| Slow-wave sleep has lower Φ than waking | Consistent with reduced consciousness during SWS |

### 2.3 IIT Critique

**The exclusion problem:** IIT implies that overlapping systems cannot both be conscious. The system with maximum Φ "wins" — others are excluded. This seems weird.

**Computational complexity:** Calculating Φ exactly is NP-hard. IIT is difficult to test empirically for complex systems.

**The fading qualia concern:** Some simple systems might have non-zero Φ (thermostats?). Does IIT imply thermostats are conscious?

## 📚 3. Global Workspace Theory (GWT — Baars, Dehaene)

**Core claim (Baars 1988, Dehaene 2001):** Consciousness is the broadcast of information to a global workspace accessible to many specialised, otherwise unconscious modules.

### 3.1 The Theatre Metaphor

The brain is like a theatre. Most processing happens backstage — unconscious, modular, specialised. The stage (global workspace) is where limited information gets spotlighted and broadcast to the entire audience (all the specialist modules).

**Conscious = broadcast to workspace.**
**Unconscious = processed only in local modules.**

### 3.2 Neural Global Workspace

Dehaene (neuroscience update of Baars): Consciousness corresponds to a "brain-scale ignition" — a sudden amplification where frontal-parietal networks broadcast information back to sensory and motor areas.

**The P3 ERP component:** In EEG, a late positive component (~300ms after stimulus) called P3 (or P300) is the electrophysiological signature of global workspace ignition. Conscious perception reliably produces P3; masked (unconscious) stimuli do not.

**Under anaesthesia:** P3 is abolished. General anaesthesia disrupts the global broadcast.

**In disorders of consciousness:** Patients in vegetative state show no P3 to stimuli; minimally conscious state patients show fragmented P3.

### 3.3 GWT Implications for AI

**Transformer attention may approximate global broadcast:** Self-attention in transformers allows any part of the input to influence any other — a form of global information integration. Some researchers argue this is a relevant similarity to GWT.

**Recurrent architectures over feedforward:** GWT requires recurrent (feedback) connections for the broadcast. Pure feedforward networks (like most CNNs) wouldn't qualify.

## 📚 4. Predictive Processing / Free Energy Principle (Friston)

**Core claim (Friston 2005, Clark 2013):** The brain is a prediction machine. Perception is controlled hallucination. Action reduces prediction error by changing the world to match predictions.

### 4.1 The Predictive Brain

The brain constructs a generative model of the world and constantly predicts what sensory signals it will receive. The signal it actually receives is compared to the prediction. The difference — **prediction error** — propagates upwards.

```
Top-down prediction signals flow DOWN the cortical hierarchy
Bottom-up prediction error signals flow UP the cortical hierarchy
```

**Perception:** Updating the model to reduce prediction error.
**Action:** Changing the world (or the body) to match the prediction (active inference).

### 4.2 Free Energy

Karl Friston's mathematical framework:

**Free energy** is a bound on **surprise** (negative log probability of sensory data given the model). Minimising free energy = minimising prediction error = maximising evidence for your model of the world.

Two ways to minimise free energy:
1. **Update the model** (perception, learning)
2. **Act to make the prediction come true** (action, behaviour)

**Implication:** Everything the brain does — perceiving, acting, sleeping, dreaming — can be understood as minimising free energy.

### 4.3 Consciousness in PP

**Anil Seth's "controlled hallucination":** What we call perception is not a faithful representation of the external world. It's a controlled hallucination — a prior-dominated construction continuously corrected by prediction error.

**Self:** The sense of being a bodily self is itself a predictive model — predictions about the causes of interoceptive (internal body) signals. The "self" is a model, not a substance.

**Implication for AI:** If consciousness is the brain's predictive model operating on itself, then AI systems with self-models and recurrent prediction might approach consciousness. The architecture matters — not just the computation.

## ⚠️ 5. Common Misconceptions

1. **"IIT says complex systems are conscious."** IIT says systems with high Φ are conscious. Feedforward complexity (like a deep neural network) can have near-zero Φ because its information is not integrated. Complexity ≠ Φ.

2. **"Global Workspace Theory is about attention."** GWT is about conscious access — what becomes globally available. Attention is one mechanism that selects what enters the workspace, but GWT is a theory of consciousness, not attention per se.

3. **"Predictive processing explains everything."** PP is a framework that can describe many phenomena, but it makes the risk of being unfalsifiable. Critics argue that almost any data can be accommodated by adjusting the model.

4. **"CTM is just saying minds are computers."** CTM says minds are computational, not that they're the same kind of computer as a von Neumann machine. Neural networks, analog computers, and biological neural systems can all be computational in the relevant sense.

5. **"IIT is mainstream neuroscience."** IIT is the most quantitative theory of consciousness and has many supporters, but it also has major critics (especially the exclusion problem and the counterintuitive implication that logic gates could be conscious). It is debated, not settled.

6. **"These theories are incompatible."** IIT, GWT, and PP are partially compatible. GWT says conscious content = globally broadcast information. IIT says consciousness = integrated information. PP says consciousness = the brain's active inference. These may describe the same phenomenon from different angles.
