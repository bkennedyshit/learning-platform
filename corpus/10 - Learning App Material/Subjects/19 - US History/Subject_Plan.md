# Subject Syllabus: 19 - US History

*Back to [[00 - 09 - Learning Index|Learning Index]] · [[Bill's Vault/05-Knowledge_Foundation/10 - Learning App Material/Grade Band Pipelines/9-12 High School Pipeline|9-12 Pipeline]]*

American history is a contested story — not because the facts are in dispute, but because the interpretation of causes, consequences, and significance is perpetually argued. This curriculum teaches history the way historians practice it: by reading primary sources, evaluating competing interpretations, and constructing arguments supported by evidence. By the end of this track, learners write like historians — not reciting timelines but making claims about why things happened and what they meant.

## 📝 3. Chapter Outline

| # | Chapter | Core skill |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 19.1 | **Colonial America (1491–1754)** | Native American societies and their diversity before contact; Spanish, French, Dutch, British colonization strategies and their differences; development of regional colonial economies (New England, Middle, Southern — and why they differed); origins and growth of African slavery in colonial America; the tension between colonial identity and British imperial control. |
| 19.2 | **Revolution & Early Republic (1754–1800)** | Seven Years' War and the debt crisis that triggered revolution; the ideological foundations of revolution (Locke, republicanism, natural rights); Declaration of Independence as a political argument; Continental Army and the war; Articles of Confederation — why they failed; Constitutional Convention debates (large vs. small states, slavery, separation of powers); Federalist Papers (Hamilton, Madison, Jay); Bill of Rights; Washington and Adams administrations; partisan origins. |
| 19.3 | **Expansion & Reform (1800–1848)** | Jeffersonian democracy and the "Revolution of 1800"; Louisiana Purchase; War of 1812; Market Revolution (transportation, manufacturing, urbanization); Jackson and the "Age of the Common Man" (spoils system, Indian Removal, nullification crisis, Second Bank); reform movements (abolition, women's rights Seneca Falls, temperance); Manifest Destiny and its ideological justifications. |
| 19.4 | **Civil War Era (1844–1877)** | Sectional crisis (Missouri Compromise, Compromise of 1850, Kansas-Nebraska Act, Bleeding Kansas, Dred Scott, John Brown); Lincoln's election and secession; Civil War military turning points (Antietam, Gettysburg, Sherman's March); Emancipation Proclamation — its limits and its meaning; Reconstruction: 13th/14th/15th Amendments, Freedmen's Bureau, radical vs. presidential reconstruction; the dismantling of Reconstruction — compromise of 1877, Black Codes, Redeemers. |
| 19.5 | **Gilded Age & Progressivism (1877–1920)** | Industrial capitalism (railroads, steel, oil — Carnegie, Rockefeller, Morgan); labor movement (strikes, Haymarket, AFL, IWW); immigration (Ellis Island, Chinese Exclusion Act); urbanization and urban poverty; Jim Crow and the nadir of American race relations; Progressive Era reforms (trust-busting, 16th/17th/18th/19th Amendments, Pure Food and Drug Act); US imperialism (Spanish-American War, Philippines, Panama Canal); WWI — neutrality, Zimmermann Telegram, war mobilization, Wilson's 14 Points, Senate rejection of League of Nations. |
| 19.6 | **Depression & World Wars (1920–1945)** | 1920s culture (Harlem Renaissance, Jazz Age, flappers) and its contradictions (Prohibition, Ku Klux Klan, Red Scare); causes of the Great Depression (overproduction, debt, tariffs, bank failures); New Deal programs (FDIC, Social Security, SEC, CCC, TVA) and debates about their success; WWII — isolationism vs. intervention; Pearl Harbor; war mobilization (women, minorities, internment); D-Day, Pacific theater, atomic bombs — the decision and its debates; Holocaust. |
| 19.7 | **Cold War America (1945–1980)** | Origins of the Cold War (containment, Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, NATO); Korean War; McCarthyism and the Red Scare at home; 1950s prosperity and conformity; Civil Rights Movement (Montgomery, Brown v. Board, SCLC, SNCC, lunch counters, Freedom Rides, Birmingham, March on Washington, Selma, Civil Rights Act 1964, Voting Rights Act 1965); Vietnam War (Gulf of Tonkin, escalation, antiwar movement, Tet Offensive, Vietnamization, fall of Saigon); Great Society; Nixon (détente, Watergate and its constitutional consequences); women's liberation, environmental movement, Roe v. Wade. |
| 19.8 | **Contemporary America (1980–present)** | Reagan Revolution (supply-side economics, deregulation, Cold War escalation, Iran-Contra); end of Cold War; Clinton era (NAFTA, tech boom, culture wars, impeachment); 9/11 and the War on Terror (Patriot Act, Iraq War, Abu Ghraib, surveillance state debates); Obama presidency (ACA, financial crisis response, drone warfare); Trump era (populist nationalism, polarization, impeachments); contemporary debates (inequality, racial justice, climate policy, immigration, democratic norms). |

## 🎯 4. APUSH Alignment

The AP US History course is organized into **nine periods**. This track's eight chapters map as follows:

| APUSH Period | Years | Chapter |
|-------------|-------|---------|
| Period 1-2 | 1491-1754 | 19.1 |
| Period 3 | 1754-1800 | 19.2 |
| Period 4-5 | 1800-1848 | 19.3 |
| Period 5 | 1844-1877 | 19.4 |
| Period 6-7 | 1877-1920 | 19.5 |
| Period 7-8 | 1920-1945 | 19.6 |
| Period 8 | 1945-1980 | 19.7 |
| Period 9 | 1980-present | 19.8 |

**AP Writing Skills:** DBQ (Document-Based Question), LEQ (Long Essay Question), SAQ (Short Answer Question). Every chapter includes primary source analysis exercises in `<details>` recall blocks modeled on APUSH free-response formats.
