# Subject Syllabus: 20 - Economics

*Back to [[00 - 09 - Learning Index|Learning Index]] · [[Bill's Vault/05-Knowledge_Foundation/10 - Learning App Material/Grade Band Pipelines/9-12 High School Pipeline|9-12 Pipeline]]*

Economics is the study of **how people make decisions under scarcity** — and how those individual decisions aggregate into market outcomes, national income levels, and global financial systems. This curriculum covers both AP Microeconomics and AP Macroeconomics, which are separate AP exams. Micro builds from individual decision-making up; macro builds from national/global aggregates down. Together they form the analytical foundation for understanding business, government policy, finance, and the forces that shape daily life.

## 📝 3. Chapter Outline

| # | Chapter | Domain | Core skill |
|---|---------|--------|-----------|
| 20.1 | **Economic Thinking & Scarcity** | Both | Scarcity and the fundamental economic problem; opportunity cost (explicit vs. implicit); marginal analysis (thinking at the margin); production possibilities frontier (PPF) — efficiency, tradeoffs, opportunity cost, economic growth; absolute vs. comparative advantage; gains from trade. |
| 20.2 | **Supply & Demand** | Micro | Law of demand (and exceptions); demand shifters (TRIBE: Tastes, Related goods prices, Income, Buyers, Expectations); law of supply; supply shifters; market equilibrium; disequilibrium and price adjustment; price elasticity of demand (PED) — calculation, determinants, total revenue test; price elasticity of supply; income elasticity; cross-price elasticity; price floors (minimum wage) and price ceilings (rent control) and their welfare effects. |
| 20.3 | **Market Structures** | Micro | Perfect competition — conditions, short-run and long-run equilibrium, profit maximization (MR=MC), economic profit vs. normal profit, firm and market supply curves; monopoly — barriers to entry, deadweight loss, price discrimination; monopolistic competition — product differentiation, long-run zero profit; oligopoly — interdependence, game theory intro, Prisoner's Dilemma, Nash equilibrium; antitrust policy. |
| 20.4 | **Factor Markets & Income Distribution** | Micro | Derived demand for labor; marginal revenue product (MRP) = demand for labor; labor supply and wage determination; monopsony; economic rent; income inequality measures (Lorenz curve, Gini coefficient); sources of income inequality; poverty and mobility. |
| 20.5 | **Market Failures & Government** | Micro | Externalities (positive/negative) and Pigouvian taxes/subsidies; public goods (non-rival, non-excludable) and free rider problem; common resources and the tragedy of the commons; information asymmetry (adverse selection, moral hazard, principal-agent problem); government intervention rationale and limitations. |
| 20.6 | **Macroeconomic Indicators** | Macro | GDP (expenditure approach: C+I+G+NX; income approach); real vs. nominal GDP; GDP deflator; limitations of GDP as a welfare measure; unemployment (frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal); natural rate of unemployment; labor force participation; inflation (CPI calculation, PPI); causes of inflation (demand-pull, cost-push, built-in); business cycle (expansion, peak, recession, trough). |
| 20.7 | **Aggregate Demand & Supply** | Macro | AD curve and shifters; SRAS curve (sticky wages/prices) and shifters; LRAS curve (full employment); short-run equilibrium; recessionary and inflationary gaps; fiscal policy (government spending multiplier, tax multiplier, balanced budget multiplier); crowding out; automatic stabilizers; discretionary fiscal policy — timing problems. |
| 20.8 | **Money, Banking & Monetary Policy** | Macro | Money functions and types; M1 and M2 money supply; fractional reserve banking; money multiplier (1/RRR); Federal Reserve structure and tools (open market operations, discount rate, reserve requirements, interest on reserves); Taylor Rule; money market model (supply and demand for money); investment demand; Keynesian transmission mechanism; quantity theory of money (MV=PQ); Phillips Curve (short-run tradeoff, long-run vertical); stagflation; international economics (exchange rates, current account, capital account, balance of payments). |

## 🎯 4. AP Alignment

| AP Exam | Chapters covered | Weight |
|---------|----------------|--------|
| **AP Microeconomics** | 20.1 (partial), 20.2, 20.3, 20.4, 20.5 | Full coverage |
| **AP Macroeconomics** | 20.1 (partial), 20.6, 20.7, 20.8 | Full coverage |

AP Economics exams are 60% multiple-choice + 40% free-response (3 FRQs: 1 long + 2 short). Each chapter's drill scripts include graph-based FRQ practice.
