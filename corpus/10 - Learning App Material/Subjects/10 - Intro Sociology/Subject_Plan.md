*Part of [[Bill's Vault/05-Knowledge_Foundation/10 - Learning App Material/README|10 - Learning App Material]]*

> **App context:** Intro Sociology is one of the most-taken college intro courses. It has no AP exam but does have a CLEP exam worth college credit. It pairs powerfully with Track 09 Psychology (individual behavior), Track 18 World History (historical structures), and Track 20 Economics (economic inequality).

## 3. Chapter Outline

| # | Chapter | Core skill |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 10.1 | **The Sociological Perspective & Research** | C. Wright Mills' sociological imagination; three major theoretical paradigms (structural-functionalism, conflict theory, symbolic interactionism); research methods (surveys, experiments, field research, secondary analysis); research ethics; quantitative vs. qualitative; social construction of reality |
| 10.2 | **Culture** | Components of culture (symbols, language, values, norms, material culture); cultural universals; ethnocentrism vs. cultural relativism; subcultures and countercultures; popular culture; media and cultural transmission; global culture and cultural imperialism |
| 10.3 | **Socialization** | Nature vs. nurture; agents of socialization (family, school, peer groups, media); Mead's looking-glass self; Cooley's "I" and "me"; Goffman's dramaturgical model (front stage/back stage, impression management); resocialization; total institutions |
| 10.4 | **Social Structure & Groups** | Statuses (ascribed vs. achieved) and roles; social groups (primary vs. secondary; in-group/out-group; reference groups); formal organizations and bureaucracy (Weber); group dynamics (groupthink, social loafing, Milgram's relevance); social networks |
| 10.5 | **Deviance & Social Control** | Defining deviance (social construction); labeling theory (Becker); strain theory (Merton); differential association (Sutherland); control theory (Hirschi); deviance and power; crime statistics and their limitations; criminal justice system sociology |
| 10.6 | **Social Stratification & Inequality** | Class systems; social mobility (structural vs. individual explanations); poverty (absolute vs. relative; causes; feminization); race and ethnicity (social construction of race; prejudice, discrimination, institutional racism; intersectionality — Kimberlé Crenshaw); gender stratification (gender vs. sex; patriarchy; gender pay gap) |
| 10.7 | **Social Institutions — Family, Education, Religion** | Family forms across cultures; marriage patterns; family functions; divorce sociology; education (hidden curriculum, credential inflation, achievement gap); religion as a social institution (Durkheim's sacred/profane; Weber on Protestantism and capitalism; secularization thesis) |
| 10.8 | **Population, Urbanization & Social Change** | Demography (birth rates, death rates, migration, demographic transition); population growth theories (Malthus, demographic transition); urbanization and city sociology; environmental sociology; theories of social change (cyclical, evolutionary, conflict); social movements (resource mobilization, new social movements) |
