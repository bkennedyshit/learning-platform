# Subject Syllabus: 18 - World History

*Back to [[00 - 09 - Learning Index|Learning Index]] · [[Bill's Vault/05-Knowledge_Foundation/10 - Learning App Material/Grade Band Pipelines/9-12 High School Pipeline|9-12 Pipeline]]*

World History is not a list of dates and names — it is a set of **analytical tools for understanding how the world got this way.** Why do some regions dominate global trade and others don't? Why did industrialization happen in Britain and not China first? Why do revolutions succeed in some places and fail in others? This curriculum answers those questions by treating history as a discipline, not a catalog. Every chapter builds the AP World History: Modern skills: causation, comparison, continuity and change over time (CCOT), and argumentation with evidence.

## 📝 3. Chapter Outline

| # | Chapter | Core skill |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 18.1 | **Foundations of Civilization (8000 BCE–600 CE)** | Neolithic Revolution (agricultural origins — why farming changed everything); river valley civilizations (Mesopotamia, Egypt, Indus Valley, Shang China); classical civilizations (Greece, Rome, Han China, Gupta India, Persia); shared features (social hierarchy, trade, religion, government); decline patterns. |
| 18.2 | **Networks of Exchange (1200–1450 CE)** | Mongol Empire and the Pax Mongolica; the Silk Roads (overland and maritime); Indian Ocean trade network; Sub-Saharan African trade (Mali, Great Zimbabwe); Black Death and its global consequences; the spread of Islam and Buddhism via trade routes. |
| 18.3 | **Land-Based Empires (1450–1750)** | Ottoman Empire (Constantinople 1453, Suleiman the Magnificent); Safavid Empire (Shia Islam, Persia); Mughal Empire (Akbar, religious tolerance, Taj Mahal); Qing China (examination system, tributary system, kangxi); Russian Empire expansion; how gunpowder transformed state power. |
| 18.4 | **Transoceanic Connections (1450–1750)** | European maritime expansion (Portugal, Spain, Netherlands, England, France); Columbian Exchange (crops, livestock, disease — its demographic catastrophe for indigenous Americans); Atlantic slave trade (scope, Middle Passage, plantation economies, African impact); European joint-stock companies and early capitalism. |
| 18.5 | **Revolutions (1750–1900)** | Enlightenment ideas (Locke, Rousseau, Voltaire — natural rights, social contract, popular sovereignty); American Revolution; French Revolution (phases: moderate → radical → Napoleonic); Haitian Revolution (the only successful slave revolt in history); Latin American independence movements; 19th-century nationalism. |
| 18.6 | **Industrialization & Imperialism (1750–1900)** | Industrial Revolution (why Britain first — coal, rivers, capital, empire); spread of industrialization; urbanization and working conditions; imperialism in Africa (Scramble for Africa, 1884-85 Berlin Conference); imperialism in Asia (British India, treaty ports in China, Opium Wars, Meiji Restoration in Japan); resistance movements. |
| 18.7 | **Global Conflict & Displacement (1900–present)** | WWI (causes, trench warfare, consequences — Ottoman collapse, Treaty of Versailles, seeds of WWII); Russian Revolution; Great Depression and rise of fascism; WWII (Holocaust, Pacific theater, atomic bombs, global scale); Cold War (containment, proxy wars, decolonization — 80+ new nations 1945-1975); major genocides of the 20th century. |
| 18.8 | **Globalization (1900–present)** | Post-WWII economic order (Bretton Woods, World Bank, IMF, GATT/WTO); technological revolution (internet, mobile, AI — and their geopolitical implications); rise of China and India; global inequality and development; climate change as a historical turning point; global health (HIV/AIDS, COVID-19 as global crises). |
