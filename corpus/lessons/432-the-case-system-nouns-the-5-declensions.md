# 43.2 — The Case System: Nouns & The 5 Declensions

*Back to [Subject_Plan](Subject_Plan) | [← 43.1](43.1---The-Latin-Alphabet,-Pronunciation-&-Quantities) | [43.3 →](43.3---Adjectives,-Pronouns-&-Agreement)*

> **Chapter goal:** Internalize Latin's case system as a type-tagging architecture — every noun carries its grammatical role encoded in its ending. Master all five declension paradigms with full singular and plural forms across all six cases.

---

## 1. The Case System — Conceptual Framework

Latin has **no fixed word order requirement** for expressing grammatical relationships. Instead, noun endings encode function:

```
Puella servum amat.       The girl loves the slave.
Servum puella amat.       The girl loves the slave.   (same meaning — word order is style, not grammar)
Puellam servus amat.      The slave loves the girl.   (endings changed → meaning changed)
```

Think of it like a **type tag** attached to every noun. The parser (your brain) reads the tag, not the position:

| Case | Function | English preposition analogy |
|------|----------|-----------------------------|
| **Nominative** | Subject of the verb | — |
| **Genitive** | Possession; "of" | of |
| **Dative** | Indirect object; "to/for" | to, for |
| **Accusative** | Direct object; object of motion-toward prepositions | — (direct object) |
| **Ablative** | Agent, means, manner, accompaniment, place where/from | by, with, from, in |
| **Vocative** | Direct address | — (Hey! / O…) |

> **The accusative and ablative are the workhorses.** Between them they govern the majority of prepositions. Learn which prepositions take which case as early as possible.

### Common Prepositions and Their Cases

**Accusative only:** *ad* (to, toward), *ante* (before), *apud* (at, near), *circum* (around), *contra* (against), *in* (into, onto — motion), *inter* (between), *per* (through, by means of), *post* (after), *propter* (because of), *trans* (across)

**Ablative only:** *a/ab* (away from, by — agent), *cum* (with), *de* (down from, about), *e/ex* (out of), *in* (in, on — location), *pro* (for, on behalf of), *sine* (without), *sub* (under — location)

**Both (different meanings):** *in* + accusative = motion into; *in* + ablative = location in/on. *sub* + accusative = motion under; *sub* + ablative = position under.

## 2. The Five Declensions

Latin nouns belong to one of five declension classes, identified by the **genitive singular ending** (always given in dictionary entries):

| Declension | Gen. sg. ending | Typical gender | Example |
|------------|----------------|---------------|---------|
| 1st | **-ae** | Feminine (some masculine) | *puella, -ae* f. (girl) |
| 2nd | **-ī** | Masculine / Neuter | *servus, -ī* m. (slave); *bellum, -ī* n. (war) |
| 3rd | **-is** | All three | *rex, regis* m. (king); *corpus, corporis* n. (body) |
| 4th | **-ūs** | Mostly masculine | *manus, -ūs* f. (hand); *cornū, -ūs* n. (horn) |
| 5th | **-ēī** | Mostly feminine | *rēs, reī* f. (thing); *diēs, diēī* m./f. (day) |

---

## 3. First Declension: *puella, puellae* f. — girl

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom.** | puell**a** | puell**ae** |
| **Gen.** | puell**ae** | puell**ārum** |
| **Dat.** | puell**ae** | puell**īs** |
| **Acc.** | puell**am** | puell**ās** |
| **Abl.** | puell**ā** (long ā) | puell**īs** |
| **Voc.** | puell**a** | puell**ae** |

> Note: nom. sg. and voc. sg. are identical (-a); gen. sg. / dat. sg. / nom. pl. / voc. pl. all share **-ae** — context disambiguates.

**Key 1st declension nouns to know:**
*puella* (girl), *fēmina* (woman), *aqua* (water), *terra* (land/earth), *vīta* (life), *patria* (fatherland), *porta* (gate), *nauta* (sailor — m.), *poēta* (poet — m.), *agricola* (farmer — m.)

## 4. Second Declension

### 4a. Masculine: *servus, servī* m. — slave

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom.** | serv**us** | serv**ī** |
| **Gen.** | serv**ī** | serv**ōrum** |
| **Dat.** | serv**ō** | serv**īs** |
| **Acc.** | serv**um** | serv**ōs** |
| **Abl.** | serv**ō** | serv**īs** |
| **Voc.** | serv**e** ← special! | serv**ī** |

> Voc. sg. of 2nd decl. -us nouns ends in **-e** (not -us): *Marce! / Serve!*
> Exception: *deus* (god) — voc. sg. = *deus* (same as nom.); *filius* (son) — voc. sg. = *fīlī* (contracts)

### 4b. Masculine -er nouns: *puer, puerī* m. — boy / *ager, agrī* m. — field

Some 2nd decl. masculines end in **-er** in nom. sg. — the stem may or may not keep the -e:

| | *puer* (keeps -e) | *ager* (drops -e) |
|--|-------------------|-------------------|
| Nom. | *puer* | *ager* |
| Gen. | *puerī* | *agrī* |
| All other cases | *puer-* stem | *agr-* stem |

> Always check the genitive to find the true stem: *puer, puerī* → stem puer-; *ager, agrī* → stem agr-.

### 4c. Neuter: *bellum, bellī* n. — war

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom.** | bell**um** | bell**a** |
| **Gen.** | bell**ī** | bell**ōrum** |
| **Dat.** | bell**ō** | bell**īs** |
| **Acc.** | bell**um** | bell**a** |
| **Abl.** | bell**ō** | bell**īs** |
| **Voc.** | bell**um** | bell**a** |

> **Neuter rule (applies to ALL declensions):** Nominative, accusative, and vocative of neuter nouns are **always identical**. Plural nominative/accusative/vocative neuter ends in **-a**.

**Key 2nd declension nouns:**
*servus* (slave), *dominus* (master/lord), *amicus* (friend), *filius* (son), *puer* (boy), *ager* (field), *liber* (book), *bellum* (war), *verbum* (word), *caelum* (sky), *oppidum* (town), *forum* (forum/marketplace)

## 5. Third Declension

The most varied declension — nouns of all three genders, many irregular nominative singulars. **The key is always to learn the genitive singular** to find the stem. The stem = genitive singular minus -is.

### 5a. Consonant-stem masculine/feminine: *rex, regis* m. — king

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom.** | rēx ← irregular | rēg**ēs** |
| **Gen.** | rēg**is** | rēg**um** |
| **Dat.** | rēg**ī** | rēg**ibus** |
| **Acc.** | rēg**em** | rēg**ēs** |
| **Abl.** | rēg**e** | rēg**ibus** |
| **Voc.** | rēx | rēg**ēs** |

Stem: rēg- (from genitive rēgis)

### 5b. Other 3rd declension m/f examples showing stem variation

| Nom. sg. | Gen. sg. | Stem | Meaning |
|----------|---------|------|---------|
| *mīles* | *mīlitis* | mīlit- | soldier |
| *urbs* | *urbis* | urb- | city |
| *nōmen* | *nōminis* | nōmin- | name |
| *corpus* | *corporis* | corpor- | body |
| *tempus* | *temporis* | tempor- | time |
| *pater* | *patris* | patr- | father |
| *māter* | *mātris* | mātr- | mother |
| *frāter* | *frātris* | frātr- | brother |
| *vōx* | *vōcis* | vōc- | voice |
| *lēx* | *lēgis* | lēg- | law |
| *dux* | *ducis* | duc- | leader |
| *mons* | *montis* | mont- | mountain |
| *pōns* | *pontis* | pont- | bridge |

### 5c. Neuter 3rd declension: *corpus, corporis* n. — body

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom.** | corpus | corpor**a** |
| **Gen.** | corpor**is** | corpor**um** |
| **Dat.** | corpor**ī** | corpor**ibus** |
| **Acc.** | corpus | corpor**a** |
| **Abl.** | corpor**e** | corpor**ibus** |
| **Voc.** | corpus | corpor**a** |

### 5d. i-stem 3rd declension (special subgroup)

Certain 3rd declension nouns are **i-stems** — their genitive plural ends in **-ium** (not -um) and some have -ī in the ablative singular. Recognition rules:
- Nouns with equal syllables in nom. and gen. sg.: *cīvis, cīvis* m. (citizen); *nāvis, nāvis* f. (ship)
- Nouns with a nominative ending in two consonants: *urbs, urbis* f.; *mons, montis* m.; *pōns, pontis* m.
- Neuter nouns ending in -al, -ar, -e: *animal, animālis* n.; *mare, maris* n.

i-stem paradigm (*cīvis, cīvis* m. — citizen):

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom.** | cīvis | cīvēs |
| **Gen.** | cīvis | cīv**ium** ← -ium not -um |
| **Dat.** | cīvī | cīvibus |
| **Acc.** | cīvem | cīvēs (or cīvīs) |
| **Abl.** | cīve (or cīvī) | cīvibus |

## 6. Fourth Declension

### 6a. Masculine: *manus, manūs* f. — hand (also masculine nouns: *exercitus* — army)

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom.** | man**us** | man**ūs** |
| **Gen.** | man**ūs** | man**uum** |
| **Dat.** | man**uī** | man**ibus** |
| **Acc.** | man**um** | man**ūs** |
| **Abl.** | man**ū** | man**ibus** |
| **Voc.** | man**us** | man**ūs** |

### 6b. Neuter: *cornū, cornūs* n. — horn

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom./Acc./Voc.** | corn**ū** | corn**ua** |
| **Gen.** | corn**ūs** | corn**uum** |
| **Dat./Abl.** | corn**ū** | corn**ibus** |

**Key 4th declension nouns:** *manus* f. (hand), *domus* f. (house — mixed 2nd/4th), *exercitus* m. (army), *senātus* m. (senate), *impetus* m. (attack), *frūctus* m. (fruit/enjoyment)

---

## 7. Fifth Declension

### *rēs, reī* f. — thing / matter / affair

| Case | Singular | Plural |
|------|---------|--------|
| **Nom.** | rēs | rēs |
| **Gen.** | reī | rērum |
| **Dat.** | reī | rēbus |
| **Acc.** | rem | rēs |
| **Abl.** | rē | rēbus |
| **Voc.** | rēs | rēs |

**Key 5th declension nouns:** *rēs* f. (thing/affair/matter — most common), *diēs* m./f. (day), *spēs* f. (hope), *fidēs* f. (faith/loyalty), *faciēs* f. (face/appearance), *aciēs* f. (battle line)

> Most 5th declension nouns occur only in nom./acc. singular — *rēs* and *diēs* are the only ones with complete paradigms in common use.

## 8. Parsing Latin Nouns — Drill Protocol

For any Latin noun form, ask:
1. What **declension** is this? (identify from nom./gen. entry in dictionary)
2. What is the **stem**? (genitive singular minus -is)
3. What **case and number** does this ending indicate?
4. What **grammatical role** does that case indicate in this sentence?

**Worked example:** *rēgum* in the sentence *"Rōma, caput rēgum, magna est."*
- Dictionary: *rēx, rēgis* m. → 3rd declension, stem *rēg-*
- Form: *rēg-* + *-um* → genitive plural
- Role: genitive = possession → "of kings"
- Translation: "Rome, the capital of kings, is great."

---

## 9. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **Learning the nominative alone:** The genitive must be memorized with every noun — it reveals the stem and declension. *rex* alone tells you nothing; *rex, regis* tells you everything.
2. **Confusing 2nd decl. dat./abl. pl. (-īs) with 1st decl. dat./abl. pl. (-īs):** They're identical — only the stem and gender distinguish them.
3. **Missing neuter agreement:** Neuter nom./acc./voc. are always the same form. Neuter plural nom./acc./voc. ends in -a (not -ae or -ī).
4. **Ignoring the vocative:** It's usually identical to nominative except in 2nd decl. -us nouns where it's -e.
5. **3rd declension stem variation:** Students try to apply regular stems to nouns like *corpus/corporis* and produce forms like *\*corpus-em* → wrong. Always strip the genitive: *corpor-em* (acc. sg.).
