# APP-03.2 — Plants — Structure, Function & Life Cycles

A bean plant on a windowsill has no mouth, no legs, and no stomach, yet it grows taller every day. It does this with parts that each have a job: roots drink, stems carry, leaves make food, and flowers make seeds. This lesson looks at each part, how a plant makes its own food, how a plant's life goes around in a cycle, and how plants spread their seeds to new places.

The course plan places this lesson in grades 2-3. The main text talks to the learner. The **Adult note** boxes are for the person guiding them.

## Prerequisites

[APP-03.1 — Living vs Non-Living & Basic Needs](/app-031-living-vs-non-living-basic-needs): plants are living, and they need light, water, air, nutrients, and space. It helps to measure length with a ruler in centimeters, as in [APP-02.7 — Measurement & Data](/app-027-measurement-data).

> **Adult note:** Useful materials: a few dry beans soaked in water overnight, a paper towel and a clear zip bag, a celery stalk with leaves, a cup of water with food coloring, and a hand lens. Optional: a carrot, a potato, an apple or tomato, and a dandelion seed head.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson the learner should be able to:

1. Name the roots, stem, leaves, flower, fruit, and seed of a flowering plant and give one job of each.
2. Say in words how a leaf makes food.
3. Put the stages of a plant's life cycle in order.
4. Use a seed's shape to predict how it travels, and explain how a plant part helps a plant live where it lives.

## Core ideas

### Plant parts and their jobs

| Part | Main jobs |
|---|---|
| **Roots** | Hold the plant in the soil; soak up water and minerals; some store food |
| **Stem** | Holds up the leaves and flowers; carries water up from the roots and food to other parts; some store food |
| **Leaves** | Make food using light; let gases in and out through tiny openings |
| **Flower** | Makes seeds |
| **Fruit** | Holds and protects the seeds and helps them travel |
| **Seed** | Holds a baby plant and stored food to help it start growing |

Inside the stem are tiny tubes. One set of tubes, called **xylem**, carries water up from the roots. Another set, called **phloem**, carries sugar made in the leaves to other parts of the plant.

Some plant parts are surprises. A **carrot** is a root that stores food. A **potato** is not a root at all. It is a swollen underground stem, and its "eyes" are buds that can grow into new shoots.

### How leaves make food: photosynthesis

Plants make their own food. This is called **photosynthesis** ("making with light"). In words:

> light energy + water + carbon dioxide → sugar + oxygen

- **Water** comes up from the roots through the stem.
- **Carbon dioxide** is a gas from the air. It enters the leaf through tiny openings called **stomata**. Many plants have most of their stomata on the underside of the leaf.
- **Light** gives the energy.
- The plant uses the **sugar** for energy and to build its body.
- The **oxygen** goes out into the air, where animals (and people) breathe it.

The green color in leaves comes from **chlorophyll**, a material that soaks up light energy.

### Flowers, pollen, and seeds

A flower has parts that make **pollen** and parts that hold tiny **ovules**, which can become seeds. To make seeds, pollen has to move to the right part of a flower. This is called **pollination**. Many flowers are pollinated by animals such as bees, butterflies, birds, and bats, which visit for sweet **nectar** and carry pollen from flower to flower. Grasses, oaks, maples, and pine trees are pollinated by wind.

After pollination, the seeds grow inside the flower, and part of the flower swells into a **fruit** around them. To a scientist, a fruit is the part that holds the seeds. So a tomato, a pea pod, and an apple are all fruits.

### The life cycle of a flowering plant

1. **Seed:** a baby plant with stored food inside a seed coat. Many seeds can rest for months or years.
2. **Germination:** with water and the right temperature, the seed swells, splits, and sends a root down and a shoot up.
3. **Seedling:** the young plant uses its stored food until its first leaves can make food by photosynthesis.
4. **Adult plant:** it grows roots, stem, and leaves.
5. **Flowering and pollination:** flowers open and pollen is moved.
6. **Fruit and new seeds:** the seeds are spread, and the cycle starts again.

### How seeds travel

A seed that lands right under its parent has to share light and water. Seeds that travel can find more room. Plants spread seeds in many ways:

| How | Clue in the seed or fruit | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Wind | Very light, with a "parachute" of fluff or a wing | dandelion, maple |
| Water | Light fruit that floats | coconut |
| Animals eat the fruit | Juicy, tasty fruit; seeds pass through and land far away | berries |
| Animals carry or bury | Hooks or sticky parts that catch on fur; nuts that squirrels bury and forget | cocklebur, acorns |
| The plant itself | Pods that dry and split open | peas |

### Plant adaptations

An **adaptation** is a body part or behavior that helps a living thing survive where it lives.

- **Desert:** a cactus has spines, which are leaves that became small and sharp, and a thick stem that stores water. Less leaf surface means less water lost.
- **Cold, snowy forest:** pine trees have thin needle leaves that lose less water.
- **Pond:** a water lily has wide, flat leaves that float, with a waxy coat that sheds water, so the leaves stay in the sunlight.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Watching water climb a stem

*Activity to try.* Put a celery stalk with leaves into a cup of water with several drops of food coloring.

1. **Predict:** where will the color go, and why?
2. **Reason:** water enters at the cut end and moves up the stem through tiny tubes (xylem) to the leaves.
3. **Observe:** check after a few hours and again the next day. Look at the leaves. Then have an adult cut across the stalk and look at the cut end with a hand lens.
4. **Look for:** small colored dots at the cut end. Each dot is a bundle of water tubes.
5. **Record** what you actually see with a drawing. This is a plan to try, not a result we already know for your celery.

### Example 2: Which way does this seed travel?

Four mystery seeds:

1. **Seed A** has a light, fluffy "umbrella." Clue: something to catch air. **Wind.**
2. **Seed B** is inside a juicy red berry. Clue: tasty fruit. **Animals eat it,** and the seed lands somewhere else in their droppings.
3. **Seed C** has tiny hooks all over. Clue: hooks catch on fur or socks. **Animals carry it.**
4. **Seed D** is inside a big, light, airtight shell that floats. **Water.**

Check: for each answer, name the part of the seed or fruit that gave you the clue.

### Example 3: Ordering a bean plant's life

*Illustrative example.* A learner's cards are mixed up: "flowers open," "seed swells in damp towel," "pods form with beans inside," "first leaves unfold," "root pokes out."

1. Find the start: a seed must take in water first. **Seed swells in damp towel.**
2. What comes out first? The root grows down before leaves grow up. **Root pokes out.**
3. Then the shoot grows and **first leaves unfold.** Now the plant can make its own food.
4. When the plant is grown, **flowers open.**
5. After pollination, **pods form with beans inside.** The new beans are seeds, so the cycle can start again.

### Example 4: Root or stem?

Is a potato a root? Use clues.

1. Roots usually do not have buds or leaves.
2. Look at a potato: it has "eyes." If you leave an old potato in a cupboard, shoots can grow from the eyes. Shoots grow from **buds**.
3. Buds grow on stems, so a potato is a **stem** that stores food underground.
4. Compare a carrot: it has no eyes, it grows straight down, and thin hair-like roots grow from its sides. A carrot is a **root** that stores food.

## Common misconceptions and boundary cases

- **"Plants get their food from the soil."** Soil gives water and minerals. The plant *makes* its food (sugar) in its leaves from water, carbon dioxide, and light energy.
- **"Every plant part under the ground is a root."** Potatoes are underground stems, and onions grow from underground bulbs that are also modified stems with fleshy leaves.
- **"Fruits are always sweet."** A scientist calls any part that holds the seeds a fruit. Tomatoes, peppers, and pea pods are fruits, even though they are not sweet.
- **"All seeds need light to start growing."** Seeds need the right conditions, and those differ by plant. Many common garden seeds sprout in a dark, damp paper towel. Some seeds need cold, light, or even fire before they will sprout.
- **"Plants only take in carbon dioxide and only give out oxygen."** Plants also use oxygen to get energy from their sugar, all day and all night. In sunlight they make more oxygen than they use.
- **"A seed is dead until it sprouts."** A resting seed is alive. Its baby plant is waiting for water and the right temperature.

## Practice

1. Which plant part soaks up water from the soil?
2. Finish the sentence: In photosynthesis, a leaf uses light energy, water, and ______ to make ______ and oxygen.
3. A maple seed has a flat wing. How do you think it travels?
4. Put these in order: seedling, adult plant with flowers, seed, germination, new seeds in a fruit.
5. A strawberry plant sends out long stems along the ground, and new plants grow where they touch the soil. Is this a seed? What part of the plant made the new plants?
6. Why do cactus spines help the cactus live in the desert?
7. *Illustrative example:* A bean seedling was 2 centimeters tall on Monday and 7 centimeters tall on Friday. How many centimeters did it grow?
8. Is a pumpkin a fruit? Explain using the word "seeds."

<details>
<summary>Answer key and self-check</summary>

1. The roots.
2. Carbon dioxide; sugar.
3. By wind. The wing catches the air so the seed spins and drifts away from the tree.
4. Seed → germination → seedling → adult plant with flowers → new seeds in a fruit.
5. No, it is not a seed. The long stems (called runners) grew new plants. This is a way of making new plants without seeds.
6. Spines are leaves that became small and thin, so they lose very little water. The thick green stem stores water and makes food instead.
7. 7 − 2 = 5 centimeters.
8. Yes. A pumpkin holds the plant's seeds, so it is a fruit.

**Adult self-check:** For answers 3, 6, and 8, the learner should name a *clue* (wing, small leaf, seeds inside) and connect it to a *job* (traveling, saving water, holding seeds). A label without a reason is half an answer.

</details>

> **Safety note:** Do not eat seeds, beans, berries, or leaves used in activities. Some plants and seeds are poisonous; the U.S. Poison Control helpline is 1-800-222-1222. Seeds and dry beans are choking hazards for small children. An adult should do any cutting. Wash hands after handling soil and plants.
