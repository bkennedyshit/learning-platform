# 🛤️ Italian — Learning Path

> This document is **hand-curated** and should remain stable.

---

## 📋 Prerequisites

Before starting this track, you should have:

1. **No prior Italian required** — the track starts from zero.
2. **Spanish or French (highly recommended)** — with [Spanish](Subject_Plan) at A2+, you already know ~80% of Italian vocabulary by root; with [French](Subject_Plan), you already know the passato prossimo structure. Without Romance background, the track still works, just at a slower initial pace.
3. **A spaced-repetition tool** — Anki. Gender is encoded in Italian nouns and must be memorized with the word.
4. **Headphones** — Italian phonetics is easy but double consonants require ear training.

### Romance Transfer Map
| Feature | Spanish equivalent | French equivalent |
|---------|------------------|------------------|
| -ARE verbs | -AR verbs | -ER verbs |
| Passato prossimo | Pretérito perfecto | Passé composé |
| Imperfetto | Imperfecto | Imparfait |
| Congiuntivo | Subjuntivo | Subjonctif |
| Essere/avere as auxiliaries | Ser/haber | Être/avoir |
| Definite article (il/la/i/le) | el/la/los/las | le/la/les |

## 📖 Recommended Chapter Order

### Phase 1: Phonetics & Foundation — A2 (Weeks 1–6)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale | Est. Time |
|-------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | [42.1 - Phonetics & The Italian Sound System](42.1---Phonetics-&-The-Italian-Sound-System) | Easiest Romance phonetics — master this in 1 week; it's a gift compared to French | 1 week |
| 2 | [42.2 - Nouns, Articles, Gender & Adjective Agreement](42.2---Nouns,-Articles,-Gender-&-Adjective-Agreement) | The article system has 7 forms — learn all of them; the lo/gli rule is non-negotiable | 1.5 weeks |
| 3 | [Ci](Ci) | Double pronouns and *ne/ci* are Italian-specific; no French/Spanish equivalent | 1.5 weeks |
| 4 | [42.4 - Verb System — Present, Imperative & Reflexive Verbs](42.4---Verb-System-—-Present,-Imperative-&-Reflexive-Verbs) | The engine — three conjugation groups + essential irregulars | 2 weeks |

**Phase 1 deliverable:** Hold a 5-minute present-tense conversation. Order in a restaurant. Navigate a train station or hotel. Pronounce double consonants correctly. Use all article forms without hesitation.

### Phase 2: Past & Future — B1 (Weeks 7–16)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 5 | [42.5 - Past Tenses — Passato Prossimo, Imperfetto & Trapassato](42.5---Past-Tenses-—-Passato-Prossimo,-Imperfetto-&-Trapassato) | The main past tense chapter — essere-vs-avere selection is the most common learner error |
| 6 | [42.6 - Future, Conditional & The Congiuntivo](42.6---Future,-Conditional-&-The-Congiuntivo) | The congiuntivo is used more in everyday Italian speech than in any other Romance language |

**Phase 2 deliverable:** Narrate a past experience. Use the conditional politely. Correctly use the congiuntivo with 5 different trigger constructions. Understand RAI news at 60%+.

### Phase 3: Fluency — B2 (Weeks 17–25)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 7 | [42.7 - Idioms, Dialects & Cultural Register](42.7---Idioms,-Dialects-&-Cultural-Register) | Cultural fluency + the dialect challenge in Italian media |
| 8 | [42.8 - Reading, Listening & Cultural Immersion](42.8---Reading,-Listening-&-Cultural-Immersion) | Transition to native content |

**Phase 3 deliverable:** Watch an Italian film (e.g., *Suburra* or *L'amica geniale*) with Italian subtitles and understand ≥75%. Read *Il Post* articles with ≤10% lookups. Recognize a Neapolitan or Sicilian accent.

## 🔀 Non-Negotiable Daily Habits (Every Phase)

| Daily habit | Time | Why |
|-------------|------|-----|
| Anki vocabulary (with article) | 15–20 min | Always learn *il/la + word* — never the word alone |
| Italian audio (Dreaming Italian / Italiano Automatico) | 20–30 min | Ear training + comprehensible input |
| Language Transfer Italian episode | 20 min (Phase 1–2) | Derives Italian grammar from its roots |
| Reading aloud | 10 min | Italian rewards pronunciation; speak every sentence you read |

> **The essere/avere trap:** Italian learners default to *avere* as the past tense auxiliary (because it's more common). The essere verbs — all reflexives + intransitive motion/change-of-state verbs — require past participle agreement with the subject. Drilling this from day one of chapter 42.5 prevents a persistent fossilized error.

After completing this Italian track:

## 🚫 What This Track Does NOT Cover

- **Italian composition** (academic, business, formal letters) — this track targets conversation and reading
- **Italian dialects in depth** — chapter 42.7 gives cultural context; deep Neapolitan or Sicilian is a separate study
- **Old Italian** (Dante's *Commedia* in the original 14th-century Italian) — achievable with additional study after completing this track
