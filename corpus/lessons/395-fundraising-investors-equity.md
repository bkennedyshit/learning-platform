# 39.5 — Fundraising, Investors & Equity

> *"If you're not embarrassed by your first offer to investors, you waited too long to fundraise — but also: don't raise unless you know what you'd do with the money."*
> — paraphrased founder wisdom

Fundraising is not the goal — it's a tool. This chapter demystifies the entire capital stack, from self-funding to Series A, including the mechanics of SAFEs, convertible notes, dilution, and cap tables. You'll leave knowing exactly what a term sheet means, how your ownership changes with each round, and — most importantly — whether you should raise at all.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Explain the **capital stack** from bootstrapping through Series A and what changes at each stage.
2. Model a **cap table** before and after a SAFE investment, including dilution.
3. Distinguish between **SAFE (post-money and pre-money)**, **convertible note**, and **priced equity round**.
4. Explain **valuation caps, discount rates, pro-rata rights**, and **liquidation preferences**.
5. Decide whether **bootstrapping, angel, pre-seed, or seed** is right for your current stage.
6. Write a **3-sentence investor pitch** and identify what traction metrics investors want to see.
7. Identify the difference between **good dilution** (diluting to build a bigger business) and **bad dilution** (giving away equity without a clear reason).

## 📚 1. Should You Raise? The Decision Framework

### 1.1 The Capital Spectrum

```
BOOTSTRAPPED ←────────────────────────────────→ VC-BACKED

No outside capital         Angels / Pre-seed        Series A+
Full control               Dilution begins          Professional investors
Revenue = oxygen           Validates with cash      Growth capital
Slow but owned             Experiments more         Scaling capital
```

Neither end is universally superior. The choice depends on:
- **What you're building** (SaaS that scales at low CAC = can bootstrap; biotech = must raise)
- **How fast the market moves** (winner-take-all = must raise; niche = can bootstrap)
- **What you want** (financial independence = bootstrap; building a $1B+ company = VC path)

### 1.2 The Two Honest Reasons to Raise

**Reason 1 — You have proven unit economics and need capital to accelerate a known channel.**
"I know that for every $1 I spend on X, I get $3 back. I need $500k to run that experiment at scale."

**Reason 2 — You're in a winner-take-all market with a real moat and need to run faster than competition.**
"There are 3 competitors building what I'm building, we all have similar traction, and whoever builds the best distribution first wins."

**Not valid reasons to raise:**
- "To have runway while I figure out PMF" (that's what your own revenue should do)
- "Because everyone else in my space has raised" (raising keeps everyone's business alive — but yours too)
- "My advisor said I should" (advisors often have incentives to get you funded)

### 1.3 Bootstrapping — The Honest Case

Bootstrapping (growing a business entirely on customer revenue) has a legitimacy renaissance in 2026:

**Advantages:**
- You keep 100% of equity
- Revenue forces immediate product-market fit validation
- No investor pressure on timeline or exit
- Infinite runway if revenue > costs

**Disadvantages:**
- Slower growth than VC-backed competitors in winner-take-all markets
- No investor network or brand associations
- More stress — your bank account is your runway

**Ramen profitable:** The YC concept — generating just enough revenue to live on while building. Allows unlimited runway and removes external pressure. Paul Graham's thesis: "Ramen profitable means having a business model that works, even if it only generates $1,000/month." — paraphrased from [paulgraham.com/ramenprofitable.html](http://www.paulgraham.com/ramenprofitable.html)

## 📚 2. The Fundraising Stages

### 2.1 Stage Map

| Stage | Typical amount | What investors expect | Typical instrument |
|-------|----------------|----------------------|-------------------|
| **Pre-seed** | $50k–$500k | Founder quality + idea + early signals | SAFE or convertible note |
| **Seed** | $500k–$3M | PMF evidence (early revenue, strong engagement) | SAFE, convertible note, or priced round |
| **Series A** | $3M–$15M | Repeatable revenue growth + clear GTM | Priced equity round |
| **Series B+** | $15M+ | Scaling a proven model | Priced equity round |

### 2.2 Who Invests at Each Stage

| Stage | Investor type | Check size | What they bring |
|-------|--------------|------------|-----------------|
| Pre-seed | Friends & family, angels, micro-VCs | $10k–$100k | Money, maybe a network |
| Seed | Angels, seed-stage VCs (First Round, Precursor, Y Combinator) | $100k–$500k | Money, intro network, operational experience |
| Series A | Institutional VCs (a16z, Sequoia, Benchmark) | $2M–$10M | Big check, board seat, portfolio network |

**Y Combinator** occupies a unique position: $500k (standard deal, post-2022 terms) for ~7% equity, with the YC brand and network being the primary value. Acceptance rate ~2–3%. — paraphrased from [ycombinator.com/apply](https://www.ycombinator.com/apply).

### 2.3 Angels vs VCs

| Dimension | Angel investors | VC funds |
|-----------|----------------|---------|
| Capital source | Their own money | LPs (limited partners — endowments, family offices) |
| Check size | $5k–$250k | $250k–$10M+ |
| Decision speed | Days to weeks | Weeks to months |
| Involvement | Varies: passive to highly active | Board seat at Series A+ |
| Return expectation | 10–50× in 5–10 years | 100× in 10 years (portfolio math) |
| What they want | Upside + to help founders | Market-scale outcomes only |

**For your stage:** Angels are the right first check. A good angel is someone who is:
1. An operator (has built/run a company) not just a finance person
2. Aligned with your market (game dev, AI, SaaS)
3. Small check ($25k–$100k) so they don't need a board seat

## 📚 3. Equity Instruments

### 3.1 The SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)

The SAFE was invented by Y Combinator in 2013 and has become the dominant seed instrument in the US. — paraphrased from [ycombinator.com/resources/safe-primer](https://www.ycombinator.com/resources/safe-primer).

**What it is:** An investor gives you money today in exchange for the right to receive equity in a *future* priced round, at a discount or with a cap.

**Why it's better than a convertible note:**
- No interest rate (not technically debt)
- No maturity date (no forced repayment)
- Simpler documents (2-4 pages vs 20+ for a priced round)
- Standardized (investors know exactly what they're getting)

### 3.2 SAFE Mechanics

**Valuation cap:** The maximum valuation at which the SAFE converts to equity.

**Example:** You raise a SAFE with a $5M cap. At Series A, you're valued at $20M. The SAFE holder's money converts at a $5M valuation (not $20M), giving them 4× more shares than if they'd invested in the priced round.

**Discount rate:** An alternative (or addition) to the cap. The SAFE converts at a percentage discount to the Series A price.

**Example:** 20% discount. Series A prices at $1/share → SAFE converts at $0.80/share → SAFE holder gets 25% more shares.

**Pro-rata rights:** The right to invest in future rounds to maintain ownership percentage. Valuable for investors who believe in the company.

### 3.3 Post-Money SAFE vs Pre-Money SAFE

**Pre-money SAFE (old):** The percentage ownership isn't locked in when you sign — it depends on what else gets raised and at what valuation.

**Post-money SAFE (current YC standard, 2018+):** The ownership percentage is calculated at signing and is fixed. "I own X% of the company after this SAFE, regardless of other SAFEs raised." Much cleaner for modeling.

**The post-money SAFE formula:**

$$\text{Ownership \%} = \frac{\text{Investment amount}}{\text{Post-money cap}}$$

Example: $100k investment on a $2M post-money cap → 5% ownership at conversion.

### 3.4 Convertible Note

A convertible note is a **loan** that converts to equity at a future financing event. Older than SAFEs; still used in some bridge situations.

Key terms:
- **Principal:** the amount invested
- **Interest rate:** typically 2–8% per year
- **Maturity date:** typically 18–24 months — after which, if no financing event, the note is due
- **Discount rate:** same as SAFE (10–25% discount on conversion)
- **Valuation cap:** same concept as SAFE

**Disadvantage over SAFE:** It's technically debt. The maturity date creates a "conversion or repay" cliff that can cause issues if the company hasn't raised a priced round.

### 3.5 Priced Equity Round

At Series A (and sometimes Seed), investors receive actual shares in exchange for investment, with a negotiated valuation.

**Key terms:**
- **Pre-money valuation:** company value before the investment
- **Post-money valuation:** pre-money + investment
- **Share price:** post-money valuation ÷ fully diluted share count
- **Fully diluted share count:** all outstanding shares + all options/warrants/SAFEs

## 📚 4. Cap Table Mechanics

### Definition 39.5.1 — Cap Table

A capitalization table (cap table) is a spreadsheet that shows who owns what percentage of a company, and how ownership changes with each financing event.

### 4.1 Starting Cap Table (Day 1 — 2 Founders)

| Shareholder | Shares | Ownership % |
|-------------|--------|-------------|
| Founder A | 5,000,000 | 50% |
| Founder B | 5,000,000 | 50% |
| **Total** | **10,000,000** | **100%** |

### 4.2 After Option Pool Creation

Before raising, investors typically require you to create an option pool for future employees. This dilutes founders **before** investment (pre-money, so investors aren't diluted by the option pool).

| Shareholder | Shares | Ownership % |
|-------------|--------|-------------|
| Founder A | 5,000,000 | 41.7% |
| Founder B | 5,000,000 | 41.7% |
| Option Pool (unissued) | 2,000,000 | 16.7% |
| **Total** | **12,000,000** | **100%** |

### 4.3 After $500k SAFE at $4M Post-Money Cap

SAFE investor ownership: $500k / $4M = **12.5%**

Post-SAFE cap table (the SAFE is "phantom" until conversion — shown as if converted):

| Shareholder | Shares | Ownership % |
|-------------|--------|-------------|
| Founder A | 5,000,000 | 36.4% |
| Founder B | 5,000,000 | 36.4% |
| Option Pool | 2,000,000 | 14.6% |
| SAFE Investor | ~1,714,000 | 12.5% |
| **Total** | **~13,714,000** | **100%** |

Founders still own ~73% combined. This is healthy at pre-seed.

### 4.4 After Seed Round ($2M at $10M Pre-Money)

Post-money = $10M + $2M = **$12M**
Investors receive: $2M / $12M = **16.7%**

Founders diluted from ~73% to: 73% × (1 - 16.7%) ≈ **60.8% combined**

### 4.5 Dilution Is Not the Enemy

The instinct to protect ownership percentage is natural but can be counterproductive. The question is not "how much do I own?" but "what is my stake worth?"

$$\text{Founder value} = \text{Ownership \%} \times \text{Company valuation}$$

10% of a $100M company = $10M.
50% of a $2M company = $1M.

Good dilution (investing in growth that increases valuation) is the goal. Bad dilution (giving equity for low-value investors, advisors, or in down-round terms) is what to avoid.

## 📚 5. The Fundraising Process

### 5.1 Preparation Before Outreach

1. **Build the deck:** 10 slides — Problem, Solution, Market, Product, Traction, Business Model, Team, Go-to-Market, Competition, Ask
2. **Prepare your data room:** Cap table, financials (or projections), MRR chart, churn data, unit economics
3. **Know your numbers cold:** MRR, growth rate, CAC, LTV, burn rate, runway, largest customers
4. **Write your 3-sentence pitch:**
   - "We're building [X] for [ICP] who struggle with [problem]."
   - "We've achieved [traction: MRR / users / growth rate]."
   - "We're raising [amount] to [specific use of funds]."

### 5.2 Finding Investors

| Method | Best for |
|--------|----------|
| **Warm introductions** | Best conversion — from portfolio founders, mutual connections |
| **AngelList / Wellfound** | Finding angels actively investing in your category |
| **YC Hacker News "Who is hiring/investing"** | Community-based discovery |
| **Attending events** | Seed-stage meetings; network quality matters |
| **Cold email to investors** | Works only with strong signal and perfect targeting |

**Rule:** Always pursue warm introductions first. A VC receives 5,000+ cold pitches per year; they meet 1–2% of them. A warm intro from a portfolio founder moves you to the top of the pile.

### 5.3 What Investors Say No To (And Why)

| Rejection reason | What it actually means |
|-----------------|----------------------|
| "Not the right fit" | You're outside their thesis / stage / sector |
| "Come back with more traction" | They like the space but need more evidence |
| "Team doesn't have enough experience" | Often code for "we don't believe you can execute" |
| "Market is too small" | They need a path to $1B+ outcome; your TAM doesn't support it |
| "We just invested in a competitor" | Real conflict — move on |

Most "no" answers are feedback on stage, traction, or fit — not on you personally. Build traction, then revisit.

## ⚠️ 7. Common Misconceptions

- **"Raising money is an achievement."** Raising is a means, not an end. Many companies that raised successfully went bankrupt anyway. Revenue is the real signal.
- **"Valuation = company value."** Valuation is a number agreed in a negotiation, not an appraisal of intrinsic value. Early-stage valuations especially are more fiction than calculation.
- **"Giving advisors 1–2% equity is normal."** It is unfortunately common, but it's expensive. Most advisors provide minimal recurring value. Cap advisor equity at 0.1–0.25%, vesting over 2 years. Only give more for operators who actively work with you monthly.
- **"Investors are selecting me."** The best deals have founders selecting investors, not the reverse. Your goal is to get multiple offers and choose the investor who adds the most value, not just take the first check.
- **"I should raise as much as possible."** More money = more dilution + more investor expectations. Raise what you need for the next 18–24 months with a clear plan for what you'll achieve. "We'll figure out how to spend it" is not a plan.
- **"I can close a SAFE in a day."** Legal review, wiring, counter-signing — expect 1–3 weeks minimum even with a pre-agreed SAFE. Include this in your fundraising timeline.
