# 42.2 — Nouns, Articles, Gender & Adjective Agreement

*Back to [Subject_Plan](Subject_Plan) | [← 42.1](42.1---Phonetics-&-The-Italian-Sound-System) | [42.3 →](42.3---Pronouns-—-Subject,-Object,-Reflexive-&-Ne-Ci)*

> **Chapter goal:** Master the Italian gender system, all seven definite article forms with their selection rules, indefinite and partitive articles, and the full adjective agreement system — the scaffolding of every Italian sentence.

---

## 1. Gender — Patterns & Prediction

Every Italian noun is either **masculine** or **feminine**. Unlike French, Italian gender is **highly predictable** from the noun ending:

### Default Gender Rules (~85% reliable)

| Ending | Default gender | Examples | Key exceptions |
|--------|---------------|---------|----------------|
| **-o** | Masculine | *il libro, il gatto, il vino, il figlio* | *la mano* (hand — fem!), *la foto* (< fotografia) |
| **-a** | Feminine | *la casa, la donna, la scuola, la pizza* | *il problema, il tema, il sistema, il poeta* (Greek -ma neuters) |
| **-e** | Either — must learn | *il fiore* (m), *la neve* (f), *il cane* (m), *la chiave* (f) | No rule — memorise with article |
| **-ione** | Feminine | *la nazione, la stazione, la conversazione* | Very reliable |
| **-tà / -tù** | Feminine | *la città, la libertà, la virtù* | Very reliable |
| **-ore** | Masculine | *il colore, il dottore, il professore* | *la signora* is not -ore |
| **-ista** | Both (agree by person) | *il / la pianista, il / la giornalista* | Depends on person referred to |

### Nouns Ending in Consonant (foreign loanwords)
Most are masculine: *il film, il bar, il computer, lo sport, lo stress*

### Plural Formation

| Singular ending | Plural | Example |
|----------------|--------|---------|
| -o (m.) | **-i** | *libro → libri* |
| -a (f.) | **-e** | *casa → case* |
| -e (m. or f.) | **-i** | *fiore → fiori; chiave → chiavi* |
| -ca / -ga | **-che / -ghe** | *amica → amiche; collega → colleghe* (preserve hard c/g) |
| -co / -go | **-chi / -ghi** or **-ci / -gi** | *fuoco → fuochi; amico → amici* (varies — must learn) |
| -cia / -gia | **-ce / -ge** (if c/g preceded by consonant) or **-cie / -gie** (if preceded by vowel) | *arancia → arance; camicia → camicie* |
| Accent on final vowel | Invariable | *città → città; caffè → caffè* |
| Foreign loanwords | Invariable | *il film → i film; il bar → i bar* |

## 2. The Definite Article — All 7 Forms

Italian has **seven** definite article forms. Selection depends on the **gender, number, and initial sound** of the following noun:

| | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Masculine plural | Feminine plural |
|--|-------------------|------------------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Before most consonants** | **il** | **la** | **i** | **le** |
| **Before vowel** | **l'** | **l'** | **gli** | **le** |
| **Before s+consonant, z, ps, gn, x, y, pn, sc+vowel** | **lo** | **la** | **gli** | **le** |

### The Lo/Gli Rule — When to Use

Use **lo** (m. sg.) / **gli** (m. pl.) before masculine nouns starting with:
- **s + consonant**: *lo studente, lo sport, gli studenti*
- **z**: *lo zaino, lo zero, gli zii*
- **ps**: *lo psicologo, gli psicologi*
- **gn**: *lo gnomo, gli gnomi*
- **x, y**: *lo xilofono, lo yogurt*
- **sc + vowel**: sometimes *lo scialle* (shawl)
- **Vowel**: *l'amico (sg.) → gli amici (pl.)*

> **Memory aid:** Lo/Gli mark nouns whose initial consonant cluster would be awkward to follow *il* — you can hear it: *"il studente"* sounds wrong in Italian; *"lo studente"* flows naturally.

### Definite Article Examples in Full

| Form | Example | Translation |
|------|---------|------------|
| *il* | *il libro* | the book |
| *lo* | *lo studente* | the student |
| *l'* (m.) | *l'amico* | the (male) friend |
| *la* | *la casa* | the house |
| *l'* (f.) | *l'amica* | the (female) friend |
| *i* | *i libri* | the books |
| *gli* | *gli studenti / gli amici* | the students / the friends |
| *le* | *le case* | the houses |

### Uses of the Definite Article (Different from English)

Italian uses the definite article where English omits it:
- **With body parts and clothing** (instead of possessive): *Mi lavo le mani.* (I wash my hands — lit. "I wash the hands to myself")
- **With languages**: *Parlo l'italiano.* / *Studio il francese.*
- **With titles before names** (speaking about, not to): *Il professore Rossi è bravo.* (But: *"Professore Rossi, buongiorno!"* — no article when addressing directly)
- **With days of the week for habitual actions**: *Il lunedì vado in palestra.* (On Mondays I go to the gym)
- **With abstract nouns**: *La libertà è importante.*
- **With geographic names**: *L'Italia è bellissima. Il Po è il fiume più lungo.*

## 3. The Indefinite Article — 4 Forms

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|--|-----------|---------|
| **Before most consonants** | **un** | **una** |
| **Before s+cons, z, ps, gn, x, y** (m.) or **vowel** (f.) | **uno** | **un'** |

| Form | Example | Translation |
|------|---------|------------|
| *un* | *un libro, un amico* | a book, a (male) friend |
| *uno* | *uno studente, uno zaino* | a student, a backpack |
| *una* | *una casa, una donna* | a house, a woman |
| *un'* | *un'amica, un'ora* | a (female) friend, an hour |

> After negation, the indefinite article is dropped: *Non ho un'auto.* → *Non ho l'auto.* (I don't have a/the car) or simply *Non ho macchina.* (I don't have a car — no article)

## 4. Partitive Articles — "Some / Any"

Formed with **di + definite article**. Used when English would say "some" with an uncountable noun or "some/any" with a plural:

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|--|-----------|---------|
| Singular | *del / dello / dell'* | *della / dell'* |
| Plural | *dei / degli* | *delle* |

| Form | Example | Translation |
|------|---------|------------|
| *del* | *Voglio del pane.* | I want some bread. |
| *dello* | *Bevo dello spumante.* | I'm drinking some sparkling wine. |
| *dell'* | *Ho dell'acqua.* | I have some water. |
| *della* | *Mangio della carne.* | I'm eating some meat. |
| *dei* | *Compro dei libri.* | I'm buying some books. |
| *degli* | *Ho degli amici.* | I have some friends. |
| *delle* | *Vorrei delle fragole.* | I'd like some strawberries. |

> In negative sentences and questions, partitive articles are often replaced by **niente / nessun(o/a)** or simply omitted: *Non ho soldi.* (I have no money.)

## 5. Preposition + Article Contractions

Italian mandatory contractions (like French *du, au*):

| Preposition | + il | + lo | + l' | + la | + i | + gli | + le |
|------------|------|------|------|------|-----|-------|------|
| **di** (of) | *del* | *dello* | *dell'* | *della* | *dei* | *degli* | *delle* |
| **a** (to/at) | *al* | *allo* | *all'* | *alla* | *ai* | *agli* | *alle* |
| **da** (from/by) | *dal* | *dallo* | *dall'* | *dalla* | *dai* | *dagli* | *dalle* |
| **in** (in) | *nel* | *nello* | *nell'* | *nella* | *nei* | *negli* | *nelle* |
| **su** (on) | *sul* | *sullo* | *sull'* | *sulla* | *sui* | *sugli* | *sulle* |

*Vado al mercato.* / *La chiave del garage.* / *Libro sulla scrivania.*

## 6. Adjective Agreement

Italian adjectives agree with the noun in **gender and number**. Most adjectives have four forms:

### Type 1: -o / -a / -i / -e (Four-form adjectives — most common)

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|--|-----------|---------|
| **Singular** | *bello* | *bella* |
| **Plural** | *belli* | *belle* |

Other examples: *piccolo/a/i/e, brutto/a/i/e, nuovo/a/i/e, vecchio/a/i/e, bianco/a/i/e*

### Type 2: -e / -i (Two-form adjectives — common for -e ending adjectives)

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|--|-----------|---------|
| **Singular** | *grande* | *grande* |
| **Plural** | *grandi* | *grandi* |

Other examples: *interessante/i, importante/i, intelligente/i, forte/i, verde/i, triste/i*

### Adjective Agreement Examples

*un libro vecchio* — an old book (m. sg.)
*una casa vecchia* — an old house (f. sg.)
*dei libri vecchi* — some old books (m. pl.)
*delle case vecchie* — some old houses (f. pl.)
*uno studente intelligente* — an intelligent student (m. sg., -e adj.)
*una studentessa intelligente* — an intelligent student (f. sg., same form)

## 7. Bello & Buono — Special Pre-Noun Forms

When placed **before** the noun, *bello* and *buono* take article-like forms mirroring the definite/indefinite article:

### *bello* before noun (beautiful/handsome/wonderful):

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|--|-----------|---------|
| Sg. before consonant | *bel* (like *il*) | *bella* |
| Sg. before s+cons, z etc. | *bello* (like *lo*) | *bella* |
| Sg. before vowel | *bell'* (like *l'*) | *bell'* |
| Pl. before consonant | *bei* (like *i*) | *belle* |
| Pl. before vowel/s+cons | *begli* (like *gli*) | *belle* |

*un bel ragazzo / un bello studente / un bell'uomo / dei bei ragazzi / degli begli uomini*

> When **after** the noun or with *essere*, *bello* uses regular four-form agreement: *Il ragazzo è bello. / Che ragazzi belli!*

### *buono* before noun (good):
Mirrors the indefinite article:
- *un buon amico* / *un buon libro* / *un buono studente* / *una buona amica* / *una buon'idea*

## 8. Adjective Placement

**Default: adjectives follow the noun** in Italian (opposite of English):
*una macchina veloce* (a fast car) / *un vino rosso* (a red wine) / *una persona interessante*

**Adjectives that commonly precede the noun** (subjective, evaluative, frequently-used):
*bello, brutto, buono, cattivo, grande, piccolo, giovane, vecchio, nuovo, lungo, breve, caro*

**Meaning shifts with placement:**

| Before noun | After noun |
|------------|-----------|
| *un grand'uomo* — a great man | *un uomo grande* — a big/tall man |
| *un vecchio amico* — a long-time friend | *un amico vecchio* — an elderly friend |
| *la mia povera nonna* — my poor (unfortunate) grandmother | *la mia nonna povera* — my poor (penniless) grandmother |
| *un certo problema* — a certain (specific) problem | *una cosa certa* — a certain (sure) thing |
| *un semplice malinteso* — a mere misunderstanding | *una soluzione semplice* — a simple solution |

---

## 9. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **Forgetting *la mano* is feminine:** One of the few -o nouns that is feminine. *la mano → le mani*.
2. **Using *il* before s+consonant:** *"il studente"* → wrong. *"lo studente."* The lo/gli rule must become automatic.
3. **Not contracting prepositions:** *"Ho un libro di il professore"* → wrong. *"...del professore."* Contractions are obligatory.
4. **Treating -e adjectives as invariable for gender:** *grande* is the same for m. and f. singular (that's correct), but students sometimes also fail to add -i for plural: *"degli uomini grande"* → wrong. *"degli uomini grandi."*
5. **Using bello's post-noun form before the noun:** *"un bello ragazzo"* → wrong. *"un bel ragazzo."*
