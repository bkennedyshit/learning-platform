## 1. Bone Tissue Biology

### 1.1 Bone as Living Tissue
Bone is **dynamic connective tissue** — continuously remodeled throughout life by two cell types:
- **Osteoblasts** — build new bone matrix (deposit collagen + mineralize with hydroxyapatite Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂)
- **Osteoclasts** — resorb bone (secrete acids + proteases to dissolve matrix)
- **Osteocytes** — mature osteoblasts trapped in lacunae; maintain matrix; serve as mechanosensors

> **Wolff's Law:** Bone remodels in response to mechanical stress — it gets denser where stressed, resorbs where unloaded. This is why astronauts lose bone in microgravity and athletes gain it.

### 1.2 Compact vs. Spongy Bone

| Feature | Compact (Cortical) | Spongy (Cancellous/Trabecular) |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| Location | Outer shell of all bones; shaft of long bones | Epiphyses of long bones; flat bones |
| Structure | Osteons (Haversian systems) | Trabeculae (lattice of bony struts) |
| Function | Strength, protection, lever mechanics | Lightweight strength; houses red marrow |
| Bone marrow | Yellow marrow in medullary cavity | Red marrow in trabeculae (hematopoiesis) |

### 1.3 Long Bone Anatomy
- **Diaphysis** — shaft; compact bone cylinder around medullary cavity
- **Epiphysis** (proximal + distal) — ends; spongy bone core + articular cartilage cap
- **Epiphyseal plate (growth plate)** — hyaline cartilage; site of longitudinal growth; becomes **epiphyseal line** in adults
- **Periosteum** — fibrous membrane covering outer surface; attachment site for tendons/ligaments; contains osteoblasts and nerve/blood supply
- **Endosteum** — thin membrane lining medullary cavity and trabecular surfaces

## 2. Skeletal Divisions: Axial vs. Appendicular

**Total: 206 bones** (adults). At birth: ~270 — many fuse during development.

### 2.1 Axial Skeleton (80 bones)

#### Skull (22 bones)
**Cranial bones (8):**
| Bone | Count | Key landmark |
|------|-------|-------------|
| Frontal | 1 | Supraorbital foramen |
| Parietal | 2 | Coronal, sagittal, lambdoid sutures |
| Temporal | 2 | External acoustic meatus, mastoid process, styloid process |
| Occipital | 1 | Foramen magnum, occipital condyles |
| Sphenoid | 1 | Sella turcica (holds pituitary), greater/lesser wings, pterygoid processes |
| Ethmoid | 1 | Cribriform plate (CN I passes through), crista galli, perpendicular plate |

**Facial bones (14):** Mandible (1), maxilla (2), nasal (2), zygomatic (2), lacrimal (2), palatine (2), inferior nasal conchae (2), vomer (1)

#### Vertebral Column (26 bones)
| Region | Count | Key features |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| Cervical | 7 (C1–C7) | C1 = atlas (no body, holds skull); C2 = axis (odontoid/dens); C3–C7 = typical; C7 = vertebra prominens |
| Thoracic | 12 (T1–T12) | Costal facets for rib articulation |
| Lumbar | 5 (L1–L5) | Largest bodies; lordosis |
| Sacrum | 1 (5 fused) | Sacral foramina; articulates with ilium at SI joint |
| Coccyx | 1 (4 fused) | Vestigial tail; attachment for pelvic floor muscles |

**Curvatures:** cervical (lordosis) → thoracic (kyphosis) → lumbar (lordosis) → sacral (kyphosis). Primary curves = thoracic/sacral (fetal position). Secondary curves = cervical (when infant lifts head) / lumbar (when standing).

#### Thoracic Cage (25 bones)
- 12 pairs of ribs (24) + sternum (1)
- **True ribs** (1–7): articulate directly with sternum via costal cartilage
- **False ribs** (8–10): cartilage joins rib 7's cartilage (not sternum directly)
- **Floating ribs** (11–12): no anterior attachment
- **Sternum:** manubrium + body + xiphoid process. Sternal angle (Louis) = T4/T5 disc, 2nd rib articulation.

### 2.2 Appendicular Skeleton (126 bones)

#### Pectoral (Shoulder) Girdle (4 bones)
- Clavicle (2) — S-shaped; only bony connection between upper limb and axial skeleton; most commonly fractured bone
- Scapula (2) — floating bone; glenoid cavity (shoulder socket); acromion; coracoid process; spine of scapula

#### Upper Limb (60 bones total, 30 per side)
| Bone | Count per side | Key landmarks |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| Humerus | 1 | Greater/lesser tubercles; surgical neck (axillary nerve); anatomical neck; medial/lateral epicondyles; olecranon fossa |
| Radius | 1 | Head (proximal); radial tuberosity; styloid process (distal) |
| Ulna | 1 | Olecranon (proximal); trochlear notch; styloid process (distal) |
| Carpals | 8 | Scaphoid, lunate, triquetrum, pisiform (proximal row); trapezium, trapezoid, capitate, hamate (distal row). "Some Lovers Try Positions That They Cannot Handle." |
| Metacarpals | 5 | Numbered I–V from thumb |
| Phalanges | 14 | Proximal, middle, distal (thumb has only proximal + distal) |

#### Pelvic (Hip) Girdle (2 bones)
- Each **os coxae (hip bone)** = ilium + ischium + pubis (fuse at pubic symphysis and acetabulum by age 23)
- **Ilium:** iliac crest, ASIS, AIIS, PSIS, greater sciatic notch
- **Ischium:** ischial tuberosity ("sitting bones"), lesser sciatic notch, ischial spine
- **Pubis:** pubic symphysis (fibrocartilaginous joint), pubic tubercle, obturator foramen
- **Acetabulum:** socket for femoral head; formed by all three bones

> **Male vs. female pelvis:** Female pelvis is broader, shallower, with wider pubic arch (>90°) and larger pelvic outlet for childbirth. Male pelvis: narrower, deeper, pubic arch <90°.

#### Lower Limb (60 bones total, 30 per side)
| Bone | Count per side | Key landmarks |
|------|---------------|--------------|
| Femur | 1 | Head, neck (angle ~126°); greater/lesser trochanter; linea aspera; medial/lateral condyles; intercondylar notch |
| Patella | 1 | Sesamoid bone in quadriceps tendon |
| Tibia | 1 | Medial condyle, tibial plateau, tibial tuberosity (patellar tendon insertion); medial malleolus |
| Fibula | 1 | Lateral malleolus; weight-bearing minimal; most commonly fractured in ankle injuries |
| Tarsals | 7 | Calcaneus (largest), talus (articulates with tibia/fibula), navicular, cuboid, 3 cuneiforms |
| Metatarsals | 5 | Numbered I–V from medial |
| Phalanges | 14 | Same arrangement as hand; great toe = hallux |

## 4. Joint Classification

### 4.1 By Structural Type

| Type | Connective tissue | Movement | Examples |
|------|-----------------|----------|---------|
| **Fibrous** | Dense CT / fibrous tissue | None (synarthrosis) to slight | Skull sutures, distal tibiofibular joint, gomphoses (teeth) |
| **Cartilaginous** | Hyaline or fibrocartilage | Slight (amphiarthrosis) | Epiphyseal plates (hyaline), pubic symphysis + intervertebral discs (fibrocartilage) |
| **Synovial** | Synovial capsule + fluid | Free (diarthrosis) | Most limb joints |

### 4.2 Synovial Joint Subtypes (by movement)

| Type | Degrees of freedom | Movement | Example |
|------|--------------------|----------|---------|
| **Hinge** | 1 | Flexion/extension only | Elbow (humeroulnar), knee (primarily), interphalangeal |
| **Pivot** | 1 | Rotation only | Atlantoaxial (C1–C2), proximal radioulnar |
| **Condyloid (ellipsoidal)** | 2 | Flexion/extension + abduction/adduction | Wrist (radiocarpal), MCP joints |
| **Saddle** | 2 | Same as condyloid + more freedom | 1st carpometacarpal (thumb) |
| **Ball-and-socket** | 3 | Flexion/extension + ab/adduction + rotation (circumduction) | Shoulder (glenohumeral), hip (coxal) |
| **Gliding (plane)** | 2 (translation) | Sliding in any direction | Acromioclavicular, intercarpal, intertarsal |

## 5. Common Clinical Correlates

| Condition | Structure affected | Mechanism |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------|
| **Clavicle fracture** | Clavicle (mid-third most common) | Fall on outstretched hand (FOOSH) |
| **Colles fracture** | Distal radius | FOOSH; "dinner fork" deformity |
| **Scaphoid fracture** | Scaphoid (carpal) | FOOSH; anatomical snuffbox tenderness; risk of avascular necrosis |
| **Femoral neck fracture** | Femoral neck | Osteoporotic fall in elderly; disrupts blood supply (retinacular vessels) → AVN risk |
| **Pott fracture** | Fibula ± medial malleolus | Ankle inversion/eversion trauma |
| **Compression fracture** | Vertebral body (T12, L1 most common) | Osteoporosis; axial load |
| **Stress fracture** | Tibia, metatarsals | Repetitive loading; military marching, runners |
| **Osteoporosis** | Systemic bone loss | Low BMD; estrogen deficiency; calcium/Vit D deficiency |
