# Subject Syllabus: 15 - Geometry

*Back to [Learning Index](00---09---Learning-Index) · [9-12 Pipeline](9-12-High-School-Pipeline)*

Geometry is the only K-12 course that teaches **formal deductive proof** — the bedrock of all mathematical reasoning. Every other math course teaches students to compute; geometry teaches them to *argue from axioms.* The proof-writing skill transfers to computer science (formal verification, algorithm correctness proofs), philosophy (logical argument), and all rigorous academic writing.

The course has two equally important halves: (1) spatial intuition — seeing and reasoning about shapes in 2D and 3D — and (2) formal logic — writing proofs from first principles. Neither half alone is sufficient.

## 📝 3. Chapter Outline

| # | Chapter | Core skill |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 15.1 | **Foundations of Geometry** | Undefined terms (point, line, plane); defined terms (segment, ray, angle); postulates and theorems; angle classification and measurement; segment and angle bisectors; midpoint and distance formulas; algebraic proof of segment/angle relationships. |
| 15.2 | **Reasoning & Proof** | Inductive reasoning and conjectures; counterexamples; deductive reasoning; law of detachment and syllogism; logical connectives (and, or, not, if-then, biconditional); converse, inverse, contrapositive; two-column proof structure; proof by contradiction. |
| 15.3 | **Parallel & Perpendicular Lines** | Parallel lines and transversals; angle pair relationships (corresponding, alternate interior/exterior, co-interior/consecutive); proving lines parallel; perpendicular lines and their properties; distance from point to line; slope connections to parallelism/perpendicularity. |
| 15.4 | **Triangles** | Triangle sum theorem; exterior angle theorem; triangle inequality theorem; classifying triangles; congruence postulates/theorems (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, HL); corresponding parts of congruent triangles (CPCTC); isosceles triangle theorem; medians, altitudes, perpendicular bisectors, angle bisectors; triangle centers (centroid, circumcenter, incenter, orthocenter). |
| 15.5 | **Similarity & Proportions** | Similar polygons; scale factor; AA similarity postulate; SAS and SSS similarity theorems; proportionality theorems (side-splitter, triangle midsegment); dilations (center, scale factor, properties); applications to maps and scale drawings. Cross-link: [44.1 - Ratios, Rates & Proportions](44.1---Ratios,-Rates-&-Proportions) |
| 15.6 | **Right Triangles & Trigonometry** | Pythagorean theorem (proof and converse); Pythagorean triples; special right triangles (45-45-90 and 30-60-90 — exact values); trigonometric ratios (SOH-CAH-TOA); finding missing sides and angles; angle of elevation and depression; the Law of Sines and Law of Cosines (intro). Cross-link: [47.4 - Trigonometry](47.4---Trigonometry) |
| 15.7 | **Circles** | Parts of a circle (chord, secant, tangent, arc, sector); central and inscribed angle theorems; arc length and sector area; tangent line properties; power of a point; equation of a circle (x-h)² + (y-k)² = r²; circle proofs. |
| 15.8 | **Area, Volume & Coordinate Geometry** | 2D area review (all polygons, circles); 3D figures — prisms, pyramids, cylinders, cones, spheres; cross-sections; surface area and volume formulas; Cavalieri's principle; coordinate proofs of geometric theorems; transformations (translation, rotation, reflection, dilation) in the coordinate plane. |

- **45 min/day, 5 days/week:** Full year course; complete in ~36 weeks
- **Key tools used every session:** GeoGebra for every construction and discovery exercise; physical compass + straightedge for proof constructions
