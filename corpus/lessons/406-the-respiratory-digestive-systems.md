## Part A: Respiratory System

## 1. Structural Organization

### 1.1 Upper Airway
| Structure | Key anatomy |
|-----------|------------|
| **Nasal cavity** | Lined by respiratory epithelium (pseudostratified ciliated columnar); turbinates (conchae) increase surface area for warming/humidifying/filtering; olfactory epithelium in roof |
| **Pharynx** | Nasopharynx (behind nasal cavity; Eustachian tube opens here; adenoids) → Oropharynx (palatine tonsils) → Laryngopharynx (hypopharynx; opens to esophagus and larynx) |
| **Larynx** | Thyroid cartilage (Adam's apple); cricoid cartilage (only complete ring); epiglottis (elastic cartilage; closes during swallowing); vocal folds (true vocal cords — vocalis muscle + vocal ligament; false vocal cords above); glottis = the opening between true cords + the space between them |

### 1.2 Lower Airway
```
Trachea (C-rings of cartilage; 10–12 cm; divides at carina at T4/T5 = sternal angle)
  ├── Right main bronchus (wider, shorter, more vertical → aspirated objects go right)
  │   ├── Right upper lobe bronchus (3 segments)
  │   ├── Right middle lobe bronchus (2 segments)
  │   └── Right lower lobe bronchus (5 segments)
  └── Left main bronchus (narrower, longer, more horizontal)
      ├── Left upper lobe bronchus (includes lingula; 4 segments)
      └── Left lower lobe bronchus (4 segments)

Lobar bronchi → Segmental bronchi → Subsegmental → Bronchioles → 
Terminal bronchioles → Respiratory bronchioles → Alveolar ducts → Alveolar sacs → Alveoli
```

> **Cartilage disappears** at bronchioles (no cartilage). Bronchioles rely on tethering by surrounding lung tissue — this is lost in emphysema, causing air trapping.

### 1.3 Lung Anatomy
| Feature | Right | Left |
|---------|-------|------|
| Lobes | 3 (upper, middle, lower) | 2 (upper, lower; lingula = part of upper) |
| Fissures | Horizontal (between upper and middle) + oblique (between middle and lower) | Oblique only |
| Special features | Cardiac notch absent | Cardiac notch + lingula accommodate the heart |

**Pleura:**
- **Visceral pleura** — adherent to lung surface; no sensory innervation (cutting it is painless)
- **Parietal pleura** — lines thoracic wall, diaphragm, mediastinum; sensory innervation (pain with pleuritis)
- **Pleural cavity** — potential space with ~5–10 mL serous fluid; negative pressure keeps lungs expanded

### 1.4 Gas Exchange
- **Alveoli:** ~300 million; ~70 m² total surface; wall = single layer of Type I pneumocytes (gas exchange) + Type II pneumocytes (surfactant production)
- **Respiratory membrane:** alveolar epithelium + basement membrane + capillary endothelium (~0.5 μm thick)
- **Surfactant:** phospholipid (DPPC) produced by Type II cells; reduces surface tension; prevents alveolar collapse (atelectasis); absent in premature infants → Respiratory Distress Syndrome (RDS)

### 1.5 Spirometry Values
| Volume / Capacity | Definition | ~Normal (70 kg male) |
|-------------------|-----------|---------------------|
| **TV (tidal volume)** | Volume per normal breath | 500 mL |
| **IRV** | Max extra volume inhaled above TV | 3,000 mL |
| **ERV** | Max extra volume exhaled below TV | 1,200 mL |
| **RV (residual volume)** | Volume remaining after max exhalation | 1,200 mL |
| **TLC (total lung capacity)** | TV + IRV + ERV + RV | 6,000 mL |
| **VC (vital capacity)** | TLC − RV | 4,800 mL |
| **FRC (functional residual capacity)** | ERV + RV | 2,400 mL |
| **FEV1** | Volume exhaled in 1st second of forced exhalation | >80% of FVC |

> **Obstructive vs. Restrictive:**
> - **Obstructive** (COPD, asthma): ↓FEV1/FVC ratio; FEV1 disproportionately reduced
> - **Restrictive** (fibrosis, obesity): ↓TLC; FEV1/FVC ratio normal or increased

### 1.6 Respiratory Muscles
- **Diaphragm** — primary muscle of inspiration; descends on contraction → ↑thoracic volume → ↓intrathoracic pressure → air flows in. Innervated by **phrenic nerve** (C3, C4, C5 — "C3, 4, 5 keep the diaphragm alive")
- **External intercostals** — inspiration; elevate ribs
- **Internal intercostals** — forced expiration
- **Accessory muscles** (scalenes, SCM, pectoralis minor) — recruited in respiratory distress

## Part B: Digestive System

## 2. GI Tract Wall Layers (Deep to Superficial)

```
Mucosa         → epithelium + lamina propria + muscularis mucosae
Submucosa      → loose CT; blood/lymph vessels; Meissner's (submucosal) plexus
Muscularis externa → inner circular + outer longitudinal smooth muscle; Auerbach's (myenteric) plexus
Serosa / Adventitia → outer coat (serosa = visceral peritoneum in intraperitoneal organs; adventitia = fibrous CT in retroperitoneal organs)
```

---

## 3. GI Tract — Station by Station

### Mouth
- Mechanical breakdown (mastication) + chemical (salivary amylase starts CHO digestion)
- **Parotid** (CN IX → auriculotemporal nerve → stensen duct → opposite upper 2nd molar), **submandibular** (CN VII → chorda tympani → wharton duct → floor of mouth), **sublingual** glands

### Pharynx → Esophagus
- **Upper esophageal sphincter** (UES) = cricopharyngeus muscle (striated, voluntary)
- Esophagus: 25 cm; 3 anatomical constrictions (CP muscle at C6, aortic arch at T4, left main bronchus/diaphragm hiatus at T10)
- **Lower esophageal sphincter** (LES) — physiological; prevents reflux; incompetent → GERD

### Stomach
- **Regions:** cardia, fundus, body, antrum, pylorus
- **Layers:** + oblique muscle layer (3 total → churning; other GI has 2)
- **Functions:** mechanical churning, HCl secretion (chief cells → pepsinogen; parietal cells → HCl + intrinsic factor), chyme formation
- **Pyloric sphincter** — regulates gastric emptying into duodenum

### Small Intestine (~6–7 m)
| Segment | Length | Key features |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| **Duodenum** | 25 cm (C-shaped) | Fixed, mostly retroperitoneal; receives bile + pancreatic juice at **ampulla of Vater** (major duodenal papilla); superior to ligament of Treitz |
| **Jejunum** | ~2.5 m | Prominent plicae circulares, villi, microvilli (brush border) → maximum absorption |
| **Ileum** | ~3.5 m | Fewer plicae, Peyer's patches; terminal ileum absorbs B12 + bile salts (specific) |

**Absorption adaptations:** plicae circulares × villi × microvilli = 600× increase in surface area (~200 m²)

### Large Intestine (~1.5 m)
```
Cecum (with appendix at McBurney's point: 1/3 along line from ASIS to umbilicus) → 
Ascending colon → Hepatic flexure → Transverse colon → Splenic flexure → 
Descending colon → Sigmoid colon → Rectum → Anal canal (internal [smooth, involuntary] + external [striated, voluntary] sphincter)
```
- **Function:** water reabsorption, electrolyte absorption, vitamin K production (gut bacteria), feces formation and storage
- **Teniae coli** — 3 longitudinal bands of smooth muscle; gather colon into haustra (sacculations)

## 4. Accessory Organs

### Liver
- **Location:** right hypochondriac + epigastric regions
- **Gross anatomy:** 4 lobes (right — largest; left; caudate; quadrate)
- **Functional anatomy:** 8 Couinaud segments (each with its own blood supply + bile duct → independent surgical resection)
- **Portal triad** (at each corner of hepatic lobule): portal venule + hepatic arteriole + bile ductule
- **Blood supply:** portal vein (75%, nutrient-rich, low O₂) + hepatic artery proper (25%, high O₂)
- **Functions:** metabolism (glucose, lipid, protein), detoxification, bile production, clotting factors synthesis, storage (glycogen, Vit A/D/E/K/B12)

### Gallbladder
- Sits in gallbladder fossa on visceral surface of right liver lobe
- Concentrates and stores bile (produced by liver)
- **Cystic duct** → joins **common hepatic duct** → **common bile duct (CBD)** → enters duodenum at ampulla of Vater with main pancreatic duct
- Murphy's sign — inspiratory arrest during right subcostal palpation → cholecystitis

### Pancreas
- Retroperitoneal; head nestled in duodenal C-loop; body crosses spine; tail reaches spleen
- **Exocrine** (99%): acinar cells secrete digestive enzymes (amylase, lipase, proteases as zymogens activated in duodenum); ductal cells secrete bicarbonate
- **Endocrine** (1%): Islets of Langerhans — α cells (glucagon), β cells (insulin), δ cells (somatostatin)

---

## 5. Peritoneal Relationships

- **Intraperitoneal** (covered by peritoneum): stomach, small intestine (except duodenum), transverse colon, sigmoid colon, spleen, liver, gallbladder
- **Retroperitoneal (primary)**: kidneys, adrenal glands, aorta, IVC
- **Retroperitoneal (secondary)**: duodenum (2nd–4th parts), ascending/descending colon, pancreas (head/body)
- **Mesentery** = double layer of peritoneum connecting intraperitoneal organs to posterior abdominal wall; carries blood vessels + nerves

---

## 6. Clinical Correlates

| Condition | Structure | Key anatomy |
|-----------|----------|-------------|
| **Appendicitis** | Appendix | McBurney's point tenderness; Rovsing's sign; perforates → peritonitis |
| **Cholecystitis** | Gallbladder | Murphy's sign; gallstones impacting cystic duct |
| **Pyloric stenosis** | Pyloric sphincter | Hypertrophy in infants; projectile non-bilious vomiting; palpable "olive" |
| **Pneumothorax** | Pleural space | Air entry → lung collapse; decreased breath sounds + resonance; tension PTX is emergency |
| **Pulmonary embolism** | Pulmonary artery branches | DVT source; sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, hypoxia |
| **Hiatal hernia** | Esophageal hiatus (T10) | Sliding (most common) vs. paraesophageal; GERD |
