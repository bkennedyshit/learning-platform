# 🛤️ Anatomy — Learning Path

> This document is **hand-curated** and should remain stable.

---

## 📋 Prerequisites

Before starting this track, you should have:

1. **Biology foundations helpful** — cell biology and tissue types from [Track 15 Biology](Subject_Plan) (chapters 02.1) will accelerate chapter 40.1. Not strictly required.
2. **Basic chemistry** — understanding pH, ions, and osmosis helps in chapters 40.5–40.7 (cardiovascular, urinary). See [Track 16 Chemistry](Subject_Plan) if needed.
3. **A spaced-repetition tool** — Anki (free). Anatomy has the vocabulary load of a foreign language; SR is non-negotiable.
4. **Access to a 3D anatomy tool** — Zygote Body (free web) or Kenhub (free tier). Spatial memory cannot be built from text alone.
5. **Notebook and pen** — label-blank practice is the single highest-yield anatomy study technique. Physical drawing matters.

## 📖 Recommended Chapter Order

### Phase 1: Framework & Structure (Weeks 1–5)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale | Est. Time |
|-------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| 1 | [40.1 - Anatomical Terminology & Body Organization](40.1---Anatomical-Terminology-&-Body-Organization) | The shared language — without directional terms and body planes, nothing else is describable | 1 week |
| 2 | [40.2 - The Skeletal System](40.2---The-Skeletal-System) | The scaffolding of the body; everything else attaches to it | 2 weeks |
| 3 | [40.3 - The Muscular System](40.3---The-Muscular-System) | Builds directly on skeletal landmarks (origins and insertions) | 2 weeks |

**Phase 1 deliverable:** Label all 206 bones on a blank diagram. Name origin, insertion, and action for all major muscle groups from memory. Correctly use all directional terms in clinical descriptions.

### Phase 2: Control & Transport Systems (Weeks 6–10)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 4 | [40.4 - The Nervous System](40.4---The-Nervous-System) | The control system — longest and most complex chapter; do not rush it |
| 5 | [40.5 - The Cardiovascular & Lymphatic System](40.5---The-Cardiovascular-&-Lymphatic-System) | The transport system — builds on nervous system (ANS control of heart rate) |

**Phase 2 deliverable:** Name and describe function of all 12 cranial nerves. Trace a red blood cell from the right atrium through the pulmonary circuit and back through the systemic circuit to the aorta. Locate all major pulse points.

### Phase 3: Organ Systems (Weeks 11–14)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 6 | [40.6 - The Respiratory & Digestive Systems](40.6---The-Respiratory-&-Digestive-Systems) | Two systems often taught together; respiratory builds on cardiovascular (gas exchange) |
| 7 | [40.7 - The Endocrine, Urinary & Reproductive Systems](40.7---The-Endocrine,-Urinary-&-Reproductive-Systems) | Hormonal and filtration systems — endocrine integrates all previous systems |

**Phase 3 deliverable:** Trace airflow from nostril to alveolus and describe gas exchange. Trace a molecule from glomerular filtration to urine excretion through the full nephron.

### Phase 4: Regional & Clinical Integration (Weeks 15–18)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 8 | [40.8 - Regional & Clinical Anatomy](40.8---Regional-&-Clinical-Anatomy) | The integration pass — revisit everything spatially, the way clinicians use it |

**Phase 4 deliverable:** Given a clinical scenario (e.g., "patient has wrist drop after humeral shaft fracture"), identify the damaged structure, its anatomical course, and expected deficits. Navigate the mediastinum, brachial plexus, and femoral triangle from memory.

## 🔀 Non-Negotiable Daily Habits (Every Phase)

| Habit | Time | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|
| Anki anatomy deck review | 15–20 min | Never skip — bone/muscle/nerve names decay fast without SR |
| Label-blank practice | 10–15 min | One diagram per session; draw from memory before checking |
| 3D spatial review | 10 min | Zygote Body or Kenhub — rotate structures in 3D once per study session |
| Clinical correlate reflection | 5 min | "What goes wrong when X is damaged?" — anchors memory to meaning |

> **The anatomy memorization trap:** Passive re-reading of anatomy notes feels productive but builds almost no durable memory. Every session must include active recall — labeling, tracing, naming without prompts. The Anki + label-blank combo is what actually works.

## ➡️ What Comes Next

After completing this anatomy track:

1. **Physiology depth** — anatomy is structure; physiology is function. Pick up OpenStax *Anatomy and Physiology 2e* chapters on specific system physiology for any system you want deeper functional understanding of.
2. **Pathology** — once you know normal anatomy, pathology becomes "what goes wrong and why." Robbins *Basic Pathology* (library) or Pathoma (YouTube) are excellent next steps.
3. **Radiology basics** — Radiopaedia.org; learn to read X-rays, CT cross-sections, and MRIs using your regional anatomy knowledge from chapter 40.8.
4. **Track 34 Biomechanics** — [Biomechanics](Subject_Plan) builds directly on the musculoskeletal system; the physics of movement becomes far richer with clinical anatomy depth.
5. **Track 05 Neuroscience** — [Neuroscience](Subject_Plan) at the computational level; chapter 40.4 is the prerequisite anatomy pass.

---

## 🚫 What This Track Does NOT Cover

- **Histology in depth** — microscopic anatomy of tissue types is introduced in 40.1 but not exhaustively covered. Moore's *Clinical Anatomy* or a dedicated histology atlas covers this layer.
- **Embryology** — developmental anatomy (how structures form) is referenced in clinical correlates but not a primary focus.
- **Physiology at the molecular level** — this track teaches structure + function at the organ/system level; biochemical/molecular physiology is in [Track 15 Biology](Subject_Plan) and [Track 16 Chemistry](Subject_Plan).
- **Clinical diagnosis** — anatomy is the foundation of clinical medicine, not medicine itself. This track gives you the map; diagnosis requires additional clinical training.
