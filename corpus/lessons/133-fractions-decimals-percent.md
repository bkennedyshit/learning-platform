# 13.3 - Fractions, Decimals & Percent

Fractions, decimals, and percents are three notations for the same rational numbers. A recipe might call for three-eighths of a cup; a calculator shows the same amount as 0.375 cup; as a percent it is 37.5% of a cup. All three name the same amount. This lesson reviews the four fraction operations, shows how to move between the three notations, explains when a decimal terminates, and introduces scientific notation for very large and very small numbers.

## Prerequisites

You need the sign rules from [13.2 - The Number System](/132-the-number-system), multiplication facts, and the idea of an equivalent fraction. Finding a least common denominator uses multiples and prime factors; percent as a ratio out of $100$ was introduced in [13.1 - Ratios, Rates & Proportions](/131-ratios-rates-proportions).

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. Compute sums, differences, products, and quotients of fractions and mixed numbers and simplify the result.
2. Convert any fraction to a decimal and a percent, and decide from its denominator whether the decimal terminates.
3. Rewrite a repeating decimal as a fraction and order a list that mixes fractions, decimals, and percents.
4. Write numbers in scientific notation and compute products and quotients in that form.

## Core ideas

### Fraction operations

- **Add or subtract:** rewrite with a common denominator (the least common denominator keeps numbers small), then add or subtract the numerators. $\tfrac{a}{d} + \tfrac{b}{d} = \tfrac{a + b}{d}$.
- **Multiply:** multiply numerators and multiply denominators, $\tfrac{a}{b} \cdot \tfrac{c}{d} = \tfrac{ac}{bd}$. You can simplify common factors before multiplying.
- **Divide:** multiply by the reciprocal, $\tfrac{a}{b} \div \tfrac{c}{d} = \tfrac{a}{b} \cdot \tfrac{d}{c}$ for $c \ne 0$. Division asks "how many groups of $\tfrac{c}{d}$ fit in $\tfrac{a}{b}$?"
- **Mixed numbers:** a mixed number such as $2\tfrac{1}{2}$ means $2 + \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{5}{2}$. Convert to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing. For subtraction you may either convert or regroup (borrow $1$ whole).

### Converting among forms

- **Fraction to decimal:** divide the numerator by the denominator. $\tfrac{3}{8} = 3 \div 8 = 0.375$.
- **Decimal to percent:** multiply by $100$ (move the decimal point two places right). $0.375 = 37.5\%$.
- **Percent to decimal:** divide by $100$. $0.5\% = 0.005$.
- **Terminating decimal to fraction:** read the place value. $0.125 = \tfrac{125}{1000} = \tfrac{1}{8}$.

### Terminating or repeating?

A fraction in **lowest terms** has a terminating decimal exactly when its denominator has no prime factors other than $2$ and $5$. That is because a terminating decimal is a fraction with denominator $10^n = 2^n 5^n$. Any other prime factor, such as $3$ in $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$, produces a repeating decimal. Long division must repeat because only finitely many remainders are possible. A bar marks the repeating block: $\tfrac{5}{12} = 0.41\overline{6}$.

### Scientific notation

A number is in **scientific notation** when it is written $a \times 10^n$ with $1 \le a \lt 10$ and $n$ an integer. Large numbers have positive exponents ($3{,}200{,}000 = 3.2 \times 10^6$); small positive numbers have negative exponents ($0.000045 = 4.5 \times 10^{-5}$). To multiply, multiply the coefficients and add the exponents; to divide, divide the coefficients and subtract the exponents. Then adjust the coefficient so it is between $1$ and $10$.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Adding and subtracting fractions

1. $\tfrac{2}{3} + \tfrac{3}{4}$: the least common denominator is $12$. $\tfrac{8}{12} + \tfrac{9}{12} = \tfrac{17}{12} = 1\tfrac{5}{12}$.
2. $\tfrac{5}{6} - \tfrac{1}{4}$: the least common denominator is $12$. $\tfrac{10}{12} - \tfrac{3}{12} = \tfrac{7}{12}$.

### Example 2: Multiplying and dividing

1. $2\tfrac{1}{2} \cdot 1\tfrac{1}{3} = \tfrac{5}{2} \cdot \tfrac{4}{3} = \tfrac{20}{6} = \tfrac{10}{3} = 3\tfrac{1}{3}$.
2. $\tfrac{3}{4} \div \tfrac{3}{8} = \tfrac{3}{4} \cdot \tfrac{8}{3} = \tfrac{24}{12} = 2$. Interpretation: two pieces of size $\tfrac{3}{8}$ fit into $\tfrac{3}{4}$, since $\tfrac{3}{8} + \tfrac{3}{8} = \tfrac{6}{8} = \tfrac{3}{4}$.

### Example 3: Subtracting mixed numbers with regrouping

Find $4\tfrac{1}{5} - 1\tfrac{2}{3}$.

1. Common denominator $15$: $4\tfrac{3}{15} - 1\tfrac{10}{15}$.
2. $\tfrac{3}{15}$ is less than $\tfrac{10}{15}$, so regroup one whole: $4\tfrac{3}{15} = 3\tfrac{18}{15}$.
3. Subtract: $3\tfrac{18}{15} - 1\tfrac{10}{15} = 2\tfrac{8}{15}$.
4. Check with improper fractions: $\tfrac{63}{15} - \tfrac{25}{15} = \tfrac{38}{15} = 2\tfrac{8}{15}$.

### Example 4: Conversions and decimal type

1. $\tfrac{3}{8}$: $8 = 2^3$, so the decimal terminates. $\tfrac{3}{8} = 0.375 = 37.5\%$.
2. $\tfrac{5}{12}$: $12 = 2^2 \cdot 3$ includes $3$, so the decimal repeats. $5 \div 12 = 0.41666\ldots = 0.41\overline{6} \approx 41.7\%$.

### Example 5: Repeating decimal to fraction

Write $0.\overline{36}$ as a fraction.

1. Let $x = 0.363636\ldots$
2. The repeating block has two digits, so multiply by $100$: $100x = 36.363636\ldots$
3. Subtract: $100x - x = 36$, so $99x = 36$ and $x = \tfrac{36}{99} = \tfrac{4}{11}$.
4. Check: $4 \div 11 = 0.3636\ldots$

### Example 6: Scientific notation

1. $3{,}200{,}000 = 3.2 \times 10^6$ and $0.000045 = 4.5 \times 10^{-5}$.
2. Multiply: $(3.2 \times 10^6)(4.5 \times 10^{-5}) = 14.4 \times 10^{1}$.
3. Adjust: $14.4 = 1.44 \times 10^1$, so the product is $1.44 \times 10^2 = 144$.
4. Check in standard form: $3{,}200{,}000 \times 0.000045 = 144$.

## Common misconceptions and boundary cases

- **Adding across.** $\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{3} \ne \tfrac{2}{5}$. With a common denominator, $\tfrac{3}{6} + \tfrac{2}{6} = \tfrac{5}{6}$. A quick sense check: $\tfrac{2}{5}$ is less than $\tfrac{1}{2}$, so it cannot be the sum.
- **Bigger denominator, bigger fraction.** For equal numerators, a larger denominator means smaller pieces: $\tfrac{1}{8} \lt \tfrac{1}{3}$.
- **"Multiplying makes bigger, dividing makes smaller."** Multiplying by a number between $0$ and $1$ makes a positive number smaller ($8 \cdot \tfrac{1}{2} = 4$), and dividing by such a number makes it larger ($8 \div \tfrac{1}{2} = 16$).
- **Percent versus decimal.** $0.5\%$ is $0.005$, not $0.5$. Percents above $100\%$ are valid: $150\%$ of $40$ is $60$.
- **Testing the denominator before simplifying.** $\tfrac{9}{12}$ has a $3$ in its denominator, yet it terminates because it simplifies to $\tfrac{3}{4} = 0.75$. Apply the $2$-and-$5$ test only to lowest terms.
- **Unnormalized scientific notation.** $14.4 \times 10^1$ equals $144$, but it is not in scientific notation because $14.4 \ge 10$.

## Practice

1. Find $\tfrac{3}{5} + \tfrac{1}{6}$.
2. Find $3\tfrac{1}{4} - 1\tfrac{5}{6}$.
3. Find $\tfrac{5}{6} \cdot \tfrac{9}{10}$.
4. Find $2\tfrac{2}{3} \div \tfrac{4}{9}$.
5. Write $\tfrac{7}{20}$ as a decimal and a percent. Write $0.125$ as a fraction in lowest terms.
6. Without dividing, decide which decimals terminate: $\tfrac{7}{40}$, $\tfrac{2}{15}$, $\tfrac{9}{12}$. Then find each decimal.
7. Order from least to greatest: $0.6$, $\tfrac{5}{8}$, $62\%$, $\tfrac{3}{5}$.
8. Compute $(6.0 \times 10^8) \div (1.5 \times 10^3)$, and write $0.00072$ in scientific notation.
