# 07.8 — Altered States, Dreams & Non-Ordinary Consciousness

> *"You are not a drop in the ocean. You are the entire ocean in a drop."* — Rumi

> *"Psychedelics are to the mind what the telescope was to astronomy."* — Timothy Leary (contested, but the metaphor has merit)

The ordinary waking state we call "consciousness" is one point in a vast landscape of possible conscious states. Exploring the others — in dreams, meditation, psychedelics, flow states, hypnagogia — reveals that what we take to be the fixed features of experience are actually contingent, modifiable, and informative about the underlying mechanism.

## 📚 1. The Sleep-Wake Cycle and Dreams

### 1.1 Sleep Architecture

Sleep cycles ~90 minutes, repeating 4–6 times per night:

| Stage | EEG pattern | Physiology | Consciousness |
|-------|------------|-----------|--------------|
| Awake | Beta/Alpha (13–30 Hz / 8–13 Hz) | Alert / relaxed | Full waking consciousness |
| Hypnagogia | Theta (4–8 Hz) | Muscle relaxation | Transitional: vivid imagery, hallucinations |
| NREM Stage 1 | Theta | Slow rolling eye movements | Light sleep, hypnic jerks |
| NREM Stage 2 | Sleep spindles (12–15 Hz bursts) | Temperature drops | Minimal consciousness, memory consolidation |
| NREM Stage 3 (SWS) | Delta (0.5–4 Hz) | Deep sleep, GH release | Near-absent consciousness; IIT Φ low |
| REM | Mixed, near-wake | Atonia (muscle paralysis), REMs | Vivid dreaming; near-waking neural activity |

### 1.2 The Neuroscience of Dreaming

**REM (Rapid Eye Movement) sleep:**
- Brain metabolism similar to waking
- Activation-synthesis theory (Hobson & McCarley, 1977): brainstem generates random signals; cortex interprets and constructs a narrative
- Predictive processing account: dream = model running offline without sensory correction; priors dominate; prediction error suppressed

**Key feature of REM:** The dorsolateral prefrontal cortex (DLPFC) is relatively deactivated. This is the region associated with critical self-monitoring, reality testing, and logical inference.

**Implication:** Dreams are bizarre and uncritical partly because the DLPFC — the "editor" — is offline.

### 1.3 Lucid Dreaming

A lucid dream is a dream in which the dreamer is aware they are dreaming and can sometimes exert control.

**Neuroscience:** Lucid dreaming shows reactivation of the DLPFC — the region suppressed in normal dreams. Self-monitoring returns.

**Philosophical significance:**
1. Demonstrates that **phenomenal consciousness can exist without external input** — the dream is entirely internally generated
2. Demonstrates that **metacognition (awareness of awareness)** is a distinct capacity that can be partially present/absent
3. Supports **GWT:** The global workspace of waking consciousness is partially restored (enough for metacognition) but not fully (reality testing still impaired)

## 📚 2. The Default Mode Network (DMN)

The **Default Mode Network** is a set of brain regions that are:
- Active during **rest** (when not engaged in a task)
- Active during **self-referential thinking** ("what do others think of me?", "what should I do tomorrow?")
- Active during **mind-wandering**, daydreaming
- **Deactivated** during focused attention to an external task

**Key regions:** Medial prefrontal cortex (mPFC), posterior cingulate cortex (PCC), angular gyrus, temporal poles.

### 2.1 The Self as a Default Mode Product

A key insight from DMN research: **the sense of self is largely generated by DMN activity**.

When DMN is active, you're thinking about yourself — your past, future, relationships, reputation. The "self" is not a static object but an ongoing narrative construction maintained by this network.

**Disruption of DMN = disruption of self:**
- Deep meditation → DMN quiets → reduced self-referential thinking
- Psychedelics → DMN fragmentation → ego dissolution
- Flow states → DMN deactivation → loss of self-consciousness
- Mindfulness → trained DMN regulation

### 2.2 Mind-Wandering

Research by Killingsworth & Gilbert (2010): mind-wandering occurs about **47% of waking hours** and is associated with **reduced happiness** compared to being present.

The wandering mind is largely DMN-driven — thinking about the past and future rather than the present. Most mind-wandering is about the self.

**Predictive processing account:** Mind-wandering = model simulation of hypothetical futures and pasts (useful for planning, learning from past) but at the cost of present engagement.

## 📚 3. Meditation and Contemplative Science

### 3.1 Two Major Types

**Focused Attention (FA) / Shamatha:**
- Directed attention to a specific object (breath, sensation)
- Detect when mind wanders → gently return
- Trains **metacognitive monitoring** (noticing mind-wandering)
- Neural: strengthens prefrontal control over DMN; reduces mind-wandering

**Open Monitoring (OM) / Vipassana:**
- Non-reactive awareness of all arising phenomena
- Don't direct attention — observe whatever arises without grasping
- Trains **equanimity** and reduced self-referential elaboration
- Neural: gamma bursts, expanded receptive field, reduced DMN self-narrative

### 3.2 Cortical Changes in Long-Term Meditators

Sara Lazar (2005): experienced meditators show greater cortical thickness in regions associated with attention and interoception (insula, sensory cortex) compared to matched controls.

**Potential confounds:** Meditators self-select. Longitudinal studies show smaller effects than cross-sectional studies.

### 3.3 Non-Dual Awareness

Advanced meditators describe a state of **non-dual awareness** — awareness that is not of any particular object, and not associated with a self. This is the target of many contemplative traditions.

**Philosophical implication:** If non-dual awareness is genuine and not merely a report, it challenges the Husserlian claim that all consciousness is intentional (directed at an object). There may be contentless awareness — "pure consciousness" without an object.

**Controversial:** Whether non-dual awareness is a genuine form of consciousness or a misinterpretation of an unusual mental state is debated.

## 📚 4. Psychedelics and the Entropic Brain

### 4.1 Pharmacology

The "classic" psychedelics (psilocybin, LSD, DMT, mescaline) all act as **agonists at 5-HT2A serotonin receptors** — primarily in the neocortex.

5-HT2A activation in cortex disrupts the top-down predictive signalling that normally suppresses spurious prediction error signals. Result: the brain can't maintain its normal hierarchical predictions.

### 4.2 The Entropic Brain Hypothesis (Carhart-Harris 2014)

**Core claim:** Psychedelics increase the **entropy** of brain activity — the system moves from a restricted set of stereotyped patterns to a much wider, more variable set.

**Quantified by:** Lempel-Ziv complexity of EEG/MEG signals. Psychedelic administration increases neural complexity (diversity of waveforms) dramatically.

**Metaphor:** The brain's predictive hierarchy becomes less constrained. Normally, the top levels dominate (constraining what the bottom levels can do). Under psychedelics, the bottom levels (sensory processing) are less constrained → flooding of sensory experience, hallucinations, dissolution of normal categorical boundaries.

### 4.3 Ego Dissolution

At high doses, many users report **ego dissolution** — a temporary loss of the sense of being a distinct, bounded self.

**Phenomenology:**
- Boundaries between self and world dissolve
- "Oceanic" sense of unity or interconnection
- Profound meaningfulness and reverence
- Sometimes terror (if unexpected/resisted)

**Neuroscience:** Ego dissolution correlates with DMN fragmentation — the regions normally generating self-narrative are no longer operating coherently as a network.

**Philosophical connection to Parfit:** Parfit's intellectual conclusion (the self is a process, not a substance) is apparently what ego dissolution FEELS LIKE from the inside. The phenomenological experience matches the philosophical analysis.

**Therapeutic significance:** Ego dissolution under therapeutic guidance is associated with lasting reductions in depression, addiction, and PTSD — possibly because it breaks rigid self-referential patterns.

## 📚 5. What Altered States Tell Us About Ordinary Consciousness

| Altered state | What it reveals |
|--------------|----------------|
| **Dreaming** | Consciousness can be entirely internally generated (no sensory input required) |
| **Lucid dreaming** | Metacognition (awareness of awareness) is a distinct, selectively available capacity |
| **Deep sleep** | Consciousness can be largely absent with the brain still active — showing function ≠ consciousness |
| **Meditation** | The self is a constructed, actively maintained process — not a fixed entity |
| **Psychedelics** | The predictive hierarchy that normally organises experience is contingent and constraining — consciousness is much "wider" than normal operation reveals |
| **Flow states** | Consciousness can be absorbed without self-monitoring — selfless but also very conscious |

**Convergent insight:** The ordinary waking self is NOT a stable, objective apprehension of reality. It is a constructed, actively maintained model — useful and adaptive, but ultimately contingent.

## ⚠️ 6. Common Misconceptions

1. **"Dreaming is unconscious."** Dreams involve vivid subjective experience — arguably as phenomenally rich as waking. The key difference is reduced metacognition and critical monitoring (DLPFC deactivated), not absence of consciousness.

2. **"Meditation is about relaxation."** Focused attention meditation can be cognitively demanding. The goal is training metacognitive awareness and regulatory capacity, not simply relaxation. Relaxation may be a side effect.

3. **"Psychedelic experiences are just hallucinations."** Psychedelics produce perceptual alterations, but the most significant effects for therapeutic purposes are emotional, relational, and self-referential — not primarily perceptual.

4. **"Ego dissolution means you lose your identity permanently."** The effect is temporary (hours). The therapeutic value may come precisely from the temporary experience of no-self, which can shift habitual self-referential patterns without permanently dissolving identity.

5. **"Mind-wandering is wasted time."** Mind-wandering serves important functions: planning, creative problem-solving, memory consolidation. It becomes problematic when it's dominated by rumination (repetitive negative self-focused thought), not simply when it occurs.

6. **"The DMN is the self."** The DMN generates self-referential processing, but "the self" is not located in any brain region. The DMN is a network that contributes to the construction of self-model — one component among others.
