# 🛤️ English — Learning Path

> This document is **hand-curated** and should remain stable.

---

## 📋 Prerequisites

Before starting this track, you should have:

1. **Native or near-native English fluency** — you read and write in English every day; this track refines, not teaches from scratch.
2. **Basic writing habit** — keeping a daily log counts. The more you write, the faster this track compounds.
3. **A writing tool** — any text editor. Grammarly optional (see §3 of Subject_Plan).

You do NOT need:
- Formal grammar terminology (we build it in 38.1)
- Prior academic writing experience (we build it in 19.4–38.6)
- Literary background (we build it in 38.8)

## 📖 Recommended Chapter Order

### Phase 1: Grammar & Mechanics (Weeks 1–3)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | [38.1 - Grammar Foundations — Parts of Speech & Sentence Structure](38.1---Grammar-Foundations-—-Parts-of-Speech-&-Sentence-Structure) | The axiomatic foundation — everything else assumes this vocabulary |
| 2 | [38.2 - Punctuation, Mechanics & Style](38.2---Punctuation,-Mechanics-&-Style) | The rules that govern sentence boundaries and clarity; fixes the most visible errors |
| 3 | [38.3 - Vocabulary — Roots, Precision & Register](38.3---Vocabulary-—-Roots,-Precision-&-Register) | Word-level precision; unlocks connotation control and register-switching |

**Phase 1 deliverable:** Edit a 500-word piece you've written; eliminate every grammar error and imprecise word. Before/after comparison.

### Phase 2: Composition (Weeks 4–6)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 4 | [38.4 - Paragraph Construction & The Essay Structure](38.4---Paragraph-Construction-&-The-Essay-Structure) | Scale up from sentence to paragraph to essay; learn the scaffold before you transcend it |
| 5 | [38.5 - Argumentation & Rhetoric](38.5---Argumentation-&-Rhetoric) | Add the persuasive layer; makes writing do work in the world |
| 6 | [38.6 - Research Writing & Academic Style](38.6---Research-Writing-&-Academic-Style) | Credibility infrastructure: sourcing, citation, synthesis |

**Phase 2 deliverable:** Write a 750–1000 word argumentative essay on any topic you care about; include at least three sources cited correctly.

### Phase 3: Advanced (Weeks 7–10)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 7 | [38.7 - Style & Voice — Writing Like a Pro](38.7---Style-&-Voice-—-Writing-Like-a-Pro) | The gap between "correct" and "compelling" — this is where voice develops |
| 8 | [38.8 - Literary Analysis & Close Reading](38.8---Literary-Analysis-&-Close-Reading) | Read like a writer; understand craft choices in the texts you consume |

**Phase 3 deliverable:** Write a 500–750 word literary analysis of a short story, poem, or essay of your choice. Apply the Toulmin argument structure to a literary claim.

## ➡️ What Comes Next

After completing this English track:

1. **Technical Writing** — writing documentation, READMEs, design docs, and specifications with professional precision (applies directly to your code projects).
2. **Creative Writing** — fiction, worldbuilding, game narrative; 38.7 and 38.8 are the foundation.
3. **Rhetoric & Persuasion** — debate, public speaking, pitch writing; 38.5 is the entry point.
4. **The Spanish and Japanese tracks** ([Track 17](Subject_Plan) and [Track 18](Subject_Plan)) — now that you have explicit grammar terminology from English, the grammar chapters in Spanish and Japanese will click faster.

---

## 🚫 What This Track Does NOT Cover

- **Creative writing craft** (character, plot, scene) — separate track; 38.7 and 38.8 are the bridge.
- **Public speaking and oral rhetoric** — separate topic; this is written communication only.
- **Business writing genres** (memos, reports, emails at scale) — this track gives you the foundation; business-genre specifics are applied practice.

These are intentional scope limits, not gaps. Master the fundamentals here first.
