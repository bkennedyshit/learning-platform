# 13.1 - Ratios, Rates & Proportions

A ratio compares two quantities by division. Ratios let you say that a recipe uses "2 cups of rice for every 3 cups of water" no matter how big the pot is, and they are the reason a map, a unit price, or a percent can describe very different amounts with one number. This lesson builds the core ideas of proportional reasoning: ratios, unit rates, proportional relationships, proportions, scale, percent change, and simple interest.

## Prerequisites

You should be comfortable multiplying and dividing whole numbers and decimals and recognizing equivalent fractions such as $\tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{8}{12}$ (multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number). Fraction and decimal arithmetic, including converting between the two, is treated in more depth in [13.3 - Fractions, Decimals & Percent](/133-fractions-decimals-percent).

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. Write a ratio three ways ($a:b$, $\tfrac{a}{b}$, "$a$ to $b$"), simplify it, and tell part-to-part from part-to-whole ratios.
2. Compute a unit rate and use it to compare two offers or predict a new amount.
3. Test a table for proportionality, find the constant of proportionality $k$, and write $y = kx$.
4. Solve a proportion for a missing value and verify it with cross products.
5. Compute percent increase or decrease and simple interest $I = Prt$.

## Core ideas

### Ratios

A **ratio** of $a$ to $b$ (with $b \ne 0$) compares the two amounts multiplicatively. It can be written $a:b$, $\tfrac{a}{b}$, or "$a$ to $b$". Order matters: the ratio of boys to girls is not the ratio of girls to boys.

Ratios simplify like fractions. Dividing both terms by their greatest common factor gives an **equivalent ratio**: $12:18 = 2:3$ because both terms were divided by $6$. Multiplying both terms by the same nonzero number also gives an equivalent ratio.

- A **part-to-part** ratio compares two parts of a whole (red marbles to blue marbles).
- A **part-to-whole** ratio compares one part to the total (red marbles to all marbles). Part-to-whole ratios are fractions of the whole and are the basis of percent.

### Rates and unit rates

A **rate** is a ratio of quantities measured in different units, such as $\tfrac{150 \text{ miles}}{3 \text{ hours}}$. A **unit rate** has a denominator of $1$: $50$ miles per hour. To find a unit rate, divide the first quantity by the second. Unit prices (dollars per ounce) are unit rates used to compare purchases of different sizes.

### Proportional relationships

Two quantities $x$ and $y$ are **proportional** when their ratio $\tfrac{y}{x}$ is the same for every pair of values. That constant is the **constant of proportionality** $k$, and the relationship can be written
$$y = kx.$$
In a table, check that $\tfrac{y}{x}$ is identical in every row. On a graph, a proportional relationship is a straight line through the origin $(0, 0)$. A line that does not pass through the origin, such as $y = 3x + 2$, is not proportional even though it is straight.

### Proportions and cross products

A **proportion** is an equation stating that two ratios are equal, $\tfrac{a}{b} = \tfrac{c}{d}$ with $b, d \ne 0$. Multiplying both sides by $bd$ gives the cross-product equation $ad = bc$. That is a consequence of the multiplication property of equality, not a separate rule, and it only applies when the equation is exactly one ratio equal to another ratio.

A **scale** on a map or drawing is a ratio of drawing length to actual length, such as $1 \text{ cm} : 2.5 \text{ m}$. Scale problems are proportions.

### Percent, percent change, and simple interest

A **percent** is a ratio out of $100$: $35\% = \tfrac{35}{100} = 0.35$. **Percent change** compares a change to the original amount:
$$\text{percent change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}} \times 100\%.$$
A positive result is an increase; a negative result is a decrease.

**Simple interest** is interest paid only on the original principal:
$$I = Prt,$$
where $P$ is the principal, $r$ is the annual rate written as a decimal, and $t$ is the time in years. The total amount is $A = P + I$.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Simplifying ratios

A bag holds $12$ red and $18$ blue marbles.

1. Red to blue: $12:18$. The greatest common factor is $6$, so the ratio is $2:3$.
2. Red to total: $12:30 = 2:5$, so $\tfrac{2}{5}$ of the marbles are red.
3. Check: $\tfrac{2}{5} = 0.4 = 40\%$, and $40\%$ of $30$ is $12$.

### Example 2: Unit rates and the better buy

An illustrative store sells a $12$-ounce box of cereal for \$3.60 and a $20$-ounce box for \$5.40.

1. Small box: $3.60 \div 12 = 0.30$ dollars per ounce.
2. Large box: $5.40 \div 20 = 0.27$ dollars per ounce.
3. The large box costs less per ounce. Comparing total prices would not answer the question, because the boxes hold different amounts.

### Example 3: Is the relationship proportional?

| $x$ | 2 | 5 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|
| $y$ | 7 | 17.5 | 28 |

1. Compute $\tfrac{y}{x}$: $\tfrac{7}{2} = 3.5$, $\tfrac{17.5}{5} = 3.5$, $\tfrac{28}{8} = 3.5$.
2. The ratio is constant, so the relationship is proportional with $k = 3.5$ and $y = 3.5x$.

Compare the table $x = 1, 2, 3$ with $y = 5, 8, 11$. The ratios are $5$, $4$, and about $3.67$, so this relationship is not proportional. It follows $y = 3x + 2$, a line that crosses the $y$-axis at $2$ instead of the origin.

### Example 4: Solving a proportion with a scale

A map has the scale $1$ inch $: 20$ miles. Two towns are $3.5$ inches apart on the map.

1. Set up matching units in matching positions: $\tfrac{1 \text{ in}}{20 \text{ mi}} = \tfrac{3.5 \text{ in}}{d \text{ mi}}$.
2. Cross products: $1 \cdot d = 20 \cdot 3.5$, so $d = 70$ miles.
3. Check: $\tfrac{3.5}{70} = 0.05 = \tfrac{1}{20}$.

### Example 5: Percent change in both directions

An illustrative price rises from \$40 to \$52, then later falls back from \$52 to \$40.

1. Increase: $\tfrac{52 - 40}{40} = \tfrac{12}{40} = 0.30$, a $30\%$ increase.
2. Decrease: $\tfrac{40 - 52}{52} = \tfrac{-12}{52} \approx -0.231$, about a $23.1\%$ decrease.
3. The dollar change is the same, but the percents differ because the original amount (the denominator) differs.

### Example 6: Simple interest

An illustrative savings deposit of \$1,500 earns $4\%$ simple interest per year for $3$ years.

1. Convert the rate: $r = 4\% = 0.04$.
2. $I = Prt = 1500 \cdot 0.04 \cdot 3 = 180$ dollars.
3. Total: $A = 1500 + 180 = 1680$ dollars.

## Common misconceptions and boundary cases

- **Additive thinking.** Adding the same number to both terms does not keep a ratio. $2:3$ and $4:5$ are not equivalent, since $\tfrac{2}{3} \approx 0.667$ but $\tfrac{4}{5} = 0.8$. Equivalent ratios come from multiplying or dividing both terms.
- **Reversing the order.** "3 teachers for every 40 students" is $3:40$ teachers to students. Writing $40:3$ answers a different question.
- **Cross-multiplying outside a proportion.** Cross products solve $\tfrac{a}{b} = \tfrac{c}{d}$. They do not apply to $\tfrac{a}{b} \cdot \tfrac{c}{d}$ or $\tfrac{a}{b} + \tfrac{c}{d}$.
- **Using the wrong base for percent change.** Always divide by the original amount. A $30\%$ increase followed by a $30\%$ decrease does not return to the start: $40 \cdot 1.30 \cdot 0.70 = 36.40$.
- **Straight line means proportional.** Only lines through the origin are proportional; check whether $y = 0$ when $x = 0$.
- **Units in interest problems.** The rate must be a decimal ($4\%$ is $0.04$, not $4$) and time must be in years ($18$ months is $1.5$ years).

## Practice

1. Simplify the ratio $45:60$.
2. A car travels $234$ miles on $9$ gallons of gas. Find the unit rate, then predict the distance on $14$ gallons at the same rate.
3. Is this relationship proportional? $x = 3, 6, 10$ and $y = 4.5, 9, 15$. If so, write its equation.
4. Solve $\tfrac{5}{x} = \tfrac{15}{27}$.
5. A floor plan uses the scale $1 \text{ cm} : 2.5 \text{ m}$. A room is $6.4$ cm long on the plan. How long is the actual room?
6. A price drops from \$80 to \$68. Find the percent change.
7. A \$25 shirt has $8\%$ sales tax added. What is the total cost?
8. Find the simple interest and the total amount for \$2,400 at $3.5\%$ per year for $18$ months.

<details>
<summary>Answer key and self-check</summary>

1. The greatest common factor is $15$: $45:60 = 3:4$.
2. $234 \div 9 = 26$ miles per gallon; $26 \cdot 14 = 364$ miles.
3. $\tfrac{4.5}{3} = \tfrac{9}{6} = \tfrac{15}{10} = 1.5$, so yes: $y = 1.5x$.
4. $15x = 5 \cdot 27 = 135$, so $x = 9$. Check: $\tfrac{5}{9} = \tfrac{15}{27}$ because both simplify to $\tfrac{5}{9}$.
5. $\tfrac{1}{2.5} = \tfrac{6.4}{d}$ gives $d = 6.4 \cdot 2.5 = 16$ m.
6. $\tfrac{68 - 80}{80} = \tfrac{-12}{80} = -0.15$, a $15\%$ decrease.
7. Tax: $25 \cdot 0.08 = 2$ dollars; total \$27. Equivalently, $25 \cdot 1.08 = 27$.
8. $t = 1.5$ years; $I = 2400 \cdot 0.035 \cdot 1.5 = 126$ dollars; total \$2,526.

Self-check method: for a proportion, substitute your answer and confirm that the two cross products are equal. For a rate or percent, multiply back: the unit rate times the quantity should return the original total, and the original amount times $(1 + \text{percent change})$ should return the new amount.
