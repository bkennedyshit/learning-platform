# 13.5 - Equations & Inequalities

An **equation** states that two expressions are equal; a **solution** is a value of the variable that makes the statement true. An **inequality** compares two expressions with $\lt$, $\gt$, $\le$, or $\ge$, and usually has infinitely many solutions. This lesson shows how to solve both by keeping the statement balanced, how to check your answer, and how to recognize the special cases where an equation has no solution or every number is a solution.

## Prerequisites

You need to simplify expressions with the distributive property and combine like terms, as in [13.4 - Expressions & Variables](/134-expressions-variables). Integer and fraction arithmetic come from [13.2 - The Number System](/132-the-number-system) and [13.3 - Fractions, Decimals & Percent](/133-fractions-decimals-percent).

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. Solve one- and two-step equations and confirm each solution by substituting it into the original equation.
2. Solve equations that require clearing fractions, distributing, or collecting variables on one side, and identify contradictions and identities.
3. Solve a linear inequality, reverse the sign when required, and graph the solution set on a number line.
4. Solve compound "and" and "or" inequalities and translate a word problem into an equation or inequality.

## Core ideas

### Properties of equality

If $a = b$, then for any number $c$:

- $a + c = b + c$ and $a - c = b - c$ (addition and subtraction properties);
- $ac = bc$, and $\tfrac{a}{c} = \tfrac{b}{c}$ when $c \ne 0$ (multiplication and division properties).

Doing the same operation to both sides keeps the equation balanced. To isolate the variable, undo operations in the reverse of the order of operations: undo addition or subtraction first, then multiplication or division.

### A general strategy

1. Simplify each side: distribute and combine like terms.
2. If there are fractions, multiply **every term** on both sides by the least common denominator.
3. Collect the variable terms on one side and the constants on the other.
4. Divide by the coefficient of the variable.
5. Check by substituting into the **original** equation.

### Special cases

If the variable disappears while solving:

- a **false** statement such as $6 = 5$ means the equation is a **contradiction** with **no solution**;
- a **true** statement such as $6 = 6$ means the equation is an **identity**; **every real number** is a solution.

### Inequalities

Inequalities follow the same properties with one exception: **multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.** For example, $2 \lt 5$, but after multiplying by $-1$, $-2 \gt -5$. Adding or subtracting any number, including a negative one, never reverses the sign.

On a number line, an **open circle** marks an endpoint that is not included ($\lt$ or $\gt$); a **closed circle** marks an included endpoint ($\le$ or $\ge$). Shade the side containing the solutions.

### Compound inequalities

- An **"and"** inequality requires both conditions. $-3 \lt x \le 3$ means $x \gt -3$ and $x \le 3$; its graph is the overlap, a segment.
- An **"or"** inequality requires at least one condition. $x \lt -1$ or $x \ge 4$ is graphed as two rays pointing away from each other.

To solve a three-part inequality such as $a \lt bx + c \le d$, perform each operation on all three parts.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Two-step equation

Solve $3x - 7 = 11$.

1. Add $7$ to both sides: $3x = 18$.
2. Divide by $3$: $x = 6$.
3. Check: $3(6) - 7 = 18 - 7 = 11$.

### Example 2: Clearing fractions

Solve $\tfrac{x}{4} + \tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{5}{6}$.

1. The least common denominator is $12$. Multiply every term by $12$: $3x + 8 = 10$.
2. Subtract $8$: $3x = 2$, so $x = \tfrac{2}{3}$.
3. Check: $\tfrac{2/3}{4} = \tfrac{1}{6}$, and $\tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{4}{6} = \tfrac{5}{6}$.

### Example 3: Variables on both sides

Solve $5(x - 2) = 2x + 11$.

1. Distribute: $5x - 10 = 2x + 11$.
2. Subtract $2x$: $3x - 10 = 11$.
3. Add $10$: $3x = 21$, so $x = 7$.
4. Check: $5(7 - 2) = 25$ and $2(7) + 11 = 25$.

### Example 4: Identity and contradiction

1. $2(x + 3) = 2x + 6$ becomes $2x + 6 = 2x + 6$. Subtracting $2x$ leaves $6 = 6$, which is always true: every real number is a solution.
2. $2(x + 3) = 2x + 5$ becomes $2x + 6 = 2x + 5$. Subtracting $2x$ leaves $6 = 5$, which is false: there is no solution.

### Example 5: Inequality with a sign reversal

Solve $-3x + 4 \le 19$ and graph the solution.

1. Subtract $4$: $-3x \le 15$.
2. Divide by $-3$ and reverse the sign: $x \ge -5$.
3. Graph: a closed circle at $-5$ with shading to the right.
4. Check the boundary and one point on each side: $x = -5$ gives $19 \le 19$ (true); $x = 0$ gives $4 \le 19$ (true); $x = -6$ gives $22 \le 19$ (false).

### Example 6: Compound inequality

Solve $-2 \lt 2x + 4 \le 10$.

1. Subtract $4$ from all three parts: $-6 \lt 2x \le 6$.
2. Divide all three parts by $2$: $-3 \lt x \le 3$.
3. Graph: an open circle at $-3$, a closed circle at $3$, and shading between them.

### Example 7: Word problem

An illustrative bike rental costs \$8 plus \$6 per hour. With \$44 to spend, how many whole hours can you rent?

1. Let $h$ be the number of hours: $8 + 6h \le 44$.
2. Subtract $8$: $6h \le 36$. Divide by $6$: $h \le 6$.
3. Answer: at most $6$ hours. Check: $8 + 6(6) = 44$, exactly the budget.

## Common misconceptions and boundary cases

- **Forgetting to reverse the sign.** Dividing $-3x \le 15$ by $-3$ gives $x \ge -5$, not $x \le -5$. Test a point to catch this.
- **Reversing when you should not.** $x - 5 \gt -2$ gives $x \gt 3$. A negative number appearing somewhere is not the trigger; only multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative is.
- **Clearing fractions in only some terms.** Multiplying $\tfrac{x}{4} + \tfrac{2}{3} = \tfrac{5}{6}$ by $12$ must affect all three terms.
- **Misreading the special cases.** $0 = 0$ does not mean $x = 0$; it means every number works. And a solution of $x = 0$ is a real solution, not "no solution".
- **Checking in a simplified equation.** If you made a mistake while simplifying, checking in your rewritten equation will not catch it. Substitute into the original.
- **Writing an impossible compound inequality.** $-3 \lt x \gt 3$ is not meaningful; "and" statements are written with both signs pointing the same way, $-3 \lt x \lt 3$.

## Practice

1. Solve $4x + 9 = -15$.
2. Solve $7 - 2y = 19$.
3. Solve $\tfrac{2x}{3} - 1 = 5$.
4. Solve $3(2n - 1) = 4n + 9$.
5. Classify each equation as having one solution, no solution, or every real number as a solution: $4(x - 1) - x = 3x - 4$ and $5x - 2 = 5(x + 1)$.
6. Solve $5 - 4x \gt 17$ and describe its graph.
7. Solve $-1 \le \tfrac{x - 3}{2} \lt 4$.
8. In an illustrative savings plan, a student has \$40 and saves \$12 per week. Write and solve an inequality for the number of weeks $w$ needed to have at least \$200.
