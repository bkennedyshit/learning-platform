## 1. CNS vs. PNS Organization

| Division | Components | Tissue enclosed by |
|----------|-----------|-------------------|
| **CNS** (central) | Brain + spinal cord | Meninges + bony skull/vertebral column |
| **PNS** (peripheral) | All nerves + ganglia outside CNS | Epineurium, perineurium, endoneurium |

**PNS subdivisions:**
- **Somatic NS** — voluntary motor (efferent to skeletal muscle) + sensory (afferent from skin/joints/muscles)
- **Autonomic NS (ANS)** — involuntary control of viscera. See §7.
  - Sympathetic ("fight-or-flight") — thoracolumbar (T1–L2)
  - Parasympathetic ("rest-and-digest") — craniosacral (CN III, VII, IX, X + S2–S4)
- **Enteric NS** — "brain of the gut"; semi-autonomous myenteric + submucosal plexuses

## 2. Neuron Anatomy

- **Soma (cell body)** — contains nucleus, ribosomes, Nissl substance (rough ER); metabolic center
- **Dendrites** — receive inputs; highly branched; no myelin
- **Axon** — single long process; transmits action potentials; may be myelinated
- **Axon hillock** — initial segment; site of action potential initiation (lowest threshold)
- **Myelin sheath** — lipid-rich insulation; formed by **oligodendrocytes** (CNS) or **Schwann cells** (PNS)
- **Nodes of Ranvier** — gaps in myelin; site of **saltatory conduction** (AP jumps node to node)
- **Axon terminal / synaptic bouton** — releases neurotransmitters into synapse

**Neuron classification:**
- By structure: multipolar (motor neurons, interneurons — most common), bipolar (retina, cochlea), unipolar/pseudounipolar (sensory DRG neurons)
- By function: sensory (afferent), motor (efferent), interneuron (integration; 99% of CNS neurons)

### Glial Cells (Support, Not Transmission)
| Cell | Location | Function |
|------|----------|---------|
| Astrocytes | CNS | Blood-brain barrier, ion buffering, synapse formation, scar formation |
| Oligodendrocytes | CNS | Myelinate CNS axons (each wraps multiple axons) |
| Microglia | CNS | CNS immune cells (macrophage-equivalent) |
| Ependymal cells | CNS | Line ventricles; produce and circulate CSF |
| Schwann cells | PNS | Myelinate PNS axons (each wraps one segment of one axon) |
| Satellite cells | PNS ganglia | Support cell bodies in ganglia |

## 3. Brain Regions

### 3.1 Cerebral Cortex — Lobes and Functions
| Lobe | Location | Key functions |
|------|----------|--------------|
| **Frontal** | Anterior to central sulcus | Primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus), Broca's area (dominant hemisphere, speech production), prefrontal cortex (executive function, personality) |
| **Parietal** | Behind central sulcus | Primary somatosensory cortex (postcentral gyrus), spatial integration, Wernicke's area (posterior parietal in some maps) |
| **Temporal** | Lateral, below lateral fissure | Primary auditory cortex, Wernicke's area (dominant — speech comprehension), hippocampus (memory consolidation), amygdala (emotion) |
| **Occipital** | Posterior | Primary visual cortex (V1); visual processing areas |
| **Insula** | Hidden within lateral sulcus | Visceral sensation, taste, interoception, pain integration |

### 3.2 Subcortical Structures
- **Basal ganglia** (caudate + putamen = striatum; globus pallidus; subthalamic nucleus; substantia nigra) — motor learning, habit formation, movement initiation. Parkinson's = dopamine loss in substantia nigra → bradykinesia, rigidity, resting tremor.
- **Thalamus** — relay station for all sensory input (except olfaction) to cortex; gating consciousness
- **Hypothalamus** — master regulator: homeostasis (temperature, hunger, thirst, circadian rhythm), controls pituitary gland ("master gland")
- **Limbic system** (hippocampus, amygdala, cingulate gyrus, parahippocampal gyrus) — emotion, memory, motivation

### 3.3 Brainstem
| Division | Contents |
|----------|---------|
| **Midbrain (mesencephalon)** | CN III (oculomotor), CN IV (trochlear); cerebral peduncles; superior/inferior colliculi (visual/auditory reflexes); reticular activating system (consciousness) |
| **Pons** | CN V (trigeminal), VI (abducens), VII (facial), VIII (vestibulocochlear); respiratory rhythmicity |
| **Medulla oblongata** | CN IX (glossopharyngeal), X (vagus), XI (accessory), XII (hypoglossal); cardiac/respiratory/vasomotor centers; pyramidal decussation (most corticospinal fibers cross here) |

### 3.4 Cerebellum
- Located posterior to brainstem, within posterior fossa
- **Function:** coordination of movement, balance, motor learning, fine-tuning motor output
- Receives input from proprioceptors and motor cortex; sends corrective output back to motor cortex via thalamus
- **Lesion signs:** ipsilateral ataxia (intention tremor, dysmetria, dysdiadochokinesia) — unlike cortical lesions, cerebellar signs are ipsilateral

## 4. Spinal Cord

### 4.1 Structure
- Extends from foramen magnum to **L1–L2** (conus medullaris)
- Below conus: **cauda equina** (horse's tail) — lumbar/sacral nerve roots floating in CSF
- **Lumbar puncture (LP)** safe at L3–L4 or L4–L5 (below conus)

### 4.2 Gray vs. White Matter
- **Gray matter** (butterfly/H-shape) — neuron cell bodies + synapses
  - **Dorsal horn** — sensory (receives incoming afferents)
  - **Ventral horn** — motor (lower motor neuron cell bodies → muscles)
  - **Lateral horn** (T1–L2 and S2–S4) — autonomic preganglionic neurons
- **White matter** — myelinated axon tracts organized into columns (funiculi)

### 4.3 Key Ascending (Sensory) Tracts
| Tract | Column | Information | Decussation |
|-------|--------|------------|-------------|
| **Dorsal columns** (gracile + cuneate) | Posterior | Fine touch, vibration, proprioception | At medulla (sensory decussation) |
| **Spinothalamic tract** | Anterolateral | Pain, temperature (lateral division); crude touch/pressure (anterior division) | At spinal cord level (1–2 segments above entry) |

### 4.4 Key Descending (Motor) Tracts
| Tract | Column | Function | Decussation |
|-------|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Lateral corticospinal** | Lateral | Voluntary motor to limbs (most fibers) | Pyramidal decussation in medulla (~85%) |
| **Anterior corticospinal** | Anterior | Voluntary motor to trunk | Decussates at segmental level |

## 5. The 12 Cranial Nerves

> Mnemonic (name): **"Oh Oh Oh, To Touch And Feel Very Good Velvet — Ahh Heaven"**
> Mnemonic (type): **"Some Say Money Matters But My Brother Says Big Brains Matter More"** (S=sensory, M=motor, B=both)

| # | Name | Type | Key functions | Clinical test / palsy |
|---|------|------|--------------|----------------------|
| I | **Olfactory** | S | Smell | Loss of smell (anosmia); cribriform plate fracture |
| II | **Optic** | S | Vision | Visual field defects; optic neuritis |
| III | **Oculomotor** | M | 4 of 6 EOMs (all except SO and LR); levator palpebrae; pupil constriction (PS) | CN III palsy: "down and out" eye, ptosis, dilated pupil; posterior communicating artery aneurysm |
| IV | **Trochlear** | M | Superior oblique (SO4) — infraduction of adducted eye | Head tilt to opposite side; vertical diplopia |
| V | **Trigeminal** | B | Face sensation (V1/V2/V3); motor to jaw muscles (V3) | Trigeminal neuralgia; jaw deviation toward weak side |
| VI | **Abducens** | M | Lateral rectus (LR6) — abduction | CN VI palsy: eye cannot abduct; diplopia on lateral gaze |
| VII | **Facial** | B | Facial expression muscles; taste (anterior 2/3 tongue); lacrimation/salivation (chorda tympani) | Bell's palsy (LMN): entire ipsilateral face droops. UMN lesion: forehead spared |
| VIII | **Vestibulocochlear** | S | Hearing (cochlear division); balance (vestibular division) | Sensorineural hearing loss; vertigo |
| IX | **Glossopharyngeal** | B | Taste (posterior 1/3 tongue); sensation from pharynx/middle ear; parotid gland; carotid body/sinus | Gag reflex (afferent limb) |
| X | **Vagus** | B | Parasympathetic to thorax + abdomen; larynx/pharynx motor; visceral sensation | Uvula deviation away from lesion; hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal nerve) |
| XI | **Accessory** | M | SCM (head rotation) + trapezius (shoulder shrug) | Shoulder drop; inability to turn head against resistance |
| XII | **Hypoglossal** | M | Tongue movements | Tongue deviates toward side of LMN lesion |

> **LMN vs. UMN CN VII:** Lower motor neuron lesion (e.g., Bell's palsy, parotid tumor) → entire ipsilateral face paralyzed (including forehead). Upper motor neuron lesion (stroke) → contralateral lower face only spared (forehead has bilateral cortical representation).

## 6. Major Peripheral Nerves

### Brachial Plexus (C5–T1)
```
Roots (C5-T1) → Trunks (upper/middle/lower) → Divisions → Cords (lateral/posterior/medial) → Terminal branches
```
| Nerve | Roots | Motor | Sensory | Injury |
|-------|-------|-------|---------|--------|
| **Musculocutaneous** | C5–C7 | Biceps, brachialis, coracobrachialis | Lateral forearm | Weakness elbow flexion/supination |
| **Axillary** | C5–C6 | Deltoid, teres minor | Regimental patch (lateral arm) | Shoulder dislocation, surgical neck # → "can't abduct arm, numb lateral arm" |
| **Radial** | C5–T1 | All posterior arm + forearm extensors | Posterior arm/forearm, dorsal hand (lateral 3.5 fingers) | Humeral shaft # → wrist drop |
| **Median** | C6–T1 | Flexor compartment forearm (except FCU + FDP medial 2), thenar muscles, lateral 2 lumbricals | Lateral palm + lateral 3.5 fingers | Carpal tunnel → "ape hand," thenar wasting, Tinel's/Phalen's + |
| **Ulnar** | C8–T1 | FCU, FDP medial 2, hypothenar, interossei, medial 2 lumbricals | Medial palm + medial 1.5 fingers | Medial epicondyle # → "claw hand," hypothenar wasting, Froment's sign |

### Lumbosacral Plexus — Key Nerves
| Nerve | Roots | Key motor | Sensory | Injury |
|-------|-------|----------|---------|--------|
| **Femoral** | L2–L4 | Quadriceps, iliopsoas | Anterior thigh + medial leg (saphenous) | Can't extend knee; lost patellar reflex |
| **Obturator** | L2–L4 | Adductors | Medial thigh | Weak hip adduction |
| **Sciatic** | L4–S3 | Hamstrings, all below knee | Posterior thigh + all below knee | Piriformis syndrome, posterior hip dislocation, deep gluteal injection |
| **Common fibular (peroneal)** | L4–S2 | Dorsiflexors, everters | Dorsum foot | Fibular neck # → foot drop, loss of eversion |
| **Tibial** | L4–S3 | Plantarflexors, toe flexors | Sole of foot | Loss of plantarflexion; less common injury |

## 7. Autonomic Nervous System (ANS)

| Feature | Sympathetic | Parasympathetic |
|---------|------------|----------------|
| Origin | Thoracolumbar (T1–L2) | Craniosacral (CN III, VII, IX, X; S2–S4) |
| Ganglia location | Close to spinal cord (paravertebral/prevertebral) | Close to or within target organ |
| Pre-ganglionic fiber | Short (myelinated) | Long (myelinated) |
| Post-ganglionic fiber | Long (unmyelinated) | Short (unmyelinated) |
| NT at ganglion | ACh (nicotinic) | ACh (nicotinic) |
| NT at target | Norepinephrine (NE) — adrenergic; except sweat glands (ACh) | ACh (muscarinic) |
| General effect | "Fight-or-flight" — ↑HR, ↑BP, dilate pupils, bronchodilate, inhibit GI | "Rest-and-digest" — ↓HR, constrict pupils, stimulate GI, empty bladder |
