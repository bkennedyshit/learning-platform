# 43.3 — Adjectives, Pronouns & Agreement

*Back to [Subject_Plan](Subject_Plan) | [← 43.2](43.2---The-Case-System-—-Nouns-&-The-5-Declensions) | [43.4 →](43.4---The-Verb-System-—-Present-System-Active-&-Passive)*

> **Chapter goal:** Master Latin adjective agreement (gender/case/number must match the noun), the 1st/2nd and 3rd declension adjective paradigms, comparison, demonstrative pronouns, personal pronouns, and the relative pronoun *quī/quae/quod*.

---

## 1. Adjective Agreement — The Core Rule

A Latin adjective must agree with the noun it modifies in **three dimensions simultaneously:**
- **Gender** (masculine, feminine, neuter)
- **Case** (nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, ablative, vocative)
- **Number** (singular, plural)

> **Critical:** Agreement in gender/case/number does **not** mean the endings are the same. An adjective modifying a 3rd declension noun may itself be 1st/2nd declension, and the endings will look different — but they agree in function.

*puella bona* — good girl (f. nom. sg.: noun 1st decl., adj. 1st/2nd decl. — endings match)
*rex bonus* — good king (m. nom. sg.)
*templum bonum* — good temple (n. nom. sg.)
*patris boni* — of the good father (m. gen. sg.: noun 3rd decl. -is; adj. 2nd decl. -ī — different endings, same case/gender/number)

## 2. First/Second Declension Adjectives (1st/2nd Decl. Adjectives)

Masculine like 2nd decl. nouns (*servus*), feminine like 1st decl. (*puella*), neuter like 2nd decl. neuter (*bellum*).

### *bonus, bona, bonum* — good

| Case | M. sg. | F. sg. | N. sg. | M. pl. | F. pl. | N. pl. |
|------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Nom.** | bonus | bona | bonum | bonī | bonae | bona |
| **Gen.** | bonī | bonae | bonī | bonōrum | bonārum | bonōrum |
| **Dat.** | bonō | bonae | bonō | bonīs | bonīs | bonīs |
| **Acc.** | bonum | bonam | bonum | bonōs | bonās | bona |
| **Abl.** | bonō | bonā | bonō | bonīs | bonīs | bonīs |
| **Voc.** | bone | bona | bonum | bonī | bonae | bona |

**Other 1st/2nd declension adjectives:** *malus* (bad), *magnus* (great/large), *parvus* (small), *multus* (much/many), *novus* (new), *antiquus* (ancient), *altus* (high/deep), *lātus* (wide), *longus* (long), *pulcher, pulchra, pulchrum* (beautiful — note -er nom. m. like *puer/ager*)

### The "UNUS NAUTA" Irregular Adjectives (Pronominal Adjectives)

Nine 1st/2nd decl. adjectives have **genitive singular -īus** and **dative singular -ī** (instead of regular -ae/-ī and -ae/-ō). The rest of their paradigm is regular:

**Mnemonic: UNUS NAUTA**
*ūnus* (one), *nūllus* (no/none), *ūllus* (any), *sōlus* (alone), *nōn + ūllus = nūllus*... Full list:
*ūnus, sōlus, tōtus, ūllus, nūllus, alius, alter, uter, neuter*

| Gen. sg. (all genders) | Dat. sg. (all genders) |
|------------------------|------------------------|
| -**īus** | -**ī** |

*ūnīus, sōlīus, tōtīus, alterīus, nūllīus...* (gen. sg.)
*ūnī, sōlī, tōtī, alterī, nūllī...* (dat. sg.)

## 3. Third Declension Adjectives

These adjectives decline like 3rd declension nouns but are **i-stems** (gen. pl. always -ium).

### Three-termination (3 endings in nom. sg.): *ācer, ācris, ācre* — sharp/keen

| Case | M. sg. | F. sg. | N. sg. | M./F. pl. | N. pl. |
|------|--------|--------|--------|----------|--------|
| **Nom.** | ācer | ācris | ācre | ācrēs | ācria |
| **Gen.** | ācris | ācris | ācris | ācrium | ācrium |
| **Dat.** | ācrī | ācrī | ācrī | ācribus | ācribus |
| **Acc.** | ācrem | ācrem | ācre | ācrēs | ācria |
| **Abl.** | ācrī | ācrī | ācrī | ācribus | ācribus |

### Two-termination (2 endings): *fortis, forte* — strong/brave

| Case | M./F. sg. | N. sg. | M./F. pl. | N. pl. |
|------|----------|--------|----------|--------|
| **Nom.** | fortis | forte | fortēs | fortia |
| **Gen.** | fortis | fortis | fortium | fortium |
| **Dat./Abl.** | fortī | fortī | fortibus | fortibus |
| **Acc.** | fortem | forte | fortēs | fortia |

**Common 2-termination adjectives:** *omnis, omne* (all/every), *gravis, grave* (heavy/serious), *brevis, breve* (short), *dulcis, dulce* (sweet), *levis, leve* (light), *tristis, triste* (sad)

### One-termination: *potens, potentis* — powerful (present participles follow this pattern too)

Nom. sg. same for all genders; stem from gen. sg.:

| Case | M./F. sg. | N. sg. | M./F. pl. | N. pl. |
|------|----------|--------|----------|--------|
| **Nom.** | potens | potens | potentēs | potentia |
| **Gen.** | potentis | potentis | potentium | potentium |
| **Dat./Abl.** | potentī | potentī | potentibus | potentibus |
| **Acc.** | potentem | potens | potentēs | potentia |

## 4. Comparison of Adjectives

### Regular Comparison

| Degree | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Formation |
|--------|-----------|---------|--------|-----------|
| **Positive** | *bonus* | *bona* | *bonum* | Base form |
| **Comparative** | *melior* | *melior* | *melius* | Add *-ior* (m/f), *-ius* (n) to stem |
| **Superlative** | *optimus* | *optima* | *optimum* | Add *-issimus/-a/-um* to stem |

Regular pattern: *fortis → fortior, fortius* (comp.); *fortissimus/-a/-um* (superl.)

**Irregular comparisons:**

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|----------|------------|------------|
| *bonus* (good) | *melior, melius* | *optimus/-a/-um* |
| *malus* (bad) | *peior, peius* | *pessimus/-a/-um* |
| *magnus* (great) | *māior, māius* | *maximus/-a/-um* |
| *parvus* (small) | *minor, minus* | *minimus/-a/-um* |
| *multus* (much) | *plūs, plūris* (n. only in sg.) | *plūrimus/-a/-um* |

### Constructions with Comparatives

- **Quam** (than) + same case as first element: *Puella est pulchrior quam servus.* — The girl is more beautiful than the slave.
- **Ablative of comparison** (omitting *quam*): *Puella est pulchrior servō.* — same meaning; ablative without *quam*.
- **Superlative**: *Puella est pulcherrima omnium.* — The girl is the most beautiful of all.

## 5. Personal Pronouns

Latin personal pronouns have no gender distinction (no he/she — that's handled by demonstratives or context):

| | 1st person | 2nd person | Reflexive (3rd) |
|--|-----------|-----------|----------------|
| **Nom.** | *ego* | *tū* | — |
| **Gen.** | *meī* | *tuī* | *suī* |
| **Dat.** | *mihi* | *tibi* | *sibi* |
| **Acc.** | *mē* | *tē* | *sē* (or *sēsē*) |
| **Abl.** | *mē* | *tē* | *sē* |

| | 1st person pl. | 2nd person pl. |
|--|--------------|--------------|
| **Nom.** | *nōs* | *vōs* |
| **Gen.** | *nostrī/nostrum* | *vestrī/vestrum* |
| **Dat.** | *nōbīs* | *vōbīs* |
| **Acc.** | *nōs* | *vōs* |
| **Abl.** | *nōbīs* | *vōbīs* |

> *cum* + personal pronoun always **attaches**: *mēcum* (with me), *tēcum* (with you), *nōbīscum* (with us), *vōbīscum* (with you pl.), *sēcum* (with himself/herself/themselves)

## 6. Demonstrative Pronouns/Adjectives

### *hic, haec, hoc* — this (near the speaker)

| | M. sg. | F. sg. | N. sg. | M. pl. | F. pl. | N. pl. |
|--|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Nom.** | hic | haec | hoc | hī | hae | haec |
| **Gen.** | huius | huius | huius | hōrum | hārum | hōrum |
| **Dat.** | huic | huic | huic | hīs | hīs | hīs |
| **Acc.** | hunc | hanc | hoc | hōs | hās | haec |
| **Abl.** | hōc | hāc | hōc | hīs | hīs | hīs |

### *ille, illa, illud* — that (away from speaker; also "the famous")

| | M. sg. | F. sg. | N. sg. | M. pl. | F. pl. | N. pl. |
|--|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Nom.** | ille | illa | illud | illī | illae | illa |
| **Gen.** | illīus | illīus | illīus | illōrum | illārum | illōrum |
| **Dat.** | illī | illī | illī | illīs | illīs | illīs |
| **Acc.** | illum | illam | illud | illōs | illās | illa |
| **Abl.** | illō | illā | illō | illīs | illīs | illīs |

### *is, ea, id* — he/she/it / this / that (the weak demonstrative; also 3rd person pronoun)

| | M. sg. | F. sg. | N. sg. | M. pl. | F. pl. | N. pl. |
|--|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Nom.** | is | ea | id | eī (iī) | eae | ea |
| **Gen.** | eius | eius | eius | eōrum | eārum | eōrum |
| **Dat.** | eī | eī | eī | eīs (iīs) | eīs | eīs |
| **Acc.** | eum | eam | id | eōs | eās | ea |
| **Abl.** | eō | eā | eō | eīs | eīs | eīs |

> *is/ea/id* is the standard 3rd person pronoun in Latin (Latin has no separate pronoun for he/she/it — demonstratives fill this role).

## 7. Relative Pronoun: *quī, quae, quod* — who/which/that

The most important pronoun in Latin — introduces relative clauses. Agrees with its **antecedent** (the noun it refers back to) in gender and number; takes its **case** from its role within the relative clause.

| | M. sg. | F. sg. | N. sg. | M. pl. | F. pl. | N. pl. |
|--|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Nom.** | quī | quae | quod | quī | quae | quae |
| **Gen.** | cuius | cuius | cuius | quōrum | quārum | quōrum |
| **Dat.** | cui | cui | cui | quibus | quibus | quibus |
| **Acc.** | quem | quam | quod | quōs | quās | quae |
| **Abl.** | quō | quā | quō | quibus | quibus | quibus |

**Worked example:**
*Servus quem puella amat bonus est.*
— The slave **whom** the girl loves is good.
- *quem* agrees with *servus* (masculine, singular) but is **accusative** because it's the direct object of *amat* within the relative clause.

*Puella cuius pater rex est pulchra est.*
— The girl **whose** father is king is beautiful.
- *cuius* agrees with *puella* (feminine, singular) but is **genitive** (possession: "of whom" = whose).

## 8. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **Assuming adjective ending = noun ending:** *rex bonus* has *rex* (3rd decl. nom.) and *bonus* (2nd decl. nom.) — different endings, but they agree in m./nom./sg.
2. **Forgetting UNUS NAUTA irregulars:** *ūnus* → gen. sg. = *ūnīus*, not *ūnī*. These pronominal adjectives trip up students constantly.
3. **Confusing *hic/haec/hoc* forms:** The accusatives (*hunc/hanc/hoc*) and ablatives (*hōc/hāc/hōc*) look similar. Drill the full table.
4. **Relative pronoun case from antecedent:** Students put the relative pronoun in the same case as the antecedent. Wrong — the antecedent determines **gender/number**; the role in the relative clause determines **case**.
5. **Not memorising *is, ea, id*:** This pronoun/demonstrative appears on almost every page of Latin prose. Its irregular forms (*eius, eī, eum, eam*) must be automatic.
