# Subject Syllabus: 21 - Earth Science

*Back to [Learning Index](00---09---Learning-Index) · [6-8 Pipeline](6-8-Middle-School-Pipeline)*

Earth science is the study of the planet we live on — its internal structure, its surface processes, its atmosphere, its oceans, and its place in the solar system and universe. It is both a **historical science** (understanding how Earth got this way over 4.5 billion years) and an **applied science** (climate change, natural hazard prediction, resource management). This track covers the NGSS middle school Earth science standards and provides the foundation for AP Environmental Science.

## 📝 3. Chapter Outline

| # | Chapter | Core skill |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 21.1 | **Earth as a System** | The four spheres (geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere, biosphere); Earth system interactions and feedback loops (positive vs. negative); energy inputs (solar radiation, internal heat); matter cycles (carbon, water, nitrogen, rock cycle) — how matter flows between spheres; Earth's layers (inner core, outer core, mantle, crust — continental and oceanic). |
| 21.2 | **Plate Tectonics** | Evidence for continental drift (Wegener); sea-floor spreading (Hess); plate tectonic theory; types of plate boundaries (divergent, convergent — oceanic-oceanic, oceanic-continental, continental-continental — and transform); landforms created at each boundary type; earthquake distribution; volcanic distribution; hot spots (Hawaii); measuring plates (GPS, paleomagnetism). |
| 21.3 | **Rocks, Minerals & the Rock Cycle** | Mineral properties (hardness, luster, cleavage, streak, color, density); the Mohs scale; common minerals (quartz, feldspar, mica, calcite, halite, magnetite); rock classification — igneous (intrusive/extrusive, granite/basalt), sedimentary (clastic/chemical/organic, sandstone/limestone/coal), metamorphic (foliated/non-foliated, slate/marble); the rock cycle — pathways and driving forces; weathering (mechanical and chemical) and erosion. |
| 21.4 | **The Water Cycle & Oceans** | Water cycle processes (evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, runoff, infiltration, percolation); freshwater distribution (ice caps, groundwater, surface water, atmosphere); aquifers and groundwater recharge; ocean floor features (mid-ocean ridge, trench, continental shelf/slope/rise); ocean water properties (salinity, temperature, density, stratification); ocean currents (surface — wind-driven; deep — thermohaline circulation, the global conveyor belt). |
| 21.5 | **Weather & Atmosphere** | Atmospheric layers (troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere); composition of atmosphere; atmospheric pressure and altitude; air masses (classification, source regions); fronts (cold, warm, stationary, occluded) and associated weather; storms (thunderstorms, tornadoes, hurricanes); weather instruments and maps; climate zones (Köppen classification — tropical, arid, temperate, continental, polar); weather vs. climate distinction. |
| 21.6 | **The Solar System & Universe** | Earth-Moon-Sun system (day/night, seasons — caused by axial tilt not distance, moon phases, tides, eclipses); inner planets vs. outer planets; characteristics and comparison; dwarf planets, asteroids, comets, Kuiper Belt, Oort Cloud; stars — life cycle (main sequence, red giant, white dwarf, neutron star, black hole); stellar classification; Hertzsprung-Russell diagram; galaxies (Milky Way structure); cosmic scale (AU, light-year, parsec); Big Bang theory (evidence: CMB, redshift). |
| 21.7 | **Earth's History** | Relative vs. absolute dating; law of superposition, original horizontality, cross-cutting relationships; index fossils; radioactive decay and half-life; geologic time scale (eons → eras → periods → epochs); major events (formation of Earth, origin of life, Cambrian explosion, mass extinction events — particularly the Big Five, rise of dinosaurs, K-Pg extinction, human evolution timeline). |
| 21.8 | **Human Impact & Environmental Science** | Climate change — evidence (temperature records, sea level, ice cores, CO₂ data from NOAA), causes (greenhouse effect, Keeling Curve, methane), projected impacts, adaptation vs. mitigation; deforestation and biodiversity loss; pollution (air — smog and acid rain; water — eutrophication; soil — heavy metals); resource management (renewable vs. nonrenewable energy; water scarcity; sustainable agriculture); the Paris Agreement and global policy response. |
