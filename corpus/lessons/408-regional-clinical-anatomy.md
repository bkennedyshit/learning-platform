## 1. Head & Neck

### 1.1 Skull Base (Internal)
Three cranial fossae (viewed from above):
| Fossa | Boundaries | Key structures |
|-------|-----------|---------------|
| **Anterior** | Frontal bone, ethmoid (cribriform plate), lesser wing sphenoid | Olfactory bulbs (CN I), frontal lobe |
| **Middle** | Greater wing sphenoid, temporal bone | Sella turcica (pituitary), CN II-VI, middle meningeal artery (temporal), cavernous sinus |
| **Posterior** | Temporal bone (petrous), occipital | Foramen magnum (brainstem/medulla), CN VII–XII, cerebellum, internal auditory canal |

**Middle meningeal artery** runs in groove on inner temporal bone. **Temporal bone fracture** → arterial bleed → **epidural hematoma** ("lucid interval" — patient conscious then deteriorates). CT: biconvex/lenticular hyperdense lesion.

### 1.2 Meninges & Intracranial Spaces
```
Skull bone
  ↓
Epidural space (potential) — epidural hematoma: arterial (MMA), biconvex
  ↓
Dura mater (outer, tough; folds: falx cerebri, tentorium cerebelli)
  ↓
Subdural space (potential) — subdural hematoma: venous (bridging veins), crescent-shaped
  ↓
Arachnoid mater (spider-web)
  ↓
Subarachnoid space (REAL space) — CSF; subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAA, aneurysm rupture) = "thunderclap headache"
  ↓
Pia mater (tightly adherent to brain surface)
```

### 1.3 Circle of Willis
- **Anterior circulation** (internal carotid → ACA + MCA): frontal lobe + parietal lobe + anterior temporal
- **Posterior circulation** (vertebral → basilar → PCA + PICA): brainstem + cerebellum + occipital lobe + posterior temporal
- **Communicating arteries:** anterior communicating (AComm — most common aneurysm site → CN III compression) + 2 × posterior communicating (PComm — aneurysm → CN III palsy)

### 1.4 Triangles of the Neck
**Anterior triangle** (medial to SCM, below mandible, to midline):
- Contains: carotid sheath (CCA + IJV + vagus), thyroid, trachea
- Subdivisions: carotid, muscular, submandibular, submental triangles

**Posterior triangle** (lateral to SCM, trapezius, clavicle):
- Contains: accessory nerve (CN XI — crosses at Erb's point, ~6 cm above clavicle; injury during neck surgery → trapezius paralysis), brachial plexus roots (C5–T1), subclavian artery, lymph nodes
- Erb's point: where C5 and C6 emerge together — important in obstetric (birth injury) brachial plexus palsy

## 2. Thorax

### 2.1 Mediastinum Divisions
| Division | Contents |
|----------|---------|
| **Superior** (above sternal angle, T4) | Aortic arch + great vessels, trachea, esophagus, thymus, thoracic duct |
| **Anterior inferior** (in front of heart) | Thymus (in children), lymph nodes, fat |
| **Middle** | Heart + pericardium, ascending aorta, SVC/IVC, pulmonary trunk, mainstem bronchi |
| **Posterior** | Esophagus, descending thoracic aorta, thoracic duct, azygos vein, sympathetic trunks |

### 2.2 Diaphragm Openings (important: what passes where)
| Level | Opening | Passes through |
|-------|---------|---------------|
| T8 | Caval hiatus | IVC + right phrenic nerve |
| T10 | Esophageal hiatus | Esophagus + vagus nerve (CN X) |
| T12 | Aortic hiatus | Aorta + thoracic duct + azygos vein |

> Mnemonic: **"I 8 (ate) 10 eggs At 12"** — IVC at T8, Esophagus at T10, Aorta at T12.

### 2.3 Breast Anatomy & Lymph Drainage
- Lies over pectoralis major; extends from rib 2–6, sternum to mid-axillary line
- **75% of breast lymph** → axillary nodes (main route of breast cancer spread)
- **Sentinel lymph node** = first axillary node in drainage chain; biopsied in breast cancer staging
- **Peau d'orange** (skin dimpling) = lymphatic obstruction by tumor → dermal lymphedema

## 3. Abdomen & Pelvis

### 3.1 Abdominal Regions — 4 Quadrants vs. 9 Regions
**Four quadrants** (clinical shorthand): RUQ, LUQ, RLQ, LLQ

**Nine regions:**
| | Left | Center | Right |
|--|------|--------|-------|
| **Upper** | Left hypochondriac (spleen) | Epigastric (stomach, liver) | Right hypochondriac (liver, gallbladder) |
| **Middle** | Left lumbar (descending colon) | Umbilical (small bowel, transverse colon) | Right lumbar (ascending colon) |
| **Lower** | Left iliac (sigmoid colon, ovary) | Hypogastric/Pubic (bladder, uterus) | Right iliac (appendix, cecum, ovary) |

### 3.2 Inguinal Canal
- **Contents in males:** spermatic cord (vas deferens + testicular vessels + pampiniform plexus + nerves)
- **Contents in females:** round ligament of uterus
- **Deep ring** (entrance, at lateral inguinal ligament) → **Superficial ring** (exit, above pubic tubercle)
- **Indirect hernia** (most common, especially young males): enters deep ring → travels whole canal
- **Direct hernia** (older males): pushes through posterior wall (Hesselbach's triangle: inferior epigastric vessels + lateral border rectus + inguinal ligament)

## 4. Upper Limb

### 4.1 Brachial Plexus — Clinical Palsies
| Injury level | Mechanism | Muscles paralyzed | Posture |
|-------------|-----------|-------------------|---------|
| **Erb's palsy (C5–C6)** | Shoulder depression + head pushed away (dystocia, fall) | Deltoid, supraspinatus, infraspinatus, biceps | "Waiter's tip" — arm adducted, internally rotated, elbow extended, forearm pronated |
| **Klumpke's palsy (C8–T1)** | Arm forcefully abducted + elevated (grabbing a branch) | Intrinsic hand muscles | Claw hand; Horner's syndrome if T1 sympathetic fibers involved |

### 4.2 Axilla
**Contents:** axillary artery (with 3 parts relative to pectoralis minor) + axillary vein + brachial plexus cords + 5 groups of axillary lymph nodes
**Axillary nerve (C5–C6):** exits posterior cord → winds around surgical neck of humerus → innervates deltoid + teres minor + sensory "regimental badge" patch. Injured in shoulder dislocation or proximal humerus fractures.

### 4.3 Cubital Fossa
Contents (lateral to medial): **TAN** — **T**endon (biceps), **A**rtery (brachial), **N**erve (median)
Floor: brachialis + supinator; roof: bicipital aponeurosis + skin

### 4.4 Carpal Tunnel
- Fibro-osseous tunnel at wrist: carpals (floor) + flexor retinaculum (roof)
- **Contents:** FDS (4), FDP (4), FPL (1) tendons + **median nerve** — 10 tendons + 1 nerve
- **NOT in tunnel:** ulnar nerve (travels in Guyon's canal, medial to carpal tunnel)
- **Carpal tunnel syndrome:** median nerve compression → pain/numbness in lateral 3.5 fingers; thenar wasting; Tinel's + Phalen's sign

## 5. Lower Limb

### 5.1 Femoral Triangle
**Boundaries:** inguinal ligament (superior) + sartorius (lateral) + adductor longus (medial)
**Contents (lateral → medial):** **NAVY** — **N**erve (femoral), **A**rtery (femoral), **V**ein (femoral), **Y**-fronts (lymphatics)
Femoral hernia: passes medial to femoral vein, below inguinal ligament — more common in women; high strangulation risk

### 5.2 Popliteal Fossa
**Contents:** popliteal artery + popliteal vein + sciatic nerve (splits into common fibular + tibial here) + posterior cutaneous nerve of thigh
**Popliteal aneurysm** — most common peripheral artery aneurysm; risk of distal embolization
**Baker's cyst** — fluid-filled bursa in popliteal fossa (semimembranosus bursa); associated with knee OA

### 5.3 Ankle & Foot
**Tarsal tunnel** (medial ankle): tibialis posterior + FDL + FHL tendons + posterior tibial artery + tibial nerve → tarsal tunnel syndrome (medial plantar nerve compression → heel/medial foot numbness)
**Anatomical structures of "ankle complex":** medial malleolus + lateral malleolus + posterior malleolus = "bony mortise"; stabilized by lateral ligaments (ATFL most commonly sprained in inversion injury) + medial deltoid ligament (stronger, torn in eversion)

## 6. Surface Anatomy — High-Yield Landmarks

| Landmark | Surface marking | Clinical use |
|----------|----------------|-------------|
| Sternal angle (Louis) | Junction manubrium + body sternum | T4/T5 disc; 2nd rib; bifurcation of trachea; aortic arch |
| McBurney's point | 1/3 of ASIS–umbilicus line | Appendix location; appendicitis tenderness |
| Femoral pulse | Mid-inguinal point (midpoint of ASIS to pubic symphysis) | NOT mid-inguinal ligament |
| Saphenofemoral junction | 4 cm below and lateral to pubic tubercle | Great saphenous vein ligation |
| L4 vertebra | Iliac crests | LP landmark; aortic bifurcation (L4) |
| Transpyloric plane | L1, midway between jugular notch and pubic symphysis | Pylorus, hilum of kidneys, SMA origin, duodenojejunal flexure |

## 7. Radiology Orientation (Clinical Integration)

### X-Ray (Chest)
- **ABCDE approach:** Airway (trachea midline?), Breathing (lung markings), Cardiac (cardiothoracic ratio <0.5), Diaphragm (clear angle, right higher), Everything else (bones, soft tissue, devices)
- Pleural effusion → blunting of costophrenic angle; >200 mL needed to be visible on PA film

### CT Abdomen (Axial Cross-Section at L1–L2)
Identify from anterior to posterior: anterior abdominal wall → bowel loops → aorta (left of IVC) → IVC → vertebral body → paraspinal muscles → kidneys (retroperitoneal — at this level: right kidney upper pole, left kidney mid-body)
