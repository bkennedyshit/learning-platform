# 🛤️ Japanese — Learning Path

> This document is **hand-curated** and should remain stable.

---

## 📋 Prerequisites

Before starting this track, you should have:

1. **No prior Japanese required** — the track starts from zero.
2. **A spaced-repetition tool** — Anki (free). Japanese vocabulary load is enormous; SR is non-negotiable.
3. **A kanji SRS** — Wanikani (free for first 3 levels / ~60 kanji) or Anki with a kanji deck. Start this immediately and run it in parallel forever.
4. **Headphones** — listening to native audio is essential from day one.
5. **English grammar terminology** helpful — knowing what a subject, object, and verb are speeds up the particles chapter enormously. See [Track 19](Subject_Plan) if needed.

## 📖 Recommended Chapter Order

### Phase 1: Writing Systems (Weeks 1–3)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale | Time to completion |
|-------|---------|-----------|-------------------|
| 1 | [37.1 - Hiragana — Foundation & Practice](37.1---Hiragana-—-Foundation-&-Practice) | The foundation of everything — you cannot read or write anything without hiragana | 1–2 weeks to 100% |
| 2 | [37.2 - Katakana — Loanwords & Foreign Names](37.2---Katakana-—-Loanwords-&-Foreign-Names) | Immediately useful (menus, signs, loanwords); builds on hiragana habits | 1–2 weeks to 100% |

**Phase 1 deliverable:** Read any hiragana and katakana text without hesitation (100% accuracy, <1 second per character). This is a binary checkpoint — don't proceed until it's solid.

### Phase 2: Grammar Foundation — JLPT N5 (Weeks 4–10)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 3 | [37.3 - Kanji — System Overview & First 100 (JLPT N5)](37.3---Kanji-—-System-Overview-&-First-100-(JLPT-N5)) | Start kanji immediately — early investment has compounding returns |
| 4 | [37.4 - Particles & Basic Sentence Structure](37.4---Particles-&-Basic-Sentence-Structure) | The axiomatic layer — particles define how every word relates in a sentence |
| 5 | [37.5 - Verb Conjugation — Polite vs Plain Forms](37.5---Verb-Conjugation-—-Polite-vs-Plain-Forms) | The workhorse of the grammar — て-form alone unlocks dozens of patterns |

**Phase 2 deliverable:** Construct basic sentences in present, past, positive, and negative. Introduce yourself, describe objects, ask for things.

### Phase 3: Intermediate Grammar — JLPT N4 (Weeks 11–18)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 6 | [37.6 - Adjectives — i-adjectives vs na-adjectives](37.6---Adjectives-—-i-adjectives-vs-na-adjectives) | Short chapter but essential — adjective errors are very visible |
| 7 | [37.7 - Politeness Levels & Honorifics (Keigo)](37.7---Politeness-Levels-&-Honorifics-(Keigo)) | Without at least teineigo, you can't function in most Japanese social contexts |

**Phase 3 deliverable:** Hold a 5-minute polite conversation. Navigate a restaurant, shop, or hotel in Japanese. Read NHK Easy News with ≤20% lookups.

### Phase 4: Immersion Transition — JLPT N3 Target (Weeks 19–30+)

| Order | Chapter | Rationale |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 8 | [37.8 - Reading, Listening & Cultural Immersion](37.8---Reading,-Listening-&-Cultural-Immersion) | The methodology for breaking out of structured study into native media |

**Phase 4 deliverable:** Watch a beginner anime episode (with Japanese subtitles) and understand 60%+. Read NHK Easy News with ≤10% lookups.

## 🔀 Non-Negotiable Daily Habits (Every Phase)

| Habit | Time | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|
| Anki vocabulary review | 15–20 min | Never skip — SR breaks without daily review |
| Kanji SRS (Wanikani or Anki) | 10–15 min | Kanji compounds; daily review is essential |
| Comprehensible input (listening) | 20–30 min | Comprehensible Japanese YouTube from Phase 2+ |
| Writing practice | 5–10 min | Write hiragana and katakana until automatic; then kanji |

> **The Japanese learning trap:** Many learners stall at "intermediate plateau" because they finish a grammar resource but haven't built enough input hours. The cure is always more comprehensible input, not more grammar study.

## ➡️ What Comes Next (N2/N1)

After reaching N3 through this track:

1. **Bunpro** — grammar SRS for N3→N2 grammar patterns (there are ~300).
2. **Yomichan / Yomitan** — browser extension for hover-lookup while reading native web content.
3. **Immersion reading** — native manga without furigana, light novels, web novels (Shōsetsuka ni Narō).
4. **Anki sentence mining** — build your own Anki deck from sentences you encounter in native media.
5. **N2/N1 kanji** — expand Wanikani or switch to KanjiStudy/RTK for the remaining ~1300 kanji.

---

## 🚫 What This Track Does NOT Cover

- **Japanese writing composition** (formal letters, business writing) — separate study; this track focuses on reading comprehension and spoken communication.
- **N2/N1 grammar** — this track targets N3; N2/N1 require a separate resource (Tobira, Bunpro).
- **Business Japanese** — Keigo in chapter 37.7 is the foundation; full business Japanese is a layer on top.
