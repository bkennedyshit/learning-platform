# 07.2 — Functionalism, Physicalism & Dualism

> *"Minds are what brains do."* — Marvin Minsky

> *"The mind is not what the brain is made of — it's what the brain does."* — Functionalism's core claim

## 📚 1. Substance Dualism (Descartes)

René Descartes (1641) argued that the mind and body are two distinct substances:
- **Res extensa:** Extended, physical substance — everything material, divisible, spatial
- **Res cogitans:** Thinking, non-extended, immaterial substance — the mind/soul

**The cogito:** "I think, therefore I am" — the one thing certain even under radical doubt is the existence of the thinking self.

**The pineal gland:** Descartes proposed the pineal gland as the point of mind-body interaction. The immaterial mind "pushes" the pineal gland to initiate physical movement.

### 1.1 The Causal Interaction Problem

**Objection (Princess Elizabeth of Bohemia, 1643):** How can an immaterial, non-spatial substance causally affect a material, spatial substance? For A to push B, A must have some spatial location and physical properties. If mind is genuinely non-physical, it can't causally interact with anything physical.

Descartes never satisfactorily answered this. It remains the defining problem for substance dualism.

**Modern status:** Most philosophers reject substance dualism. Neuroscience provides overwhelming evidence that mental states correlate with brain states, are affected by brain damage, drugs, and disease — suggesting the mind is not independent of the physical.

## 📚 2. Physicalism — The Identity Theory

**Type Identity Theory** (Smart, Place, Armstrong): Mental states ARE physical states. Pain is identical to C-fibre firing. Hunger is identical to some brain state. This is an empirical theory — the identity is discovered, not defined.

### 2.1 The Multiple Realizability Objection (Putnam 1967)

**Objection:** Type identity theory says pain = C-fibre firing IN ALL CREATURES. But pain can be realised in:
- Human C-fibres
- Octopus pain-receptor analogues (which fire differently)
- Hypothetical Martian pain systems (silicon-based?)

If pain can be realised in different physical substrates, then pain is NOT identical to any one physical type.

**Token Identity Theory:** The response — maybe mental state tokens are identical to physical state tokens. THIS pain in THIS moment = this particular neural firing event. But different instances of pain can be different physical types.

**Problem with token identity:** It becomes uninformative. If every mental token is just some physical token, we learn nothing about what KIND of physical states mental states are.

## 📚 3. Eliminative Materialism (Churchland)

The most radical physicalist position: **folk psychology is false and will be replaced by neuroscience**.

Folk psychology explains behaviour using terms like "belief," "desire," "intention," "pain." Eliminativists claim these concepts do not carve nature at its joints — there are no such things as beliefs or desires in the brain, any more than there is "phlogiston" or "caloric."

**Paul and Patricia Churchland:** Just as we replaced folk chemistry (phlogiston theory) with atomic chemistry, we will replace folk psychology with a mature neuroscience that uses entirely different conceptual vocabulary.

**Implication for AI:** If beliefs and desires don't exist, then AI systems that "have beliefs" in the folk-psychological sense don't "have" them in any deep sense either — they implement something our folk vocabulary misleadingly maps onto.

**The self-refutation objection:** If there are no beliefs, how can Churchland believe eliminativism is true? The position seems self-undermining.

---

## 📚 4. Functionalism

**Functionalism** (Putnam 1967, Lewis 1972): Mental states are defined by their **functional roles** — their causal relations to inputs, outputs, and other mental states.

Pain = the state that is caused by tissue damage, that causes avoidance behaviour, that causes the desire for the damage to stop, that causes reports of "ouch."

The identity is functional, not physical. Multiple realizability is thus NO PROBLEM for functionalism: anything that plays the functional role of pain IS pain, regardless of physical substrate.

### 4.1 Functionalism and AI

**Direct implication for AI:** If functionalism is true, any system that implements the correct functional organisation has mental states — including consciousness. A silicon computer running the right program would have beliefs, desires, and possibly phenomenal experience.

This is the philosophical foundation for strong AI (AGI = artificial general intelligence with mental states).

### 4.2 The Chinese Room Argument (Searle 1980)

**Searle's thought experiment:** Imagine a person who doesn't understand Chinese, locked in a room. They receive Chinese characters (inputs), look up rules in a book of rules (the program), output Chinese symbols accordingly. From outside, the room appears to understand Chinese perfectly.

**Searle's claim:** The room implements the correct function for understanding Chinese — but the person inside has NO understanding. Syntax (symbol manipulation) is not semantics (meaning). Programs are syntactic; minds are semantic.

**Implication:** A computer running a language program does not understand language, regardless of how well it passes the Turing Test.

**Replies to the Chinese Room:**

1. **Systems Reply:** The individual person doesn't understand Chinese, but the *system* (person + rules + room) does. Searle identifies with the wrong level of description.
   - Searle's counter: Internalise the entire system (memorise the rules). Still no understanding.

2. **Robot Reply:** Add sensors and actuators. The Chinese Room now interacts with the world. Grounding symbols in perception gives them meaning.
   - Searle's counter: Adding causal inputs doesn't change the syntactic character of the processing.

3. **Brain Simulator Reply:** If the program exactly simulates the functional organisation of a Chinese speaker's brain at the neuron level, would the room understand?
   - Most philosophers find this reply most challenging for Searle.

4. **Other Minds Reply:** We attribute understanding to other humans on behavioural evidence only — the same evidence we'd use for the room.

## 📚 5. Inverted Qualia Objection to Functionalism

**Inverted spectrum thought experiment:** Imagine two people who are functionally identical — red things cause them both to say "red," pick ripe fruit, stop at red lights. But internally, the subjective experience of red for Person A is what the subjective experience of green is for Person B, and vice versa.

If functionally identical people can have different qualia, then qualia are NOT functionally defined. Functionalism is incomplete — it cannot account for the subjective character of experience.

**The absent qualia objection:** Could there be a system that is functionally identical to a human but has NO qualia at all? This is the philosophical zombie question from [07.1](07.1---The-Hard-Problem-of-Consciousness) applied to functionalism.

## ⚠️ 7. Common Misconceptions

1. **"Functionalism is the view that minds are computer programs."** Functionalism doesn't commit to Turing-machine computation specifically. Anything that realises the right causal-functional organisation counts — this could be neural, biological, silicon, or hydraulic.

2. **"Searle's Chinese Room proves computers can't be conscious."** Searle argues computers can't UNDERSTAND (have semantics). Whether they could be phenomenally conscious is a separate question. Some philosophers grant Searle's point about understanding but reject his broader conclusion about consciousness.

3. **"Type identity theory is obviously false."** Type identity theory with appropriate grain of description (neurological rather than behavioural) is still debated. "C-fibre firing" was a simplification — modern identity theorists are more careful.

4. **"Physicalism means everything reduces to particle physics."** Not necessarily. Physicalism says mental states are physical states; it doesn't say they must be described at the particle-physics level. Many physicalists are non-reductive physicalists (the mental supervenes on but isn't reducible to the physical).

5. **"Eliminative materialism is obviously self-refuting."** The self-refutation objection is popular but disputed. Churchland's response: perhaps "the Churchlands hold the belief that eliminativism is true" is itself eliminable — replaced by a neural state description.

6. **"Dualism is religious and physicalism is scientific."** Many purely secular philosophers defend forms of property dualism (Chalmers, Nagel). And physicalism makes strong positive metaphysical claims that go beyond what current science establishes.
