# APP-03.3 — Animals — Behavior, Adaptations & Life Cycles

A monarch caterpillar and a monarch butterfly look like two different animals, but they are the same animal at different stages of its life. A duckling follows its mother everywhere without being taught, while a dog has to be taught to sit. A polar bear has thick fur, and a monarch flies thousands of miles before winter. This lesson is about how animals grow up, how they behave, and the body parts and behaviors that help them survive.

The course plan places this lesson in grades 3-4. The main text talks to the learner. The **Adult note** boxes are for the person guiding them.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson the learner should be able to:

1. Compare **complete** and **incomplete metamorphosis** and decide which one an insect has.
2. Describe a frog's life cycle and compare it with a mammal's or bird's.
3. Sort behaviors into **inherited** and **learned**.
4. Tell a **physical adaptation** from a **behavioral adaptation**, and sort animals into **vertebrates** and **invertebrates**.

## Core ideas

### Life cycles

A **life cycle** is the set of stages an animal goes through from the start of its life until it can have young of its own. Then the cycle starts again.

**Complete metamorphosis** has four stages that look very different:

1. **Egg**
2. **Larva:** a caterpillar or grub that eats and grows. It sheds its outer covering (molts) several times as it gets bigger.
3. **Pupa:** a resting stage. A butterfly pupa is called a **chrysalis**. Inside, the body is rebuilt.
4. **Adult:** usually has wings and can lay eggs.

Butterflies, moths, beetles, ants, and flies have complete metamorphosis.

**Incomplete metamorphosis** has three stages:

1. **Egg**
2. **Nymph:** looks like a small adult without full wings. It molts several times, and its wing buds get bigger each time.
3. **Adult**

Grasshoppers, crickets, and cockroaches have incomplete metamorphosis. In both kinds, an insect does all its growing in the young stages. A small fly is not a baby fly; it is a kind of fly that is small as an adult.

**Monarch butterfly facts** from the U.S. Forest Service: a female lays 100 to 300 eggs in her life; eggs hatch about four days after they are laid; a newly hatched caterpillar is less than 1 centimeter long and grows to about 5 centimeters; and the change from pupa to adult can take as few as five days.

**Frogs** also go through metamorphosis. Most frogs lay eggs in water or damp places. The egg hatches into a **tadpole**, which has gills, a long tail, and no legs. During metamorphosis, the gills and tail disappear and four legs grow. The adult frog breathes air and catches insects. A few kinds of frogs skip the tadpole stage and hatch from the egg as tiny froglets.

**Mammals and birds** do not have metamorphosis. A puppy or a chick already has the same body plan as its parents and gradually grows bigger. Most mammals are born live and drink milk from their mother. Birds hatch from eggs with hard shells.

### Inherited and learned behaviors

A **behavior** is something an animal does.

- **Inherited behaviors** (also called **innate** behaviors or instincts) are passed from parents through genes. The animal can do them the first time, without practice. Examples: a reflex, like your knee jerking when a doctor taps it; and **migration**, the long trip some animals make each season to find food or warmer weather.
- **Learned behaviors** come from experience, so they can change when the world changes. Examples:
  - **Habituation:** an animal stops reacting to something harmless that happens again and again. Prairie dogs that live near walking paths stop sounding an alarm at human footsteps, but they still call when a predator comes.
  - **Imprinting:** at a certain young age, an animal learns quickly who to follow. Newly hatched ducklings bond with the first adult they see, usually their mother.
  - **Training:** a dog learns to sit because sitting gets it a treat. Scientists call this conditioning.

### Physical and behavioral adaptations

An **adaptation** is an inherited feature that helps a kind of animal survive and have young where it lives.

- **Physical adaptations** are body parts or body features: a duck's webbed feet, an owl's sharp talons, a polar bear's thick fur and fat, a frog whose skin color blends in with leaves (**camouflage**), and the bright warning colors of some poisonous frogs.
- **Behavioral adaptations** are things animals do: geese flying south for the winter, monarchs migrating to Mexico, or animals that sleep through cold winters (**hibernation**).

### Vertebrates and invertebrates

**Vertebrates** have a **backbone** (a spine made of small bones called vertebrae). Fish, amphibians (frogs and salamanders), reptiles, birds, and mammals are vertebrates.

**Invertebrates** have no backbone. Insects, spiders, worms, snails, crabs, and jellyfish are invertebrates. Many invertebrates, such as insects, spiders, and crabs, have a hard outer covering called an **exoskeleton** and legs with joints.

- **Insects** have six legs and three body parts: head, thorax, and abdomen.
- **Spiders** have eight legs and two main body parts. Spiders are not insects.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Which kind of metamorphosis?

Description: "A young ladybug is a spiky, wingless grub that looks nothing like the adult. Later it attaches itself to a leaf and stays still inside a hard case. A spotted adult with wings comes out."

1. Is there a stage that looks nothing like the adult? Yes, the grub (a **larva**).
2. Is there a still, resting stage? Yes, inside the hard case (a **pupa**).
3. Egg, larva, pupa, adult is four stages. **Complete metamorphosis.**
4. Check by contrast: a young grasshopper already looks like a small adult without full wings (a **nymph**) and has no resting stage, so a grasshopper has **incomplete metamorphosis.**

### Example 2: Monarch numbers

Use the Forest Service facts above.

1. **Egg to caterpillar:** about 4 days.
2. **Pupa to adult:** as few as 5 days.
3. Those two stages together take at least 4 + 5 = **9 days**. (The caterpillar stage comes in between and adds more time.)
4. **Growth:** a caterpillar grows from less than 1 centimeter to about 5 centimeters. Since it started below 1 centimeter, it grows more than 5 − 1 = 4 centimeters.
5. **Migration:** the last generation of the year flies 2,000 to 3,000 miles to Mexico. The difference between the shortest and longest trips is 3,000 − 2,000 = **1,000 miles**.

### Example 3: Inherited or learned?

1. **A spider spins a web the first time it tries.** No one taught it, and all spiders of its kind build the same type of web. **Inherited.**
2. **A horse in a field stops running away from a passing tractor after many days.** It stopped reacting to something harmless that kept happening. **Learned** (habituation).
3. **A parrot says "hello" in its owner's voice.** It copied sounds it heard. **Learned.**
4. **A baby sea turtle crawls toward the sea right after hatching.** It does this the first time, without practice. **Inherited.**

Check: the question to ask is "Could the animal do this the very first time, with no practice or teaching?"

### Example 4: Sorting with a key

Sort a robin, an earthworm, a salmon, a beetle, a lizard, and a crab.

1. **Does it have a backbone?** Robin, salmon, lizard: yes, **vertebrates**. Earthworm, beetle, crab: no, **invertebrates**.
2. Among the vertebrates, **feathers?** Robin: bird. **Scales and fins, lives in water?** Salmon: fish. **Dry scales, breathes air, lays eggs on land?** Lizard: reptile.
3. Among the invertebrates, **six legs and three body parts?** Beetle: insect. **Hard shell, jointed legs, lives in water?** Crab: a crustacean. **Soft body, no legs?** Earthworm: a worm.

## Common misconceptions and boundary cases

- **"A caterpillar and a butterfly are different animals."** They are the same animal at different life stages.
- **"Small insects are babies that will grow."** Insects stop growing once they become adults. All growth happens in the larva or nymph stage.
- **"An animal grows an adaptation because it needs it."** A single animal cannot grow thick fur because it is cold. Adaptations are inherited features that a kind of animal has developed over many, many generations. (You will learn how in [APP-03.7 — Evolution & Natural Selection](/app-037-evolution-natural-selection).)
- **"Learned behaviors are passed on to the babies."** A dog that learns to sit does not have puppies that already know how. Learned behaviors must be learned again by each animal.
- **"Spiders are insects."** Spiders have eight legs and two body parts. Insects have six legs and three body parts.
- **"All frogs start as tadpoles."** Most do, but some kinds hatch directly as tiny froglets.

## Practice

1. Name the four stages of complete metamorphosis in order.
2. A young cricket looks like a small adult without full wings. What is this stage called, and which kind of metamorphosis does a cricket have?
3. Name two body changes that happen when a tadpole becomes a frog.
4. Inherited or learned: (a) a newly hatched duckling follows its mother; (b) a cat comes running when it hears the can opener; (c) a bird flies south in fall for the first time.
5. Physical or behavioral adaptation: (a) a snowshoe hare's fur turns white in winter; (b) bears sleeping through the winter in a den; (c) an owl's sharp talons.
6. Vertebrate or invertebrate: a snail, a shark, a frog, a butterfly, a whale.
7. A beetle has six legs and three body parts. Is it an insect? Is a tick with eight legs an insect?
8. A monarch female lays 300 eggs and another lays 100. How many more eggs did the first female lay?

<details>
<summary>Answer key and self-check</summary>

1. Egg, larva, pupa, adult.
2. A nymph. Crickets have incomplete metamorphosis.
3. Any two: the tail disappears; the gills disappear; four legs grow; the jaws get bigger for catching insects.
4. (a) Learned: this is imprinting, a fast kind of learning that happens at a young age. (b) Learned: the cat connected the sound with food. (c) Inherited: migration is built in; the bird does it the first time.
5. (a) Physical. (b) Behavioral. (c) Physical.
6. Vertebrates: shark, frog, whale. Invertebrates: snail, butterfly.
7. Yes, the beetle is an insect. No, the tick is not an insect; with eight legs it belongs with spiders and mites.
8. 300 − 100 = 200 more eggs.

**Adult self-check:** For questions 4 and 5, ask the learner to give the clue that decided the answer ("it did it the first time," "it is a body part"). Imprinting is a borderline case worth discussing: the duckling is ready to follow from birth, but *who* it follows is learned.
