# Subject Syllabus: 17 - Statistics & Probability

*Back to [Learning Index](00---09---Learning-Index) · [9-12 Pipeline](9-12-High-School-Pipeline)*

Statistics is the language of data — the discipline that separates **information from noise.** In the 21st century, statistical literacy is as fundamental as reading literacy. Every scientific study, every election poll, every A/B test, every AI model evaluation lives and dies by statistical reasoning. This track builds from raw data through probability distributions to formal inference — covering the full AP Statistics curriculum while maintaining the deeper mathematical understanding that makes the AI/ML track accessible.

## 📝 3. Chapter Outline

| # | Chapter | Core skill |
|---|---------|-----------|
| 17.1 | **Exploring Data (1-Variable)** | Types of data (categorical, quantitative); distribution shapes (symmetric, skewed, bimodal, uniform); center (mean, median, mode — and when each is appropriate); spread (range, IQR, standard deviation, variance); five-number summary; boxplots; histograms; stem-and-leaf plots; dot plots; outliers (1.5×IQR rule); effect of transformations on center/spread. |
| 17.2 | **Exploring Data (2-Variable)** | Scatterplots; direction/form/strength/outliers of association; correlation coefficient r (interpretation and limitations); least-squares regression line (slope and y-intercept interpretation); coefficient of determination r²; residuals and residual plots; influential points; lurking variables and the correlation ≠ causation principle. |
| 17.3 | **Sampling & Experimental Design** | Census vs. sample; sampling methods (SRS, stratified, cluster, systematic); bias types (voluntary response, undercoverage, nonresponse, wording bias); observational studies vs. experiments; elements of a well-designed experiment (control, randomization, replication, blinding); confounding variables; block designs; scope of inference — when can we generalize? when can we claim causation? |
| 17.4 | **Probability** | Basic rules (addition, multiplication, complement); sample spaces; conditional probability P(A\|B); independence; Bayes' theorem (intro); Venn diagrams and two-way tables; Law of Total Probability; Law of Large Numbers; simulations for estimating probability. Cross-link: [44.8 - Probability](44.8---Probability) |
| 17.5 | **Discrete Random Variables & Distributions** | Random variables (discrete vs. continuous); probability distribution tables; expected value E(X) and variance Var(X); binomial distribution (conditions, mean, standard deviation, binomial formula, binomial tables/calculator); geometric distribution; transformations of random variables; sums and differences of independent random variables. |
| 17.6 | **Normal Distribution & Sampling Distributions** | Normal distribution (shape, empirical rule 68-95-99.7); standard normal (z-scores, z-table); using inverse normal; assessing normality (normal probability plots); sampling distribution of the sample mean x̄ (Central Limit Theorem — the most important theorem in statistics); sampling distribution of the sample proportion p̂; conditions for CLT application. |
| 17.7 | **Confidence Intervals** | Logic of confidence intervals; 1-sample z interval for proportion; 1-sample t interval for mean (t-distribution, degrees of freedom); 2-sample t interval for difference of means; conditions (random, normal/large sample, independent); margin of error; correct interpretation of confidence level; determining sample size. |
| 17.8 | **Significance Tests (Hypothesis Testing)** | Null and alternative hypotheses; one-tailed vs. two-tailed tests; test statistic; p-value (definition and interpretation); significance level α; Type I and Type II errors; power of a test; 1-proportion z-test; 1-sample t-test; 2-sample t-test; matched pairs t-test; chi-square goodness of fit; chi-square test of independence/homogeneity; ANOVA (intro). |
