# 40.1 — Anatomical Terminology & Body Organization

*Back to [Subject_Plan](Subject_Plan) | [Learning Index](00---09---Learning-Index)*

> **Chapter goal:** Master the shared language of anatomy — directional terms, body planes, cavities, tissue classification, and levels of organization — so every subsequent chapter can be described with precision.

---

## 1. Anatomical Position

**Definition:** The standard reference posture from which all anatomical descriptions are made.

- Body erect, feet together, face forward
- Arms at sides, palms facing **anteriorly** (forward)
- All directional terms are defined relative to this position — regardless of the body's actual posture during observation

> ⚠️ **Common misconception:** "Anterior" means "front of the body in any position." It means front of the body *in anatomical position*. A patient lying prone has their dorsal surface facing up, but it remains "posterior."

---

## 2. Directional Terms

All terms come in **opposing pairs**:

| Term | Meaning | Opposite | Example |
|------|---------|----------|---------|
| **Superior** (cranial) | Toward the head | Inferior (caudal) | The heart is superior to the liver |
| **Inferior** (caudal) | Toward the feet | Superior (cranial) | The stomach is inferior to the diaphragm |
| **Anterior** (ventral) | Toward the front | Posterior (dorsal) | The sternum is anterior to the heart |
| **Posterior** (dorsal) | Toward the back | Anterior (ventral) | The vertebrae are posterior to the esophagus |
| **Medial** | Toward the midline | Lateral | The trachea is medial to the lungs |
| **Lateral** | Away from the midline | Medial | The ears are lateral to the nose |
| **Proximal** | Closer to the point of origin/attachment | Distal | The elbow is proximal to the wrist |
| **Distal** | Farther from the origin/attachment | Proximal | The fingers are distal to the elbow |
| **Superficial** | Toward the body surface | Deep | The skin is superficial to muscle |
| **Deep** | Away from the body surface | Superficial | The femur is deep to the quadriceps |
| **Ipsilateral** | Same side of the body | Contralateral | The right arm and right leg are ipsilateral |
| **Contralateral** | Opposite side of the body | Ipsilateral | Left brain controls contralateral (right) body |

> **For limbs only:** proximal/distal replace superior/inferior.

## 3. Body Planes

A **plane** is an imaginary flat surface that divides the body.

| Plane | Also called | Divides body into | Produces |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|---------|
| **Sagittal** | Median (if midline) | Left and right halves | Sagittal section |
| **Frontal** | Coronal | Anterior and posterior halves | Frontal/coronal section |
| **Transverse** | Horizontal, axial | Superior and inferior halves | Cross-section |
| **Oblique** | — | At any other angle | Oblique section |

> **Midsagittal (median) plane** = the sagittal plane exactly at the midline. Any sagittal plane *not* at the midline is a **parasagittal** plane.

**Clinical use:** CT scans are acquired in the **transverse (axial)** plane. MRIs are routinely reconstructed in all three planes. Radiologists describe anatomy using all three.

## 4. Body Cavities

### Dorsal Cavity (posterior)
Contains the nervous system structures:
- **Cranial cavity** — brain
- **Vertebral (spinal) canal** — spinal cord
- Lined by the **meninges** (dura mater, arachnoid mater, pia mater)

### Ventral Cavity (anterior)
Separated from the dorsal cavity by the body wall:
- **Thoracic cavity**
  - **Pleural cavities** (2) — each lung surrounded by its pleura
  - **Pericardial cavity** — heart
  - **Mediastinum** — the central partition between the two pleural cavities; contains heart, great vessels, trachea, esophagus, thymus
- **Abdominopelvic cavity**
  - **Abdominal cavity** — stomach, small/large intestine, liver, gallbladder, spleen, pancreas, kidneys (retroperitoneal)
  - **Pelvic cavity** — urinary bladder, rectum, reproductive organs

**Separating structures:**
- **Diaphragm** separates thoracic from abdominal cavities
- **Pelvic brim (pelvic inlet)** separates abdominal from pelvic cavities

> ⚠️ **Retroperitoneal structures** lie *behind* the peritoneum, not within the peritoneal cavity. The kidneys, adrenal glands, aorta, IVC, pancreas (body/tail), duodenum (2nd–4th parts), and ascending/descending colon are retroperitoneal. Surgeons access these differently.

## 5. Tissue Types — The Four Fundamental Classes

Every organ is built from combinations of these four:

### 5.1 Epithelial Tissue
- **Function:** covers surfaces, lines cavities, forms glands
- **Classification:** simple (one layer) vs. stratified (multiple layers) × squamous / cuboidal / columnar / pseudostratified
- **Key property:** avascular (no blood vessels); nourished by diffusion from underlying connective tissue
- **Examples:** skin epidermis (stratified squamous), small intestine lining (simple columnar with microvilli), tracheal lining (pseudostratified ciliated columnar)

### 5.2 Connective Tissue
- **Function:** binds, supports, protects, transports (blood), stores energy (fat)
- **Components:** cells embedded in extracellular matrix (ground substance + fibers — collagen, elastic, reticular)
- **Subtypes:** loose CT (areolar, adipose, reticular), dense CT (regular tendon/ligament, irregular dermis), cartilage (hyaline, fibrocartilage, elastic), bone, blood, lymph

### 5.3 Muscular Tissue
- **Function:** movement via contraction
- **Types:**
  - **Skeletal** — striated, voluntary, attached to bone via tendons
  - **Cardiac** — striated, involuntary, intercalated discs (gap junctions), found only in heart
  - **Smooth** — non-striated, involuntary, found in walls of hollow organs (GI tract, blood vessels, bladder, uterus)

### 5.4 Nervous Tissue
- **Function:** detect stimuli, integrate information, generate responses
- **Components:** neurons (excitable cells — soma, axon, dendrites) + neuroglia (support cells)
- **Location:** brain, spinal cord, nerves, ganglia

## 6. Levels of Organization (Bottom-Up)

```
Chemical level        → atoms, molecules (H₂O, proteins, DNA)
        ↓
Cellular level        → cells (the smallest living unit)
        ↓
Tissue level          → groups of cells with shared function
        ↓
Organ level           → two or more tissue types forming a structure with a specific function
        ↓
Organ system level    → organs that cooperate for a major body function
        ↓
Organism level        → all systems integrated = a living human being
```

## 7. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **"Ventral = front always."** Only in anatomical position. Ventral and anterior are synonymous for humans (bipedal). In quadrupeds, ventral = belly side (which is their inferior surface). For the brain, "anterior" and "ventral" are not always the same due to the cephalic flexure — extra care needed in neuroanatomy.

2. **Proximal/distal applied to the trunk.** These terms apply to *limbs* and *hollow organs* (proximal vs. distal bowel). They don't describe trunk structures.

3. **The mediastinum is not a cavity.** It's a region (partition) within the thoracic cavity, not a separate cavity itself.

4. **"Superficial" doesn't mean "less important."** Superficial structures are closer to the skin surface; depth has no clinical value judgment.
