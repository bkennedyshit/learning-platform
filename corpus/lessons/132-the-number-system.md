# 13.2 - The Number System

Arithmetic with only counting numbers cannot describe a temperature below zero, a bank overdraft, or an elevation below sea level. Extending the number line to the left of zero gives the integers, and filling in the gaps between integers gives the rational numbers. This lesson defines those sets, explains why the sign rules for integer arithmetic work, and shows how to compare and order any rational numbers.

## Prerequisites

You should be fluent with whole-number addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, and know what a fraction represents. Ratio language from [13.1 - Ratios, Rates & Proportions](/131-ratios-rates-proportions) helps, because a rational number is literally a ratio of integers.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

1. Place integers and rational numbers on a number line, name opposites, and evaluate absolute value as distance from $0$.
2. Add and subtract integers and explain the result with the chip model or a number line.
3. Multiply and divide integers, including evaluating powers such as $(-2)^4$ and $-2^4$ correctly.
4. Classify a number as whole, integer, rational, or irrational, and order a mixed list of rational numbers.

## Core ideas

### Number sets

- **Whole numbers:** $0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$
- **Integers:** the whole numbers and their opposites, $\dots, -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, \dots$
- **Rational numbers:** numbers that can be written as $\tfrac{p}{q}$ where $p$ and $q$ are integers and $q \ne 0$. Every integer is rational ($-5 = \tfrac{-5}{1}$). As decimals, rational numbers either terminate ($0.75$) or repeat ($0.333\ldots$).
- **Irrational numbers:** numbers on the number line that cannot be written as a ratio of integers, such as $\sqrt{2}$ and $\pi$. Their decimals neither terminate nor repeat.

Zero is an integer and a rational number, but it is neither positive nor negative.

### Opposites and absolute value

Two numbers are **opposites** if they are the same distance from $0$ on opposite sides: $7$ and $-7$. The opposite of $a$ is written $-a$, so $-(-7) = 7$.

The **absolute value** $\lvert a \rvert$ is the distance from $a$ to $0$. Distance is never negative, so $\lvert 7 \rvert = 7$, $\lvert -7 \rvert = 7$, and $\lvert 0 \rvert = 0$. The distance between two numbers $a$ and $b$ on a number line is $\lvert a - b \rvert$.

### Adding integers

In the **chip model**, a positive chip and a negative chip form a **zero pair** that adds to $0$. To add, combine chips and remove zero pairs. This gives two rules:

- **Same signs:** add the absolute values and keep the common sign. $-6 + (-9) = -15$.
- **Different signs:** subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger and take the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. $-8 + 5 = -3$.

On a number line, adding a positive number moves right and adding a negative number moves left.

### Subtracting integers

Subtraction is defined as adding the opposite:
$$a - b = a + (-b).$$
So $4 - 11 = 4 + (-11) = -7$, and $-5 - (-12) = -5 + 12 = 7$. In the chip model, subtracting $-12$ from $-5$ means you must remove $12$ negative chips; to do that you first add zero pairs, which leaves positive chips behind.

### Multiplying and dividing integers

- The product or quotient of two numbers with the **same sign** is positive.
- The product or quotient of two numbers with **different signs** is negative.

One way to see why $(-3)(-4) = 12$: look at the pattern $(-3)(2) = -6$, $(-3)(1) = -3$, $(-3)(0) = 0$. Each time the second factor decreases by $1$, the product increases by $3$, so $(-3)(-1) = 3$ and $(-3)(-4) = 12$. The rule also keeps the distributive property true.

Division by $0$ is undefined: there is no number $q$ with $0 \cdot q = 5$. But $0$ divided by a nonzero number is $0$.

For powers, the exponent applies only to what it touches. $(-2)^4 = (-2)(-2)(-2)(-2) = 16$, but $-2^4 = -(2^4) = -16$. An odd number of negative factors gives a negative product; an even number gives a positive product.

### Comparing rational numbers

On a horizontal number line, numbers increase from left to right. For negative numbers, the one closer to zero is greater: $-2 \gt -9$. To compare fractions, rewrite them with a common denominator or convert them to decimals.

## Worked examples

### Example 1: Adding with chips

1. $-8 + 5$: start with $8$ negative chips and $5$ positive chips. Remove $5$ zero pairs. Three negative chips remain, so the sum is $-3$.
2. $-6 + (-9)$: there are no zero pairs. All $15$ chips are negative, so the sum is $-15$.

### Example 2: Subtracting by adding the opposite

1. $4 - 11 = 4 + (-11) = -7$.
2. $-5 - (-12) = -5 + 12 = 7$.
3. An illustrative temperature reading rises from $-3^\circ\text{C}$ in the morning to $8^\circ\text{C}$ in the afternoon. The change is $8 - (-3) = 11$ degrees.

### Example 3: Products, quotients, and powers

1. $(-6)(4) = -24$ (different signs).
2. $(-7)(-3) = 21$ (same signs).
3. $-48 \div (-6) = 8$.
4. $(-2)^3 = -8$ (three negative factors), $(-2)^4 = 16$ (four negative factors), and $-2^4 = -16$.

### Example 4: Distance with absolute value

How far apart are $-4$ and $9$ on a number line? $\lvert 9 - (-4) \rvert = \lvert 13 \rvert = 13$. Subtracting in the other order gives $\lvert -4 - 9 \rvert = \lvert -13 \rvert = 13$, so the order does not matter for distance.

### Example 5: Ordering a mixed list

Order from least to greatest: $-1.5$, $\tfrac{3}{4}$, $-\tfrac{7}{4}$, $0$, $-\lvert -2 \rvert$, $\lvert -1 \rvert$.

1. Convert to decimals: $-1.5$, $0.75$, $-1.75$, $0$, $-2$, $1$.
2. Order: $-2 \lt -1.75 \lt -1.5 \lt 0 \lt 0.75 \lt 1$.
3. Answer: $-\lvert -2 \rvert$, $-\tfrac{7}{4}$, $-1.5$, $0$, $\tfrac{3}{4}$, $\lvert -1 \rvert$.

## Common misconceptions and boundary cases

- **"Two negatives make a positive" in addition.** That phrase describes multiplication. $-3 + (-5) = -8$, not $8$.
- **Bigger digit means bigger number.** For negatives the reverse holds: $-9 \lt -2$ because $-9$ is farther left.
- **Absolute value "makes everything positive."** It measures distance. $-\lvert -5 \rvert = -5$, because the negative sign outside the bars is applied after the absolute value.
- **Subtraction is commutative.** $3 - 8 = -5$ but $8 - 3 = 5$. Only distances, $\lvert a - b \rvert$, ignore order.
- **Powers of negatives.** $-2^4 = -16$ while $(-2)^4 = 16$. Use parentheses when substituting a negative number.
- **Dividing by zero.** $\tfrac{0}{5} = 0$, but $\tfrac{5}{0}$ is undefined, not $0$ and not "infinity" in ordinary arithmetic.

## Practice

1. Find $-14 + 9$.
2. Find $-7 - (-15)$.
3. Find $(-3)(-4)(-5)$.
4. Find $-72 \div 8$ and $0 \div (-5)$.
5. Order from least to greatest: $-0.4$, $-\tfrac{1}{2}$, $-\tfrac{3}{8}$, $\tfrac{1}{3}$.
6. In an illustrative dive, a diver at $-18$ m rises $7$ m and then descends $12$ m. What is the final depth?
7. Evaluate $\lvert -6 \rvert - \lvert 4 - 10 \rvert$.
8. Classify each number as whole, integer, rational, or irrational (list every set that applies): $-5$, $0.\overline{3}$, $\sqrt{9}$, $\sqrt{5}$.
