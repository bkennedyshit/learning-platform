# 42.3 — Pronouns — Subject, Object, Reflexive & Ne/Ci

*Back to [Subject_Plan](Subject_Plan) | [← 42.2](42.2---Nouns,-Articles,-Gender-&-Adjective-Agreement) | [42.4 →](42.4---Verb-System-—-Present,-Imperative-&-Reflexive-Verbs)*

> **Chapter goal:** Master all Italian pronoun types including the uniquely Italian particles *ne* and *ci*, and the double-pronoun (combined clitic) table.

---

## 1. Subject Pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|--------|---------|--------|
| 1st | *io* | *noi* |
| 2nd informal | *tu* | *voi* |
| 2nd formal | *Lei* (capital L) | *Loro* (rare; *voi* used in practice) |
| 3rd masc. | *lui / egli* (egli = literary) | *loro / essi* (essi = literary) |
| 3rd fem. | *lei / ella* (ella = literary) | *loro / esse* (esse = literary) |

> **Subject pronouns are optional** in Italian — verb endings encode person and number, so the pronoun is dropped unless needed for emphasis or clarity: *Parlo italiano* (I speak Italian), not usually *Io parlo italiano* unless contrasting.

---

## 2. Direct Object Pronouns

| Person | Unstressed (clitic) | Stressed (disjunctive) |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| me | *mi* | *me* |
| you (sg.) | *ti* | *te* |
| him/it (m.) | *lo* (*l'*) | *lui* |
| her/it (f.) | *la* (*l'*) | *lei* |
| us | *ci* | *noi* |
| you (pl.) | *vi* | *voi* |
| them (m.) | *li* | *loro* |
| them (f.) | *le* | *loro* |

---

## 3. Indirect Object Pronouns (a + person)

| Person | Unstressed | Stressed |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| to me | *mi* | *a me* |
| to you (sg.) | *ti* | *a te* |
| to him/her | ***gli / le*** | *a lui / a lei* |
| to us | *ci* | *a noi* |
| to you (pl.) | *vi* | *a voi* |
| to them | ***gli*** (modern) / *loro* (literary) | *a loro* |

> **Italian gli = to him AND to them (masc.)** in modern usage. This merging is a key difference from French (where *lui* = to him/her, *leur* = to them).

---

## 4. Double Pronouns (Combined Clitics)

When a direct and indirect object pronoun appear together, the indirect goes first and changes form:

| Indirect | + lo | + la | + li | + le | + ne |
|----------|------|------|------|------|------|
| *mi* → *me* | *me lo* | *me la* | *me li* | *me le* | *me ne* |
| *ti* → *te* | *te lo* | *te la* | *te li* | *te le* | *te ne* |
| *gli/le* → *glie-* | *glielo* | *gliela* | *glieli* | *gliele* | *gliene* |
| *ci* → *ce* | *ce lo* | *ce la* | *ce li* | *ce le* | *ce ne* |
| *vi* → *ve* | *ve lo* | *ve la* | *ve li* | *ve le* | *ve ne* |

Examples:
- *Te lo do.* — I give it to you.
- *Glielo spiego.* — I explain it to him/her.
- *Ce ne sono molti.* — There are many of them (for us).

---

## 5. The Particle NE

*Ne* is one of the most distinctively Italian features — it replaces phrases with *di + noun*:

### 5.1 Partitive ne (replaces "some/any of it/them")
- *Quanti libri hai? Ne ho tre.* — How many books do you have? I have three (of them).
- *Vuoi del vino? Sì, ne voglio un po'.* — Do you want wine? Yes, I want a bit (of it).

### 5.2 Genitive ne (replaces "of it/them" after verbs taking di)
- *Sono contento della situazione. Ne sono contento.* — I'm happy with it.
- *Parla spesso dell'università. Ne parla spesso.* — He talks about it often.

> **Agreement:** When *ne* replaces a partitive direct object in compound tenses, the past participle agrees with the implied noun: *Ne ho comprate tre.* (I bought three of them — *tre* books, fem. pl.)

---

## 6. The Particle CI

*Ci* has two distinct functions:

### 6.1 Locative ci (replaces "there / in/at that place")
- *Vai a Roma? Sì, ci vado.* — Are you going to Rome? Yes, I'm going there.
- *C'è un problema.* / *Ci sono molte persone.* — There is a problem. / There are many people.

### 6.2 Reflexive ci (1st person plural reflexive)
- *Ci alziamo alle otto.* — We get up at eight. (reflexive use of *alzarsi*)

### 6.3 Reciprocal ci
- *Ci vediamo domani.* — We'll see each other tomorrow.
- *Ci amiamo.* — We love each other.

---

## 7. Reflexive Verbs (Verbi Riflessivi)

Italian reflexive verbs are conjugated with reflexive pronouns:

| Person | Pronoun | Example: *lavarsi* (to wash oneself) |
|--------|---------|-------------------------------------|
| io | *mi* | *mi lavo* |
| tu | *ti* | *ti lavi* |
| lui/lei/Lei | *si* | *si lava* |
| noi | *ci* | *ci laviamo* |
| voi | *vi* | *vi lavate* |
| loro | *si* | *si lavano* |

**Common reflexive verbs:**
- *svegliarsi* (wake up), *alzarsi* (get up), *vestirsi* (get dressed), *lavarsi* (wash oneself)
- *sentirsi* (feel), *chiamarsi* (be called), *trovarsi* (be located), *ricordarsi* (remember)
- *arrabbiarsi* (get angry), *annoiarsi* (get bored), *preoccuparsi* (worry), *innamorarsi* (fall in love)

> **All reflexive verbs use essere as auxiliary** in passato prossimo and other compound tenses. Past participle agrees with subject: *Mi sono svegliato/a.*
