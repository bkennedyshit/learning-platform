# 36.2 — Nouns, Articles & Gender

> *"Gender in Spanish is not about biology — it's about grammar. Once you accept that, it stops being confusing and becomes a system."*

Spanish grammatical gender is the concept that trips up English speakers most in the early stages. English has no grammatical gender — *the* is *the* regardless of what follows. Spanish assigns every noun a gender — masculine or feminine — and that gender cascades through every article, adjective, and pronoun that agrees with it. Think of it as a **type system**: the noun declares its type, and everything that modifies it must match.

---

## 🎯 Learning Objectives

By the end of this chapter you will be able to:

1. Assign the correct gender to nouns based on ending and category patterns.
2. Use the full article system: *el/la/los/las* (definite), *un/una/unos/unas* (indefinite), *lo* (neuter).
3. Apply gender agreement to adjectives correctly in singular and plural.
4. Handle the special exceptions: *el agua*, *el hambre*, and masculine articles with feminine nouns.
5. Form plurals correctly for all noun types.

---

## 📚 1. Grammatical Gender — The Type System

Every Spanish noun is either **masculine** (M) or **feminine** (F). This is grammatical, not biological. The gender of a noun is a property of the word, not the object.

*el libro* (the book) — masculine
*la mesa* (the table) — feminine
*el problema* (the problem) — masculine (despite -a ending)
*la mano* (the hand) — feminine (despite -o ending)

> **Key mindset shift:** There is no logical reason for most genders. Learn the gender with the noun, not separately. When you learn *mesa*, don't memorize "*mesa* = table, it's feminine." Memorize "*la mesa* = the table." The article is part of the word.

---

## 📚 2. Gender Patterns — The Tendencies

These are strong tendencies (not absolute rules). When in doubt, trust them — they're right ~80% of the time.

### Usually Masculine

| Pattern | Examples | Exceptions |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Nouns ending in **-o** | *el libro, el gato, el vino* | *la mano* (hand), *la foto* (photo), *la moto* (motorbike) |
| Nouns ending in **-or** | *el color, el calor, el amor* | *la flor* (flower), *la labor* (work) |
| Nouns ending in **-aje** | *el viaje, el garaje, el mensaje* | Very few |
| Nouns ending in **-án, -ón, -és** | *el refrán, el botón, el inglés* | — |
| Days, months, numbers, languages | *el lunes, el enero, el español* | — |
| Male beings | *el hombre, el padre, el rey* | — |

### Usually Feminine

| Pattern | Examples | Exceptions |
|---------|---------|-----------|
| Nouns ending in **-a** | *la casa, la mesa, la hora* | *el problema, el tema, el clima, el día, el mapa, el planeta, el idioma, el sistema* (Greek-origin -ma words) |
| Nouns ending in **-ción, -sión** | *la nación, la televisión, la canción* | Very few |
| Nouns ending in **-dad, -tad, -tud** | *la ciudad, la libertad, la virtud* | — |
| Nouns ending in **-ez, -eza** | *la vejez, la belleza* | — |
| Nouns ending in **-umbre** | *la costumbre, la lumbre* | — |
| Female beings | *la mujer, la madre, la reina* | — |
| Letters of the alphabet | *la a, la efe* | — |

> **The -ma trap:** Greek-origin masculine words ending in *-ma* are among the most common errors. Memorize this list: *el problema, el tema, el sistema, el programa, el clima, el idioma, el drama, el poema, el fantasma, el esquema, el dilema, el enigma, el trauma*.

## 📚 3. The Article System

### 3.1 Definite Articles (the)

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|----------|---------|
| **Singular** | el | la |
| **Plural** | los | las |

*el libro → los libros*
*la mesa → las mesas*

**When to use definite articles (differs from English!):**
- With nouns used in a **general or abstract sense**: *Me gusta **el** café.* (I like coffee — coffee in general, not a specific cup)
- With titles when talking about someone: ***El** señor García está aquí.* (but NOT when addressing them: *Buenos días, señor García.*)
- With days of the week (meaning "on ___"): ***El** lunes voy al gimnasio.* (On Mondays I go to the gym)
- With body parts and clothing (instead of possessives): *Me duele **la** cabeza.* (My head hurts)

**When NOT to use (unlike English):**
- After *ser* with professions: *Soy ingeniero.* (not *soy **el** ingeniero*)
- With unspecified quantities: *Tengo hambre.* (not *tengo **la** hambre*)

### 3.2 Indefinite Articles (a/an, some)

| | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|----------|---------|
| **Singular** | un | una |
| **Plural** | unos | unas |

*un libro → unos libros*
*una mesa → unas mesas*

*Unos/unas* = some, a few — used for approximate quantities:
*Tengo **unos** veinte años.* (I'm about twenty years old.)

### 3.3 The Neuter Article: LO

*Lo* is used to nominalize adjectives and to form abstract concepts. There is no neuter gender for nouns — only for this article.

*Lo + adjective* = "the ___ thing/part/aspect"
- ***Lo** bueno es que aprendemos.* (The good thing is that we're learning.)
- ***Lo** difícil del español es el subjuntivo.* (The difficult part of Spanish is the subjunctive.)
- ***Lo** más importante es practicar.* (The most important thing is to practice.)

### 3.4 Special Case: EL + Feminine Noun

When a feminine noun **beginning with stressed A- or HA-** is immediately preceded by an article, use **el** (not la) in the singular — to avoid the vowel clash. **This does not change the noun's gender** — it is still feminine.

*el agua* (the water) → *las aguas* (the waters) ← plural reverts to *las*
*el hambre* (the hunger)
*el águila* (the eagle)
*el hacha* (the axe)

> *El agua está fría.* (El for article, but fría is feminine — the adjective still agrees with the gender!)

## 📚 4. Noun Plurals

| Noun ending | Plural rule | Example |
|------------|------------|---------|
| Vowel (a, e, i, o, u) | + **s** | *casa → casas, libro → libros, café → cafés* |
| Consonant (not s) | + **es** | *ciudad → ciudades, árbol → árboles, canción → canciones* |
| Ends in **s** (unstressed final syllable) | No change | *el lunes → los lunes, la crisis → las crisis* |
| Ends in **z** | Change **z → c** then + **es** | *voz → voces, lápiz → lápices, vez → veces* |

**Accent mark changes in plurals:**
- Some words lose their accent in the plural (stress shifts back to penultimate): *nación → naciones*, *canción → canciones*
- Some words gain an accent in the plural (to maintain stress): *joven → jóvenes*, *examen → exámenes*

## 📚 5. Gender Agreement — How It Propagates

Gender doesn't stay in the noun — it propagates to every adjective, article, and determiner that modifies it.

*el libro viejo* (the old book) M sg
*la mesa vieja* (the old table) F sg
*los libros viejos* (the old books) M pl
*las mesas viejas* (the old tables) F pl

**Adjective endings:**

| Adjective type | M sg | F sg | M pl | F pl |
|---------------|------|------|------|------|
| Ends in **-o** | *viejo* | *vieja* | *viejos* | *viejas* |
| Ends in **-e** or consonant | *inteligente, fácil* | same | *inteligentes, fáciles* | same |
| Ends in **-or, -ón, -án, -és** | *hablador* | *habladora* | *habladores* | *habladoras* |
| Nationality adj. ending in consonant | *español* | *española* | *españoles* | *españolas* |

**Mixed gender plural:** If a group contains both masculine and feminine nouns, the masculine plural is used:
*El libro y la mesa son viejos.* (The book and the table are old — *viejos* because mixed or all-male)

## ⚠️ 6. Common Errors

| Error | Wrong | Right |
|-------|-------|-------|
| Greek -ma words | *la problema* | ***el** problema* |
| La mano / la foto | *el mano, el foto* | ***la** mano, **la** foto* |
| Forgetting lo + adj | *la cosa buena es…* | ***lo** bueno es…* |
| El with stressed feminine a- | *la agua* | ***el** agua* (but *las aguas*) |
| Not propagating gender | *el mesa viejo* | ***la** mesa vieja* |
