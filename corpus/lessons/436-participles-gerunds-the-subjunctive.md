# 43.6 — Participles, Gerunds & The Subjunctive

*Back to [Subject_Plan](Subject_Plan) | [← 43.5](43.5---The-Perfect-System-&-Infinitives) | [43.7 →](43.7---Advanced-Syntax-—-Subordinate-Clauses-&-Rhetorical-Style)*

> **Chapter goal:** Master all four Latin participles, the ablative absolute construction, the gerund and gerundive, and the four tenses of the subjunctive mood — the tools that unlock Caesar's prose and all advanced Latin syntax.

---

## 1. The Four Latin Participles

A participle is a **verbal adjective** — it has verb properties (tense, voice, takes objects) and adjective properties (agrees with a noun in gender/case/number).

| Participle | Tense/Voice | Formation | Time relative to main verb |
|-----------|------------|-----------|--------------------------|
| **Present active** | present, active | present stem + *-ns/-ntis* | simultaneous |
| **Perfect passive** | perfect, passive | 4th principal part (PPP) | prior |
| **Future active** | future, active | PPP stem + *-ūrus/-ūra/-ūrum* | subsequent |
| **Future passive** (gerundive) | future, passive | present stem + *-ndus/-nda/-ndum* | obligation |

### 1.1 Present Active Participle (PAP)

Formation: present stem + **-ns** (nom. sg.) / **-ntis** (gen. sg.) → declines like a 3rd declension adjective (one-termination)

| Verb | PAP nom. sg. | PAP gen. sg. | Meaning |
|------|------------|------------|---------|
| *amō* | *amāns* | *amantis* | loving |
| *moneō* | *monēns* | *monentis* | warning |
| *agō* | *agēns* | *agentis* | driving/doing |
| *audiō* | *audiēns* | *audientis* | hearing |
| *esse* | *—* | *—* | (no present active participle for *esse*) |

Example: *Puellam ambulantem vīdī.* — I saw the girl walking. (*ambulantem* — acc. f. sg., agrees with *puellam*)

> **Ablative singular exception:** PAP uses **-e** (not -ī) in the ablative singular when used as a pure adjective. But when used as a circumstantial participle or in ablative absolute, it uses **-ī**.

### 1.2 Perfect Passive Participle (PPP)

The 4th principal part — already covered in [43.5](43.5---The-Perfect-System-&-Infinitives). Declines exactly like 1st/2nd declension adjective (*bonus/bona/bonum*).

*Hostēs victōs Rōmam dūxit.* — He led the conquered enemies to Rome.
(*victōs* = PPP of *vincō*, m. acc. pl., agrees with *hostēs*)

### 1.3 Future Active Participle (FAP)

Formation: PPP stem + **-ūrus/-ūra/-ūrum** → declines like *bonus/-a/-um*

| Verb | PPP | FAP m. nom. sg. | Meaning |
|------|-----|----------------|---------|
| *amō* | *amātum* | *amātūrus* | about to love |
| *agō* | *āctum* | *āctūrus* | about to do |
| *veniō* | *ventum* | *ventūrus* | about to come |
| *esse* | (—) | *futūrus* | about to be |

Primary use: **active periphrastic** (see §3) and in indirect statement (future infinitive: *amātūrum esse*).

### 1.4 Future Passive Participle / Gerundive

Formation: present stem + **-ndus/-nda/-ndum** → declines like *bonus/-a/-um*

As a standalone adjective: expresses **obligation or necessity** (the thing must be done):
*Carthāgō dēlenda est.* — Carthage must be destroyed. (Cato's famous phrase)
*Amandus est.* — He must be loved. / He is to be loved.

When used with a dative of agent, expresses "X must be done by Y":
*Nōbīs labōrandum est.* — It must be worked by us. / We must work.

## 2. The Ablative Absolute

**The single most frequent participial construction in Caesar and all classical prose.** A noun/pronoun + participle both in the **ablative case**, grammatically independent of the main clause — expressing time, cause, condition, or concession.

### Formation

```
[Ablative noun/pronoun] + [Ablative participle agreeing in gender/case/number]
                               while / when / since / although / after / if...
```

### Examples

*Caesare duce, mīlitēs fortiter pugnāvērunt.*
→ Caesar being the leader, the soldiers fought bravely.
→ = **With Caesar as their leader** / **Under Caesar's command**, the soldiers fought bravely.
(*Caesare* abl. noun; *duce* abl. noun-predicate — no participle needed when two nouns)

*Urbe captā, hostēs fūgērunt.*
→ The city having been captured, the enemies fled.
→ = **After the city was captured**, the enemies fled.
(*Urbe* abl. f. sg.; *captā* PPP of *capiō*, abl. f. sg., agrees)

*Sole oriente, exercitus profectus est.*
→ The sun rising, the army set out.
→ = **As the sun rose** / **At sunrise**, the army set out.
(*Sole* abl. m. sg.; *oriente* PAP of *orior*, abl. m. sg., agrees)

> **Rule:** The noun/pronoun in the ablative absolute must NOT be the subject or object of the main clause. It is grammatically independent — that is the point. If it referred to the main subject, Latin would use a participle agreeing with that subject directly.

---

## 3. Active and Passive Periphrastic

### Active Periphrastic: *about to do* (futūrum esse + FAP)

FAP + *esse* (any tense) = expresses intention or impending action:
*Caesar hostēs oppugnātūrus erat.* — Caesar was about to attack the enemies.

### Passive Periphrastic: *must be done* (gerundive + esse)

Gerundive + *esse* + dative of agent = obligation:
*Nōbīs Rōma dēfendenda est.* — Rome must be defended by us. / We must defend Rome.

## 4. The Gerund

The **gerund** is a **verbal noun** (neuter, 2nd declension, occurs only in gen./dat./acc./abl. — never nom./voc.; the infinitive serves as nom./voc.). It expresses the act of doing something.

Formation: present stem + **-ndī/-ndō/-ndum/-ndō** (gen./dat./acc./abl.)

| Case | *amō* | *agō* | Meaning |
|------|-------|-------|---------|
| Gen. | *amandī* | *agendī* | of loving / of doing |
| Dat. | *amandō* | *agendō* | for loving / for doing |
| Acc. | *amandum* | *agendum* | loving / doing (after prepositions: *ad amandum*) |
| Abl. | *amandō* | *agendō* | by loving / in doing |

*Cupidus discendī* — eager for learning (gen.)
*Ad discendum vēnit.* — He came for the purpose of learning. (acc. with *ad*)
*Discendō sapientia acquīritur.* — Wisdom is acquired by learning. (abl.)

## 5. The Subjunctive Mood — Overview

Latin subjunctive does not translate into a single English construction — it expresses **possibility, will, wish, doubt, purpose, result, fear, condition, reported thought, and subordination**. It appears in both independent and subordinate clauses.

**Four tenses of the Latin subjunctive:**

| Tense | Formation | Primary use |
|-------|-----------|------------|
| **Present** | present stem + subjunctive sign | in primary sequence |
| **Imperfect** | present infinitive + personal endings | in secondary sequence |
| **Perfect** | perfect stem + *-eri-* + endings | in primary sequence (completed) |
| **Pluperfect** | perfect stem + *-isse-* + personal endings | in secondary sequence (completed) |

---

## 6. Forming the Subjunctive

### Present Subjunctive

**Sign:** 1st conj. uses **-ē-**; 2nd/3rd/4th use **-ā-** (mnemonic: **"she" / "we** fear lies**"** — SHE for 1st: s → **e**; WE FEAR LIES for 2nd-4th: **ā**)

| | 1st (*amō*) | 2nd (*moneō*) | 3rd (*agō*) | 4th (*audiō*) |
|--|------------|--------------|------------|--------------|
| sg. 1 | *amem* | *moneam* | *agam* | *audiam* |
| sg. 2 | *amēs* | *moneās* | *agās* | *audiās* |
| sg. 3 | *amet* | *moneat* | *agat* | *audiat* |
| pl. 1 | *amēmus* | *moneāmus* | *agāmus* | *audiāmus* |
| pl. 2 | *amētis* | *moneātis* | *agātis* | *audiātis* |
| pl. 3 | *ament* | *moneant* | *agant* | *audiant* |

*esse* present subjunctive: *sim, sīs, sit, sīmus, sītis, sint*

### Imperfect Subjunctive

**Formation:** present **active infinitive** + active personal endings (easiest tense to form in Latin)

*amāre* + endings → *amārem, amārēs, amāret, amārēmus, amārētis, amārent*
*esse* → *essem, essēs, esset, essēmus, essētis, essent*
*agere* → *agerem, agerēs, ageret...*
*audīre* → *audīrem, audīrēs, audīret...*

### Perfect Subjunctive Active

**Formation:** perfect stem + **-eri-** + personal endings
*amāverim, amāverīs, amāverit, amāverīmus, amāverītis, amāverint*

### Pluperfect Subjunctive Active

**Formation:** perfect stem + **-isse-** + personal endings
*amāvissem, amāvissēs, amāvisset, amāvissēmus, amāvissētis, amāvissent*

---

## 7. Sequence of Tenses

Which subjunctive tense to use depends on the tense of the **main verb**:

| Main verb tense | Called | Subordinate subjunctive for... | |
|----------------|--------|-------------------------------|--|
| Present, Future, Future Perfect | **Primary sequence** | simultaneous/subsequent action → **Present subj.** | prior action → **Perfect subj.** |
| Imperfect, Perfect, Pluperfect | **Secondary sequence** | simultaneous/subsequent → **Imperfect subj.** | prior → **Pluperfect subj.** |

---

## 8. Key Subjunctive Constructions

### Purpose Clause (*ut/nē* + subjunctive)
*Vēnit ut tē vidēret.* — He came in order to see you. (imperfect subj. — secondary sequence)
*Venit ut tē videat.* — He comes in order to see you. (present subj. — primary sequence)
*Fūgit nē caperētur.* — He fled so that he would not be captured.

### Result Clause (*ita/tam/adeo...ut* + subjunctive)
*Tam fortis erat ut hostēs fugerent.* — He was so brave that the enemies fled.
(Note: result clauses often have a signal word in the main clause: *tam, ita, adeo, tantus, talis*)

### Indirect Question (*-ne/num/cur/quid/ubi...* + subjunctive)
*Rogāvit ubi essēs.* — He asked where you were. (imperfect subj. — secondary sequence)
*Nescīō quid faciās.* — I don't know what you are doing. (present subj. — primary)

### Fear Clauses (*timeō nē/ut* + subjunctive)
*Timeō nē veniat.* — I fear that he is coming. (nē = that, in fear clause)
*Timeō ut veniat.* — I fear that he is not coming. (ut = that...not, in fear clause — counterintuitive!)

---

## 9. Common Misconceptions / Where Students Fail

1. **Forgetting the 1st conj. present subjunctive uses -ē- not -ā-:** *amem* (not *amam*). The mnemonic "SHE/WE FEAR LIES" helps.
2. **Ablative absolute with a noun that IS the main subject:** *"Caesare proficīscente, Caesar mīlitēs dūxit"* is illogical — Caesar cannot be in the ablative absolute if he's also the main subject.
3. **Confusing gerund and gerundive:** Gerund = verbal noun (no noun to modify); gerundive = verbal adjective (agrees with a noun). *"Amandī causa"* (gerund — for the sake of loving) vs. *"Puellae amandae"* (gerundive — the girl must be loved / for the sake of loving the girl).
4. **Wrong sequence of tenses:** Primary-sequence main verb requires present or perfect subjunctive, not imperfect. *"Venit ut tē vidēret"* is secondary sequence (past main verb); if main verb is present, use *videat*.
5. **Fear clause direction:** *nē* in a fear clause means "that" (the feared outcome), not "lest." *timeō nē veniat* = I fear he IS coming.
